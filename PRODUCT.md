# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Readers first: students, autodidacts, and scholars of intellectual history who want to actually read classical works — Greek, Latin, Sanskrit, Urdu, Kawi, and more — in English, free, without academic paywalls or PDF scans. They arrive curious, often from a search or a shared link to a single work, on any device from a phone on a commute to a large desktop monitor. Secondary audience: AI agents and researchers using the corpus through the engine surface (MCP, llms.txt, the eval) — machines are first-class readers here. A third, quieter audience: funders, researchers, and high-signal contacts who will judge the project by the artifact itself (the project's rule is show-don't-tell; the site is the pitch).

## Product Purpose

Falsafa is a free, open library of ~2,000 classical works across nine languages and twenty-five centuries, carried into English — paired with a citation-grounded ontology (the atlas) extracted from the source texts: who cites whom, with what stance, where ideas like justice and dharma appear, every claim anchored to a verbatim quotable paragraph. The reading library is the welcome mat; the ontology is the durable asset. Success looks like: people devour the texts, the atlas becomes the way people traverse the corpus, and every claim on the site can be clicked through to the exact paragraph that proves it.

## Positioning

The only library where twenty-five centuries of thought — across nine languages — is both readable in English and mapped: every citation, figure, and idea traced to the verbatim paragraph that carries it.

## Conversion & proof

- Primary CTA: start reading a work (enter the library).
- Secondary CTA: explore the atlas (traverse the graph of figures, ideas, citations).
- The line a visitor remembers after 10 seconds: "Twenty-five centuries of thought, carried into English — and mapped."
- Belief ladder: (1) this is a real, serious library, not a scrape — the texts are complete and readable; (2) the scholarship is honest — claims are anchored to verbatim quotes, coverage is stated plainly; (3) this is alive and growing — the atlas updates as the harvest advances; (4) I can use this — read, cite, plug my own AI into it.
- Proof on hand: the corpus itself (works/chapters/languages counts drawn live from the manifest); the ontology harvest (entities, citations, quote-anchored evidence, drawn live from the synthesis artifacts); the eval and thesis pages on the engine surface. No testimonials — the artifact is the proof.

## Brand Personality

Learned, warm, unhurried. The voice of a good university library reading room, not a startup: editorial confidence in the tradition of the NYRB, LRB, and Aeon. Serif-led, typography-first, decoration only with intent. It should evoke quiet trust and the pleasure of reading — never urgency, never salesmanship. The multilingual nature is part of the personality: Nastaliq, Devanagari, and polytonic Greek are brand material, not edge cases.

## Anti-references

- SaaS landing pages: hero metrics with gradient accents, identical card grids, uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, numbered section scaffolding.
- AI-generated "editorial" pastiche: italic display serif + mono labels + ruled three-column restraint applied as costume. Falsafa is a real library; the editorial register must be earned by content, not worn as an aesthetic.
- Academic-database dreariness (JSTOR, Perseus's own UI): functional but joyless. Free and open should not mean utilitarian.
- Knowledge-graph sci-fi: glowing force-directed node blobs as decoration. The graph is scholarship, not a screensaver.

## Design Principles

- Show, don't tell: the library and the atlas are their own pitch; never a marketing claim where a real number or a real quote could stand.
- No claim without a verbatim quote: every edge, figure, and stance shown on the site is backed by a paragraph-anchored quotation the reader can click through to.
- Honest about coverage: the harvest is partial and skewed; the design states what is mapped and what is not, and grows gracefully as data arrives.
- The text is the interface: typography does the work; chrome recedes; the nine scripts of the corpus are treated as first-class visual material.
- Built for scale and change: pages derive from live data (manifest, beacon, synthesis artifacts) — never hardwired counts, never snapshot-frozen views.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA minimum: body text ≥4.5:1 contrast in all three themes (warm light, dark, sepia); 44×44px touch targets; full keyboard navigation on reader and atlas surfaces; ARIA landmarks per the design system; `prefers-reduced-motion` alternatives for all animation. Multilingual scripts (Nastaliq, Devanagari, polytonic Greek) must render correctly with proper fonts and line-height accommodation — script support is an accessibility concern here, not a nicety. The site must work fully from 320px phones to 2560px monitors.
