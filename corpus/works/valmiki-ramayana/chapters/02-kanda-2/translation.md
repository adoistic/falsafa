**2.1.1**  Now, after some time had passed, king Daśaratha, that joy of the Raghu line, spoke to his son Bharata, the son of Kaikeyī.

**2.1.2**  "My son, this man who is staying here is the son of the king of the Kekayas — your maternal uncle Yudhājit has come, O hero, to take you away."

**2.1.3**  Having heard this from Daśaratha, Bharata, son of Kaikeyī, prepared to depart, accompanied by Śatrughna.

**2.1.4**  Taking leave of his father, and of Rāma who never wearied in action, and of his mothers as well, that best of men, the valiant Bharata, departed together with Śatrughna.

**2.1.5**  Yudhājit, overjoyed at receiving Bharata along with Śatrughna, that hero entered his own city, and his father was greatly pleased.

**2.1.6**  There Bharata dwelt together with his brother, honoured with great hospitality, and fondly indulged by his maternal uncle Aśvapati with a father's affection.

**2.1.7**  Even while dwelling there and being satisfied in every desire, the two heroic brothers kept remembering the aged king Daśaratha.

**2.1.8**  The king too, of great majesty, remembered his two sons who were away — both Bharata and Śatrughna, comparable to Mahendra and Varuṇa.

**2.1.9**  Yet all four of his sons, those bulls among men, were dear to him alike, as though they were four arms issuing from his own body.

**2.1.10**  Yet among them all, the great-spirited Rāma, who gave delight to his father, excelled in virtues, as the Self-born Brahmā excels among all beings.

**2.1.11**  After Bharata had departed, Rāma and the mighty Lakṣmaṇa honoured their father, who was like unto a god.

**2.1.12**  Placing his father's command foremost, Rāma, whose soul was set on righteousness, carried out all the duties toward the citizens — whatever was pleasing and whatever was beneficial.

**2.1.13**  With the utmost self-restraint he performed the duties owed to his mothers, and at each proper time he attended to the duties owed to his teachers.

**2.1.14**  By Rāma's character and conduct, Daśaratha was pleased, as were the brāhmaṇas, the townspeople, and all the inhabitants of the kingdom.

**2.1.15**  For he was ever tranquil of soul and spoke with gentle words first; even when addressed harshly, he returned no harsh reply.

**2.1.16**  He was pleased by even a single service however slight, and through the mastery of his own self he did not remember even a hundred injuries done to him.

**2.1.17**  He was ever in conversation with good men who were advanced in conduct, knowledge, and age, even in the intervals between his exercises in archery.

**2.1.18**  He was of noble birth, virtuous, undepressed in spirit, truthful in speech, upright, and well-trained by elder twice-born men who understood righteousness and expediency.

**2.1.19**  He understood the true nature of dharma, artha, and kāma; he was endowed with memory and swift wit; he was well-versed and expert in worldly convention and social conduct.

**2.1.20**  He was learned in the śāstras and grateful for benefits received; he was skilled in reading the characters of men, and discerning in the proper exercise of restraint and favour.

**2.1.21**  He understood the means for raising revenue, and was knowledgeable about the workings of expenditure; he had attained eminence among the whole body of sciences, including the mixed branches of learning.

**2.1.22**  He grasped both artha and dharma together, was devoted to his own ease yet never indolent; he understood the principles and divisions of the practical arts.

**2.1.23**  He was accomplished in the mounting and training of elephants and horses; he was the foremost among those learned in the science of archery, and acknowledged as an atiratha — a supreme chariot warrior — in the world.

**2.1.24**  He was a bold attacker and a powerful striker, skilled in the movement of armies, and unconquerable in battle even by the wrath of gods and asuras alike.

**2.1.25**  He was free from envy, had conquered anger, was not arrogant, not jealous, did not belittle any being, and was not enslaved by the force of circumstance.

**2.1.26**  Thus endowed with the highest virtues, this son of the king was honoured among the people, respected in all three worlds for his earth-like quality of forbearance, equal to Bṛhaspati in wisdom, and equal to the lord of Śacī in valour.

**2.1.27**  And so Rāma shone forth with virtues that were dear to all people and that inspired the father's love, blazing like the sun in his rays.

**2.1.28**  The earth itself desired him as her lord — him who was endowed with such conduct and character, whose valour was irresistible, who was comparable to the guardians of the worlds.

**2.1.29**  Beholding his son endowed with these many incomparable virtues, king Daśaratha, that scorcher of foes, fell to pondering deeply.

**2.1.30**  "This supreme joy that revolves within my heart — when indeed shall I behold my beloved son anointed as crown prince?"

**2.1.31**  "For he desires the welfare of the world, he has compassion for all beings, and he is dearer to the world than I am — like rain-bearing Parjanya is dearer than those who receive his rain."

**2.1.32**  "Equal to Yama and Śakra in valour, equal to Bṛhaspati in wisdom, equal to a mountain in steadfastness — and yet surpassing even me in virtue."

**2.1.33**  "Having seen my own son governing this entire earth, and at this age, may I thereby attain to heaven."

**2.1.34**  Considering him to be endowed with all these gathered virtues, the great king deliberated with his ministers and resolved to appoint him crown prince.

**2.1.35**  The lord of the earth summoned the chief men dwelling in many different cities and separate provinces of the land.

**2.1.36**  Then upon the various seats distributed by the king's command, the assembled kings took their places, facing toward the king himself.

**2.1.37**  The king shone forth, surrounded by those honoured and humble kings, the dwellers in cities, and the men from the provinces seated nearby — even as the blessed thousand-eyed Indra shines surrounded by the immortals.

**2.2.1**  Then the lord of the earth addressed the entire assembly and spoke this unequalled speech, beneficial and heartening.

**2.2.2**  With his great voice, deep and reverberating like the sound of a kettledrum, the king thundered like a rain-cloud.

**2.2.3**  "I, who have guarded this world that was protected by the former kings of the Ikṣvāku line, now desire to yoke it to greater prosperity — this entire world that is worthy of happiness."

**2.2.4**  "By me also, following the path walked by those of old, the subjects have been protected constantly, without fatigue, to the full extent of my power."

**2.2.5**  "This body of mine has grown old in the shade of this white royal umbrella, while I wandered about for the welfare of the whole world."

**2.2.6**  "Having lived many thousands of years of life, I now desire rest for this aged body of mine."

**2.2.7**  "I am weary from bearing this heavy burden of dharma for the world — a burden invested with royal dignity, difficult to carry for those who have not conquered their senses."

**2.2.8**  "Thus I desire rest, having set my son in care of the people, and with the assent of all these eminent twice-born men assembled here."

**2.2.9**  "For my eldest son has been born with all my virtues — Rāma, equal to Purandara in valour, the conqueror of enemy citadels."

**2.2.10**  "With joy I intend to invest that best of those who uphold dharma, that bull among men, with the dignity of crown prince — even as the moon is joined with the asterism Puṣya."

**2.2.11**  "That lord of fortune, the elder brother of Lakṣmaṇa, is a fitting master for you — one by whose lordship even the three worlds would be better governed."

**2.2.12**  "Having forthwith yoked this earth to this greater good, and having entrusted it to that son, I shall be free of toil."

**2.2.13**  The assembled kings, joyful, applauded the king as he spoke thus — like peacocks crying out in acclaim of a great rain-bearing cloud that thunders.

**2.2.14**  Having understood in full the intent of that king who was learned in dharma and artha, and having known the aged king Daśaratha's mind, they said:

**2.2.15**  "O king, you are aged, having lived many thousands of years; therefore anoint Rāma as crown prince, O lord of the earth."

**2.2.16**  Having heard those words pleasing to their hearts, the king, as if wishing to enquire further and not yet knowing their full mind, spoke these words.

**2.2.17**  "How is it that while I am righteously governing the earth, you all wish to see my son as crown prince?"

**2.2.18**  They, along with the citizens and the people of the provinces, addressed that great-souled king: "O king, your son possesses many auspicious virtues."

**2.2.19**  "Rāma, equal to Śakra in divine qualities and true in his valour, surpasses indeed all the Ikṣvākus, O lord of the people."

**2.2.20**  "Rāma is a good man in the world, devoted to true dharma, knowing righteousness, true to his word, of excellent character, and free from envy."

**2.2.21**  "He is forbearing, a conciliator, gentle, grateful, master of his senses, soft-spoken, firm of mind, ever auspicious, and without envy."

**2.2.22**  "That Rāghava speaks kindly to all beings and speaks the truth; he is an attendant upon brāhmaṇas who are greatly learned and advanced in age."

**2.2.23**  "By reason of this, his incomparable glory, fame, and splendour grow here; and he is proficient in all the weapons of gods, asuras, and men."

**2.2.24**  "Whenever he goes into battle for the sake of a village or the city, going along with Saumitri (Lakṣmaṇa), he never returns without victory."

**2.2.25**  "Returning from battle, whether on elephant or chariot, he always enquires after the welfare of the townspeople as though they were his own kin."

**2.2.26**  "Regarding their sons, sacred fires, wives, servants and pupils — fully, in proper order, like a father with his own born sons."

**2.2.27**  "'Are your pupils diligently attending you, equipped for their duties?' — thus that tiger among men, Rāma, always addresses us."

**2.2.28**  "In the misfortunes of men he is greatly grieved, and at all their festivals he rejoices like a father."

**2.2.29**  "A speaker of truth, a great bowman, a servant of elders, master of his senses — by good fortune this Rāghava has been born as your son in a good hour; by good fortune you, endowed with the virtues of your son, are like Kaśyapa blessed with Mārīca."

**2.2.30**  "All the people in the kingdom and in the royal city pray for the strength, health, and long life of Rāma, that self-knowing one."

**2.2.31**  "The people of the inner and outer city, the town-dwellers and villagers, women old and young, morning and evening with concentrated mind —"

**2.2.32**  "— they bow in reverence to all the gods for the sake of the glorious Rāma; O lord, may their prayers be fulfilled by your grace."

**2.2.33**  "We wish to see Rāma, dark-complexioned as the blue lotus, the vanquisher of all enemies, your best of sons, established as crown prince."

**2.2.34**  "That son of yours, who is like unto the god of gods, who is devoted to the welfare of all the world — you, O granter of boons, should swiftly and joyfully anoint him who is endowed with nobility, for our own welfare."

**2.3.1**  Having received from all of them their lotus-like folded hands raised in salutation, the king spoke words both pleasing and beneficial to them.

**2.3.2**  "Ah, I am supremely delighted, and my majesty is unequalled, in that you desire my dear eldest son to be installed as crown prince."

**2.3.3**  Having thus honoured them in return, the king spoke these words to the brāhmaṇas, to Vasiṣṭha and Vāmadeva, while those same men listened.

**2.3.4**  "This auspicious month of Caitra, sacred and with forests in full bloom — let everything be made ready for the installation of Rāma as crown prince."

**2.3.5**  "It shall be done" — so said those two foremost of twice-born men, Vasiṣṭha and Vāmadeva, approaching the lord of the world, pleased and filled with joy, in accordance with his spoken command.

**2.3.6**  Then the radiant king spoke to Sumantra: "Let Rāma, that pure-souled one, be brought here by you swiftly."

**2.3.7**  Sumantra, having promised "So be it" and acting on the king's command, brought Rāma, the best of chariot-warriors, there by chariot.

**2.3.8**  Then the kings from the east, north, west, and south who were seated there around king Daśaratha —

**2.3.9**  — and the Mlecchas and the Āryas and all others who dwelt in forests and mountain borders — all waited upon him as the gods wait upon Vāsava (Indra).

**2.3.10**  That royal seer, standing in his palace among them like Vāsava among the Maruts, saw his son coming on the chariot.

**2.3.11**  — like unto the king of the Gandharvas, of renowned valour in the world, long-armed, of great being, walking with the gait of a rutting elephant —

**2.3.12**  — Rāma, whose face was fair as the moon, exceedingly pleasing to behold, whose beauty, nobility, and virtues stole away the eyes and minds of men —

**2.3.13**  — like rain-bearing Parjanya gladdening people scorched by summer heat — the lord of men, gazing upon him as he approached, was not satiated.

**2.3.14**  Sumantra, having helped that Rāghava down from the excellent chariot, followed him with joined palms as he walked toward his father's presence.

**2.3.15**  That bull among men, that Rāghava, ascended the palace that gleamed like the peak of Kailāsa to see his father, accompanied by the charioteer.

**2.3.16**  Approaching with joined palms, bowing low near his father, causing his own name to be announced, Rāma reverenced his father's feet.

**2.3.17**  Seeing him bowed at his side with joined palms, the king took his hands, drew him close, and embraced his beloved son.

**2.3.18**  And the glorious king directed Rāma to that excellent seat of honour which had been brought forward, adorned with gems and gold.

**2.3.19**  Having taken that best of seats, Rāghava illumined it by his own radiance, as the pure sun at its rising illumines Mount Meru.

**2.3.20**  Illumined by him, the assembly hall shone forth splendidly — like the autumnal sky, clear of planets and stars, shining by the moon.

**2.3.21**  Gazing upon his beloved son, the king was pleased, as if beholding his own adorned self placed in the surface of a mirror.

**2.3.22**  Addressing his son with a smile, the king, best of those who have sons, spoke these words — like Kaśyapa addressing the lord of the gods.

**2.3.23**  "You are a fitting son born of my foremost wife who is fit for you; you have arisen as my dearest son Rāma, excelling in virtue, born of my own self."

**2.3.24**  "Since these subjects are won over by your own virtues, therefore you shall receive the dignity of crown prince under the asterism Puṣya."

**2.3.25**  "By your very nature you are disciplined and virtuous of your own accord; yet even to one already virtuous, I shall speak what is beneficial for you, out of affection, my son."

**2.3.26**  "Cultivating humility still more, be always master of your senses; and abandon the vices that arise from desire and anger."

**2.3.27**  "Whether acting in matters seen or unseen, win the affection of all the constituents of the state, beginning with the ministers."

**2.3.28**  "He who governs the earth with satisfied and devoted subjects — his allies rejoice as the immortals rejoice on obtaining ambrosia. Therefore, my son, conduct yourself only after restraining your own self."

**2.3.29**  Having heard this, the friends of Rāma who loved to do him service hastened swiftly and went to announce the news to Kausalyā.

**2.3.30**  That best of women, Kausalyā, distributed gold, cows, and various jewels to those who had brought her the glad tidings.

**2.3.31**  Then, having paid his respects to the king, Rāghava mounted the chariot and departed to his own radiant dwelling, honoured by the throngs of people.

**2.3.32**  And the townspeople too, having heard that word of the king, as if obtaining a cherished gain, took leave of the lord of men and went to their homes, and worshipped the gods with exceeding joy.

**2.4.1**  Then, after the citizens had departed, the king once more deliberated together with his ministers; and he who was wise in firm resolve made a firm resolve.

**2.4.2**  "Tomorrow is the Puṣya asterism; tomorrow let my son Rāma — his eyes red as lotus — be consecrated to the office of crown prince." So declared the lord.

**2.4.3**  Then king Daśaratha entered his private apartments and commanded his charioteer: "Bring Rāma back here again."

**2.4.4**  Receiving that command, the charioteer once more went swiftly to Rāma's dwelling to bring Rāma back again.

**2.4.5**  The doorkeepers announced to Rāma the charioteer's renewed arrival; and even as Rāma heard of his coming, he was filled with apprehension.

**2.4.6**  Having quickly admitted him, Rāma spoke these words: "Tell me fully and completely the purpose that brings you here again."

**2.4.7**  Then the charioteer said to him: "The king desires to see you; having heard his word, you may decide whether to go or not."

**2.4.8**  Hearing the charioteer's words, Rāma then set out in haste to the royal palace to see the lord of men once more.

**2.4.9**  King Daśaratha, upon hearing that Rāma had arrived, caused his beloved and most excellent son to be ushered into the inner chamber.

**2.4.10**  The glorious Rāghava, even as he entered his father's dwelling, saw his father from afar, bowed down before him with hands cupped in reverence.

**2.4.11**  The lord of the earth raised him as he bowed, embraced him, directed a splendid seat to be given him, and then spoke again.

**2.4.12**  "Rāma, I am old and have lived a long life; I have enjoyed all the pleasures I desired; I have performed hundreds of sacrifices with lavish food and generous dakṣiṇā-offerings.

**2.4.13**  You, O best of men, have been born to me as the beloved and peerless offspring on this earth; I have made the desired gifts and have pursued the desired learning.

**2.4.14**  O hero, I have also experienced the desired pleasures; I am free from debt to the gods, to the sages, to the ancestors, to the Brāhmaṇas, and to my own self.

**2.4.15**  There is nothing left for me to accomplish other than your consecration; therefore whatever I tell you, you ought to carry it out for my sake.

**2.4.16**  Today all the ministers of state desire you as their sovereign; therefore, my son, I shall consecrate you as crown prince."

**2.4.17**  "Moreover, Rāma, today I see inauspicious and terrible dreams: great meteors accompanied by thunderbolts fall here with a mighty roar.

**2.4.18**  And the astrologers report to me, Rāma, that my birth star is beset by terrible planets — the Sun, Mars, and Rāhu.

**2.4.19**  For when such omens as these appear, a king generally meets with death or encounters a dreadful calamity.

**2.4.20**  Therefore, Rāghava, while my mind is still unconfused, let the consecration be performed without delay, for the minds of living beings are ever unstable.

**2.4.21**  Today the moon has come to Punarvasu, one asterism before Puṣya; tomorrow the astrologers will certainly announce the conjunction with Puṣya.

**2.4.22**  Under that Puṣya asterism let the consecration take place — my mind urges me toward haste. O scorcher of foes, tomorrow I shall consecrate you to the office of crown prince.

**2.4.23**  Therefore tonight you must observe a religious vow, with your mind disciplined and controlled, lying upon a bed of darbha-grass together with your wife, fasting the night.

**2.4.24**  And let your trusted friends guard you on all sides without negligence today, for undertakings of this kind are attended by many obstacles.

**2.4.25**  And since Bharata is absent from the city, this is precisely the opportune moment for your consecration — such is my considered view.

**2.4.26**  To be sure, your brother Bharata stands firm in the conduct of the virtuous — he is obedient to his elder, righteous in spirit, compassionate, and master of his senses.

**2.4.27**  Yet the human mind is fickle — such is my conviction; and even of the virtuous who are ever steadfast in righteousness, the character can be enhanced by what is already accomplished, O Rāghava.

**2.4.28**  Thus addressed and granted leave regarding the consecration to take place on the morrow with the word "Go," Rāma paid obeisance to his father and proceeded to his own dwelling.

**2.4.29**  Having entered his own dwelling, and learning of the consecration appointed by the king, he departed that very instant and went to his mother's inner apartments.

**2.4.30**  There he found his mother, clad in silk garments, engrossed in silent prayer in the household shrine, beseeching the goddess of prosperity.

**2.4.31**  Sumitrā had already arrived there, as had Lakṣmaṇa; and Sītā too had been summoned upon hearing the glad news of Rāma's consecration.

**2.4.32**  At that time Kausalyā stood with half-closed eyes in meditation, attended by Sumitrā, by Sītā, and by Lakṣmaṇa.

**2.4.33**  Having heard of her son's consecration as crown prince under the Puṣya asterism, she was engaged in breath-control exercises while meditating upon the divine Janārdana.

**2.4.34**  Approaching his mother who was thus absorbed in her religious observances and paying reverence to her, Rāma then spoke these words, gladdening her heart.

**2.4.35**  "Mother, I have been appointed by my father to the task of protecting the subjects; my consecration will take place tomorrow — such is my father's command to me.

**2.4.36**  Tonight Sītā and I must keep a fast together — so has my father commanded me, in the presence of the officiating priests and the preceptors."

**2.4.37**  "Whatever auspicious rites are appropriate here for the consecration tomorrow — arrange them today for me and for the Vaidehi as well."

**2.4.38**  Hearing this long-cherished news, Kausalyā spoke these words to Rāma, her voice broken with tears of joy.

**2.4.39**  "My son Rāma, live long; may your enemies be slain. Endowed with royal splendour, bring joy to me and to Sumitrā and to all our kinsmen.

**2.4.40**  "Truly you were born to me under an auspicious star, my son — for by your virtues you have won the deepest affection of your father Daśaratha.

**2.4.41**  "Surely my patient endurance in bearing you, O man with eyes like lotus-petals, has not been in vain — for the royal splendour of the Ikṣvāku line shall now, my son, find its refuge in you."

**2.4.42**  Thus addressed by his mother, Rāma spoke these worthy words, glancing with a smile at Lakṣmaṇa who sat with folded hands and bowed head.

**2.4.43**  "Lakṣmaṇa, rule this earth together with me — you who are as my second self, my inner soul; this royal fortune has come to you as much as to me."

**2.4.44**  "Son of Sumitrā, enjoy the pleasures you desire and all the fruits of kingship; for it is for your sake that I long for both my life and the kingdom."

**2.4.45**  Having thus spoken to Lakṣmaṇa, Rāma paid obeisance to both mothers, obtained the permission of Sītā, and departed to his own dwelling.

**2.5.1**  Having given instructions to Rāma regarding the consecration on the morrow, the king summoned his family priest Vasiṣṭha and spoke these words.

**2.5.2**  "Go, O wealth of austerity, and cause the descendant of Kakutstha today to observe a fast, keeping his vow diligently together with his wife, for the attainment of royal glory, fame, and sovereignty."

**2.5.3**  Replying "So be it" to the king, the venerable Vasiṣṭha — best among those learned in the Veda — himself went to Rāma's dwelling.

**2.5.4**  That best of sages, reaching Rāma's dwelling which shone like a dense white cloud, entered three successive courtyards in his chariot itself.

**2.5.5**  Rāma, eager to honour him who was worthy of honour, went out of his dwelling to receive the arriving sage, hastening with a manner of respectful agitation.

**2.5.6**  Approaching quickly to the proximity of the sage's chariot, he then helped the wise one down from the chariot, taking hold of him himself.

**2.5.7**  Seeing him humble and reverent, the family priest addressed him with pleased and gracious words, gladdening Rāma who was so deserving of joy, and spoke thus.

**2.5.8**  "Rāma, your father is pleased; you shall attain the office of crown prince. Let Your Honour today observe a fast together with Sītā."

**2.5.9**  "For in the morning your father, King Daśaratha, will consecrate you to the office of crown prince with loving delight, even as Nahuṣa once consecrated Yayāti."

**2.5.10**  Having spoken thus, the sage then caused Rāma, diligently keeping his vow, to observe the fast together with the Vaidehi, in accordance with the prescribed Vedic rites.

**2.5.11**  Then, having been duly worshipped by Rāma according to proper custom, the royal preceptor took his leave of the descendant of Kakutstha and departed from Rāma's dwelling.

**2.5.12**  Rāma too, having been honoured there by his well-wishers, took leave of them all and entered his dwelling, dismissing them all with his blessing.

**2.5.13**  Rāma's dwelling, thronged with rejoicing women and men, shone at that time like a lake filled with fully bloomed lotuses and joyful flocks of birds.

**2.5.14**  Vasiṣṭha, departing from that Rāma's dwelling which rivalled the royal palace, beheld the roads filled with crowds of people.

**2.5.15**  In Ayodhyā the royal highways in every direction were completely crowded and thronged with people eager with curiosity, gathered in groups upon groups.

**2.5.16**  At that time the noise of the royal highway, resounding with the joyful sounds of human waves and surging crowds, was like the roar of the ocean.

**2.5.17**  That day the city of Ayodhyā, adorned with garlands of forest-flowers and with banners raised above its houses, had its streets sprinkled and swept clean.

**2.5.18**  For then all the inhabitants of Ayodhyā — women, children, the aged, and the strong alike — yearning for Rāma's consecration, longed for the rising of the sun.

**2.5.19**  The people became eager to behold that great festival of Ayodhyā which was the very ornament of the subjects and the increaser of the people's joy.

**2.5.20**  Thus the family priest, as if cleaving through that mass of people, made his way slowly through the crowd-thronged royal highway toward the royal palace.

**2.5.21**  Ascending the palace whose pinnacles resembled white clouds, he came before the king of men, even as Bṛhaspati comes before Śakra, lord of the gods.

**2.5.22**  Beholding him as he arrived, the king rose from his royal seat and inquired; and Vasiṣṭha duly reported to him that the matter had been accomplished.

**2.5.23**  Dismissed by his preceptor, the king, having dispersed those throngs of men, entered his inner apartments like a lion entering a mountain cave.

**2.5.24**  That dwelling, thronged with women adorned in finest attire and rivalling the palace of the great Indra, the king made to radiate with his glorious entry, even as the moon illumines the sky crowded with multitudes of stars.

**2.6.1**  After the departure of the family priest, Rāma, having bathed and with mind controlled and composed, approached Nārāyaṇa together with his wide-eyed wife.

**2.6.2**  Taking on his head the vessel of the oblation offering, he then, in accordance with the prescribed rites, poured the clarified butter as a libation into the blazing fire for the great divinity.

**2.6.3**  And having consumed the remainder of the oblation, invoking blessings upon himself, meditating upon the divine Nārāyaṇa, he lay upon a well-spread bed of kuśa-grass.

**2.6.4**  Maintaining silence, with controlled mind, the son of the best of men lay in the beautiful shrine of Viṣṇu, together with the Vaidehi.

**2.6.5**  When but one watch of the night remained, he awoke and caused the entire prescribed arrangement of decorations to be prepared throughout the dwelling.

**2.6.6**  There, listening to the pleasant words of the panegyrists — bards, heralds, and eulogists — he attended to the rites of the early morning twilight and performed his silent prayers with concentrated mind.

**2.6.7**  He also hymned and bowed with his head before the slayer of Madhu, clad in spotless silken garments, and caused the Brāhmaṇas to recite sacred texts.

**2.6.8**  Then their holy-day proclamations — deep, melodious, and resonant — filled all of Ayodhyā, re-echoing with the sound of musical instruments.

**2.6.9**  All the inhabitants of Ayodhyā, upon hearing that the Rāghava had kept his fast together with the Vaidehi, were exceedingly joyful.

**2.6.10**  Then all the citizens of the city, having heard of Rāma's consecration and seeing the night give way to dawn, made the city resplendent once more with supreme adornment.

**2.6.11**  In the temples of the gods whose pinnacles were like white clouds, at the crossroads, on the thoroughfares, at the sacred shrines, and on the turrets and towers —

**2.6.12**  — in the merchants' stalls stocked with various wares, and in the prosperous and beautiful houses of householders —

**2.6.13**  — in all the assembly halls and even on the visible trees, decorated banners were raised and colourful flags were displayed at that time.

**2.6.14**  And from here and there were heard the words — pleasing to both the mind and the ear — of troupes of actors and dancers and of singers as they sang.

**2.6.15**  People conversed among themselves about matters relating to Rāma's consecration; at the crossroads and in their homes, with Rāma's consecration having arrived, they talked of nothing else.

**2.6.16**  Even children at play, gathered in groups at the doorways of houses, spoke among themselves of nothing else but what pertained to Rāma's consecration.

**2.6.17**  The royal highway was made magnificent by the citizens on the occasion of Rāma's consecration, adorned with flower offerings and pervaded with the fragrance of incense.

**2.6.18**  And fearing the coming of night — wishing to keep it illuminated — they also erected lamp-trees all along the streets in every direction.

**2.6.19**  Having thus adorned the city in this manner, all the inhabitants of the city, longing for the consecration of Rāma to the office of crown prince —

**2.6.20**  — assembled together in groups at the crossroads and in the assembly halls, speaking to one another and praising the lord of men.

**2.6.21**  "Ah, how magnanimous is this king, this joy of the Ikṣvāku line — who, recognizing himself as old, will consecrate Rāma to the kingship!"

**2.6.22**  "We are all truly blessed, in that Rāma — a seer of all worlds, high and low — will be our protector and lord of the earth for a long time to come."

**2.6.23**  "A man of humble mind, learned, righteous in spirit, devoted to his brothers — as the Rāghava is affectionate to his brothers, so is he to us as well."

**2.6.24**  "Long may the blameless and righteous king Daśaratha live — by whose grace we shall behold the consecrated Rāma!"

**2.6.25**  While the city-dwellers were conversing in this manner, people from the rural districts arrived — those who had already heard the news — coming from all the quarters.

**2.6.26**  These rural folk, arriving at the city from all the quarters to witness Rāma's consecration, filled Rāma's city to overflowing.

**2.6.27**  From those spreading surges of human multitudes a roar was heard there, like the roar of the ocean whose currents surge and swell during the days of the full and new moon.

**2.6.28**  Then that city, resembling the abode of Indra, became on all sides noisy and crowded with the rural people who had come to behold it, even as the waters of the ocean are crowded and teeming with the creatures of the sea.

**2.7.1**  A servant-maid born of Kaikeyī's kinsmen, who had lived there alongside her, happened to climb the palace that shone like the moon.

**2.7.2**  From that palace Mantharā gazed out over the whole of Ayodhyā — its royal roads sprinkled with water and strewn with lotus blossoms and blue water-lilies.

**2.7.3**  The city was adorned with precious banners and pennants, sprinkled with sandalwood water, and thronged with people who had bathed and washed their hair.

**2.7.4**  Seeing a nurse standing nearby, Mantharā — filled with great curiosity and intent on learning the reason — questioned her.

**2.7.5**  "Why is Rāma's mother distributing wealth to the people? Tell me what is the reason for this extraordinary rejoicing. And what is the king so delighted about that he is about to do?"

**2.7.6**  Bursting with joy and with supreme delight, the nurse then disclosed to the hunchback all the great glory that was about to come upon Rāghava.

**2.7.7**  "Tomorrow, under the Puṣya constellation, King Daśaratha will consecrate the sinless Rāma, conqueror of anger, as crown prince."

**2.7.8**  Upon hearing the nurse's words, the hunchback was immediately seized with indignation and descended swiftly from the palace, which had the appearance of a Kailāsa peak.

**2.7.9**  Burning with rage, Mantharā, that one of evil vision, went to Kaikeyī as she lay resting and spoke these words to her.

**2.7.10**  "Arise, fool! Why do you lie there? Fear is fast approaching you. Submerged in a great flood, do you not understand your own situation?"

**2.7.11**  "You boast of your good fortune though its form is ill-omened. Your prosperity is as fleeting as the current of a river in summer."

**2.7.12**  Addressed thus with harsh words by the evil-sighted hunchback in her anger, Kaikeyī fell into the deepest distress.

**2.7.13**  Kaikeyī then said to the hunchback: "Is everything not well with you, Mantharā? I see your face is downcast and you seem deeply distressed."

**2.7.14**  Having heard Kaikeyī's sweet words, Mantharā — that one skilled in speech — replied with words filled with rage.

**2.7.15**  Becoming ever more despondent, the hunchback — claiming to act in Kaikeyī's interest — spoke to grieve her and to drive a wedge against Rāghava.

**2.7.16**  "A great misfortune has come about, O queen — your ruin is at hand. King Daśaratha will consecrate Rāma as crown prince."

**2.7.17**  "I am plunged into a bottomless fear, overcome with grief and sorrow, burning as if in fire — and yet I have come here for your sake."

**2.7.18**  "Your grief becomes my great grief, Kaikeyī, and your prosperity becomes my prosperity — of this there is no doubt."

**2.7.19**  "Born into a royal lineage, you are the chief queen of the king. How is it, O goddess, that you do not understand the harsh realities of royal duty?"

**2.7.20**  "Your husband, though he speaks of righteousness, is a deceiver; though he speaks gently, he is cruel. In your pure-hearted state you do not know this, and so he has outwitted you."

**2.7.21**  "Your husband deploys soothing words toward you as a mere pretense — when it comes to actual benefit, today he will endow Kausalyā."

**2.7.22**  "That evil-souled king, having sent Bharata away among your kinsmen, will tomorrow establish Rāma in the kingdom with all obstacles removed."

**2.7.23**  "An enemy under the guise of a husband, who appears to wish you well as a mother would — like a venomous serpent clasped to one's bosom — that is who you have taken to heart, dear girl."

**2.7.24**  "Just as a serpent or an enemy left unheeded would act, King Daśaratha has treated you together with your son today."

**2.7.25**  "By the wicked man's false soothing words, O young one, ever accustomed to comfort — by his installing Rāma in the kingdom — you together with your kin are truly ruined."

**2.7.26**  "Therefore, Kaikeyī, act at once while there is still time — do what is for your good. Save your son, save yourself, and save me too, O you of wondrous appearance."

**2.7.27**  Having heard Mantharā's words, the lovely-faced Kaikeyī rose from her couch and gave a fine ornament to that hunchback.

**2.7.28**  Having given the ornament to the hunchback, Kaikeyī, most excellent of women, joyfully said this again to Mantharā:

**2.7.29**  "This that you have told me, Mantharā, is most pleasing to me. What more can I do for you who have brought me such welcome news?"

**2.7.30**  "I see no difference between Rāma and Bharata. Therefore I am pleased that the king will consecrate Rāma in the kingdom."

**2.7.31**  "There is nothing you could tell me that would please me more, O worthy one, well-spoken and excellent. Since you have spoken words that are all the more dear, I offer you a choice boon — choose it."

**2.8.1**  But Mantharā, envying her, flung back that ornament and spoke these words then filled with anger and sorrow:

**2.8.2**  "Why are you rejoicing so foolishly at the wrong moment, childish woman? Do you not understand that you stand in the midst of an ocean of grief?"

**2.8.3**  "How fortunate indeed is Kausalyā, whose son will be consecrated tomorrow under the Puṣya star with the great ceremony of crown prince by the foremost of brahmins."

**2.8.4**  "That Kausalyā, triumphant and her enemies destroyed, will have attained supreme delight — and you will wait upon her with hands folded like a servant girl."

**2.8.5**  "The chief wives of Rāma will indeed be joyful. But your daughters-in-law will be joyless at Bharata's ruin."

**2.8.6**  Seeing Mantharā speaking thus, filled with great grief, Queen Kaikeyī praised Rāma's virtues.

**2.8.7**  "Rāma knows dharma, is restrained by his elders, is grateful, truth-speaking, and pure. As the king's eldest son, he rightly deserves the position of crown prince."

**2.8.8**  "Long-lived, he will care for his brothers and servants as a father does. How can you grieve, hunchback, upon hearing of Rāma's consecration?"

**2.8.9**  "Bharata too, that bull among men, will certainly obtain the ancestral kingdom — his father's and grandfather's — after a hundred years in succession to Rāma."

**2.8.10**  "When prosperity has arrived, and is present, and good fortune is yet to come, why do you grieve, Mantharā? And moreover, Rāma serves me more than he does Kausalyā."

**2.8.11**  Having heard Kaikeyī's words, Mantharā, deeply grieved, sighed a long, hot sigh and said this to Kaikeyī:

**2.8.12**  "Blind to your own ruin through foolishness, you do not perceive your own situation — you who are sinking into an ocean of grief, wide with sorrow and calamity."

**2.8.13**  "Rāghava will be king, and then whoever is Rāghava's son — but Bharata, Kaikeyī, will be excluded from the royal line."

**2.8.14**  "Not all sons of a king remain in the kingdom, O passionate one — for if all were to be installed, the transgression would be very great."

**2.8.15**  "Therefore, Kaikeyī, kings establish the mechanisms of rule in the eldest son — O you of faultless limbs — even when the others too are possessed of virtues."

**2.8.16**  "Your son will be utterly destitute, like one without a protector, cut off from comforts and from the royal line, dear one."

**2.8.17**  "I have come for your sake, yet you do not understand me — you who wish to give me a gift meant to celebrate a co-wife's advancement."

**2.8.18**  "Once Rāma has obtained an uncontested kingdom, he will certainly send Bharata away to another land — or even to another world."

**2.8.19**  "Bharata was sent to his maternal uncle's house while still a child and was not brought back by you — for affection grows even in stationary beings through proximity."

**2.8.20**  "Saumitri Lakṣmaṇa is Rāma's protector, and Rāghava is Lakṣmaṇa's — their fraternal bond is renowned in the worlds, like that of the twin Aśvins."

**2.8.21**  "Therefore Rāma will do no harm to Lakṣmaṇa — but that Rāma will do harm to Bharata, of this there is no doubt."

**2.8.22**  "Therefore let your son depart to the forest directly from the king's house. This is what I strongly favor, and it is greatly for your benefit."

**2.8.23**  "Thus it will be well for your kinsmen's side too, if Bharata obtains by right the paternal kingdom."

**2.8.24**  "Your boy, accustomed to comfort, is Rāma's natural enemy — deprived of wealth while the other is prosperous, how will he live in subjection?"

**2.8.25**  "Like the lord of an elephant herd in a forest attacked by a lion, Bharata being overwhelmed by Rāma — you ought to save him."

**2.8.26**  "Rāma's mother, your co-wife, was formerly humiliated by you in your pride and good fortune — how would she not now take revenge?"

**2.8.27**  "When Rāma obtains the earth, Bharata will certainly be destroyed. Therefore think at once of securing the kingdom for your own son — and of a cause for banishing the other's."

**2.9.1**  Thus addressed, Kaikeyī — her face blazing with anger — breathed a long, hot breath and said this to Mantharā:

**2.9.2**  "Today I will send Rāma away from here quickly to the forest, and will swiftly consecrate Bharata as crown prince."

**2.9.3**  "Now look at this: by what means, Mantharā, will Bharata obtain the kingdom and Rāma in no way obtain it?"

**2.9.4**  Thus addressed by the queen, Mantharā, evil-visioned, eager to harm Rāma's cause, said this to Kaikeyī:

**2.9.5**  "Very well — I shall tell you now, Kaikeyī; listen to me and hear how your son Bharata alone will obtain the kingdom."

**2.9.6**  Having heard those words of Mantharā's, Kaikeyī rose somewhat from her well-spread couch and spoke thus:

**2.9.7**  "Tell me the means — by what means, Mantharā, will Bharata obtain the kingdom and Rāma in no way obtain it?"

**2.9.8**  Thus addressed by the queen, Mantharā, evil-visioned, eager to harm Rāma's cause, the hunchback spoke these words:

**2.9.9**  "In the war between the gods and asuras, your husband went, taking you with him along with royal sages, to render aid to the king of gods."

**2.9.10**  "Heading toward the southern direction, toward the Daṇḍaka region, toward the city known as Vaijayanta, where dwelt Timidhvaja —"

**2.9.11**  "— that one known as Śambara, the great asura of a hundred illusions, who had not been defeated by the hosts of gods, gave battle to Śakra."

**2.9.17**  "You are always dear to your husband — of this I have no doubt. For your sake the great king would even enter fire."

**2.9.18**  "He cannot bear to make you angry, nor to face you when you are angry. Indeed, the king would give up his very life for what pleases you."

**2.9.19**  "The lord of the earth is not capable of transgressing your word. Know your own power of good fortune, you who are slow to understand your own nature."

**2.9.20**  "King Daśaratha would give gems, pearls, gold, and various jewels — do not set your mind on those."

**2.9.21**  "Those two boons that Daśaratha granted in the war between the gods and asuras — remind him of them, O illustrious one. Do not let that matter pass you by."

**2.9.22**  "When Rāghava himself rises to grant you a boon, having settled the great king, then ask for this boon."

**2.9.23**  "Send Rāma to wander in the forest for fourteen years; let Bharata be made king over the earth — that bull among kings."

**2.9.24**  "Thus banished, Rāma will be without Rāma's estate, and Bharata, with his enemies destroyed, will become king for you."

**2.9.28**  "Hunchback, I did not know you — the best of those who speak of the best! Among all hunchbacks on earth you are the greatest in the resolution of intelligence."

**2.9.29**  "You alone are ever engaged in my interests and wish me well. I would not have understood what the king intends, O hunchback."

**2.9.32**  "Your hips are well-formed, adorned with the girdle's cord; your calves are well-placed, and both your feet are long."

**2.9.41**  "A dam is not built, dear one, after the waters have gone. Arise, do what is auspicious — show yourself to the king."

**2.9.43**  The beautiful woman removed her pearl garland worth many hundreds of thousands, and also her fine and worthy ornaments.

**2.9.44**  Then Kaikeyī, golden in complexion, brought under the spell of the hunchback's words, lay down upon the ground there and said this to Mantharā:

**2.9.45**  "Either, hunchback, you will report me dead here — or, when Rāghava has gone to the forest, Bharata will obtain the earth."

**2.9.47**  Her face veiled in the darkness of surging rage, her finest garlands and ornaments thus cast off, the wife of the lord of men became despondent — like the sky obscured by darkness, its stars submerged.

**2.10.1**  Having ordered the consecration of Rāghava, the great king — self-controlled and desirous of bearing glad tidings to one who deserved gladness — entered the inner apartments.

**2.10.2**  There the lord of the earth saw her fallen to the floor, lying prostrate in a manner wholly unbecoming her — he, who was himself as though scorched with grief.

**2.10.3**  That aged king — himself without guilt — beheld upon the ground his young wife, dearer to him than life itself, yet harbouring wicked intent.

**2.10.4**  As a great elephant in the forest tenderly touches a she-elephant pierced by a hunter's poisoned arrow, so did he, in affection, stroke her.

**2.10.5**  Having touched her with his hands, his heart greatly alarmed, the passion-bound king spoke thus to the lotus-eyed woman.

**2.10.6**  "I do not perceive what anger has taken hold in your heart, O queen. By whom have you been aggrieved? By whom have you been dishonoured?"

**2.10.7**  "O auspicious one, why do you lie thus in the dust for my grief? Why do you lie upon the ground, tormenting my heart — you whose heart is auspicious — as though possessed by an evil spirit?"

**2.10.9**  "Whose favour shall I do for you? Who has done you displeasure? Who shall today receive great benefit, or who shall receive great harm?"

**2.10.10**  "Shall one who is not to be slain be slain, or shall one condemned to death be freed? Shall a poor man be made wealthy, or a wealthy man be stripped of all?"

**2.10.12**  "Speak, even at the cost of my own life, whatever your heart desires — the entire earth over which the wheel of sovereignty turns is mine."

**2.10.14**  "I have not been wronged by anyone, O lord, nor dishonoured. But there is a certain desire of mine — I wish it to be fulfilled by you."

**2.10.15**  "Make me a promise — swear that you will do it — and then I shall speak what it is that I desire."

**2.10.17**  "O proud one, do you not know — there is no person dearer to me than you, save that tiger among men, Rāma?"

**2.10.41**  That lord of the earth, lamenting like one helpless, seized by the woman in her exceeding firmness, fell — and, like one sick, could not quite reach the two extended feet of the queen.

**2.12.2**  "As though having committed a sin, why do you lie collapsed on the floor after having heard my promise? You ought to stand firm in your resolve."

**2.12.3**  "Those who know dharma declare that truth is the highest dharma. Relying on truth, I have urged you toward dharma as well."

**2.12.4**  "Śaibya the lord of the earth, O king, having pledged and then given his own body to the hawk, attained the highest destiny."

**2.12.5**  "Similarly the splendid Alarka, when a brahmin learned in the Vedas begged of him, plucked out his own eyes and gave them without hesitation."

**2.12.6**  "The lord of rivers, bound to truth, does not, by its truth-compact, transgress the appointed shoreline at the proper season, however narrow that boundary."

**2.12.7**  "And if you will not honour this noble compact of mine, then, before your very eyes, having been abandoned, I shall abandon my life."

**2.12.8**  Thus pressed by the fearless Kaikeyī, the king was unable to free himself from the snare — like Bali from the bond fashioned by Indra.

**2.12.9**  Bewildered in his heart, he became pale and voiceless — like a draught-ox struggling between the yoke and the wheel.

**2.12.10**  The lord of the earth, his eyes unsteady and as though unseeing, summoning his composure with great difficulty through his fortitude, spoke thus to Kaikeyī.

**2.12.11**  "The hand of yours which I clasped in fire at the sacred rite, O sinful woman — that I renounce, and your son who is my own kin, and you along with him."

**2.12.12**  Then Kaikeyī — wicked in her conduct, yet skilled in speech — overcome with rage, spoke harsh words to the king once more.

**2.12.13**  "What is this you say, O king — words like a poison's sting? You should have Rāma, your son, brought here without delay."

**2.12.14**  "Having established my son in the kingdom, having sent Rāma to dwell in the forest, and having left me without a rival — you will then be one whose purpose is accomplished."

**2.12.15**  Goaded repeatedly like a noble horse urged by a sharp goad, the king spoke thus to Kaikeyī.

**2.12.16**  "I am bound by the bond of dharma and my understanding has forsaken me. I wish to see Rāma, my beloved eldest son, that righteous one."

**2.12.17**  Hearing the king's words, Kaikeyī thereupon herself spoke to the charioteer: "Go, bring Rāma here."

**2.12.18**  Then that majestic and righteous king, his joy extinguished in regard to his son, his eyes red with grief, looked up at the charioteer and spoke.

**2.12.19**  Sumantra, having heard the piteous words and seen the wretched state of the king, withdrew a little from that spot with hands folded in reverence.

**2.12.20**  When the lord of the earth was unable out of misery to speak himself, then Kaikeyī — well-versed in counsel — spoke to Sumantra.

**2.12.21**  "Sumantra, I wish to see Rāma — bring the handsome one quickly." He, thinking this to be auspicious, rejoiced in his heart.

**2.12.22**  Sumantra reflected, hastened on by her: "Clearly Rāma, who knows dharma, will come here for the consecration."

**2.12.23**  Having so resolved, the charioteer of great splendour went forth once more, with great joy, eager to behold Rāghava.

**2.12.24**  Then, having gone forth suddenly from the front, looking about at those standing at the gateway of the lords of the earth, he saw various townspeople of great wealth who had come and stood assembled at the door.

**2.13.1**  Having spent that night, the Brāhmaṇas who were masters of the Vedas presented themselves for the morning audience together with the royal chaplain.

**2.13.2**  Ministers, commanders of the army, and the leading men of the guilds — all delighted — assembled together for the consecration of Rāghava.

**2.13.3**  When the pure sun had risen and the day of the Puṣya asterism had arrived, the foremost twice-born had prepared everything for the consecration of Rāma.

**2.13.4**  Golden water-jars were arrayed, an auspicious throne was richly adorned, and a resplendent tiger-skin had been properly spread upon it for Rāma.

**2.13.5**  Water had been brought from the sacred confluence of the Gaṅgā and Yamunā, and from other holy rivers, lakes, wells, and ponds.

**2.13.6**  From streams flowing eastward, northward, and laterally, water had been carefully gathered, and likewise from all the oceans.

**2.13.7**  Honey, curds, clarified butter, parched grain, darbha-grass, flowers, and milk — golden and silver jars covered with milk-plants, adorned with lotuses and blue water-lilies, gleamed, filled to the brim with the finest water.

**2.13.8**  A splendid white yak-tail fan, bright as moonbeams, adorned with jewels, stood ready for Rāma.

**2.13.9**  And a white parasol resembling the orb of the moon — radiant, magnificent, and placed in readiness for the consecration — stood waiting.

**2.13.10**  A white bull stood ready, a white horse was properly stationed, and a majestic rutting elephant — fit for a king to ride — waited in attendance.

**2.13.11**  Eight auspicious maidens adorned with all their ornaments, all manner of musical instruments, and likewise bards and panegyrists — all were assembled.

**2.13.12**  In the manner customary within the kingdom of the Ikṣvākus for a royal consecration, all the prescribed requisites for the anointing of a prince had been assembled.

**2.13.13**  Those who had assembled there by the king's command, not seeing the lord of the earth, said among themselves: "Who now will announce us to the king?"

**2.13.14**  "We do not see the king, yet the sun has risen, and all is prepared for the consecration of wise Rāma as heir-apparent."

**2.13.15**  While they were speaking thus, Sumantra — honoured by the king — addressed all those sovereign lords of the earth with these words.

**2.13.16**  "On the king's command I shall inquire after the well-being of these long-lived lords, and also after the reason for their coming, once the king has been awakened."

**2.13.17**  Having said this, the knower of ancient lore went to the inner-palace gate and praised Rāghava with auspicious benedictions full of virtuous import.

**2.13.18**  "The blessed night has passed, an auspicious day has come. Awaken, O tiger among kings, and proceed to your duty without delay."

**2.13.19**  "Brāhmaṇas, commanders of the army, and the guild-leaders have all come, O king, awaiting your audience. Awaken, O Rāghava."

**2.13.20**  Having awakened while that charioteer Sumantra, skilled in counsel, was thus praising him, the king then spoke these words.

**2.13.21**  "I have not yet slept — bring Rāghava quickly." With these words king Daśaratha once more commanded the charioteer.

**2.13.22**  Hearing the king's command and honouring it by bowing his head, Sumantra departed from the royal dwelling, thinking this a great favour.

**2.13.23**  Setting out along the royal road adorned with pennants and banners, that charioteer heard conversations all concerning Rāma.

**2.13.24**  Then Sumantra beheld Rāma's beautiful dwelling — radiant as Mount Kailāsa, in splendour equal to the abode of Indra.

**2.13.25**  It was closed by great doors, beautiful with hundreds of terraced pavilions, adorned with golden statues throughout, with arched gateways of gems and coral.

**2.13.26**  White as the thick clouds of autumn, blazing with light, resembling the caverns of Mount Meru, it was adorned with very long garlands of the finest flowers.

**2.13.27**  That charioteer, arriving with his horse-yoked chariot and beholding the vast, opulent royal palace crowded with people, was filled with delight — his hair standing on end with joy.

**2.13.28**  That charioteer entered unimpeded into the palace — resembling mountain-peaks, massive rocks, and clouds, like a great assemblage of the finest mansions, abounding in treasures — as a sea-creature enters the ocean.

**2.14.1**  Having passed through the inner-palace gate thronged with people, the knower of ancient lore reached a secluded inner courtyard.

**2.14.2**  It was guarded by young men bearing javelins and bows, wearing polished earrings, vigilant, single-minded, and deeply devoted.

**2.14.3**  There at the door he saw elderly women superintendents of the inner apartments, wearing ochre robes, holding cane-staffs, richly ornamented, and deeply composed.

**2.14.4**  Seeing him approach and wishing to please Rāma, they immediately announced Sumantra to Rāma together with his wife.

**2.14.5**  Learning that the charioteer had been announced as coming from his father within, Rāghava had him brought in at once, out of a desire to do what was pleasing.

**2.14.6**  The charioteer saw him — resembling Vaiśravaṇa himself, richly adorned — seated on a couch covered with a golden spread and upper cloth.

**2.14.7**  The scorcher of foes was anointed with the most exquisite sandal-paste — pure, fragrant, and the hue of boar's blood.

**2.14.8**  With Sītā standing at his side, yak-tail fan in hand, he was further graced, like the moon attended by the splendid star Citrā.

**2.14.9**  Sumantra, like a trained panegyrist observant of protocol, reverently saluted him — the granter of boons, blazing like the sun in his own radiance.

**2.14.10**  With hands joined in reverence, after inquiring about his comfort in recreation, sleep, and rest, Sumantra — honoured by the king — said this to the prince.

**2.14.11**  "O lord, the auspicious Kausalyā and your father wish to see you, together with the queen Kaikeyī. Go there without delay."

**2.14.12**  Thus addressed, that lion among men of great radiance was filled with joy; he then honoured Sītā and spoke to her thus.

**2.14.13**  "O lady, the king and the queen — consulting together in my presence — are surely deliberating something connected with my consecration."

**2.14.14**  "For she — gracious and fair-handed, intoxicating-eyed — having perceived the king's intention and being eager to please me, is urging the king on my behalf."

**2.14.15**  "Such is the assembly gathered there, and such is the messenger who has come — the king will certainly consecrate me as heir-apparent this very day."

**2.14.16**  "Come now, I shall go quickly hence and see the lord of the earth. You remain here comfortably with your retinue and rejoice."

**2.14.17**  Sītā, honoured by her husband, the dark-eyed lady, followed her lord as far as the door, silently invoking auspicious blessings upon him.

**2.14.18**  He saw all the petitioners who had gathered, greeted them affectionately, and then mounted his splendid chariot that blazed like fire.

**2.14.19**  That chariot seemed to steal away all eyes with its dazzling golden radiance, and was yoked with the finest horses swift as young elephants.

**2.14.20**  Like thousand-eyed Indra mounting a tawny-horsed chariot, Rāghava blazing with royal splendour swiftly set forth, having taken his seat.

**2.14.21**  Resounding like a rain-cloud in the sky, the illustrious Rāma emerged from his dwelling as the moon issues from a great cloud.

**2.14.22**  Lakṣmaṇa, the younger brother of Rāghava, parasol and yak-tail fan in hand, mounted the chariot and guarded his brother from behind.

**2.14.23**  Then a great tumultuous roar arose on all sides from the multitude of people as he came forth.

**2.14.24**  Rāghava heard there the conversations and talk of the assembled people, and the varied utterances — extolling him — of the joyful citizens of the city.

**2.14.25**  "Today Rāghava, by the king's favour, is going to receive immense prosperity. We — whose ruler this one will be — are all our desires fulfilled. It is the gain of these people that he will obtain this entire kingdom for a long time."

**2.14.26**  Accompanied by resonant-sounding horses and elephants preceding him, honoured by svastika-reciters, bards, and panegyrists, praised by the finest musicians, he proceeded like Vaiśravaṇa himself.

**2.14.27**  Rāma beheld the beautiful great highway — teeming with she-elephants, bull-elephants, chariots, and horses, with its open spaces filled by great crowds of people, abounding in treasures and extensive stores of merchandise.

**2.15.1**  Illustrious Rāma, having mounted his chariot, with friends and well-wishers delighted around him, gazed upon the city thronged with multitudes of various people.

**2.15.2**  Rāma proceeded along the royal road, beautiful with white houses resembling clouds, and perfumed throughout with the smoke of burning agaru wood.

**2.15.3**  That excellent royal road was brilliant and unobstructed, filled with various wares and with foods of every kind, both fine and coarse.

**2.15.4**  Hearing many benedictions voiced by his well-wishers, and honouring all men as befitted them, he rode on.

**2.15.5**  "The path that was walked by your grandfathers and great-grandfathers — take it up from this day forward, once consecrated, and guard it well."

**2.15.6**  "As we were cherished by your father, as by the grandfathers of old — even more happily shall we all dwell under Rāma as our king."

**2.15.7**  "Enough today with eating, enough with all our other concerns — so long as we can see Rāma riding forth, established in his kingdom."

**2.15.8**  "For nothing shall be dearer to us, nor shall any other thing equal it, than the consecration of Rāma of boundless glory to his kingdom."

**2.15.9**  Hearing these and other such auspicious words from well-wishers — words that honoured him personally — Rāma rode with equanimity along the great highway.

**2.15.10**  For no person could turn either mind or eyes away from that best of men, even after Rāghava had passed by.

**2.15.11**  For that righteous-souled one shows compassion to all the four varṇas and to all four stages of life; therefore they were all devoted to him.

**2.15.12**  Reaching the royal palace — comparable to the abode of great Indra — that prince, blazing with royal splendour, entered his father's dwelling.

**2.15.13**  Having passed through all the courtyards, that son of Daśaratha dismissed all his attendants and proceeded to the pure inner apartments.

**2.15.14**  Then, after the prince had entered his father's presence, all that joyful multitude waited for his re-emergence, as the ocean awaits the rising of the moon.
