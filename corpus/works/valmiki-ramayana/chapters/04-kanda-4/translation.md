**4.1.1**  Having reached that lotus-pond teeming with blue lotuses and fish, Rāma, accompanied by Saumitri, lamented with distraught senses.

**4.1.2**  At the very sight of it his senses trembled with delight; overpowered by longing, he spoke thus to Saumitri.

**4.1.3**  "O Saumitri, behold the beautiful forest of Pampā, where trees that resemble mountains stand splendid, as if with crests."

**4.1.4**  "Yet anguishes torment me, scorched as I am by grief — by the sorrow of Bharata and by the abduction of Vaidehī."

**4.1.5**  "This meadow of blue and yellow gleams all the more richly, spread as if with garlands of the many-coloured blossoms of the trees."

**4.1.6**  "O Saumitri, this season's breeze is pleasant; Kāma reigns abundantly; the month is fragrant and sweet-scented, the trees laden with flowers and fruit."

**4.1.7**  "See, O Saumitri, the beauty of these flower-bearing forests as they shed showers of blossoms, like rain-clouds releasing rain."

**4.1.8**  "On the charming rocky ledges, diverse forest trees, shaken by the rush of the wind, scatter flowers upon the earth."

**4.1.9**  "The wind blows with a pleasant touch, cool as sandalwood, in the honey-scented forests that hum with swarms of bees."

**4.1.10**  "On the charming hillslopes, the mountains blaze with glory, their peaks interlocked with great trees full of blossoms."

**4.1.11**  "See these karṇikāra trees in full bloom all around, covered with golden flowers — like men robed in yellow garments."

**4.1.12**  "This spring, O Saumitri, resounding with many birds, fans the flames of my grief for one who is parted from Sītā."

**4.1.13**  "Manmatha scorches me, overwhelmed by grief, while the joyful cuckoo calls and seems to challenge me."

**4.1.14**  "This water-cock, joyful in the lovely forest waterfall, will make me grieve still more, O Lakṣmaṇa, stricken as I am with desire."

**4.1.15**  "Birds of mingled kinds, gladdened by their own flocking together, rejoicing with the king-bee, sing in sweet voices, O Saumitri."

**4.1.16**  "The cruel wind of Caitra's forest scorches me, O Saumitri, overwhelmed by anguished grief for that doe-eyed one."

**4.1.17**  "Peacocks attended by peahens on the mountain ridges intensify the longing of one — me — already consumed by longing."

**4.1.18**  "See, Lakṣmaṇa, the peacock dancing — the peahen, filled with desire, dances beside her lord on the mountain ridge."

**4.1.19**  "Surely the beloved of the peacock has not been stolen by a rākṣasa in the forest; but my dwelling without her in the month of flowers is utterly unbearable."

**4.1.20**  "See, Lakṣmaṇa, the flowers become fruitless for me, even though the forests are rich with burdens of blossoms at winter's end."

**4.1.21**  "Birds in joyful flocks cry out their melodies, calling to one another as if challenging — driving me mad with desire."

**4.1.22**  "Surely Sītā, fallen into another's power, grieves even as I do — that dark-complexioned one, lotus-petal-eyed, gentle in speech, my beloved."

**4.1.23**  "This flower-laden wind, pleasant to the touch and carrying coolness, is like fire to me as I think longingly of that beloved."

**4.1.24**  "Without her, that bird — a crow perched in a tree — cries out joyfully, calling in its delight."

**4.1.25**  "Surely this very bird shall be the herald of Vaidehī there; the bird will lead me to the side of the wide-eyed one."

**4.1.26**  "See, Lakṣmaṇa, the chorus in the forest — heightening intoxication — of birds calling among the flower-crowned trees."

**4.1.27**  "O Saumitri, see how in the variegated groves of Pampā the lotuses shine in the water like the newly-risen sun."

**4.1.28**  "Here is Pampā with her clear waters, stretching with lotuses and blue water-lilies, swarming with swans and kāraṇḍava ducks, rich with white saugandhika flowers."

**4.1.29**  "She is ever graced with cakravāka birds, her variegated banks enclosed by groves, and she gleams with herds of elephants and deer that seek the water."

**4.1.30**  "The eye, on beholding the petals of lotus buds, imagines they resemble the eye-cups of Sītā, O Lakṣmaṇa."

**4.1.31**  "Mingled with lotus pollen, issuing from among the trees, the enchanting breeze blows like the breath of Sītā."

**4.1.32**  "O Saumitri, see on the southern ridge of Pampā the supremely beautiful flowering stalk of the karṇikāra tree."

**4.1.33**  "This king of mountains, adorned yet more by its mineral veins, sheds a many-coloured dust driven loose by the force of the wind."

**4.1.34**  "O Saumitri, the hillslopes everywhere seem like lamps with the kiṃśuka trees in full bloom on all sides, leafless and charming."

**4.1.35**  "These clustered, honey-fragrant trees growing on the bank of Pampā — thickets of mālatī and mallikā — and the karavīra trees are in bloom."

**4.1.36**  "Ketakī, sinduvāra, and vāsantī are beautifully flowered; the mādhavī vines are full of fragrance, and kunda shrubs bloom everywhere."

**4.1.37**  "Ciribilvā, madhūka, vañjula, bakula, campaka, tilaka, and nāgavṛkṣa trees are all in bloom."

**4.1.38**  "Nīpa, varaṇa, and kharjūra trees are beautifully blossoming; aṅkola, kuraṇṭa, cūrṇaka, and pāribhadraka bloom as well."

**4.1.39**  "Mango, pāṭalā, and kovidāra trees are in flower; mucukunda and arjuna too are visible on the mountain ridges."

**4.1.40**  "Ketaka, uddālaka, śirīṣa, śiṃśapā, dhava, śālmalī, kiṃśuka — red and blooming — kurabaka, tiniśa, naktamālā, sandal, and syandana trees as well."

**4.1.41**  "The rocky ledges on the mountain ridges, O Saumitri, are made golden and red by the varied blossoms of those diverse trees scattered upon them."

**4.1.42**  "See, O Saumitri, the flowering of trees at winter's end; in the month of flowers the trees bloom as if through mutual friction."

**4.1.43**  "See this cool-watered lake, O Saumitri, filled with lotuses, attended by cakravāka birds, frequented by kāraṇḍava ducks, teeming with plavas and cranes, and visited by boars and deer."

**4.1.44**  "Pampā shines all the more with its calling birds."

**4.1.45**  "Various joyful birds seem to kindle my desire as I remember my beloved — dark-complexioned, moon-faced, lotus-eyed."

**4.1.46**  "See the deer accompanied by does on the variegated ridges — and me, bereft of Vaidehī of the doe-like eyes."

**4.1.47**  Thus lamenting there, his mind stricken by grief, he gazed upon the auspicious Pampā, her lovely waters flowing, fair and beautiful.

**4.1.48**  The great-souled one, gazing suddenly at the whole forest with its waterfalls and ravines, agitated of heart, deliberated together with Lakṣmaṇa, and then set forth, stricken by sorrow.

**4.1.49**  Those two set forth together toward Ṛśyamūka, frequented by Sugrīva and his band of monkeys. On seeing those two — the mighty Rāghava and Lakṣmaṇa — the monkeys were struck with fear.

**4.2.1**  On seeing those two great-souled brothers, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, those heroes bearing excellent weapons, Sugrīva became suspicious.

**4.2.2**  With an anxious heart, scanning all directions, that foremost of monkeys could not stay in any one place.

**4.2.3**  His mind would not settle in one spot as he watched those two of great strength; the mind of the monkey, utterly terrified, faltered.

**4.2.4**  Having pondered and weighed what was grave and what was slight, Sugrīva, greatly agitated, together with all his attendants —

**4.2.5**  — then that lord of leaping monkeys, Sugrīva, spoke to his counsellors, utterly alarmed, watching those two — Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.2.6**  "These two, surely, have been sent here by Vālin into this impenetrable forest — moving about in disguise, clad in bark garments, they have come here."

**4.2.7**  Then the counsellors of Sugrīva, seeing those two supreme archers, left that mountain-ledge and went to another excellent peak.

**4.2.8**  Those horde-leaders quickly approached and surrounded that bull among herds, that best of monkeys, and stood in attendance.

**4.2.9**  Going one by one on a single path, leaping from mountain to mountain, with their speed they shook the peaks of the mountains.

**4.2.10**  Then all those powerful branch-animals, leaping, broke down the flowering trees that grew in those difficult places.

**4.2.11**  Those foremost of tawny monkeys, leaping around that great mountain on all sides, went forth frightening the deer, wildcats, and tigers.

**4.2.12**  Then the counsellors of Sugrīva, having taken refuge on the king of mountains, gathered before that foremost of monkeys and all stood with hands folded in reverence.

**4.2.13**  Then Hanūmān, skilled in speech, spoke to Sugrīva who was trembling with fear and suspected the sin of Vālin.

**4.2.14**  "The one on account of whom you flee with agitated heart, O foremost of monkeys — that cruel-looking, cruel Vālin — I do not see him here."

**4.2.15**  "The one from whom you fear, dear friend — your elder brother of wicked deeds — that evil-souled Vālin is not here; I see no danger for you."

**4.2.16**  "Ah, your monkey-nature is evident indeed, O leaping one — through such fickleness of mind you cannot steady yourself in thought."

**4.2.17**  "Act in everything by signs, endowed with intelligence and discernment; for a king without wisdom cannot rule all beings."

**4.2.18**  Sugrīva, having heard all of Hanūmān's fair words, then spoke an even fairer speech to Hanūmān.

**4.2.19**  "Long-armed, broad-eyed, bearing arrows, bows, and swords — who would not feel fear on seeing these two who resemble sons of the gods?"

**4.2.20**  "I suspect these two foremost of men have been sent by Vālin; kings have many friends, and trust is not appropriate here."

**4.2.21**  "Enemies must be recognised by a man though they move in disguise; the untrustworthy, after gaining trust, strike at vulnerabilities."

**4.2.22**  "Vālin is clever in his undertakings; kings of wide vision are destroyers of enemies — ordinary men must be alert to that."

**4.2.23**  "Go to them yourself in an ordinary guise, O leaping one, and learn who they are through the means used with those who are suspected — by form and by speaking with them."

**4.2.24**  "Observe the disposition of those two; if their minds are joyful, reassure them again and again with praise and with signs."

**4.2.25**  "Standing in my very presence, ask them, O foremost of monkeys, what is the purpose of those bow-bearers' entry into this forest."

**4.2.26**  "If these two have pure souls, so learn, O leaping one; their wickedness, if any, will be discerned through their speech or through their forms."

**4.2.27**  Thus directed by the king of monkeys, the son of the Wind God resolved to go where those two — Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa — were.

**4.2.28**  Having honoured the words of that greatly fearful, unapproachable monkey with "so be it," the great-spirited Hanūmān then went to where Rāma of surpassing strength and Lakṣmaṇa were.

**4.3.1**  Hanūmān, having understood the words of the great-souled Sugrīva, leaped from the mountain Ṛśyamūka to where the two Rāghavas were.

**4.3.2**  Having gone there, the powerful Hanūmān, foremost of monkeys, of true valour, approached those two with gentle words.

**4.3.3**  Abandoning his own form, the monkey approached in the guise of a mendicant and spoke to those two heroes and praised them as was fitting.

**4.3.4**  "You two ascetics, resembling royal sages or gods, firm in vows and of excellent complexion — how have you arrived in this place?"

**4.3.5**  "Frightening the herds of deer and other forest-dwellers, gazing in all directions at the trees that grow on the banks of Pampā —"

**4.3.6**  "— you who adorn this river of auspicious waters, you who are swift, steadfast, golden in lustre — who are you two, clad in bark garments?"

**4.3.7**  "Heroes with the side-glance of lions, with the surpassing might and gait of lions, holding bows resembling Indra's bow in your wide arms —"

**4.3.8**  "— endowed with majesty and beauty, with the gait of the foremost of bulls, with arms like an elephant's trunk, radiant, O bulls among men —"

**4.3.9**  "— by whose radiance this king of mountains is illumined, you two worthy of kingship, resembling immortals — how have you come to this place?"

**4.3.10**  "Lotus-petal-eyed heroes, wearing circles of matted locks, heroes alike to one another — you seem to have come from the world of the gods."

**4.3.11**  "As if by chance arrived on this earth — like the moon and the sun — broad-chested heroes, human in form but divine in appearance."

**4.3.12**  "With lion shoulders, of great spirit, equal in pride like bull-oxen, your arms long and well-rounded, like the finest iron bars, worthy of all ornaments — why then are you not adorned?"

**4.3.13**  "I consider you both fit to protect this entire earth — with its oceans and forests, adorned with Vindhya and Meru."

**4.3.14**  "And these two bows, beautiful and smooth, adorned with wondrous decorations, shine like Indra's thunderbolt decked with gold."

**4.3.15**  "And the quivers, filled with sharp arrows and fair to behold, are terrible, life-ending — like blazing serpents."

**4.3.16**  "These two swords, of great size and broad, adorned with refined gold, blaze with glory like serpents that have shed their skins."

**4.3.17**  "Thus I address you — why do you not speak to me?"

**4.3.18**  "There is one named Sugrīva, a righteous-souled leader of a monkey troop, a hero wronged by his brother, who wanders the world in misery."

**4.3.19**  "I have come, sent by that great-souled Sugrīva, king among the foremost of monkeys — I am a monkey named Hanūmān."

**4.3.20**  "The righteous-souled Sugrīva desires friendship with you two; know me as his minister, a monkey, the son of the Wind God."

**4.3.21**  "Concealed in the guise of a mendicant, able to go wherever I will, able to take any form I wish, I have arrived here from Ṛśyamūka out of desire to please Sugrīva."

**4.3.22**  Having thus spoken to those two heroes, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, who were versed in speech, the one skilled in speech said nothing more.

**4.3.23**  Having heard his words, the glorious Rāma, with a delighted face, spoke to Lakṣmaṇa, his brother standing at his side.

**4.3.24**  "This is the minister of Sugrīva, the great-souled lord of monkeys, for whom I have been longing — he has come to my presence."

**4.3.25**  "Address him, O Saumitri — this monkey who is Sugrīva's minister, versed in speech — with sweet words full of affection, O subduer of foes."

**4.4.1**  Then Hanūmān, greatly delighted, on hearing those sweet-spoken words — "one whose purpose is accomplished" — went in his mind to Sugrīva.

**4.4.2**  "The return of the kingdom is fitting for that great-souled Sugrīva, since one whose purpose is accomplished has arrived, and this very purpose has come to meet him."

**4.4.3**  Then Hanūmān, the foremost of monkeys, supremely delighted and skilled in speech, replied with these words to Rāma.

**4.4.4**  "For what purpose have you come, along with your younger brother, to this dreadful and inaccessible forest adorned by the Pampā woodland and filled with all manner of serpents and wild beasts?"

**4.4.5**  Hearing those words of his, Lakṣmaṇa, prompted by Rāma, described the great-souled Rāma, son of Daśaratha.

**4.4.6**  "There was a king named Daśaratha, resplendent and devoted to righteousness; this man is his elder son, known among people by the name of Rāma."

**4.4.7**  "A refuge for all living beings, one who has perfectly fulfilled his father's command, this hero is the most virtuous among the sons of Daśaratha."

**4.4.8**  "Fallen from his kingdom, he came here to dwell in the forest together with me; self-controlled and of great splendour, he was followed by his wife Sītā — like the sun at the close of day attended by its radiance."

**4.4.9**  "I am his younger brother, who by reason of his virtues have taken upon myself the condition of a servant; my name is Lakṣmaṇa — of one who is grateful and widely knowledgeable."

**4.4.10**  "Of one worthy of happiness, worthy of greatness, whose soul is devoted to the welfare of all beings — now stripped of sovereignty and resorting to forest-dwelling."

**4.4.11**  "His wife has been carried off by a rākṣasa who assumed any form at will, seizing his moment when they were alone; nor is it known which rākṣasa it was that abducted his wife."

**4.4.12**  "Danu by name, the son of Śrī, who had become a rākṣasa through a curse, informed him of Sugrīva, the capable lord of monkeys."

**4.4.13**  "'That mighty hero will know the abductor of your wife' — having said thus, Danu departed radiant to heaven in peace."

**4.4.14**  "All this has been truthfully told to you as you inquired; both Rāma and I have come to Sugrīva for refuge."

**4.4.15**  "This man, having formerly been lord of the world and having bestowed wealth and attained unsurpassed renown, now desires Sugrīva as his master."

**4.4.16**  "With Rāma overwhelmed by grief, afflicted by sorrow and seeking refuge, it is fitting that Sugrīva, together with his troop-leaders, show him his favour."

**4.4.17**  To Lakṣmaṇa, who was speaking thus in a pitiful manner with tears falling, Hanūmān, skilled in speech, replied with these words.

**4.4.18**  "Such men as these — endowed with wisdom, who have conquered anger and mastered their senses — are indeed to be met by the lord of monkeys; by good fortune they have come into view."

**4.4.19**  "For he too has been driven from his kingdom, has been made an enemy by Vālin, his wife has been taken, he wanders in the forest in fear, and has been grievously wronged by his brother."

**4.4.20**  "Sugrīva, son of the Sun-god, together with us, will render assistance to you both in the search for Sītā."

**4.4.21**  Having said thus, Hanūmān spoke smoothly and with sweet speech to Rāghava: "Let us go to Sugrīva."

**4.4.22**  The righteous-souled Lakṣmaṇa, having duly honoured Hanūmān who had spoken thus, said this to Rāghava.

**4.4.23**  "As this son of the Wind-god, the monkey, reports with delight — one whose purpose is accomplished has also arrived here; your purpose too is as good as accomplished, O Rāghava."

**4.4.24**  "His countenance is serene and bright, and he speaks with evident gladness; the heroic Hanūmān, son of Vāyu, will not speak anything untrue."

**4.4.25**  Thereupon the greatly wise Hanūmān, son of the Wind-god, took the two heroes, the Rāghava princes, and went to the king of the monkeys.

**4.4.26**  That foremost of monkeys, the son of the Wind, of vast renown and great valour, with auspicious mind, as though his task were already accomplished, set forth in great delight along with Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa toward that best of mountains.

**4.5.1**  And Hanūmān, going from Ṛśyamūka to that Malaya mountain, then announced the two heroes, the Rāghava princes, to the king of monkeys.

**4.5.2**  "This is Rāma, of great wisdom and firm valour, who has arrived along with his brother Lakṣmaṇa — this Rāma of true heroism."

**4.5.3**  "Born in the lineage of the Ikṣvākus, the son of Daśaratha, Rāma is renowned for righteousness and has honoured his father's command."

**4.5.4**  "While this great-souled, disciplined man was dwelling in the forest, his wife was carried off by a rākṣasa; he has come to you for refuge."

**4.5.5**  "He is one by whom the sacrificial fire has been gratified with Rājasūya and Aśvamedha rites, and by whom gifts of cows numbering hundreds of thousands have been distributed as sacrificial fees."

**4.5.6**  "He by whose ascetic power and truthful speech the earth was governed — for the sake of a woman, that son, this Rāma, has come to you for refuge."

**4.5.7**  "These two brothers, Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, who desire friendship with you — receive them and honour them; both are most worthy of honour."

**4.5.8**  Having heard the words of Hanūmān, Sugrīva was glad at heart; the dreadful fear he had felt on account of the Rāghava princes left him, and his anxiety was gone.

**4.5.9**  Sugrīva, the lord of monkeys, assuming human form and having become most handsome in appearance, addressed Rāghava with affection.

**4.5.10**  "You are disciplined in righteousness, valiant, and affectionate toward all; your virtues have been truthfully described to me by the son of Vāyu."

**4.5.11**  "Therefore this is indeed my honour and the highest gain for me, O lord, that you desire friendship with me, a mere monkey."

**4.5.12**  "If friendship pleases you, here is my arm extended; let hand take hand, and let the bond of that compact be made firm."

**4.5.13**  Hearing those well-spoken words of Sugrīva, Rāma, with a joyful heart, clasped that hand in his own hand and embraced him tightly, taking that heartfelt friendship to himself.

**4.5.14**  Then Hanūmān, the subduer of enemies, casting aside his mendicant's form, kindled fire in his own true form from two sticks of wood.

**4.5.15**  Having then worshipped and honoured the blazing fire with flowers, he, greatly pleased and with full concentration, placed it between those two.

**4.5.16**  Then Sugrīva and Rāghava both circumambulated the blazing fire and thus entered into the state of friendship.

**4.5.17**  Then those two — the monkey and the Rāghava — both with greatly gladdened hearts, gazed at each other without finding satiation.

**4.5.18**  Then the spirited Sugrīva, with one-pointed mind, spoke these words to Rāma, son of Daśaratha, who is versed in all matters.

**4.6.1**  "O Rāma, this counsellor of mine, that best of ministers Hanūmān, has told me the reason for which you have come to this desolate forest."

**4.6.2**  "While you were dwelling in the forest together with your brother Lakṣmaṇa, your wife Maithilī, the daughter of Janaka, was carried off by a rākṣasa."

**4.6.3**  "She was crying, separated from you and from the wise Lakṣmaṇa; that one, seeking his opportunity, after slaying the vulture Jaṭāyus —"

**4.6.4**  "The sorrow born of separation from your wife — you shall be freed from it before long; I will bring her back, as a lost Vedic text is recovered."

**4.6.5**  "Whether she abides in the netherworld or in the sky above, O subduer of foes, I will bring your wife back and give her to you."

**4.6.6**  "Know this to be the truth of my word, O Rāghava; cast aside grief, O mighty-armed one — I will bring that beloved wife to you."

**4.6.7**  "By inference I know — there is no doubt — it is that Maithilī; I saw her being carried away by a rākṣasa of cruel deeds."

**4.6.8**  "She was crying out 'Rāma! Rāma!' and 'Lakṣmaṇa!' loudly, writhing in the arms of Rāvaṇa like the wife of the king of serpents in his coils."

**4.6.9**  "Having seen me standing on the mountain slope as the fifth among my companions, she cast down her upper garment and her bright ornaments."

**4.6.10**  "Those were gathered up and stored away by us, O Rāghava; I will bring them — you should identify them."

**4.6.11**  Then Rāma said to Sugrīva, who had spoken such pleasing words: "Bring them quickly, my friend — why do you delay?"

**4.6.12**  Thus addressed, Sugrīva quickly entered the deep cave of the mountain, out of desire to please the Rāghava.

**4.6.13**  And taking the upper garment and the bright ornaments, the monkey showed them to Rāma saying, "Look at these."

**4.6.14**  Then, having taken that garment and those bright ornaments, he was choked with tears — like the moon obscured by mist.

**4.6.15**  Overwhelmed by the flood of tears welling up from his love for Sītā, crying out "Ah, my beloved!" he abandoned his composure and fell to the ground.

**4.6.16**  Pressing those finest ornaments again and again to his heart, he heaved deep sighs — like an angered serpent in its burrow.

**4.6.17**  With an unbroken stream of tears, Rāma looked to Lakṣmaṇa at his side and, in his wretchedness, began to lament.

**4.6.18**  "Look, Lakṣmaṇa — this upper garment cast away upon the ground by the daughter of Videha as she was being carried off, and these ornaments from her body."

**4.6.19**  "On the grassy ground it was surely cast by Sītā as she was carried away; this ornament is seen here, of just such an appearance as hers."

**4.6.20**  "Tell me, Sugrīva — in what direction was she seen by you as she was being carried off by the fearsome-formed rākṣasa — she who is as dear to me as my own life?"

**4.6.21**  "Or where does that rākṣasa dwell — that great affliction of mine — on whose account I shall destroy all the rākṣasas?"

**4.6.22**  "By the one who carried off Maithilī and who has grievously enraged me, the door of death has been opened for the end of his own life."

**4.6.23**  "She who is dearest to me has been seized and carried off from the forest by a night-wanderer who overpowered her — tell me who that enemy of mine is, O lord of monkeys; today I will send him to the presence of Yama."

**4.7.1**  Thus addressed by the grief-stricken Rāma, the monkey Sugrīva spoke with hands folded in reverence, his voice choked with tears and his speech broken by weeping.

**4.7.2**  "I do not know the abode of that wicked demon in any way, nor his power, his prowess, nor indeed the lineage or family of that base-born one.

**4.7.3**  Yet I truly promise you: abandon your grief, O subduer of enemies — I shall make such effort that you will recover the daughter of Mithilā.

**4.7.4**  Having slain Rāvaṇa together with his retinue and having satisfied my own valor, I shall act in such a way that before long you will be pleased.

**4.7.5**  Enough of clinging to this despondency — recall the inner fortitude natural to you. Such mental feebleness does not befit one of your kind.

**4.12.18**  Those two brothers, overcome with rage, struck each other in combat with palms like thunderbolts and fists like vajras.

**4.12.19**  Then Rāma, bow in hand, observing both of them — those two heroes who resembled each other mutually, like two divine Aśvins —

**4.12.20**  — since the Rāghava could not distinguish Sugrīva from Vālī, he did not then resolve to release the death-dealing arrow.

**4.12.21**  In this interval, Sugrīva was broken by Vālī and, not seeing the Rāghava as his protector, fled headlong to Ṛśyamūka.

**4.12.22**  Exhausted, his body drenched in blood, shattered by blows, pursued by Vālī in wrath, he entered the great forest.

**4.12.23**  Seeing him enter the forest, the mighty Vālī, out of fear of the curse, said 'You are freed' and turned back.

**4.12.24**  The Rāghava too, together with his brother and also with Hanūmat, came to that very forest where the monkey Sugrīva was.

**4.12.25**  Seeing Rāma arrive with Lakṣmaṇa, Sugrīva, ashamed and dejected, looking down at the earth, spoke these words:

**4.12.26**  "Having told me 'Challenge him,' and having displayed your prowess, and then having had me struck down by my enemy — what is this that you have now done?"

**4.12.27**  "At that very moment you should have told me truly, O Rāghava, 'I will not slay Vālī' — then I would not have departed from here."

**4.12.28**  As the great-souled Sugrīva spoke thus with pitiable and mournful voice, the Rāghava again replied:

**4.12.29**  "Hear me, dear Sugrīva, and let your anger be dispelled — hear the reason why that arrow was not released by me."

**4.12.30**  "In ornaments, dress, build, and gait, you and Vālī, O Sugrīva, are alike to each other."

**4.12.31**  "In voice, in lustre, in glance, O monkey, in manner of movement, and in speech — I could not discern any mark of distinction between you two."

**4.12.32**  "Thus, O foremost of monkeys, confounded by the similarity of form, I did not release that swift, enemy-destroying arrow."

**4.12.33**  "But see now — this very moment I shall fell Vālī in battle with a single arrow, writhing on the ground."

**4.12.34**  "Make some distinguishing mark upon yourself, O lord of monkeys, by which I may recognise you as you come to single combat."

**4.12.35**  "Uprooting this blossoming gajapuṣpī creeper of auspicious marks, place it, O Lakṣmaṇa, around the neck of this great-souled Sugrīva."

**4.12.36**  Then, having uprooted from the mountain slope that gajapuṣpī laden with blossoms, Lakṣmaṇa placed it around his neck.

**4.12.37**  He, the glorious one, thus adorned with the creeper clinging to his neck, shone like a rain-cloud with the Saṃdhyā glow, like a garland of cranes.

**4.12.38**  Shining brilliantly in form, confirmed by Rāma's words, he went together with Rāma to Kiṣkindhā, governed by Vālī.

**4.13.1**  That righteous-souled elder brother of Lakṣmaṇa departed from Ṛśyamūka together with Sugrīva toward Kiṣkindhā, which was guarded by the might of Vāli.

**4.13.2**  Rāma took up his great bow adorned with gold, and grasped his battle-winning arrows, blazing like the sun.

**4.13.3**  In front of that great-souled Rāghava went Sugrīva with his powerful neck, and mighty Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.13.4**  Behind came the heroic Hanūmān, the monkey Nala, Nīla, and the great-splendored Tāra—leaders of the monkey troops.

**4.13.5**  They gazed upon trees bending under the weight of blossoms, and upon rivers bearing clear waters flowing toward the ocean.

**4.13.6**  They looked upon caves and mountain ranges, cascading waterfalls, caverns, prominent peaks, and lovely rocky clefts.

**4.13.7**  Along the way they beheld ponds full of water, beautified by leaves clear as vaidūrya gems and by lotuses with sky-reaching buds.

**4.13.8**  Those ponds resounded with kāraṇḍava ducks, sārasa cranes, haṃsa geese, vañjūla birds, water-hens, cakravāka pairs, and many other birds.

**4.13.9**  On all sides they saw deer standing on open ground, ranging the forest fearlessly, feeding on tender shoots of fresh grass.

**4.13.10**  They also saw fearsome wild elephants adorned with white tusks, lone wanderers, enemies of embankments, striding alone through the forest.

**4.13.11**  Beholding yet other forest-dwelling creatures and sky-roaming birds, those who were subject to Sugrīva's command advanced quickly.

**4.13.12**  As they hastened along, Rāma the delight of the Raghu lineage, catching sight of a grove of trees, spoke to Sugrīva.

**4.13.13**  "This cluster of trees gleams in the sky like a cloud—vast as a mass of storm-clouds, its borders ringed with plantain trees."

**4.13.14**  "I wish to know what this is, my friend—I am curious. I want you to relieve my curiosity."

**4.13.15**  Hearing those words of the great-souled Rāghava, Sugrīva, even as he walked on, described that great forest.

**4.13.16**  "O Rāghava, this is a vast hermitage, a destroyer of fatigue, endowed with gardens and woodland, rich in sweet roots, fruits, and water."

**4.13.17**  "Here dwell sages known as the Seven Persons, of sharpened vows—seven in number, who remain with heads downward, ever lying in the water."

**4.13.18**  "They eat once every seven nights, they subsist on air, dwelling in the forest; over seven centuries they have ascended to heaven, still embodied."

**4.13.19**  "By their power this hermitage, encircled by a wall of trees, has become utterly unconquerable—even by gods and demons together with Indra."

**4.13.20**  "Birds avoid this place, as do other forest-wanderers; those who enter it through delusion do not return."

**4.13.21**  "From here, O Rāghava, are heard the distinct sounds of ornaments, and strains of music and song, and a divine fragrance."

**4.13.22**  "The three sacred fires still blaze here; that smoke is visible, a dense pigeon-gray-red column, wrapping itself around the treetops."

**4.13.23**  "O righteous-souled Rāghava, offer obeisance to them—reverently, with your brother Lakṣmaṇa, with palms joined."

**4.13.24**  "For those who bow to these enlightened sages, O Rāma, no misfortune whatsoever is seen to afflict their bodies."

**4.13.25**  Thereupon Rāma, together with his brother Lakṣmaṇa, with palms joined, directed his obeisance toward those great-souled sages and reverently saluted them.

**4.13.26**  Having paid their salutations, the righteous-souled Rāma, his brother Lakṣmaṇa, Sugrīva, and the monkeys proceeded with joyful hearts.

**4.13.27**  Having traveled a great distance from that hermitage of the Seven Persons, they beheld Kiṣkindhā, that impregnable city, ruled by Vāli.

**4.14.1**  All of them, having swiftly reached Kiṣkindhā ruled by Vāli, concealed themselves among trees and stood in the dense forest.

**4.14.2**  Casting his glance all around the forest, Sugrīva who delighted in the forest—broad-necked—roused his anger fiercely.

**4.14.3**  Then, surrounded by his retinue, he let out a terrible roar, as if splitting the sky with his cries, challenging Vāli to battle.

**4.14.4**  Then Sugrīva, radiant as the morning sun, with the gait of a proud lion, seeing Rāma skilled in action, spoke these words.

**4.14.5**  "We have arrived at the city of Kiṣkindhā belonging to Vāli—filled with monkey nets, with portals of burnished gold, abundant with flagstaffs and devices."

**4.14.6**  "The pledge you made earlier, O hero, regarding the slaying of Vāli—now fulfill it swiftly, like the season of fruiting come for the vine."

**4.14.7**  Thus addressed by Sugrīva, that righteous-souled Rāghava, destroyer of enemies, then spoke these words to Sugrīva.

**4.14.8**  "You must bear a distinguishing mark, by means of this garland of gaja flowers, so that I may know you—as the sun in the sky is distinct from the garland of stars."

**4.14.9**  "Today, O monkey, I shall free you in a single stroke—releasing one arrow in battle—from both the fear and the enmity arising from Vāli."

**4.14.10**  "Show me Vāli, the enemy of Sugrīva who wears the form of a brother—so that he may writhe in the forest dust when struck down."

**4.14.11**  "If he comes within range of my sight and yet retreats alive, then let that fault not fall on me—and you must not blame me then."

**4.14.12**  "Before your eyes I pierced seven śāla trees with a single arrow—know by that power that Vāli is today already slain by me."

**4.14.13**  "I have never spoken an untruth, O hero, even in the most difficult circumstances, overwhelmed though I may be by desire for righteousness—nor shall I ever speak one."

**4.14.14**  "I shall fulfill my pledge—abandon your agitation. As Indra of a hundred sacrifices brings rain to make the sown grain sprout in the field, so shall it be."

**4.14.15**  "Therefore, for the purpose of that challenge, O Sugrīva, make that sound which will cause the golden-garlanded Vāli, that monkey, to rush forth."

**4.14.16**  "That Vāli who glories in victory, who boasts of conquest, who loves combat—having been challenged and provoked by you, he will spring forth from the city without hesitation."

**4.14.17**  "Heroes do not endure provocation by enemies in battle, especially when they know their own valor—and all the more so before the eyes of women."

**4.14.18**  Then Sugrīva, tawny as gold, hearing Rāma's words, roared with a fierce and terrible cry, as if cleaving the sky asunder.

**4.14.19**  Terrified by his sound, cows scattered, their brightness dimmed, distraught like noblewomen afflicted by the vice of a king.

**4.14.20**  Deer fled swiftly, like horses broken and routed in battle; birds fell to the ground, like planets whose merit is exhausted.

**4.14.21**  Then that son of the Sun, with valor-enhanced splendor, confident and swift, sent forth a roar like the thunder of gathered storm-clouds—like the lord of rivers whose waves are stirred by the wind.

**4.15.1**  Then Vāli, intolerant of his brother, who was within the inner apartments, heard that roar of the great-souled Sugrīva.

**4.15.2**  Hearing that roar which made all creatures tremble, in an instant his intoxication vanished and great fury descended upon him.

**4.15.3**  Vāli, his body suffused with rage, blazed like the reddened sun at twilight—and instantly became as it were stripped of his calm radiance.

**4.15.4**  Vāli with his fearsome fangs, resembling blazing fire in his wrath, shone like a pond whose lotuses have sprung up together with their stalks.

**4.15.5**  Hearing that unbearable sound, the monkey lord sprang forth with such speed that the stamp of his feet seemed to split the earth.

**4.15.6**  But Tārā, embracing him and showing her affection from love, frightened and agitated, spoke to him these words bearing beneficial consequences.

**4.15.7**  "It is well, O hero—restrain this anger that has swept in like a river in spate; having just risen from your bed at daybreak, put it away like a garland already enjoyed."

**4.15.8**  "Your sudden sally does not please me. Hear me—I shall tell you the reason why you are being restrained."

**4.15.9**  "Before, he came upon you in anger and challenged you to combat; but when he was repulsed by you and lay nearly slain, he fled in all directions."

**4.15.10**  "That he returns here now to challenge you again, after having been so utterly routed and afflicted by you—this arouses my suspicion."

**4.15.11**  "The pride and resolve that can be read in his roaring, and the fierce urgency of that cry—this is no small cause for concern."

**4.15.12**  "I do not think Sugrīva has come here without allies; he roars here relying on some powerful support."

**4.15.13**  "That monkey is by nature shrewd and wise; Sugrīva would not go to combat alongside one whose valor he has not tested."

**4.15.14**  "Even before now, O hero, I heard words from Prince Aṅgada's account; I shall now speak to you words that are for your good."

**4.15.15**  "Your brother's ally is the renowned and battle-hardened Rāma, crusher of enemy armies, arisen like the fire of the world's end."

**4.15.16**  "He is the sheltering tree of the virtuous, the ultimate refuge of those who are distressed, the support of the afflicted, and the sole vessel of glory."

**4.15.17**  "Endowed with knowledge and realized wisdom, devoted to his father's command—like the king of mountains is to minerals, he is a great mine of virtues."

**4.15.18**  "Therefore it is not fitting that you enter into conflict with that great-souled Rāma, who is unconquerable and immeasurable in the deeds of battle."

**4.15.19**  "O hero, I will tell you something—I do not wish to be censured for it. Listen, and do as I counsel; I shall tell you what is for your welfare."

**4.15.20**  "Quickly and properly install Sugrīva as crown prince; do not, O hero and king, enter into conflict with a brother who is backed by a mightier power."

**4.15.21**  "I truly believe what befits you is friendship with Rāma and reconciliation with Sugrīva—casting enmity far away."

**4.15.22**  "This younger monkey is your brother who deserves to be cherished; whether he is there or here, he is in every way still your kinsman."

**4.15.23**  "If my good is dear to you, and if you consider me well-wishing, then, earnestly entreated, heed my words well."

**4.16.1**  To Tārā who spoke thus — she whose face resembles the lord of stars — Vālī spoke words of rebuke and said this:

**4.16.2**  "For what reason should I endure the fury and roaring of him who is both my brother and my enemy? Tell me, O lovely-faced one.

**4.16.3**  For heroes who have never been humbled, who never turn back in battle — to endure insult, O timid one, is worse than death.

**4.16.4**  I am incapable of enduring in combat the fury and roaring of Sugrīva, that wretched-necked one who thirsts for battle.

**4.16.5**  You should not grieve on my account concerning Rāghava; how would one who knows dharma and is grateful commit sin?

**4.16.6**  Turn back with the women — why do you follow me further? O Tārā, your friendship toward me has been shown; your devotion has been rendered.

**4.16.7**  I shall go and fight Sugrīva — abandon your agitation. I will crush his arrogance; he shall not escape with his life.

**4.16.8**  I swear by my own life — turn back. I shall return victorious after defeating that brother of mine in battle."

**4.16.9**  Then Tārā, that sweet-spoken one, embraced Vālī and, weeping softly, performed the auspicious circumambulation of him.

**4.16.10**  Then, having performed the benedictory rites as prescribed by injunction, she who desired his victory entered the inner apartments together with the women, overcome with grief.

**4.16.11**  When Tārā had entered her dwelling together with the women, the angry Vālī went forth from the city, hissing like a great serpent.

**4.16.12**  That swift Vālī, utterly furious, cast his gaze in all directions, eager for the sight of his enemy.

**4.16.13**  Then that glorious one beheld Sugrīva — tawny as gold, well-girded, steadfast, blazing like a fire.

**4.16.14**  Seeing Sugrīva of great might standing ready for battle, the utterly furious Vālī girded his garment tightly.

**4.16.15**  That valiant Vālī, firmly girded, raised his fist and advanced straight toward Sugrīva, resolved to do battle.

**4.16.16**  Sugrīva too, raising his tightly-clenched fist and coming forward with even greater fury, aimed himself at Vālī who wore a golden garland.

**4.16.17**  To Sugrīva the battle-expert, who came rushing with great speed, Vālī — his eyes red with rage — spoke these words:

**4.16.18**  "This fist of mine, tightly closed with fingers firmly set — when I hurl it with full force, it will depart taking your life with it."

**4.16.19**  Thus addressed, Sugrīva in anger replied to Vālī: "Let my fist fall upon your head, bearing away your very life."

**4.16.20**  Struck by him, Vālī in great fury rushed forward with speed and began to pour forth blood, like a mountain gushing with a spring.

**4.16.21**  By Sugrīva, who with his might uprooted a sāla tree and struck Vālī across the limbs, Vālī was shaken like a great mountain struck by a thunderbolt.

**4.16.22**  Vālī, assaulted and reeling from the blow of the sāla tree, became like a vessel in the ocean weighed down by a heavy load.

**4.16.23**  Those two, of fearsome strength and prowess, with the speed of Suparṇa, towering and terrible in form, were like the sun and moon in the sky.

**4.16.24**  Sugrīva, his pride shattered by Vālī and his strength failing, yet showing his agility in anger, displayed his nimbleness before Vālī.

**4.16.25**  Then Rāghava, fitting to his bow an arrow like a venomous serpent, loosed that mighty shaft and drove it into the chest of Vālī.

**4.16.26**  Struck with great force, Vālī fell to the earth.

**4.16.27**  Then, drenched with streams of blood-water, like a blossoming aśoka tree tossed by the wind, the son of Indra — his senses fled — fell to the ground in battle like the banner of Indra cast down.

**4.17.1**  Then Vālī, that hardened warrior, struck by Rāma's arrow, fell suddenly like a felled tree.

**4.17.2**  His whole body laid upon the earth, adorned with ornaments of refined gold, he fell like the banner of the king of gods when its ropes are loosed.

**4.17.3**  When that lord of the host of monkeys fell to the ground, the earth ceased to shine, like the sky when the moon is lost.

**4.17.4**  Even as that great-souled one lay fallen on the earth, neither glory, nor life, nor splendour, nor heroic power departed from his body.

**4.17.5**  The excellent garland given by Śakra — golden, adorned with gems — upheld the life, the radiance, and the glory of that foremost among monkeys.

**4.17.6**  That hero, lord of the monkey hosts, wreathed in that golden garland, its borders fringed as if with twilight hues, resembled a rain-cloud.

**4.17.7**  His garland, his body, and the arrow that had pierced his vitals — as though the glory were fashioned in three parts — shone even in his fallen state.

**4.17.8**  That weapon, hurled from Rāma's bow, lighting the path to heaven for that hero, conveyed him toward the highest destiny.

**4.17.9**  Him thus fallen in battle — like a fire whose flames have departed, like Yayāti fallen from the celestial world when his merit was exhausted —

**4.17.10**  like the sun cast down to earth by time at the end of an age, like Mahendra himself impossible to assail, like Mahendra himself impossible to endure —

**4.17.11**  — Vālī, son of Mahendra, fallen, wearing his golden garland, lion-chested, mighty-armed, blazing-faced, with tawny eyes — Rāma, followed by Lakṣmaṇa, beheld and approached him.

**4.17.12**  Seeing Rāghava and the mighty Lakṣmaṇa, Vālī spoke words that were at once respectful and harsh, yet in accord with dharma:

**4.17.13**  "What virtue have you gained by slaying one turned away from you? For it is because of you that I, inflamed for battle, have met my death.

**4.17.14**  'Rāma is of noble lineage, endowed with virtue, radiant, faithful to his vows, compassionate, and devoted to the welfare of his subjects' —

**4.17.15**  'He is merciful, of great energy, knowing the appointed times, firm in his vows' — thus all beings speak of your fame upon this earth.

**4.17.16**  Weighing those virtues and your high lineage — though restrained by Tārā — I came forth to meet Sugrīva.

**4.17.17**  'He should not strike me — enraged and unwary as I am — from the side of another' — such was the thought that arose in me at not seeing you openly.

**4.17.18**  I did not know you to be this — a man of destroyed soul, bearing the banner of dharma yet unrighteous, of evil conduct, like a well covered with grass.

**4.17.19**  Bearing the guise of the virtuous yet evil, like a fire concealed — I did not recognize you, wrapped in the disguise of dharma.

**4.17.20**  When I have done you no harm in your domain or in your city, and I do not even know you, why do you slay the innocent?

**4.17.21**  I, a monkey who always subsists on fruits and roots, who roams the forest — who was here engaged in combat with another, not with you —

**4.17.22**  You are known as the son of a king of men, pleasing in appearance; even the mark of dharma can be seen upon you, O king.

**4.17.23**  Who, born in a kṣatriya family, learned, with doubts resolved, wearing the mark of dharma as a guise, would commit such a cruel act?

**4.17.24**  Rāma — born in a royal lineage, renowned as righteous — why do you roam about, unworthy yet wearing a worthy form?

**4.17.25**  Conciliation, gift, forbearance, dharma, truth, steadfastness, and heroism — these are the qualities of kings, O king; and punishment too, for those who transgress.

**4.17.26**  We, O Rāma, are forest-dwellers, animals who eat roots and fruits — that is our nature. You are a man, a lord of men.

**4.17.27**  Land, gold, and silver — these are reasons for punishment. What greed of yours in the forest, or for my fruits, is there?

**4.17.28**  Both policy and propriety, both punishment and favour — the conduct of kings is unsullied. Kings do not act by the rule of passion.

**4.17.29**  But you are governed by passion, irascible, unstable, your royal conduct muddled, and devoted only to the bow.

**4.17.30**  You have no reverence for dharma, your mind is not fixed on what is right; enslaved by passion through the senses, you are dragged about, O lord of men.

**4.17.31**  Having slain me here with an arrow — me, the guiltless one — O Kākutstha, what will you say in the assembly of the virtuous, having committed this censurable deed?

**4.17.32**  Slayer of a king, slayer of a brahmin, killer of cows, thief, one addicted to killing living beings, an atheist, one who marries before his elder brother — all these go to hell.

**4.17.33**  My hide is not to be worn by the virtuous; my hair and bones are to be avoided; my flesh is not to be eaten by those like you who practise dharma.

**4.17.34**  Five five-clawed animals are permitted as food for brāhmaṇas and kṣatriyas, O Rāghava: the porcupine, the śvāvidha, the monitor lizard, the hare, and the tortoise as the fifth.

**4.17.35**  My hide and bones, O king, the wise do not touch, and my flesh is not to be eaten — such am I, a five-clawed animal, slain by you.

**4.17.36**  With you as its lord, O Kākutstha, the earth is truly without a worthy lord — like a virtuous woman fallen prey to a scoundrel.

**4.17.37**  Treacherous, deceitful, petty, false in your outward show of humility — how is it that you, a sinner, were born of the great-souled Daśaratha?

**4.17.38**  By the elephant that is Rāma — whose girths of good conduct are severed, who transgresses the dharma of the virtuous, who has cast off the goad of righteousness — I have been slain.

**4.17.39**  Had you fought openly against me in battle, O son of a king, today you would have met the god Vaivasvata, slain by my hand.

**4.17.40**  But I, formidable as I am, have been slain in battle by you who remained unseen — like a man overpowered by drink, bitten by a snake while asleep.

**4.17.41**  Though you have slain me for the sake of pleasing Sugrīva, I could have bound Rāvaṇa by the neck and delivered him to you unslain in battle.

**4.17.42**  I would have found out for you, at your command, where Maithilī is hidden — whether in the ocean's waters or in the underworld — as one traces a white she-mule.

**4.17.43**  It would be fitting that Sugrīva obtain the kingdom on my death by natural causes — but it is unfitting that I have been slain by you through unrighteousness in battle.

**4.17.44**  Let the world be thus disposed by time as it will — if indeed you have a fitting response, let it be well considered."

**4.17.45**  Having spoken thus, his mouth parched, the great-souled son of the king of the immortals — tormented by the wound of the arrow — gazed upon Rāma who blazed like the sun, and fell silent.

**4.18.1**  Thus addressed with words at once respectful, grounded in dharma and good counsel, yet harsh — words spoken by the stricken and senseless Vālī — Rāma replied.

**4.18.2**  To that foremost of monkeys who had spoken his words — like the sun bereft of radiance, like a cloud that has released its water, like a fire that has subsided —

**4.18.3**  — endowed with the qualities of dharma and statecraft, that peerless lord of monkeys, by whom Rāma had been rebuked — Rāma then spoke to Vālī:

**4.18.4**  "Without understanding dharma, artha, kāma, and worldly convention, how do you in your childishness rebuke me here today?

**4.18.5**  Without consulting the wise elders honoured by teachers, O gentle one, you wish to speak against me here out of mere monkey frivolity.

**4.18.6**  This entire earth with its mountains, forests, and groves — and the power of punishment and favour over animals, birds, and men alike — belongs to the Ikṣvākus.

**4.18.7**  That earth is governed by the righteous-souled Bharata — truthful, upright, knowing the true nature of dharma, kāma, and artha, devoted to punishment and favour alike.

**4.18.8**  In whom both policy and propriety, and truth too are well-established, and whose heroism has been witnessed — he is the king who knows place and time.

**4.18.9**  Acting on his orders established in dharma, we and the other kings roam the entire earth, desiring the perpetuation of righteousness.

**4.18.10**  While that tiger among kings, Bharata — devoted to dharma — governs the entire earth, who could transgress the bounds of righteous rule?

**4.18.11**  We, established in our highest dharma, placing Bharata's command foremost, restrain those who have strayed from the right path, according to due procedure.

**4.18.12**  But you, your dharma sullied, censured by your deeds, governed by the rule of passion — you have not stood on the path of kingship.

**4.18.13**  An elder brother, a father, and one who confers knowledge — these three are to be known as fathers; and they who walk the path of dharma.

**4.18.14**  A younger brother, one's own son, and a pupil endowed with virtues — these three are to be thought of as sons, if dharma is the governing principle here.

**4.18.15**  The dharma of the virtuous is subtle and supremely difficult to know, O leaper; dwelling in the heart, the ātman of all beings knows the auspicious and the inauspicious.

**4.18.16**  Fickle as you are, deliberating with fickle monkeys of unformed selves — like one born blind deliberating with the congenitally blind — what indeed can you see?

**4.18.17**  I shall make this matter clear to you. Surely you ought not to rebuke me from mere anger alone.

**4.18.18**  Observe then this reason for which you have been slain by me: you cohabit with your brother's wife, having abandoned the eternal law of dharma.

**4.18.19**  While the great-souled Sugrīva is still alive, you in your lust cohabit with Rumā — who is as a daughter-in-law to you — O perpetrator of evil deeds.

**4.18.20**  Therefore, this punishment has been meted out to you — who have transgressed dharma and acted by passion — for this violation of your brother's wife, O monkey.

**4.18.21**  For one who has acted contrary to dharma and strayed from worldly convention, I see no restraint other than punishment, O lord of monkey hosts.

**4.18.22**  A man who from lust makes advances upon his own natural sister, or upon the wife of his younger brother — for him death is prescribed as the punishment.

**4.18.23**  Bharata is the sovereign of the earth; we act on his command; and you have transgressed dharma — how could this be overlooked?

**4.18.24**  The wise Bharata, governing righteously those who transgress the dharma of their superiors, is established in the restraint of those who act by passion.

**4.18.25**  And we, O lord of monkeys, making Bharata's command our rule, are established in the restraint of those like you who have broken the boundaries of propriety.

**4.18.26**  My friendship with Sugrīva is the same as with Lakṣmaṇa; and he who is devoted to highest good — his cause concerning wife and kingdom is mine as well.

**4.18.27**  And a pledge was given by me then in the presence of the monkeys — how could one such as I leave a pledge unregarded?

**4.18.28**  Therefore, by all these reasons, great and in accord with dharma, let you accept that which is a fitting chastisement for you.

**4.18.29**  In every way, your restraint is to be seen as dharma itself; and to one who looks upon dharma as supreme, it is a service to a friend.

**4.18.30**  Men who have committed sins and been punished by kings come forth purified and reach heaven, like the virtuous who have performed meritorious deeds.

**4.18.31**  By the noble Māndhātṛ of mine, a grievous calamity was once sought — just as the sin was committed by an ascetic in that case, so the sin has been committed by you.

**4.18.32**  Even sins committed by other heedless sovereigns — they perform atonement for them, and by that the taint is quenched.

**4.18.33**  Therefore, enough of anguish — this death has been ordained according to dharma, O tiger among monkeys; we do not act by our own will.

**4.18.34**  With snares, nooses, and various traps, both hidden and visible, men take very many animals — whether running, frightened, calm, or standing still.

**4.18.35**  Men seeking meat strike animals forcefully whether they are heedless or heedful, and even those with their backs turned — and there is no fault in this.

**4.18.36**  Royal sages too skilled in dharma go hunting here. Therefore you, O monkey, have been slain in battle by me with an arrow — whether you fought back or not — for you are a tree-dwelling animal.

**4.18.37**  Of the rare gift of dharma and of auspicious life, O foremost of monkeys, kings are the givers — there is no doubt.

**4.18.38**  One should not injure them, nor revile them, nor offend them, nor speak unpleasant words to them — these kings walk the earth in human form as gods.

**4.18.39**  But you, not knowing dharma, clinging to mere rage, seek to sully me — who am established in the dharma inherited from my fathers and forefathers."

**4.18.40**  Thus addressed by Rāma, Vālī, greatly pained, then replied to Rāma with joined palms — that lord of monkeys:

**4.18.41**  "What you have said, O best of men — it is indeed so; there is no doubt in this. Against the exalted, the lowly is not capable of reply.

**4.18.42**  Whatever unbecoming and unpleasant word I spoke before through heedlessness — even for that you should not find fault with me, O Rāghava.

**4.18.43**  For you are the knower of the true nature of things that are seen, devoted to the welfare of your subjects; your imperishable intellect is clear in determining cause and effect.

**4.18.44**  Me too — who am recognized as one who has placed transgression of dharma foremost — protect with words in accord with dharma, O knower of dharma.

**4.18.45**  Then Vālī, his throat choked with tears, in a voice full of grief and sorrow, gazing at Rāma softly spoke — like an elephant mired in mud:

**4.18.46**  "It is not for myself that I grieve, nor for Tārā, nor for my kinsmen — but for my son Aṅgada, golden-limbed, foremost in virtues.

**4.18.47**  He who, cherished from his very childhood, will be wretched at not seeing me — like a pool whose waters have been drunk, he will wither away.

**4.18.48**  Bestow your highest regard upon both Sugrīva and Aṅgada — for you are the chastener and the protector, established in the rule of what should and should not be done.

**4.18.49**  The same conduct that you observe, O lord of men, toward Bharata and Lakṣmaṇa — that same conduct toward Sugrīva and Aṅgada, O king, you should think fit to show.

**4.18.50**  You should conduct yourself in such a way that Sugrīva does not despise that afflicted Tārā — blameless on her own account yet tainted by my fault.

**4.18.51**  For one favoured by you, walking under your sway and following your will, the kingdom can be enjoyed in peace."

**4.18.52**  Rāma then consoled Vālī, who was now clear of vision.

**4.18.53**  "Neither should you be anxious about us, nor about yourself, O best of monkeys — we have acted with special regard for you, our resolve established in dharma.

**4.18.54**  He who metes out punishment to one deserving punishment, and he who deserves it and is punished — both, their purpose of cause and effect accomplished, do not fall into disgrace.

**4.18.55**  Therefore you, through this contact with punishment, freed from taint, have returned to your own righteous nature, walking the path as seen by dharma.

**4.18.56**  Hearing those sweet, collected words of the great-souled Rāma — who treads the path of dharma, who crushes the arrogant in battle — those well-reasoned words, the monkey replied:

**4.18.57**  "O lord, whatever offensive words I, scorched by the arrow and out of my senses, not knowing, spoke against you — for this, O one comparable to Mahendra in fearsome prowess, be pleased and forgive me, O lord of the earth."

**4.29.10**  "Having heard the cries of the cakravāka birds with their companions — she whose eyes are wide as a white lotus — how will she fare?"

**4.29.11**  "Wandering among lakes, rivers, pools, forests, and groves — without her, whose eyes are like a fawn's, I find no happiness today."

**4.29.12**  "Surely separation from me and her own delicateness must not long torment that passionate woman — love continuously accompanied by autumnal qualities."

**4.29.13**  Thus and in like vein the best of men, that prince, lamented — like a sāranga bird seeking water from the lord of the thirty gods.

**4.29.14**  Then, having roamed the lovely mountain slopes in search of fruit and turning back, the glorious Lakṣmaṇa saw his elder brother.

**4.29.15**  Seeing him overwhelmed by unbearable grief, barely conscious, alone in a deserted place, the high-souled son of Sumitrā, distressed and pained by his brother's despair, spoke to Rāma:

**4.29.16**  "Why, noble one, surrender to the power of desire? Why overpower your own manly strength? This steadiness of mind is always to be gathered in — what is accomplished here by abandoning such discipline?"

**4.29.17**  "Apply yourself to action, to the tranquility of mind, to the time suited to disciplined effort, to the capacity of your allies — O you of undaunted spirit, make the cause of your own deeds your motive."

**4.29.18**  "O lord of the human lineage, Jānakī is not easily gained by another while she has you as her protector; and approaching a blazing fire crowned with flame, no one, O you worthy of the foremost of heroes, goes unburned."

**4.29.19**  Then Rāma, together with Lakṣmaṇa, spoke to the indomitable Lakṣmaṇa a natural word, both beneficial and wholesome, attached to prudent policy and composed with conciliation, duty, and purpose:

**4.29.20**  "The task must undoubtedly be attended to; indeed the particular undertaking must be pursued; surely for a difficult task once set in motion, the fruit need not be anxiously considered — it follows in course."

**4.29.21**  Then, brooding upon Maithilī whose eyes are like lotus petals, Rāma spoke to Lakṣmaṇa with a face that had grown parched:

**4.29.22**  "The thousand-eyed Indra, having satisfied the earth with water, having caused the crops to ripen, stands established having done his work.

**4.29.23**  "The clouds, their sound deep and resonant, with mountains and trees preceding them, having released their waters, are now exhausted, O prince.

**4.29.24**  "Dark as petals of the blue lotus, having darkened all ten directions, the rain-clouds, like elephants out of rut, have quieted their rush.

**4.29.25**  "The rain-winds, laden with water, of great speed, fragrant with kuṭaja and arjuna blossoms, having coursed their way, have ceased, O gentle one, though they were fully roused.

**4.29.26**  "The sound of the clouds, of elephants, of peacocks, and of mountain torrents, O Lakṣmaṇa — O sinless one — has suddenly grown still.

**4.29.27**  "Drenched by the great clouds, the mountains with their varied slopes shine clear, gleaming as if anointed with the rays of the moon.

**4.29.28**  "The autumnal rivers slowly, slowly reveal their sandbanks — like women bashful at a new union revealing their hips.

**4.29.29**  "The bodies of water, O gentle Lakṣmaṇa, their waters clear, resounding with the cries of kurara birds, and filled with flocks of cakravākas, shine beautifully.

**4.29.30**  "For those kings who are bound together in mutual enmity and eager for victory, O prince, O gentle one, the season of enterprise has arrived.

**4.29.31**  "This is the foremost season of campaign for kings, O prince — yet I do not see Sugrīva, nor any such effort on his part.

**4.29.32**  "The four months of the rains have passed, each seeming like a hundred years to me, scorched by grief as I am, not seeing Sītā, O gentle Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.29.33**  "Bereft of my beloved, afflicted by sorrow, my kingdom taken, banished from home — yet king Sugrīva shows no compassion toward me, O Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.29.34**  "Without a protector, his kingdom stolen, humiliated by Rāvaṇa, wretched, far from home, maddened with desire — he came to me as his refuge.

**4.29.35**  "For all these reasons, O gentle scorcher of foes, I am being scorned by that evil-souled king of monkeys.

**4.29.36**  "He who, having reckoned the time for the search for Sītā and made a compact, now that his purpose is achieved, that evil-minded one does not remember.

**4.29.37**  "You — enter Kiṣkindhā and speak to that foremost of monkeys, that fool attached to rustic pleasures: speak to Sugrīva on my behalf, this word:

**4.29.38**  "He who, having promised hope to supplicants who have come to him — and those who have also previously done him service — then destroys that hope, is the most wretched of men in the world.

**4.29.39**  "He who, whatever word is spoken — auspicious or inauspicious — grasps it with truthfulness: he is a hero, he is the best of men.

**4.29.40**  "Those who, having achieved their own ends, cease to be friends to those whose ends are not yet achieved — even flesh-eaters do not consume those ingrates when they die.

**4.29.41**  "Surely he wishes to see the form of my golden-backed bow, drawn in battle, like a cluster of lightning.

**4.29.42**  "He wishes to hear again the terrible sound of my bowstring when I am angry in combat — a sound like the crash of Indra's thunderbolt.

**4.29.43**  "Though things have come to such a pass, O hero, O prince, if his valor is well-known and I have you as my ally, I need have no worry.

**4.29.44**  "The purpose for which this undertaking was made — O conqueror of enemy cities — the lord of monkeys, having achieved his own aim, does not honor the compact.

**4.29.45**  "Having pledged the rainy season as the time, the lord of monkeys sports through the four months that have passed and takes no heed.

**4.29.46**  "Sporting with his ministers and assembly, he engages only in drinking; Sugrīva shows no compassion toward us who are wretched with grief.

**4.29.47**  "Go, and tell Sugrīva — O mighty son, let him be told what the nature of my wrath is; and speak this word to him:

**4.29.48**  "The path by which Vālin went when he was slain has not narrowed, O Sugrīva — stand by your compact; do not follow the path of Vālin."

**4.29.49**  "Vālin alone was slain by my arrow in battle; but you, who have transgressed truthfulness, I shall slay together with your kinsmen."

**4.29.50**  "With affairs having come to such a pass, speak whatever is beneficial, O bull among men, O best of men — make haste; time is being transgressed."

**4.29.51**  "Fulfill toward me, O lord of monkeys, what was promised, looking to the eternal law of duty; do not, after death, go to Yama's abode to behold Vālin there — struck down today by my arrows sent upon you."

**4.29.52**  He — the lord of monkeys and lord of the race of men, of fierce splendor — having observed his elder brother lamenting, his rage intensely grown, wretched, formed a resolute and fierce intention.

**4.30.1**  He — the son of the lord of men, Rāma's younger brother, of undaunted spirit — spoke thus to his elder, that son of the lord of gods, who was afflicted by desire, wretched, overwhelmed by grief, his anger surging:

**4.30.2**  "A monkey will not stand firm in righteous conduct, nor reflect on the fruits that follow from deeds, nor will he abandon the prosperity of the monkey-kingdom — for such is the bent of his mind.

**4.30.3**  "Through the decay of his mind, addicted to rustic pleasures, incapable of requiting your grace — let him, slain, behold his elder Vālin; kingship ought not to be given to one so lacking in virtue.

**4.30.4**  "I cannot contain the wrath of surging force — I shall slay that untruthful Sugrīva today; then let the son of Vālin, with the foremost heroes of the monkeys, carry out the search for the wife of the lord of men."

**4.30.5**  To him who had seized his bow and arrows and was about to spring forward, his fierce battle-rage declared, Rāma — slayer of enemy heroes — spoke a word of his own judgment, with gentle entreaty:

**4.30.6**  "Truly, one like you in the world would not act in such an evil manner; he who, though noble, destroys evil — he is a hero, he is the best of men.

**4.30.7**  "This course, O Lakṣmaṇa of righteous conduct, ought not to be taken by you today; pursue rather that affection, and the compact made at our previous association.

**4.30.8**  "With words employing conciliation, avoiding harshness, you ought to speak to Sugrīva — who has let time pass in the course of seasons."

**4.30.9**  Thus duly instructed in purpose by his elder, the heroic Lakṣmaṇa, slayer of enemy heroes, that bull among men, entered the city.

**4.30.10**  Then Lakṣmaṇa, of auspicious mind and wisdom, devoted to the well-being and good of his brother, his anger fully roused, went toward the palace of the monkey.

**4.30.11**  Seizing his bow resembling the bow of Indra, his wrath like that of Death at the end of time, like Mount Mandara with its peaks — 

**4.30.12**  the younger brother of Rāma, a doer of what was commanded, considering counter-word after counter-word, in wisdom equal to Bṛhaspati —

**4.30.13**  enveloped in the fire of his brother's wrath arising from desire and anger, Lakṣmaṇa went forth then like an angry tempest.

**4.30.14**  Swiftly felling many śāla, tāla, and aśvakarṇa trees, circling mountain peaks and other trees with speed,

**4.30.15**  shattering rocks with his feet like a swift elephant, covering great distances in a single stride, he moved quickly under the urgency of the task.

**4.30.16**  That tiger of the Ikṣvāku line beheld the great city of the monkey-king Kiṣkindhā, filled with hosts of monkeys, set in the mountain gorge, difficult to approach.

**4.30.17**  Lakṣmaṇa, his lips quivering with rage toward Sugrīva, saw the fearsome monkeys moving outside Kiṣkindhā.

**4.30.18**  The monkeys, resembling elephants, were seizing hundreds of mountain crags and full-grown great trees among the mountain hollows.

**4.30.19**  Seeing those monkeys who had taken up weapons, Lakṣmaṇa became doubly enraged, like a fire fed with much fuel.

**4.30.20**  Seeing him in fury, like Death at the end of time, those monkeys were seized with terror in all their limbs and fled in hundreds in every direction.

**4.30.21**  Then those foremost monkeys, entering the palace of Sugrīva, reported both the anger and the arrival of Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.30.22**  But the bull of monkeys, filled with desire, was absorbed in private company with Tārā; at that moment he did not hear the words of those monkey-heroes.

**4.30.23**  Then, dispatched by the ministers, monkeys that made the hair stand on end, resembling mountain-elephants and clouds, issued forth from the city.

**4.30.24**  Armed with claws and fangs, all terrible, all of distorted appearance, all with the pride of tigers, all of monstrous aspect —

**4.30.25**  some having the strength of ten elephants, some ten times that and more, and some were equal in valor to a thousand elephants —

**4.30.26**  the angry Lakṣmaṇa saw all of Kiṣkindhā, that impregnable city, pervaded by those powerful monkeys who bore trees in their hands.

**4.30.27**  Then all those monkeys came forth from within the ramparts and moats and stood there displaying themselves, their spirits erect.

**4.30.28**  Reflecting on Sugrīva's negligence and his afflicted elder brother, the self-possessed hero, under the sway of wrath, went forward again.

**4.30.29**  He breathed long, hot, great sighs; his eyes reddened with fury; that tiger of men blazed like a fire with smoke.

**4.30.30**  His tongue darting like an arrow's point, his body the coil of bow and arrows, the accumulated venom of his own splendor — he was like a five-headed serpent.

**4.30.31**  Approaching him who blazed like the fire of Time, who was like an enraged lord of serpents, Aṅgada fell into deep despondency from fear.

**4.30.32**  Then Sugrīva, of great glory, his eyes copper-red with rage, commanded Aṅgada: "Go, dear son, and announce my coming."

**4.30.33**  "The subduer of foes, Lakṣmaṇa — the younger brother of Rāma — has come to your vicinity; scorched by his brother's misery, he stands at the gate."

**4.30.34**  Having heard Lakṣmaṇa's words, Aṅgada, overwhelmed with grief, came near his father and said: "The son of Sumitrā has arrived here."

**4.30.35**  Seeing him, whose roar was like a great flood, whose sound was like a thunderbolt, those monkeys together raised a lion's roar near Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.30.36**  Roused by that great sound, the monkey awoke — his eyes copper-red, dazed with intoxication, his garlands and ornaments in disarray.

**4.30.37**  Then, having heard Aṅgada's words and come together by the same impetus, two ministers of the monkey-king who were respected and of broad vision —

**4.30.38**  Plakṣa and Prabhāva, ministers versed in purpose and duty, both reported to him the arrival of Lakṣmaṇa, who had come to speak of matters high and low.

**4.30.39**  Having propitiated Sugrīva with words firmly set in conciliation, they sat in attendance upon him as he was seated — even as one attends upon Indra, lord of the Maruts.

**4.30.40**  "The two brothers Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, true to their pledges, of great fortune, who have attained the status of friends, who are worthy of kingdoms and givers of kingdoms —

**4.30.41**  "— of those two, one, Lakṣmaṇa, stands at the gate with bow in hand; from fear of him the monkeys tremble and let out cries.

**4.30.42**  "He — this brother of Rāghava, Lakṣmaṇa, whose charioteer is speech, whose chariot is resolve — has arrived by the command of that Rāma.

**4.30.43**  "O king, bow your head to him and, together with your son and kinsmen, stand firm in your proper compact; be one who is true to his promise."

**4.31.1**  Having heard the words of Aṅgada, and having heard that Lakṣmaṇa was enraged, the self-possessed Sugrīva rose from his seat together with his counsellors.

**4.31.2**  Skilled in counsel and thoroughly versed in deliberation, he weighed the gravity and lightness of the matter and spoke these words to his ministers who were wise in counsel.

**4.31.3**  "I have spoken no ill word, nor have I done anything amiss. Why then is Lakṣmaṇa, the brother of Rāghava, angered? This I ponder."

**4.31.4**  "By my ill-wishers and enemies, who ever seek to sow discord, Rāghava's younger brother has been made to hear of faults in me that do not exist."

**4.31.5**  "In this matter, you must all carefully and methodically ascertain the true state of things, each according to his understanding and with due deliberation."

**4.31.6**  "Indeed I have no fear of Lakṣmaṇa, nor of Rāghava; yet a friend angered without proper cause does indeed cause anxiety."

**4.31.7**  "In every way it is easy to acquire a friend but difficult to maintain that friendship; for because of the inconstancy of minds, affection is sundered even by a trifle."

**4.31.8**  "For this very reason I am afraid regarding the great-souled Rāma: that the benefit he has conferred upon me I am unable to repay."

**4.31.9**  When Sugrīva had spoken thus, Hanūmān, foremost among the monkeys, spoke his own reasoned counsel in the assembly of the monkey ministers.

**4.31.10**  "In every respect it is no wonder, O lord of the monkey host, that you do not forget the auspicious act of deep affection and service rendered to you."

**4.31.11**  "By the heroic Rāghava, casting fear far aside, Vālin — whose prowess equalled Indra's — was slain for your sake alone."

**4.31.12**  "Without any doubt Rāghava is angry out of affection; it is for that reason he has dispatched his brother Lakṣmaṇa, the increaser of glory."

**4.31.13**  "You have been negligent and do not perceive the season, O best of those who know the proper time: the auspicious autumn has arrived, dark-green with flowering saptacchada trees."

**4.31.14**  "The sky is clear, its planets and stars unclouded; the storm-clouds have dispersed; all the quarters, the rivers and the lakes are bright and serene."

**4.31.15**  "Yet you do not recognise the season of effort that has arrived, O foremost of monkeys. It is manifest that you are heedless — and so Lakṣmaṇa has come here."

**4.31.16**  "The harsh words of the great-souled Rāghava — who is afflicted and whose wife has been stolen — spoken through an intermediary, must be endured by you."

**4.31.17**  "For one who has committed an offence such as yours, I see no other fitting course save joining your palms and propitiating Lakṣmaṇa."

**4.31.18**  "A king must inevitably be told what is for his benefit by the ministers he has appointed; and so it is that, setting fear aside, I speak these resolute words."

**4.31.19**  "For Rāghava, when truly enraged and raising his bow, has the power to bring the entire world — gods, asuras, and gandharvas included — under his sway."

**4.31.20**  "He who must afterwards be propitiated again ought not to be provoked; especially so by one who is grateful and remembers the prior benefit received."

**4.31.21**  "Bowing your head before him, together with your son and your allies, O king, abide by your own covenant and remain under his command, as a wife obeys her husband."

**4.31.22**  "The command of Rāma and Rāma's younger brother, joined with the authority of the lord of monkeys, cannot be set aside even in thought; for your mind will come to know the human might of this Rāghava, who blazes with the splendour of Indra."

**4.32.1**  Then Lakṣmaṇa, slayer of enemy heroes, having been duly announced, entered the fearsome cavern-city of Kiṣkindhā by the command of Rāma.

**4.32.2**  The great-bodied and mighty monkeys stationed at the gate, upon seeing Lakṣmaṇa, all stood with their palms joined in reverence.

**4.32.3**  Seeing the son of Daśaratha breathing heavily with rage, the monkeys became frightened and did not surround him.

**4.32.4**  The glorious Lakṣmaṇa beheld that vast and divine cavern, filled with jewels, adorned with flowering groves, charming, and strewn all about with gems.

**4.32.5**  It was crowded with mansions and palace-towers, beautified by merchandise of every kind, and adorned by flowering trees bearing all desired fruits.

**4.32.6**  It was graced by monkeys who were sons of gods and gandharvas, shapeshifters wearing divine garlands and garments, delightful to behold.

**4.32.7**  Its broad avenues were suffused with the fragrances of sandalwood, agaru, and lotuses, and with the sweet scents of maireyā liquors and honey.

**4.32.8**  Rāghava saw there multi-storeyed palaces resembling the slopes of Vindhya and Meru, and clear mountain streams as well.

**4.32.9**  He saw the charming mansions of Aṅgada and Mainda, of Dvivida, Gavaya, Gavākṣa, Gaja, and Śarabha;

**4.32.10**  of Vidyunmālin, Saṃpāti, Sūryākṣa, Hanūmat, Vīrabāhu, Subāhu, and the great-souled Nala;

**4.32.11**  of Kumuda, Suṣeṇa, Tāra, Jāmbavat, Dadhivaktra, Nīla, Supāṭala, and Sunetra.

**4.32.12**  Lakṣmaṇa saw the principal dwelling-places of these great-souled monkey chiefs, standing along the royal road, vast and of great substance.

**4.32.13**  They shone white as autumn clouds, adorned with divine garlands, filled with abundant wealth and grain, and beautified by women of surpassing excellence.

**4.32.14**  Surrounded by a white rocky hill and thus impregnable, the charming abode of the lord of monkeys resembled the dwelling of great Indra.

**4.32.15**  It was adorned with bright, towering palace-summits resembling the peaks of Kailāsa, and by well-nourished trees bearing all desired fruits.

**4.32.16**  Gifted by great Indra, the magnificent trees resembling dark rain-clouds bore divine flowers and fruits, gave cool shade, and were wondrously beautiful.

**4.32.17**  Its gateway was guarded by powerful monkeys armed with weapons, was bright and pure, wreathed in divine garlands, and its archway was of burnished gold.

**4.32.18**  The mighty son of Sumitrā entered the charming abode of Sugrīva, unhindered, like the sun entering a great cloud.

**4.32.19**  The righteous Lakṣmaṇa passed through seven inner courtyards filled with vehicles and seats, and then beheld the vast and well-guarded inner apartments.

**4.32.20**  It was adorned throughout with beds of gold and silver, with many excellent seats, and furnished with coverings of the highest quality.

**4.32.21**  As he continued to enter, he heard without pause a melodious sound — a song composed of well-formed syllables, mingled with lute music.

**4.32.22**  The mighty Lakṣmaṇa saw many women of varied forms in Sugrīva's palace, proud of their beauty and youth.

**4.32.23**  He observed them to be of noble birth, wearing garlands of many-hued flowers, occupied with making choice garlands, and adorned with the finest ornaments.

**4.32.24**  Lakṣmaṇa observed that Sugrīva's attendants too were neither unsatisfied nor overly agitated, and were attired in noble dress.

**4.32.25**  Then he saw Sugrīva seated on a supreme golden throne spread with the finest covering, blazing like the sun.

**4.32.26**  His body was adorned with divine ornaments, his form was divine, his fame resplendent; wearing divine garlands and garments, he was unconquerable as great Indra, and surrounded by women decked in divine ornaments and garlands.

**4.32.27**  The heroic Sugrīva, tawny as fine gold, seated on his splendid throne and holding Rumā in a close embrace, caught sight of the undaunted Lakṣmaṇa of wide eyes — he himself wide-eyed.

**4.33.1**  Seeing Lakṣmaṇa, that bull among men, enter unimpeded and in a rage, Sugrīva was seized with distress in all his senses.

**4.33.2**  Seeing the son of Daśaratha — breathing in rage, blazing as it were with energy, scorched by anguish over his brother's misfortune —

**4.33.3**  the foremost of monkeys leapt up, abandoning his golden throne, like the great well-adorned banner of Mahendra rising aloft.

**4.33.4**  As Sugrīva leapt up, Rumā and the other women rose after him, as a cluster of stars follows the full moon across the sky.

**4.33.5**  The glorious Sugrīva, his eyes flushed red, approached with palms joined and stood there like a great wish-fulfilling tree.

**4.33.6**  Then the angry Lakṣmaṇa addressed Sugrīva, who stood in the midst of the women with Rumā at his side, as if addressing the moon ringed by its stars.

**4.33.7**  "A king endowed with valour and noble birth, compassionate, with his senses mastered, grateful, and truthful in speech — such a king is honoured in the world."

**4.33.8**  "But what king is more cruel than one who abides in unrighteousness and makes a false promise to friends who have rendered him service?"

**4.33.9**  "A falsehood concerning a horse destroys a hundred; a falsehood concerning a cow destroys a thousand; but a man who utters a falsehood concerning a man destroys himself and all his kindred."

**4.33.10**  "He who, his purpose having been served, does not requite his friends — that ingrate deserves to be slain by all beings, O lord of the leaping ones."

**4.33.11**  "This verse was sung by Brahmā himself, revered by all the worlds — uttered in wrath upon beholding an ingrate. Hear it, O monkey."

**4.33.12**  "For the slayer of a brahmin, for the drunkard, for the thief, for the one who has broken his vow — the righteous have prescribed expiation; but for an ingrate there is no expiation."

**4.33.13**  "You are ignoble, ungrateful, and a speaker of falsehood, O monkey — for having had your purpose served by Rāma, you do not requite him for it."

**4.33.14**  "Surely you, O monkey, having had your purpose served, ought — if you truly wish to act rightly — to exert yourself in the search for Sītā on Rāma's behalf."

**4.33.15**  "Yet you are absorbed in vulgar pleasures and have given a false promise. Rāma did not know you to be like a serpent that merely croaks like a frog."

**4.33.16**  "O sinful one, you of wicked soul were given sovereignty over the monkeys by the greatly blessed and compassionate Rāma, that great-souled one."

**4.33.17**  "If you do not acknowledge the deed done by Rāma, whose actions are tireless, you will this very moment, struck by his sharp arrows, behold Vālin."

**4.33.18**  "The path by which Vālin went after being slain has not grown narrow. Abide by your covenant, Sugrīva; do not follow the path of Vālin."

**4.33.19**  "Surely you do not see the arrows — thunderbolt-like — released from the bow of that foremost scion of the Ikṣvākus; it is only therefore that, at ease, you indulge in pleasures and do not attend even in mind to Rāma's cause."

**4.34.1**  As Saumitri spoke thus, blazing as it were with energy, Tārā — whose face resembled the lord of stars — addressed Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.34.2**  "Do not speak thus, Lakṣmaṇa; the lord of the monkeys does not deserve to hear harsh words, especially from your mouth.

**4.34.3**  Sugrīva is not ungrateful, nor is he deceitful or cruel; the lord of monkeys is not a speaker of falsehood, O hero, nor is he dishonest.

**4.34.4**  This brave monkey Sugrīva has not forgotten the service rendered by Rāma in battle — what others would find impossible to do.

**4.34.5**  By Rāma's grace, Sugrīva has here obtained glory, the eternal kingship of the monkeys, Rumā, and myself, O scorcher of foes.

**4.34.6**  Having lain previously in great misery and then attaining this supreme pleasure, he does not perceive the passing of time — just as the sage Viśvāmitra did not.

**4.34.7**  Absorbed in Ghṛtācī for ten years, O Lakṣmaṇa, the righteous great sage Viśvāmitra reckoned it as but a single day.

**4.34.8**  If even Viśvāmitra of great splendor, the foremost among knowers of time, did not perceive time thus passed, what then of an ordinary man?

**4.34.9**  For this one who has succumbed to the ways of the body, who is weary, O Lakṣmaṇa, and who is not yet sated in sensual pleasures — Rāma should bear with him here.

**4.34.10**  You should not, dear one, fall under the sway of anger, O Lakṣmaṇa, or act rashly without ascertaining the truth, like a common man.

**4.34.11**  Men of true virtue such as yourself, O bull among men, do not suddenly fall under the sway of anger without deliberation.

**4.34.12**  O knower of righteousness, I earnestly beseech you on Sugrīva's behalf — this great agitation born of anger should be relinquished.

**4.34.13**  It is my opinion that Sugrīva would give up Rumā, myself, the kingship of the monkeys, and all wealth, grain, and treasures for Rāma's sake.

**4.34.14**  Sugrīva will reunite Rāghava with Sītā — like the moon reunited with Rohiṇī — after slaying Rāvaṇa in battle.

**4.34.15**  In Laṅkā there are, it is said, hundreds of thousands of crores of rākṣasas, and thirty-six ten-thousands plus thousands and hundreds more.

**4.34.16**  Without slaying those invincible rākṣasas who change form at will, it is not possible to slay Rāvaṇa, by whom the Maithilī was abducted.

**4.34.17**  They cannot be slain in battle without allies, O Lakṣmaṇa, and especially not Rāvaṇa of cruel deeds by Sugrīva alone.

**4.34.18**  Thus spoke Vālin, who was knowledgeable in these matters, the lord of monkeys. My information is not from direct knowledge — I speak from what I heard from him.

**4.34.19**  Indeed for the purpose of your assistance, foremost monkeys have been dispatched to bring back very many monkey troops — the leaders of monkey battalions for battle.

**4.34.20**  Awaiting those valiant and supremely powerful ones, the lord of monkeys does not depart — for the purpose of accomplishing Rāghava's goal.

**4.34.22**  Thousands of crores of bears and hundreds of golāṅgūlas will approach you today — abandon your anger, O subduer of foes! Many crores of brilliantly glowing monkeys will come to you, O scion of the Ikṣvāku line.

**4.35.1**  Thus addressed by Tārā with words that were humble and imbued with righteousness, Saumitri — gentle by nature — accepted those words.

**4.35.2**  When that speech was accepted, the lord of the monkey host cast off his great fear of Lakṣmaṇa as one sheds a wet garment.

**4.35.3**  Then Sugrīva, the lord of monkeys, removed the variegated, many-stringed, great garland from his neck and, free from intoxication, came to his senses.

**4.35.4**  Sugrīva, the best of all monkeys, addressed Lakṣmaṇa of fearsome might with humble words, gladdening him.

**4.35.5**  "Lost prosperity, glory, and the eternal kingship of the monkeys — all this has been regained by me through Rāma's grace, O Saumitri.

**4.35.6**  Who is capable of repaying such valor to that godlike hero, renowned by his own deeds, O warrior, O subduer of enemies?

**4.35.7**  The righteous Rāghava will recover Sītā and will slay Rāvaṇa by his own might — with me serving only as an ally.

**4.35.8**  What need has he of allies — he by whom seven great trees, a mountain, and the very earth were pierced with a single arrow?

**4.35.9**  He at whose mere twanging of the bow the earth with its mountains trembled, O Lakṣmaṇa — what need does such a one truly have of allies?

**4.35.10**  I shall accompany the lord of men, O bull among men, as he goes to slay the enemy Rāvaṇa along with his entourage.

**4.35.11**  If I have transgressed at all out of trust or affection — as a servant — that should be forgiven me; there is no one who does not err.

**4.35.12**  As the great-souled Sugrīva spoke thus, Lakṣmaṇa became pleased and spoke to him with affection.

**4.35.13**  "In every way my brother has a protector, O lord of monkeys — especially with you, O Sugrīva, as his modest and devoted guardian.

**4.35.14**  Such is your power, O Sugrīva, and such is your supreme purity — you deserve to enjoy the unsurpassed glory of the kingship of the monkeys.

**4.35.15**  With you as his ally, O Sugrīva, the mighty Rāma will slay his enemies in battle before long — there is no doubt in this.

**4.35.16**  The words you have spoken, O Sugrīva, are fitting and proper, coming from one who knows righteousness, is grateful, and never turns back in battle.

**4.35.17**  Who else, being capable and knowing what is fitting, deserves to speak thus — save my elder brother and yourself, O best of monkeys?

**4.35.18**  You are a match for Rāma in valor and strength — you have been given by the gods as his ally for a long time, O foremost of monkeys.

**4.35.19**  But now, O hero, depart quickly from here with me and console your friend who is afflicted with grief over the abduction of his wife.

**4.35.20**  And the harsh words with which I addressed you, having heard Rāma's words spoken in his grief-stricken state — that too you should forgive."

**4.36.13**  Hundreds, thousands, and crores of lion-like monkeys, abiding by my will, are to set out in the various directions at my command.

**4.36.14**  Resembling clouds and mountains, seemingly covering the sky, the foremost monkeys of fearsome appearance are to depart from here at my command.

**4.36.17**  Traversing the path measured by Viṣṇu's stride, following the route of birds and lightning, those monkeys sent forth by the king departed instantly.

**4.36.18**  Those monkeys spurred on all monkeys — in the oceans, on mountains, in forests, and along rivers — for the sake of Rāma.

**4.36.19**  Hearing the command of Sugrīva, king of kings, which was like the call of death, the monkeys came — having perceived the terror of Sugrīva's wrath.

**4.36.20**  Then three crores of leaping monkeys, resembling collyrium, of great speed, issued forth from that mountain to where Rāghava was.

**4.36.21**  From that excellent mountain where the sun sets, where they delight — those resembling refined gold in their brilliance — ten crores issued forth.

**4.36.22**  From the peaks of Kailāsa came thousands of crores of monkeys with the lustre of lion manes.

**4.36.23**  Those who live on fruits and roots and dwell on Himavat — from among them thousands of crores arrived, numbering a thousand.

**4.36.24**  From the Vindhya, resembling burning coals, fearsome and of fearsome deeds — thousands of crores of monkeys rushed forth swiftly.

**4.36.25**  Those dwelling on the shores of the milk ocean, inhabiting forests of tamāla trees, and feeding on coconuts — their number is beyond reckoning.

**4.36.26**  From forests, from caverns, and from rivers, the monkey army came at great speed — as if drinking up the sun.

**4.36.27**  Those valiant monkeys who had gone to hasten all the monkeys beheld the Himavat mountain with its great trees.

**4.36.28**  On that excellent, beautiful mountain a great sacrifice had once been performed by Maheśvara — divine, enchanting, and pleasing to the minds of all gods — and its glory still shone.

**4.36.29**  There the monkeys saw roots and fruits born from the flowing of the sacrificial food — tasting like nectar in flavor.

**4.36.30**  That divine, enchanting fruit and root born of the sacrificial food — whoever eats even once becomes satisfied for a full month.

**4.36.31**  Those fruit-eating leaders of monkey troops gathered those divine roots, fruits, and divine herbs.

**4.36.32**  Going there, the monkeys also brought back fragrant flowers from that sacrificial ground — for the sake of pleasing Sugrīva.

**4.36.33**  And all those foremost monkeys, having swiftly urged on all the monkeys throughout the earth, marched at the head of the troops.

**4.36.34**  In that very hour, those swift-acting troop leaders swiftly reached Kiṣkindhā, where Sugrīva the monkey dwelt.

**4.36.35**  Those monkeys, having taken all the herbs, fruits, and roots, presented them to him and spoke these words.

**4.36.36**  "All the mountains, oceans, and forests have been traversed; all the monkeys of the earth are coming at your command."

**4.36.37**  Hearing this, Sugrīva, lord of the leaping monkeys, was overjoyed and gladly accepted all the gifts they had brought.

**4.37.1**  Having received all that had been brought and presented as tribute, and having consoled all the monkeys, he dismissed them all.

**4.37.2**  Having dismissed those heroic monkeys who had accomplished their task, he considered both himself and the mighty Rāghava to have attained their purpose.

**4.37.3**  Then Lakṣmaṇa, delighting Sugrīva, spoke deferential words to that best of all monkeys possessed of terrible strength: "Come forth from Kiṣkindhā, O gentle one, if it pleases you."

**4.37.4**  Hearing those well-spoken words of Lakṣmaṇa, Sugrīva, supremely pleased, spoke these words in reply.

**4.37.5**  "So be it; let us go — I must abide by your command." Having spoken thus to the auspicious-marked Lakṣmaṇa, Sugrīva then...

**4.37.7**  Hearing his command, the monkeys — all of those who were fit to appear before women — came quickly with palms joined in reverence.

**4.37.8**  To those who had arrived, the king, whose radiance resembled the sun's, then said: "Monkeys, quickly bring my palanquin forward."

**4.37.9**  Hearing his words, the monkeys of swift step at once brought forward a beautiful palanquin pleasing to behold.

**4.37.10**  Seeing the palanquin brought forth, the lord of monkeys said to Saumitri: "Lakṣmaṇa, mount this quickly."

**4.37.11**  Having spoken thus, Sugrīva mounted together with Lakṣmaṇa the golden conveyance that resembled the sun, drawn by large monkeys.

**4.37.12**  A white royal parasol was held above his head, and white yak-tail fans were waved about him on all sides.

**4.37.13**  Acclaimed by bards and heralded by the sounds of conches and kettledrums, Sugrīva, having attained the unsurpassed glory of kingship, set forth.

**4.37.14**  Surrounded by many hundreds of fierce monkeys bearing weapons in hand, he proceeded to the place where Rāma was stationed.

**4.37.15**  Reaching that excellent place frequented by Rāma, the great-spirited one descended from the palanquin together with Lakṣmaṇa.

**4.37.16**  Approaching Rāma, he stood with palms joined in reverence; and as he stood thus with joined palms, the monkeys did likewise.

**4.37.17**  Seeing that vast army of monkeys — like a lake full of closed lotuses — Rāma was filled with affection for Sugrīva.

**4.37.18**  When the lord of monkeys fell at his feet, touching them with his head, Rāghava raised him up and embraced him with affection and deep regard.

**4.37.19**  Having embraced him, the righteous-souled one said: "Be seated." Then, seeing him seated upon the ground, Rāma spoke these words.

**4.37.20**  "O hero, O best of monkeys — the one who constantly pursues righteousness, wealth, and pleasure each in its proper time and measure, he is a true king.

**4.37.21**  But one who, abandoning righteousness and wealth alike, indulges only in pleasure, wakes to find himself fallen — like a man who fell asleep at the top of a tree.

**4.37.22**  A king who is engaged in the destruction of enemies, devoted to the protection of allies, and who enjoys the fruit of the threefold human aims — he is joined to righteousness.

**4.37.23**  The time for exertion has now arrived, O destroyer of enemies. Take counsel, O tawny-eyed lord, together with the monkeys and your ministers."

**4.37.24**  Thus addressed, Sugrīva spoke these words to Rāma.

**4.37.25**  "Prosperity, fame, and the eternal kingdom of the monkeys — all of it had been lost to me, O mighty-armed one, and has been regained through your grace.

**4.38.1**  While Sugrīva spoke thus, Rāma, best among those who uphold righteousness, embraced him with both arms and replied to him who stood with palms joined.

**4.39.59**  In all those mountains, caves, and forests, and in those regions not named by me, Jānakī must be searched for in all of them.

**4.40.46**  Furthermore, there is none dearer to me than he — above even my own life — and one who has wronged me many times shall become my kinsman.

**4.40.47**  You are of immeasurable strength and valor, born in lineages of great virtue; undertake this highest of human goals so that you may find the daughter of the king of men.

**4.43.13**  "Your resolution, O hero, and your valour combined with fortitude, together with the message of Sugrīva, seem to declare success to me."

**4.43.14**  Taking that ring, the foremost of monkeys placed it upon his head, bowed with joined palms, paid reverence to Rāma's feet, and set forth — that best of leapers.

**4.43.15**  Urging onward the great army of monkeys, the heroic son of the Wind became like the moon with its clear disc in a sky cleared of clouds, adorned by the host of stars.

**4.43.16**  "Relying on your exceedingly mighty strength and the many feats of valour befitting the foremost of monkeys, O son of the Wind, do whatever is necessary so that the daughter of Janaka may be reached, O Hanūmān."

**4.44.1**  Understanding the stern command of their lord, those foremost of monkeys set forth, covering the earth like locusts.

**4.44.2**  Rāma resided there on mount Prasravaṇa together with Lakṣmaṇa, awaiting that month which had been appointed for the finding of Sītā.

**4.44.3**  The heroic monkey Śatabali then swiftly set out toward the northern quarter, beautiful and encompassed by the king of mountains.

**4.44.4**  Vinatā, the leader of the monkey troop, marched toward the eastern quarter.

**4.44.5**  The son of the Wind, that leaping monkey who leads the monkey host, set out toward the southern quarter — the region traversed by Agastya — accompanied by Tārā, Aṅgada, and others.

**4.44.6**  Suṣeṇa, the lord of leaping monkeys, that tiger among monkeys, set forth toward the formidable western quarter, guarded exceedingly by Varuṇa.

**4.44.7**  Then the king, having dispatched in due order the chief leaders of the monkey army to all the quarters, rejoiced in full contentment.

**4.44.8**  Thus urged by the king, all the leaders of the monkey bands set out swiftly, each heading toward his own assigned quarter.

**4.44.9**  Shouting and roaring and thundering, the leaping monkeys — those best of leapers — went on, bounding and running, crying: "We shall bring back Sītā and slay Rāvaṇa!"

**4.44.10**  "I alone will slay Rāvaṇa when I encounter him in battle, and then I shall swiftly seize and bring away the daughter of Janaka."

**4.44.11**  "Let those of you trembling with fatigue stay where you are — I alone shall bring back Jānakī even from Pātāla itself."

**4.44.12**  "I shall tear up trees, I shall split open mountains, I shall cleave the earth asunder, I shall churn the oceans into turmoil."

**4.44.13**  "I am capable of leaping a hundred yojanas in count — nay, more than a hundred yojanas — of this there is no doubt."

**4.44.14**  "Whether on the surface of the earth, in the ocean, upon the mountains, in the forests, or even in the depths of Pātāla — my movement cannot be arrested."

**4.44.15**  Thus each of them, one by one, spoke their boastful words in the presence of the king of monkeys, intoxicated with the pride of their strength.

**4.45.1**  When the monkey lords had departed, Rāma said to Sugrīva: "How did you, without any battle, come to know the entire orb of the earth?"

**4.45.2**  Sugrīva then spoke to Rāma with a humble heart: "Hear, O bull among men — I shall recount everything in full detail."

**4.45.3**  "When Vāli was pursuing the dānava named Dundubhi, who had taken the form of a great buffalo, in the direction of Mount Malaya —"

**4.45.4**  "— then the buffalo entered a cave of Mount Malaya, and Vāli too entered there into Malaya, intent on killing him."

**4.45.5**  "I was left posted at that cave, like a water-vessel set at a gate, and Vāli did not emerge — a full year having passed."

**4.45.6**  "Then that cave became filled with a torrent of blood; seeing this I was overcome with amazement and tormented by grief for my brother."

**4.45.7**  "Then, having resolved that my elder brother had surely been slain, I placed at the mouth of the cave a boulder resembling a mountain, so that the buffalo, unable to emerge, would perish within."

**4.45.8**  "Then I came to Kiṣkindhā, despairing of his life. A great kingdom fell to me, and together with Tārā, with Rumā, and with allies, I dwelt there, freed from anxiety."

**4.45.9**  "Then Vāli returned, having slain that bull among dānavas, and I surrendered the kingdom to him out of deference, constrained by fear."

**4.45.10**  "That wicked-souled Vāli, his senses disturbed, wishing to slay me, pursued me in anger together with his ministers as I fled."

**4.45.11**  "Then I was chased by Vāli together with his retinue, beholding various rivers, forests, and cities as I fled."

**4.45.12**  "Then the earth appeared to me like the surface of a mirror, resembling a whirling firebrand, and in my speed it seemed no larger than a cow's hoofprint."

**4.45.13**  "Then I first went east, then took refuge in the south, and again turned to the west, trembling with fear; and as I was heading north, Hanūmān spoke to me."

**4.45.14**  "Now I recall, O king, how Vāli, that lord of monkeys, was formerly cursed by the sage Mataṅga within this hermitage precinct."

**4.45.15**  "If Vāli should enter here, his head would shatter into a hundred pieces; our dwelling there would be easy and free from fear."

**4.45.16**  "Then, reaching Mount Ṛśyamūka, O son of a king, Vāli did not enter it then — out of fear of the curse of Mataṅga."

**4.45.17**  "Thus, O king, the entire orb of the earth was directly observed by me at that time, and then I came to take refuge in this cave."

**4.46.1**  For the sake of finding Vaidehī, the leaders of the monkey troops, dispatched by the monkey-king as instructed, set forth swiftly in all directions.

**4.46.2**  They searched all around through lakes, rivers, thickets, the sky, and cities, through river-fastnesses and mountains on every side.

**4.46.3**  All the leaders of the monkey troops thoroughly searched the regions named by Sugrīva, along with their mountains, forests, and woodlands.

**4.46.4**  Having searched throughout the day, all the monkeys, resolute in finding Sītā, would return to the earth when night came.

**4.46.5**  In regions bearing fruit in every season, the monkeys would come to fruit-laden trees and make their nightly resting places, day after day.

**4.46.6**  Having completed the first appointed day, the leaders of the monkey troops returned to Prasravaṇa at the end of the month and met with the monkey-king, having found nothing.

**4.46.7**  Having searched the eastern direction as instructed, along with his ministers, mighty Vinatā returned without having seen Sītā.

**4.46.8**  And that great monkey, the valiant Śatabali, having searched the entire northern direction, returned together with his army.

**4.46.9**  Suṣeṇa, having searched the western quarter together with the monkeys, approached Sugrīva when the full month was spent.

**4.46.10**  Approaching Sugrīva, who was seated on the summit of Prasravaṇa together with Rāma, they saluted him and spoke these words.

**4.46.11**  "All the mountains have been searched, and all the forests and cities, the rivers reaching to the ocean, and all the inhabited lands.

**4.46.12**  All the caves named by you have been searched, and the great thickets overgrown and interlaced with creepers have been searched as well.

**4.46.13**  In dense, inaccessible, and rugged regions, creatures of immense size have been searched out and slain; and those dense places have been searched again and again.

**4.46.14**  That great-souled foremost of monkeys, born of noble spirit, will behold Maithilī; for Hanūmān, son of the Wind, has taken the very direction in which Sītā went."

**4.47.1**  Then that monkey Hanūmān, going together with Tārā and Aṅgada, approached the region indicated by Sugrīva.

**4.47.2**  Having gone far, together with all those best of monkeys, he searched the caves and dense forests of the Vindhya range.

**4.47.3**  The summits of mountains, river-fastnesses, lakes, vast trees, diverse groves of trees, and mountains thick with foliage—

**4.47.4**  —all those valiant monkeys, searching in every direction, did not find Sītā, the daughter of Janaka, Maithilī.

**4.47.5**  Eating roots and various fruits as they searched, those formidable ones dwelt here and there; for that region was vast, hard to search, full of caves and dense forest.

**4.47.6**  Leaving that region then, all the leaders of the monkey troops, fearless and undaunted, entered another forbidding land.

**4.47.7**  Where the trees bore no fruit, were flowerless and stripped of leaves; where the rivers were waterless, and roots were extremely scarce—

**4.47.8**  —where there were no buffaloes, no deer, no elephants, no tigers, no birds, nor any other forest-dwelling creatures—

**4.47.9**  —where on dry land there were lotus-pools with gleaming, full-blown lotuses, beautiful and fragrant, yet forsaken even by bees—

**4.47.10**  —there lived a great sage named Kaṇḍu, most glorious, truth-speaking, rich in austerities, utterly intolerant, and unapproachable through his self-discipline—

**4.47.11**  —whose son, a boy of ten years, was lost to death in that very forest; the great sage, enraged, cursed that place—

**4.47.12**  —by that righteous-souled one the entire great forest was cursed: desolate, forbidding, devoid of beasts and birds.

**4.47.13**  The borders of that forest, the ravines of the mountains, and the headwaters of rivers they searched with careful attention.

**4.47.14**  And there too those great-souled ones, devoted to Sugrīva's good, did not see the daughter of Janaka, nor Rāvaṇa, her abductor.

**4.47.15**  Entering that terrible region overgrown with creepers and thickets, they saw a demon of cruel deeds, fearless even of the gods.

**4.47.16**  Seeing that terrible one standing there like another mountain, all the monkeys drew closely together upon beholding that mountain-like figure.

**4.47.17**  That powerful one said to all the monkeys, "You are finished!" and, raising his clenched fist, charged at them in great fury.

**4.47.18**  As he rushed forward, Aṅgada, son of Vālin, recognizing him as Rāvaṇa, struck him on the spot with the flat of his palm.

**4.47.19**  Struck by the son of Vālin and vomiting blood from his mouth, that demon fell to the ground like a mountain toppled over.

**4.47.20**  While he lay breathless, the monkeys, exulting in victory, searched most of that mountain-gorge region all around.

**4.47.21**  Having searched that forest, all of them then entered yet another terrible mountain-cavern.

**4.47.22**  Searching again, exhausted, they leapt out and came together, sitting down at the foot of a solitary tree with downcast hearts.

**4.48.1**  Then Aṅgada, most wise, exhausted, gently composing himself, spoke these words to all the monkeys.

**4.48.2**  "Forests, mountains, rivers, fastnesses, dense thickets, cliffs, and mountain-caves — all have been searched by us on every side.

**4.48.3**  Everywhere, even among us, Jānakī cannot be seen — Sītā, equal to the daughter of the gods, carried off by some demon.

**4.48.4**  A great time has already passed for us, and Sugrīva is fierce in his commands; therefore let all of you together continue to search on every side.

**4.48.5**  Abandoning sloth, grief, and the sleep that has come upon you, search so that we may find Sītā, the daughter of Janaka.

**4.48.6**  Perseverance, skill, and the unconquered spirit of the mind — these are said to bring success in one's tasks; therefore I say this to you.

**4.48.7**  Even today let the forest-dwellers search this inaccessible forest; abandoning weariness, let this entire forest be searched once more.

**4.48.8**  The fruit of an action diligently performed is surely seen; enough of despondency — for dejection is not fitting for us.

**4.48.9**  Sugrīva, O monkeys, is a wrathful king, swift to punish; one must always be wary of him, and of the great-souled Rāma as well.

**4.48.10**  This has been said for your own good; act on it if it pleases you, or else say, O monkeys, what is proper for all of us together."

**4.48.11**  Having heard Aṅgada's words, Gandhamādana spoke in a faint voice, weakened by thirst and fatigue.

**4.48.12**  "Indeed, what Aṅgada has said is worthy of you — it is both beneficial and agreeable; let what he has said be done."

**4.48.13**  "Let us search once more: mountains, ravines, and cliffs as well, the empty woodlands and the mountain-springs."

**4.48.14**  "Let all search everywhere through the forest and the mountain-fastnesses, exactly as all of them were indicated by great-souled Sugrīva."

**4.48.15**  Then those mighty monkeys rose up again and roamed the southern direction, which was interspersed with Vindhya forests.

**4.48.16**  Those monkeys climbed the glorious Silver Mountain, resembling an autumn cloud, full of peaks and caves.

**4.48.17**  There the best of monkeys, eager to behold Sītā, searched through the charming lodhra-forests and the saptaparṇa-groves.

**4.48.18**  Climbing to its summit, those great-striding ones, though exhausted, did not see Vaidehī, the beloved queen of Rāma.

**4.48.19**  Having surveyed that mountain of many caverns to the full extent of their sight, the monkeys descended, looking all around.

**4.48.20**  Having descended to the ground, exhausted and dispirited, they rested for a moment and then took shelter at the foot of a tree.

**4.48.21**  Having recovered for a moment, somewhat relieved of exhaustion, they rose once more, determined to search the entire southern direction.

**4.48.22**  Those best of leaping monkeys, with Hanūmān at their head, set forth and ranged all around the Vindhya from its very beginning.

**4.49.1**  Together with Tārā and Aṅgada, Hanūmān the monkey searched through the caves and thickets of the Vindhya.

**4.49.2**  They searched all around through caves frequented by lions and tigers, and through the great cascading waterfalls in the rough terrain of that lord of mountains.

**4.49.3**  As they remained searching there, the allotted time passed away.

**4.49.4**  That region was hard to explore, vast and filled with caves and dense thickets; yet there Vāyu's son searched every part of the mountain.

**4.49.5**  Separated from one another yet not far from each other, Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, and Gandhamādana searched.

**4.49.6**  Mainda and Dvivida, and likewise Hanūmān and Jāmbavān, and the crown prince Aṅgada, and Tāra who roams the forest.

**4.49.7**  Having searched the southern quarter through regions covered with mountain ranges, overcome by hunger and thirst, exhausted and in need of water, they caught sight of a great cave overgrown with creepers and trees.

**4.49.8**  Then cranes and geese and sārasas were flying out of it, and wet cakravākas with their limbs reddened by lotus pollen.

**4.49.9**  Then, having approached that cave which was fragrant and impassable, those bull-like monkeys became minds filled with wonder and eagerness.

**4.49.10**  Seized by curiosity, those foremost among the leaping monkeys, glowing with energy and possessed of great strength, approached that cave with great excitement.

**4.49.11**  Then Hanūmān, son of the Wind-god, resembling a mountain peak and skilled in wilderness and forests, addressed all the monkeys.

**4.49.12**  "Having searched the southern quarter through regions covered with mountain ranges, we are all exhausted, and I do not see the Maithilī."

**4.49.13**  "And from this cave, geese and cranes together with sārasas, and water-wet cakravākas, fly out on all sides."

**4.49.14**  "Surely there is water here, whether a well or a pool; and likewise these trees standing at the cave entrance are fresh and lush."

**4.49.15**  Spoken to thus, all the monkeys entered that cave enveloped in darkness, without moon or sun, a sight that made the hair stand on end.

**4.49.16**  Then in that cave, difficult to traverse and filled with many kinds of trees, clasping one another mutually, they advanced a distance of a yojana.

**4.49.17**  Losing their senses, thirsty, bewildered, and desperately seeking water, they wandered about within that cave for some time without rest.

**4.49.18**  Those leaping ones, now gaunt, with wretched faces, thoroughly exhausted—those heroes then saw a light ahead, at that point despairing of their lives.

**4.49.19**  Then, arriving at that place, they saw a pleasant forest free from darkness—golden trees blazing with the radiance of the kindled fire Vaiśvānara.

**4.49.20**  Śāla trees, palms, puṃnāgas, kakubhas, vañjulas, dhavas, campankas, nāga trees, and karṇikāras in full bloom.

**4.49.21**  Resembling the young sun in color, with altars made of vaidūrya gems, and lotus ponds the hue of blue vaidūrya, thronged with birds.

**4.49.22**  Encircled by great golden trees resembling the young sun, and with great fish made of pure gold and tortoises in the waters.

**4.49.23**  There they saw lotus ponds filled with clear water, and likewise aerial palaces of gold and of silver.

**4.49.24**  With windows of refined gold and covered with lattices of pearls, the floors laid with gold and silver and inset with vaidūrya gems.

**4.49.25**  The monkeys saw there on all sides the finest of dwellings, and trees with flowers and fruit, resembling coral and precious gems.

**4.49.26**  And golden bees on every side, and honeycombs, and beds and seats marvellous with gems and gold.

**4.49.27**  They saw on all sides vehicles of great worth, and accumulations of vessels made of gold, silver, and bronze.

**4.49.28**  And stores of divine aloe wood and sandalwood, and pure edible roots and fruits.

**4.49.29**  And precious drinks and rich honeys, and stores of divine garments of great worth, and heaps of variegated blankets and of skins.

**4.49.30**  Searching here and there in that cave, those radiant and heroic monkeys saw, not far away, a certain woman.

**4.49.31**  Seeing her, they were greatly startled—a female ascetic clad in bark garment and black antelope skin, of regulated diet, blazing as it were with her own splendor.

**4.49.32**  Then Hanūmān, resembling a mountain, with hands joined in reverence, greeted that venerable woman and asked: "Who are you? Tell us whose is this dwelling, this cave, and these treasures."

**4.50.1**  Having spoken thus, Hanūmān there again addressed that illustrious female ascetic clad in black antelope skin, that one devoted to dharma.

**4.50.2**  "We have entered suddenly into this cave enveloped in darkness, overcome by hunger and thirst, thoroughly exhausted and distressed in every way."

**4.50.3**  "Thirsty, we entered this great golden cavity; and having seen these various conditions of such astonishing kind, we are disturbed, bewildered, and have lost our wits."

**4.50.4**  "Whose are these golden trees resembling the young sun, and the pure edible roots and fruits?"

**4.50.5**  "Whose are these aerial palaces of gold and the houses of silver, with windows of refined gold and covered with lattices of gems?"

**4.50.6**  "These trees in flower and fruit, holy and fragrant—these trees made of jāmbūnada gold—by whose power do they exist?"

**4.50.7**  "And golden lotuses grown in the clear water—how is it that golden fish move about together with tortoises?"

**4.50.8**  "The self, the greatness, and the ascetic power—whose is all this? Since all of us do not know, you ought to explain everything to us."

**4.50.9**  Addressed thus by Hanūmān, the female ascetic devoted to dharma, delighting in the welfare of all beings, replied to Hanūmān.

**4.50.10**  "Maya by name—a great-spirited dānava of powerful illusion, the foremost among dānavas—by him all this golden forest was fashioned through māyā."

**4.50.11**  "Formerly, he was the Viśvakarman among the foremost of dānavas; by him this divine golden mansion of supreme excellence was fashioned."

**4.50.12**  "He performed austerities for thousands of years in the great forest and obtained from the Pitāmaha a boon—all the wealth of Uśanas."

**4.50.13**  "Having created everything, that powerful one, master of all desires, dwelt happily for some time in this great forest."

**4.50.14**  "That bull among dānavas, having become attached to the apsarā Hemā, was slain by the lord Purandara, who took up his thunderbolt and struck him by force."

**4.50.15**  "And this supreme forest was given by Brahmā to Hemā, along with the eternal enjoyment of desires and this golden dwelling."

**4.50.16**  "I am Svayaṃprabhā, the daughter of Merusāvarṇi, her attendant; I guard this dwelling of Hemā, O best of monkeys."

**4.50.17**  "Hemā is my dear friend, skilled in dance and song; given the boon by her, I guard this supreme dwelling."

**4.50.18**  "What is your purpose, and for what reason do you traverse the wildernesses? And how was this difficult-to-find forest discovered by you?"

**4.50.19**  "After eating these edible things, the roots and fruits, and drinking the water, you ought to tell me everything."

**4.51.1**  Then with single-pointed concentration, the female ascetic devoted to dharma spoke these words to all those troop-leaders among the monkeys who had rested.

**4.51.2**  "O monkeys, if your fatigue has passed from eating the fruits, and if this is something I should hear, I wish to hear it—let it be told."

**4.51.3**  Hearing those words of hers, Hanūmān, son of the Wind-god, began to relate truthfully and straightforwardly exactly what had occurred.

**4.51.4**  "The glorious Rāma Dāśarathi, king of all the world, comparable to Mahendra and Varuṇa, entered the Daṇḍaka forest."

**4.51.5**  "Together with his brother Lakṣmaṇa and his wife the Vaidehī; his wife was carried off by force from Janasthāna by Rāvaṇa."

**4.51.6**  "That hero's friend, that king's companion, is a monkey named Sugrīva, king of the foremost monkeys; it is by him that we were dispatched."

**4.51.7**  "The southern quarter—traversed by Agastya, guarded by Yama—we, together with these foremost monkeys led by Aṅgada."

**4.51.8**  "Were charged altogether: 'Search for Rāvaṇa, that rākṣasa who can assume any form at will, together with Sītā the Vaidehī.'"

**4.51.9**  "Having searched the whole of the southern quarter, all of us, hungry and exhausted, took shelter at the foot of a tree."

**4.51.10**  "All with pallid faces, all absorbed in thought, we could find no end, sunken as we were in the great ocean of worry."

**4.51.11**  "Then casting our eyes about, we saw the great cave, covered over with creepers and trees, enveloped in darkness."

**4.51.12**  "Geese wet with water, their wings beaded with water droplets, ospreys and sārasas flying out from it—I said to the leaping ones, 'Come, let us enter here.'"

**4.51.13**  "And for all of them too the reasoning prevailed: 'Let us go in and enter'—urged on as they were by eagerness for their master's business."

**4.51.14**  "Then, having fallen into close formation, grasping one another's hands, we suddenly entered this cave enveloped in darkness."

**4.51.15**  "This is our situation; it is with this purpose that we came here; and it is to you that all of us have come, exhausted and famished."

**4.51.16**  "The roots and fruits given by the law of hospitality—these have been consumed by us, who were tormented by hunger."

**4.51.17**  "Since all of us were saved by you when dying of hunger, tell us—for the sake of return service—what the monkeys may do for you."

**4.51.18**  Thus addressed by those monkeys, the all-knowing Svayaṃprabhā then replied to all those leaders of monkey troops.

**4.51.19**  "I am fully satisfied with all these swift monkeys; while I practise my dharma, there is no service here to be done for me by anyone."

**4.52.1**  Thus addressed by the auspicious, dharma-laden words of the female ascetic, Hanūmān spoke his words to that woman of blameless conduct.

**4.52.2**  "We have all taken refuge in you, O lady of righteous conduct. The time-limit set for us by the great-souled Sugrīva has passed, and we have been dwelling inside the cave."

**4.52.3**  "You yourself should rescue us from this dreadful cave."

**4.52.4**  "Therefore please save all of us, who have overstepped Sugrīva's command, whose lives are as good as forfeit, and who are filled with dread of Sugrīva."

**4.52.5**  "There is also a great task that must be accomplished by us, O righteous lady, and that task too has not been done by us who have been dwelling here."

**4.52.6**  Thus addressed by Hanūmān, the female ascetic spoke: "I think it is exceedingly difficult for any living being who has entered here to turn back."

**4.52.7**  "But by the power of my austerities and by virtue of the self-discipline I have acquired, I shall draw all these monkeys out of this cave."

**4.52.8**  "Close your eyes, all you bull-like monkeys. For it is not possible to emerge with eyes open."

**4.52.9**  Then all of them, eager and desirous of departing, instantly covered their eyes with their soft-fingered hands and shut them tight.

**4.52.10**  Those high-souled monkeys, their faces covered by their hands, were lifted out of the cave by her in the mere twinkling of an eye.

**4.52.11**  Then the virtuous female ascetic, having brought all those monkeys forth from that treacherous place, reassured them and spoke thus.

**4.52.12**  "This is the glorious Vindhya mountain, covered with many trees and creepers. This is the Prasavaṇa peak. And this is the ocean, the great sea."

**4.52.13**  "May good fortune attend you; I will return to my abode, O bull-like monkeys." Having said this, Svayaṃprabhā entered that beautiful cave once more.

**4.52.14**  Then they beheld the dreadful ocean, abode of Varuṇa, boundless and roaring loudly, churned by terrible waves.

**4.52.15**  While they had been searching through that mountain stronghold fashioned by Maya's magic, the month set as the time-limit by the king had passed.

**4.52.16**  Sitting at the foot of the Vindhya mountain, beneath fully flowering trees, those great and fortunate ones then fell into anxious thought.

**4.52.17**  Then, seeing the springtime trees laden with blossoms at their crowns and encircled by hundreds of creepers, they became filled with fearful apprehension.

**4.52.18**  Informing one another that spring had arrived, and recognizing that the time and purpose of their mission were lost, they sank down upon the ground.

**4.52.19**  Then Aṅgada, the crown prince, that great wise monkey with the shoulders of a lion and bull, with broad long arms, spoke.

**4.52.20**  "We all set out under the command of the monkey-king; a full month has passed while we were dwelling in the cave — do the monkeys not perceive this?"

**4.52.21**  "Now that the time-limit set by Sugrīva himself has expired, it is fitting for all of us forest-dwellers to undertake a fast unto death."

**4.52.22**  "Sugrīva is by nature harsh, and he is firm in his role as master; he will not pardon all of us who have transgressed and return before him."

**4.52.23**  "And without news of Sītā, he will surely do evil. Therefore it is proper for us to undertake a fast unto death right here today."

**4.52.24**  "Before the king slaughters all of us who have returned from here — having abandoned sons, wives, wealth, and homes — with a punishment beyond fitting, death here itself is better for us."

**4.52.25**  "Moreover, I was not consecrated as crown prince by Sugrīva; it was by Rāma, the lord of men, whose deeds are effortless, that I was consecrated."

**4.52.26**  "That king who bore enmity toward me before, seeing my transgression, will put me to death with a sharp punishment, his resolve fixed."

**4.52.27**  "What use are my friends witnessing my calamity at the moment of death? I shall sit here in fast unto death on this sacred shore of the ocean."

**4.52.28**  Having heard these words spoken by the young crown prince, all those foremost monkeys spoke pitifully in reply.

**4.52.29**  "Sugrīva is by nature harsh, and Rāghava is ardently attached to his beloved; with Vaidehī not found, and seeing us return together —"

**4.52.30**  "— for Rāghava's sake and to please him, he will undoubtedly have us killed. And it is not proper for those who have transgressed to go before their master."

**4.52.31**  Then Tāra, hearing the words of the terror-stricken monkeys, spoke thus: "Enough of despair. Let us all enter the cave and dwell there, if you agree."

**4.52.32**  "For this cave wrought by Maya's magic is most inaccessible; it has abundant trees, water, food, and drink. Here we need fear neither Indra, nor Rāghava, nor the monkey-king."

**4.52.33**  Hearing even Aṅgada's favorable response, all the monkeys, encouraged, said: "Let a plan by which we shall not be slain be devised for us without delay, this very day."

**4.53.1**  While Tāra, who shone with the splendor of the lord of stars, spoke thus, Hanūmān perceived that the kingdom was being seized by Aṅgada.

**4.53.2**  Hanūmān judged Vālin's son to be endowed with the eight qualities of intellect, possessed of the fourfold strength, and graced with fourteen royal virtues.

**4.53.3**  He saw him ever increasing in splendor, strength, and valor, growing in royal majesty like the moon waxing from the first night of the bright fortnight.

**4.53.4**  He was Bṛhaspati's equal in intellect, his father's equal in valor, and was attending upon Tāra as Śukra attends upon Indra.

**4.53.5**  Then Hanūmān, perceiving him exhausted in his master's cause and skilled in all the sciences, began to address Aṅgada with persuasive words.

**4.53.6**  Describing the third of the four political means — dissension — he split apart all those monkeys with the excellence of his speech.

**4.53.7**  When all those monkeys had been thus divided, he then alarmed Aṅgada with many frightening words, employing the means of intimidation with anger.

**4.53.8**  "You, O son of Tārā, are more capable than your father in battle; you are able to bear the burden firmly and rule the monkey kingdom just as your father did."

**4.53.9**  "For monkeys are by nature fickle-minded, O bull among monkeys; without you they will not endure being commanded, apart from their sons and wives."

**4.53.10**  "These very ones — Jāmbavān, Nīla, and the great monkey Suhotra — would follow you; I tell you this plainly."

**4.53.11**  "Neither I nor all these others can be drawn away from Sugrīva by conciliation, gifts, or any other means — nor by force — except by you."

**4.53.12**  "They say that even taking a position in defiance of a stronger power is self-preserving for the weak; therefore the weak should not enter into conflict."

**4.53.13**  "And this cave that you regard as a protective refuge — it is said that Lakṣmaṇa's arrows would make short work of tearing it open."

**4.53.14**  "Indra once accomplished little by hurling his thunderbolt; Lakṣmaṇa would split it apart with his sharp arrows as though slicing a leaf-cup, and Lakṣmaṇa has many iron-shafted arrows of that kind."

**4.53.15**  "The very moment you, O afflictoer of enemies, sit down in the fast unto death, all the monkeys, their resolve firm, will abandon you."

**4.53.16**  "Remembering their sons and wives, ever anxious, hungry, worn out by uncomfortable beds, they will put you behind them."

**4.53.17**  "You, bereft of friends and of kinsmen who wish you well, will be fearful even of a trembling blade of grass."

**4.53.18**  "And Lakṣmaṇa's dreadful arrows, swift and irresistible, intent on killing the fugitive, will certainly pierce you."

**4.53.19**  "But if you come with us, presenting yourself humbly, Sugrīva will in due course establish you in the kingdom."

**4.53.20**  "Your uncle is devoted to dharma, steadfast in his vows, pure, and true to his promises; he will never seek to harm you."

**4.53.21**  "He also desires your mother's well-being, and his very life exists for her sake; he has no other offspring. Therefore, Aṅgada, let us go."

**4.54.1**  Hearing Hanūmān's words — modest, filled with dharma, and accompanied by due honor to his master — Aṅgada replied.

**4.54.2**  "Steadfastness, complete purity of soul, compassion, uprightness, valor, and courage — none of these are to be found in Sugrīva."

**4.54.3**  "He who, while his elder brother yet lives, takes for himself that brother's cherished queen-wife — she who should rightfully be regarded as a mother — is contemptible."

**4.54.4**  "How can he know dharma — that evil-souled one who, while his brother was engaged in battle, blocked the mouth of the cave?"

**4.54.5**  "He who has forgotten Rāghava — who took his hand in truth, who has done him service, who is of great renown — whose good deed would such a one remember?"

**4.54.6**  "He who, not through fear of adharma but through fear of Lakṣmaṇa alone, commanded us to search for Sītā — how can there be dharma in such a one?"

**4.54.7**  "Who among the noble, who is well-born and desires to live, would ever trust that sinner, that ingrate, that forgetful and unstable soul?"

**4.54.8**  "Having established his own son in the kingdom — whether that son be virtuous or not — how will Sugrīva allow me to live, born of an enemy's line?"

**4.54.9**  "With my counsel betrayed, myself in transgression, and my power diminished, how could I reach Kiṣkindhā and live there, helpless and weak like an orphan?"

**4.54.10**  "That deceitful, cruel, and pitiless Sugrīva would have me punished in secret and put in bonds, for the sake of securing his kingdom."

**4.54.11**  "A fast unto death is better for me than imprisonment and degradation. Grant me leave, all of you; let the monkeys go to their homes."

**4.54.12**  "I declare to you: I will not go to the city. I shall sit here in fast unto death; death itself is better for me."

**4.54.13**  "And my younger uncle Sugrīva, lord of the monkeys, should first be given my respectful salutation and then asked about his welfare."

**4.54.14**  "Ask about the good health and welfare of Rumā as well, my mother. And you should please console my mother Tārā."

**4.54.15**  "She is by nature devoted to her son, compassionate, a woman of many austerities; upon hearing that I have perished here, she will surely abandon her life."

**4.54.16**  Having said this much and having also bowed to the elders, Aṅgada lay down upon the ground on the darbha grass, weeping and downcast in spirit.

**4.54.17**  As he lay there, the bull-like monkeys, weeping, stricken with grief, shed hot tears from their eyes.

**4.54.18**  Blaming Sugrīva and extolling Vālin, they all surrounded Aṅgada and resolved to undertake the fast unto death.

**4.54.19**  Knowing the intention of Vālin's son, those bull-like monkeys — all having touched water, facing east, seated on southward-tipped darbha grass, on the bank of the ocean — settled down together.

**4.54.20**  That mountain, as many monkeys massive as great mountain peaks lay down upon it —

**4.54.21**  — resounded through its ravines and cascades, as though with the violent tumult of roaring rain-clouds.

**4.55.1**  While all those monkeys sat fasting at the mountain place prepared for death, the king of vultures approached that very spot.

**4.55.2**  That long-lived bird was named Saṃpāti, splendid and renowned for his strength and valor — the elder brother of Jaṭāyu.

**4.55.3**  Emerging from a cave of the great Vindhya mountain and seeing the monkeys seated there, he spoke with a delighted heart.

**4.55.4**  "Fate in this world surely follows its own ordinance — how fitting that this destined prey has come to me at last after so long."

**4.55.5**  "I shall devour these monkeys one by one as they die, each in turn" — so the bird spoke, gazing upon those leaping creatures.

**4.55.6**  Hearing the words of that bird hungry for prey, Aṅgada, greatly distressed, then spoke to Hanūmān.

**4.55.7**  "Look — under the pretext of Sītā, Yama, the son of Vivasvān himself, has come to this place for the destruction of us monkeys."

**4.55.8**  "Rāma's task has not been accomplished, the king's command has not been fulfilled — this unforeseen calamity has suddenly come upon the monkeys."

**4.55.9**  "The deed done by Jaṭāyu, the king of vultures, out of love for Vaidehī — all of that you have already heard in full."

**4.55.10**  "Even so, all beings — even those born into animal wombs — do Rāma's bidding, abandoning their lives just as we ourselves do."

**4.55.11**  "Exhausted in the service of Rāghava, we have given up our lives; we entered the wilderness — yet we have not found the princess of Mithilā."

**4.55.12**  "That king of vultures is fortunate — slain in battle by Rāvaṇa, he was freed from all fear of Sugrīva and has attained the highest state."

**4.55.13**  "By the destruction of Jaṭāyu, by the death of King Daśaratha, and by the abduction of Vaidehī, the monkeys have fallen into peril."

**4.55.14**  "The forest-dwelling of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa together with Sītā, and likewise the slaying of Vālin by Rāghava's arrow —"

**4.55.15**  "And the total destruction of the rākṣasas through Rāma's wrath — all this calamity has been wrought by the boon granted to Kaikeyī."

**4.55.16**  Upon hearing those words uttered from Aṅgada's mouth, the vulture with his sharp beak spoke in a loud voice.

**4.55.17**  "Who is this who proclaims with his voice the slaying of my brother Jaṭāyu, dearer to me than life itself, making my heart tremble?"

**4.55.18**  "How did the battle between the rākṣasa and the vulture take place at Janasthāna? After so long, I hear today this name of my brother."

**4.55.19**  "I wish to hear the end of my younger brother — he who knew virtue and was praiseworthy for his valor. Tell me, O best of monkeys."

**4.55.20**  "My brother Jaṭāyu dwelt at Janasthāna, and Daśaratha was that very brother's dear friend — he whose beloved eldest son Rāma is devoted to his elders. How did this come to pass?"

**4.55.21**  "Since my wings were burned by the rays of the sun, I cannot spread them; I wish to descend from this mountain, O destroyers of enemies."

**4.56.1**  Though the leaders of the monkey troops heard those words — his voice broken by grief — they did not trust them at all, suspicious of his intentions.

**4.56.2**  Those monkeys, seated in their fast unto death, saw the vulture and formed this terrible thought: "He will devour all of us."

**4.56.3**  "If he eats us while we sit here fasting in any case, we shall have achieved our purpose — swiftly obtaining release from this place."

**4.56.4**  After all those foremost monkeys formed this resolve, Aṅgada then addressed the vulture who had descended from the mountain peak.

**4.56.5**  "There was a powerful lord of monkeys named Kṣaraja — he was my noble king, O bird, and righteous were both of his sons."

**4.56.6**  "Sugrīva and Vālin — both sons endowed with torrential strength. King Vālin, renowned throughout the world for his deeds, was my father."

**4.56.7**  "Rāma the son of Daśaratha, the great warrior and splendid king of the entire world among the Ikṣvākus, entered the Daṇḍaka forest."

**4.56.8**  "Together with his brother Lakṣmaṇa and his wife Vaidehī, devoted to his father's command and treading the righteous path — his wife was forcibly abducted from Janasthāna by Rāvaṇa."

**4.56.9**  "Jaṭāyu the vulture-lord, a friend of Rāma's father, saw Sītā Vaidehī being carried away through the sky."

**4.56.10**  "He made Rāvaṇa chariotless and caused Maithilī to be set down — but exhausted and old, he was slain in battle by Rāvaṇa."

**4.56.11**  "Thus that vulture, slain by the mighty Rāvaṇa, was duly cremated by Rāma and attained the highest destination."

**4.56.12**  "Then Rāghava formed a friendship with Sugrīva, my great-souled paternal uncle — and he slew my father Vālin."

**4.56.13**  "For Sugrīva, estranged from my father, together with his ministers — after slaying Vālin, Rāma then consecrated Sugrīva as king."

**4.56.14**  "That Sugrīva, lord of monkeys, was installed in the kingdom by Rāma as king of the foremost monkeys — and it is he who dispatched us."

**4.56.15**  "Thus sent forth by Rāma, searching here and there, we have not found Vaidehī — like seeking the sun's radiance in the night."

**4.56.16**  "We searched the Daṇḍaka forest carefully — and then, unknowingly, we entered a cave that had opened in the earth."

**4.56.17**  "That cave was fashioned by Maya's magic, and while we searched within it, the month that the king had fixed as our deadline passed."

**4.56.18**  "We, who are all subject to the monkey-king's command, have exceeded the appointed time — and so out of fear we undertake this fast unto death."

**4.56.19**  "With Kākutstha Rāma angry, and Sugrīva together with Lakṣmaṇa wrathful, there is no life for us even if we were to return — so we have resolved to die here."

**4.57.1**  Thus addressed with piteous words by the monkeys who had abandoned hope of life, the vulture, with tears in his eyes, replied to the monkeys in a great voice.

**4.57.2**  "That younger brother of mine named Jaṭāyu, whom you have told me was slain in battle by the powerful Rāvaṇa —"

**4.57.3**  "On account of old age and my winglessness, even hearing this I must endure it — for I have no strength today to avenge my brother's enemy."

**4.57.4**  "Long ago, when the slaying of Vṛtra had occurred, both he and I, eager for victory, flew up toward the blazing sun wreathed in rays."

**4.57.5**  "Covering the path through the sky with great speed we flew — but when the sun reached its zenith, Jaṭāyu began to sink."

**4.57.6**  "Seeing that brother of mine tormented by the sun's rays and utterly overwhelmed, out of affection I shielded him with my wings."

**4.57.7**  "My wings scorched and burned, I fell upon the Vindhya, O best of monkeys — and dwelling here, I have had no news of my brother."

**4.57.8**  When Saṃpāti had spoken thus of Jaṭāyu, the crown prince Aṅgada of great wisdom then replied.

**4.57.9**  "If you are truly the brother of Jaṭāyu, you have heard what I have told you — now tell us, if you know, the abode of that rākṣasa."

**4.57.10**  "That Rāvaṇa, lord of rākṣasas, who is lacking in foresight — whether nearby or far, if you know, inform us."

**4.57.11**  Then the elder brother of Jaṭāyu, blazing with great energy, spoke words befitting his own nature, filling the monkeys with joy.

**4.57.12**  "Though I am a vulture with scorched wings and diminished strength, O leaping ones, I will render supreme aid to Rāma — at least by means of speech."

**4.57.13**  "I know the worlds of Varuṇa and the three strides of Viṣṇu as well; I know the battles of gods and asuras, and the churning of the nectar."

**4.57.14**  "As for this task of Rāma's, it should have been done first by me — but old age has stolen my vigor and my life-breath is weakened."

**4.57.15**  "I saw a young woman of perfect beauty, adorned with all ornaments, being carried off by Rāvaṇa, that wicked-souled one."

**4.57.16**  "She was crying out 'Rāma! Rāma!' and 'Lakṣmaṇa!' — that radiant woman, flinging off her ornaments and writhing her limbs."

**4.57.17**  "Her finest silk garment, like the sun's glow atop a mountain, shone against that dark rākṣasa as lightning blazes against a rain cloud."

**4.57.18**  "By the mention of Rāma's name, I believe that woman is Sītā. Now hear from me as I describe the dwelling of that rākṣasa."

**4.57.19**  "The rākṣasa named Rāvaṇa — the own son of Viśravas and brother of Vaiśravaṇa — dwells in the city of Laṅkā."

**4.57.20**  "From here, a full hundred yojanas across the ocean on an island, the lovely city of Laṅkā was built by Viśvakarman."

**4.57.21**  "In that city dwells Vaidehī, wretched, wearing silk, confined in Rāvaṇa's inner quarters and closely guarded by rākṣasī women."

**4.57.22**  "You will behold that Maithilī, the daughter of King Janaka, there in Laṅkā — protected on all sides by the ocean."

**4.57.23**  "Reaching the ocean's far shore, a full hundred yojanas away, and arriving at the southern bank, you will there behold Rāvaṇa."

**4.57.24**  "Make haste there at once and strive with valor, O leaping ones! I truly see this with my inner sight — having found her, you will return."

**4.57.25**  "The first path belongs to the sparrows and those other birds that live on grain; the second to the crows and those that eat tree-fruits."

**4.57.26**  "The bhāsa birds travel the third path, and the krauñcas together with the kurara birds; hawks travel the fourth, and vultures travel the fifth."

**4.57.27**  "The sixth path belongs to the swans endowed with strength, valor, beauty, and youth — and beyond that is the way of Vainateya Garuḍa. From Vainateya descends the lineage of all of us, O best of monkeys."

**4.57.28**  "A reprehensible deed was done by which we became flesh-eaters — yet even standing here, I behold Rāvaṇa and Jānakī with my sight."

**4.57.29**  "We too possess a divine golden power of vision — and so by the power of our sustenance and by our very nature, O monkeys, we always see as far as a full hundred yojanas and more."

**4.57.30**  "Our livelihood is ordained by nature to be found at a distance; the livelihood of those who fight on foot is ordained to be found at their very feet."

**4.57.31**  "Let some means be found for crossing the salt ocean — having reached Vaidehī, you will return with your purpose fulfilled."

**4.57.32**  "I wish to go with you to the ocean, abode of Varuṇa — I will offer water for my great-souled brother who has gone to heaven."

**4.57.33**  Then those mighty monkeys of great valor, having carried Saṃpāti — his wings burned away — to the shore of the lord of rivers and streams,

**4.57.34**  brought back that lord of birds to that very place; and the monkeys became filled with joy, having obtained this news to act upon.

**4.58.1**  Then, having heard those words of the king of vultures — words sweet as nectar — those foremost of monkeys were filled with joy as he spoke.

**4.58.2**  Jāmbavān, the foremost of monkeys, together with all the leaping ones, suddenly rose from the ground and addressed the king of vultures.

**4.58.3**  "Where is Sītā? By whom was she seen? Or who has carried off the daughter of Mithilā? Tell us all of this, sir — be a guide to us forest-dwellers."

**4.58.4**  "Who would not regard the valor of the arrows of Daśaratha's son — falling with the speed of the thunderbolt — and of Lakṣmaṇa himself, who has been spoken of?"

**4.58.5**  He, pleased, reassuring those monkeys who were filled with affection and attentive to hear of Sītā, spoke these words again.

**4.58.6**  "Hear now how the abduction of the Vaidehī was heard by me, by whom it was reported to me, and where the wide-eyed one is."

**4.58.7**  "I, old and my vital strength and prowess diminished, fell long ago upon this difficult mountain extending many yojanas."

**4.58.8**  "My son, named Supārśva, foremost among birds, sustains me in this condition with food at the proper time."

**4.58.9**  "Gandharvas are sharp in their desires, serpents sharp in their anger, beasts sharp in their fears — and we birds are sharp in our hunger."

**4.58.10**  "Once, when the sun had set and I was afflicted by hunger and longing for food, my son came bringing no flesh."

**4.58.11**  "That youth, foremost among birds, was rebuked by me — overcome by hunger and thirst, and on account of old age and anger."

**4.58.12**  "He, the joy-increaser, distressed by my withholding of food, paid his respects and then spoke these words truthfully."

**4.58.13**  "'Father, at the proper time I took to the sky in search of flesh, and stationed myself blocking the gateway of Mount Mahendra."

**4.58.14**  "'There, facing downward, I alone set myself to obstruct the path of the thousands of creatures that roam the far reaches of the ocean."

**4.58.15**  "'There I saw someone resembling a heap of broken collyrium, carrying a woman blazing like the rising sun, and going away."

**4.58.16**  "'I, bent on seizing food, had made my resolve upon seeing them, but I was entreated concerning the path by him with gentle conciliation."

**4.58.17**  "'Indeed, nowhere is there anyone who would strike those who approach with conciliation — not even some person of low birth, much less one like me."

**4.58.18**  "'He went through the sky with his brilliance, compressing it as it were with his speed. Then I was approached and honored by the aerial beings."

**4.58.19**  "'"By good fortune you live, father," said the great seers to me. "He who passed with his wife has gone well — there is no doubt."""

**4.58.20**  "'Thus addressed by those supremely splendid perfected sages, I was informed that he was Rāvaṇa, king of the rākṣasas."

**4.58.21**  "'He was carrying off the wife of Daśaratha's son Rāma — Janaka's daughter — her ornaments fallen, clad in silk, overcome by the force of grief."

**4.58.22**  "'She was crying out the names of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, her hair loosened. "This is a waste of time" — so said Supārśva, the foremost among those skilled in speech.'

**4.58.23**  "Supārśva reported all this matter in full to me. Yet even upon hearing this, my mind had no inclination toward any heroic action."

**4.58.24**  "For how can a bird without wings undertake any deed? Yet what can be done by me — proceeding by the power of speech and intelligence —"

**4.58.25**  "Hear what I shall declare, relying on your valor. Through speech and thought I shall accomplish what is dear to all of you, for whatever is the task of Daśaratha's son is indeed mine — of this there is no doubt."

**4.58.26**  "You are supreme in wisdom, powerful, high-minded, united with the king of monkeys — unapproachable even by the gods."

**4.58.27**  "And the sharpened arrows of Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa, feathered with heron-plumes, suffice for protecting and subduing all three worlds."

**4.58.28**  "Indeed Daśagrīva is endowed with brilliance and strength — yet for you who are capable, nothing whatsoever is difficult."

**4.58.29**  "Therefore, enough with the delay of time — let a firm resolve of the mind be made. For the wise, such as yourselves, do not hesitate in undertakings."

**4.59.1**  Then the leaders of the monkey troops seated themselves on the difficult mountain all around that vulture who had bathed and performed his water-rites.

**4.59.2**  Saṃpāti, his trust established, spoke again with joy to Aṅgada seated there, surrounded by all those monkeys.

**4.59.3**  "Let the monkeys fall silent and listen with one-pointed attention. I shall declare truthfully, as I know it, where the daughter of Mithilā is."

**4.59.4**  "I fell long ago upon the summit of this Vindhya in the forest, my limbs overcome by the heat of the sun, burnt by the rays of the sun."

**4.59.5**  "Regaining consciousness only after six nights, helpless and bewildered as it were, scanning all directions, I knew nothing at all."

**4.59.6**  "Then, surveying the oceans, mountains, all rivers and lakes, forests and wilderness regions, my understanding returned."

**4.59.7**  "Filled with flocks of joyful birds, having peaks and cave-interiors, on the shore of the southern ocean — I concluded: 'This is the Vindhya.'"

**4.59.8**  "And here there was a holy hermitage, honored even by the gods, in which the sage named Niśākara practiced severe austerities."

**4.59.9**  "Eight thousand years I dwelt here, O knowers of dharma, without that sage — for Niśākara had gone to heaven."

**4.59.10**  "Descending with difficulty and slowly from the uneven summit of the Vindhya, I returned with hardship to the earth sharp with kuśa-grass."

**4.59.11**  "Desirous of seeing that sage, I came with great difficulty; for that sage had been visited many times by Jaṭāyu and by me."

**4.59.12**  "Near the site of his hermitage, sweet-scented winds blew; no tree was seen that was not flowering or not bearing fruit."

**4.59.13**  "And having arrived at the holy hermitage and taken shelter at the foot of a tree, desiring to see him, I waited for the blessed Niśākara."

**4.59.14**  "Then I saw the sage not far off — blazing with energy, having completed his ablutions, irresistible, turned face northward."

**4.59.15**  "Bears, deer, tigers, lions, elephants, and serpents came surrounding him, as living beings approach a benefactor."

**4.59.16**  "Then, knowing the sage had arrived, those creatures departed, as all the ministers and forces withdraw when the king has entered."

**4.59.17**  "But the sage, seeing me, was pleased, entered the hermitage again, and after just a moment came out and asked about my purpose."

**4.59.18**  "'Gentle one, seeing the loss of your feathers I do not comprehend — these two wings of yours are burnt by fire, and your skin too is wounded."

**4.59.19**  "'Two vultures have been seen by me before, equal to the wind in speed, kings of vultures, brothers who could assume any form at will."

**4.59.20**  "'You are Saṃpāti the elder, and Jaṭāyu is your younger brother. Assuming a human form, let them both grasp my feet."

**4.59.21**  "'What is the origin of your affliction? How did the fall of your wings come about? Or has this punishment been imposed by someone? Tell me all, as I ask.'"

**4.60.1**  Then Saṃpāti narrated to the sage in full that terrible deed done rashly and with difficulty — and likewise the pursuit of the sun.

**4.60.2**  "Blessed one, on account of my wounds and with senses disturbed by shame, I am exhausted and cannot speak at length."

**4.60.3**  "Both I and Jaṭāyu, deluded by pride through rivalry, being heroes, soared into the sky wishing to surpass each other's valor."

**4.60.4**  "Having made a wager on the summit of Kailāsa before the sages, the sun was to be followed until it reached the great western mountain of setting."

**4.60.5**  "Then we two, arriving simultaneously, looked down at the earth's surface and saw separate cities the size of chariot wheels."

**4.60.6**  "Somewhere we heard the sounds of musical instruments and the sounds of Vedic recitation, and we saw many women singing, clothed in red garments."

**4.60.7**  "Swiftly soaring and taking the path of the sun, we two looked back and saw that forest carpeted in green grass."

**4.60.8**  "The earth appeared covered as if by smooth stones — by masses of rock, and the earth was wrapped with rivers as if by threads."

**4.60.9**  "The Himavat and the Vindhya and the very great mountain Meru appeared on the earth's surface like serpents in a lake."

**4.60.10**  "Intense perspiration and distress and fear beset us both then; confusion overcame us, and from confusion a terrible swooning."

**4.60.11**  "The southern direction could not be distinguished, nor the northeastern, nor the western — the world was fixed as at the end of an age, stricken, as if consumed by fire."

**4.60.12**  "With great effort the sun was perceived once more — the maker of light appeared to us as equal in extent to the entire earth."

**4.60.13**  "Jaṭāyu, without asking me, plunged down to the earth. Seeing him, I swiftly released myself from the sky."

**4.60.14**  "Shielded by my wings, Jaṭāyu was not burnt; but through my own negligence I was completely burnt there, falling from the wind-path."

**4.60.15**  "I suspect that Jaṭāyu fell in Janasthāna; but I fell on the Vindhya, with burnt wings, rendered motionless."

**4.60.16**  "Bereft of my kingdom, of my brother, of both wings and of my valor, wishing in every way only to die, I shall fall from the mountain peak."

**4.61.1**  Having spoken thus to that foremost of sages, I wept, greatly afflicted by grief. Then the blessed one, having reflected for a moment, spoke these words.

**4.61.2**  "Your wings and secondary feathers shall grow anew; and likewise your eyes, your vitality, your valour, and your strength shall be restored to you."

**4.61.3**  "For I have heard of a great deed yet to come, spoken of in ancient times; I have perceived it through my austerities, and it is known to me from what I have heard."

**4.61.4**  "There is a king named Daśaratha, a scion of the Ikṣvāku dynasty; his son, of immense splendour, shall be named Rāma."

**4.61.5**  "He, whose valour is true, being engaged in that task by his father, shall go to the forest along with his brother Lakṣmaṇa."

**4.61.6**  "Rāvaṇa, lord of the Rākṣasas, a demon by birth, invincible to gods and Dānavas, shall steal his wife from Janasthāna."

**4.61.7**  "Though tempted with pleasures and with foods to eat and drink, the glorious Maithilī, that illustrious one, sunk in sorrow, shall not partake of them."

**4.61.8**  "But Vāsava, knowing Vaidehī's plight, shall give her food of the highest excellence — food akin to ambrosia and rare even among the gods."

**4.61.9**  "Receiving that food and recognising it as coming from Indra, Maithilī shall take a portion from the top and scatter it on the ground as an offering to Rāma."

**4.61.10**  "Saying, 'If my lord, the master, still lives together with Lakṣmaṇa — or even if they have attained divinity — let this food be theirs.'"

**4.61.11**  "Leaping monkeys, serving as Rāma's messengers, shall come searching for her. You, O bird, must declare to them that she is Rāma's queen."

**4.61.12**  "In any case you must not go anywhere — where would one in your condition go? Wait for the right place and time; you shall regain your wings."

**4.61.13**  "I could restore your wings to you even today, but remaining here you shall accomplish a task beneficial to all the worlds."

**4.61.14**  "Indeed that task must be done by you — it is the task of those two princes, of the Brāhmaṇas, of the gods, of the sages, and of Vāsava himself."

**4.61.15**  "I too wish to behold the brothers Rāma and Lakṣmaṇa; I do not wish to hold on to life much longer — I shall abandon this body."

**4.62.1**  With these and many other words, that master of speech, having praised me and granted me leave, entered his own hermitage.

**4.62.2**  Creeping out of the cave of that mountain, slowly and gradually, I climbed the Vindhya and have been awaiting you here.

**4.62.3**  And now more than a full hundred years of that time have passed; keeping the sage's words in my heart, I have been waiting for the right place and moment.

**4.62.4**  After Niśākara undertook his great final journey and passed to heaven, grief consumes me, beset as I am by many apprehensions.

**4.62.5**  The resolve toward death that arises in me I turn back by means of the sage's words; the counsel he gave me for the preservation of my life dispels my sorrow as a blazing tongue of flame dispels darkness.

**4.62.6**  And knowing the might of evil-souled Rāvaṇa, his son was only threatened with words — why then was Maithilī not rescued?

**4.62.7**  Having heard her lament, and seeing those two bereft of Sītā, the son born out of Daśaratha's love for me did not perform what was dear to me.

**4.62.8**  And while Saṃpāti was speaking thus in the company of the monkeys, his two wings suddenly sprouted forth before the eyes of those forest-dwellers.

**4.62.9**  Seeing his own body adorned with wings spread out and feathered with a reddish hue, he experienced incomparable joy and spoke these words to the monkeys.

**4.62.10**  "By the power of the great sage Niśākara, of immeasurable soul, my wings, scorched by the rays of the sun, have grown again."

**4.62.11**  "The valour I possessed in my youth — that very strength and manliness I perceive in myself today."

**4.62.12**  "Strive by all means — you shall attain Sītā. This regaining of my wings is for you a token of certain success."

**4.62.13**  Having spoken thus to all those monkeys, Saṃpāti, foremost of fliers, leapt from the summit of the mountain, eager to test his movement through the sky.

**4.62.14**  Hearing his words, those tiger-like monkeys were overjoyed and delighted in their hearts, and turned their faces toward the rising of their prowess.

**4.62.15**  Then those best of leapers, with the swiftness of the wind, their manliness recovered, set out in the direction of Abhijit, eager to search for the daughter of Janaka.

**4.63.1**  Having been told by the king of vultures, the leaping monkeys sprang up together, filled with joy, and roared with the valour of lions.

**4.63.2**  Having heard Saṃpāti's words regarding the destruction of Rāvaṇa, the joyful monkeys, eager to find Sītā, came to the ocean.

**4.63.3**  Approaching that place, those monkeys of awesome valour beheld it — standing as if it were a reflection of the entire great world.

**4.63.4**  Having reached the northern shore of the southern ocean, those foremost of monkeys then encamped together.

**4.63.5**  They saw the ocean thronged with immense and monstrous creatures of various kinds sporting in the water, with gaping mouths and enormous bodies, and filled with surging waves.

**4.63.6**  In some places it seemed as if asleep, in other places as if at play, and here and there it was surrounded by masses of water as vast as mountains.

**4.63.7**  Seeing it crowded with great Dānava lords dwelling in the depths of Pātāla, a sight that made the hair stand on end, those elephant-like monkeys fell into despondency.

**4.63.8**  Gazing at the ocean, which seemed as uncrossable as the sky itself, all the monkeys suddenly fell into despair, saying, "How shall the task be accomplished?"

**4.63.9**  Seeing the army thus cast into despondency by the sight of the ocean, the foremost of monkeys consoled those monkeys who were afflicted by fear.

**4.63.10**  "We have no need of despair; despondency is the greater fault. Despondency destroys a person as an enraged serpent destroys a child."

**4.63.11**  "This despondency overpowers one when the moment for valiant action arrives; stripped of its energy, the purpose of man cannot be achieved."

**4.63.12**  When that night had passed, Aṅgada convened another counsel together with the monkeys, assembling with the elder leaders of the monkey host.

**4.63.13**  That army of monkeys, surrounding Aṅgada, stood resplendent — even as the host of Maruts stands surrounding Vāsava.

**4.63.14**  "Who else would be capable of steadying that monkey army other than the son of Vālin and other than Hanūmat?"

**4.63.15**  Then the glorious Aṅgada, the subduer of foes, having paid his respects to those elder monkeys and to the army, spoke words full of purpose.

**4.63.16**  "Who among you, O mighty one, shall now leap over the ocean? Who shall fulfil the purpose of Sugrīva, true to his pledge and the subduer of enemies?"

**4.63.17**  "What hero, O best of leapers, could vault a hundred yojanas? Who could deliver all these troop leaders from this great peril?"

**4.63.18**  "By whose grace, having turned back from here with our purpose accomplished, might we joyfully see again our wives, our children, and our homes?"

**4.63.19**  "By whose grace might we joyfully approach Rāma and the mighty Lakṣmaṇa, and the mighty Sugrīva?"

**4.63.20**  "If any monkey among you is capable of crossing the ocean, let him quickly grant us here the auspicious gift of fearlessness."

**4.63.21**  Having heard Aṅgada's words, not one of them said anything; that entire monkey army stood there motionless as if frozen.

**4.63.22**  Again Aṅgada, foremost of monkeys, addressed those monkeys: "You are all the foremost of the powerful, of steadfast valour."

**4.63.23**  "You are born in distinguished lineages and are honoured repeatedly; there is no obstacle for any of you, at any time, in going across."

**4.63.24**  "Declare, O best of leapers, whatever capacity each of you has for making the crossing."

**4.64.1**  Then, having heard the words of Aṅgada, all those foremost of monkeys declared, each in turn, their own eagerness and capacity for the journey.

**4.64.2**  Gaja, Gavākṣa, Gavaya, Śarabha, Gandhamādana, Mainda and Dvivida, Suṣeṇa, and likewise Jāmbavān—

**4.64.3**  Gaja spoke up there: "I can leap ten yojanas." Gavākṣa declared he would travel twenty yojanas.

**4.64.4**  The monkey Gavaya then said to those monkeys: "I shall travel thirty yojanas, O leapers."

**4.64.5**  The monkey Śarabha then said to those monkeys: "I shall travel forty yojanas—there is no doubt."

**4.64.6**  The greatly resplendent Gandhamādana said to those monkeys: "I shall travel fifty yojanas—there is no doubt."

**4.64.7**  The monkey Mainda then said to those monkeys: "I am capable of leaping fully sixty yojanas."

**4.64.8**  Then the greatly resplendent Dvivida replied there: "I shall travel seventy yojanas—there is no doubt."

**4.64.9**  Suṣeṇa, foremost among monkeys, declared to those best of monkeys: "I can leap eighty yojanas, O bulls among leapers."

**4.64.10**  While they were speaking thus, honoring them all, then Jāmbavān, the eldest among them, spoke in reply.

**4.64.11**  "Formerly we too had some prowess in speed and movement; but now we have reached the far shore of old age."

**4.64.12**  "Yet in this situation it is not possible to neglect this task for which the king of monkeys and Rāma are both firmly resolved."

**4.64.13**  "Know now the movement that is available to me, given the changes of time: I shall travel ninety yojanas—there is no doubt."

**4.64.14**  And Jāmbavān addressed all those foremost monkeys again: "Indeed my prowess in movement was not merely this much—

**4.64.15**  "In the past, Viṣṇu the eternal, of great might, striding with his three steps at the sacrifice, was circumambulated by me."

**4.64.16**  "Now I am old and slow in my leaping; yet in youth at that time my strength was unmatched by any foe."

**4.64.17**  "Now I reckon my capacity for travel to be this much; yet by this alone the full accomplishment of this task will not come about."

**4.64.18**  Then the greatly wise Aṅgada, honoring Jāmbavān the great monkey, spoke words of noble import in reply:

**4.64.19**  "I shall travel this great distance of one hundred yojanas; but whether I have the strength to return is not certain."

**4.64.20**  The foremost of monkeys, Jāmbavān, skilled in speech, said to him: "Your capacity for the going is well known, O best of monkeys and bears—

**4.64.21**  "A hundred thousand yojanas even you are capable of traveling and returning—this is not the point that is spoken of here.

**4.64.22**  "A master who sends others forth should never himself be sent on a mission; all this company is meant to be sent by you, O best of leapers.

**4.64.23**  "You stand established as our protector in the position of master; a master is the companion of the army—this is the accepted way, O scorcher of foes.

**4.64.24**  "Therefore, dear one, you must always be guarded like a companion; and moreover, O destroyer of enemies, you are the very root of this enterprise.

**4.64.25**  "The root of an enterprise must be protected—such is the policy of those skilled in affairs; for when the root endures, its qualities flourish like flowers and fruit.

**4.64.26**  "Therefore you, of true valor, endowed with the strength of both intelligence and might, O scorcher of foes, are the cause in the accomplishment of this enterprise.

**4.64.27**  "For you are both elder and the son of an elder to us, O foremost of monkeys; relying on you, we are capable of accomplishing the task."

**4.64.28**  Then Aṅgada, son of Vālin, the great monkey, replied with these words to the greatly wise Jāmbavān who had spoken:

**4.64.29**  "If I shall not go, and no other bull among monkeys will either, then surely what remains for us is once again to sit in fasting unto death.

**4.64.30**  "For without fulfilling the command of that wise lord of monkeys, I see no preservation of life even if we were to go there.

**4.64.31**  "For that monkey lord is supreme in both grace and in wrath; by transgressing his command, destruction would come upon our going.

**4.64.32**  "Therefore, so that this enterprise does not go otherwise, you who see the purpose clearly are the one who ought to consider this."

**4.64.33**  Then that heroic bull among leapers, Jāmbavān, having been answered by Aṅgada, spoke these further words to Aṅgada:

**4.64.34**  "O hero, nothing of this enterprise of yours shall be lacking; I am about to spur on the one who will accomplish it."

**4.64.35**  Then that hero among monkeys, approaching the foremost among leapers—renowned, sitting apart at ease—urged on that very hero among monkeys, Hanūmān himself.

**4.65.1**  Beholding thus the monkey host of many hundreds of thousands, dejected, Jāmbavān then spoke to Hanūmān:
