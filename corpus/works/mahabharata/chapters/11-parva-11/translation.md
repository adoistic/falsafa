**11.1.1**  Janamejaya said: When Duryodhana had been slain and the whole army utterly destroyed, what did the great king Dhṛtarāṣṭra do, O sage, on hearing of it?

**11.1.2**  And likewise the great-souled Kaurava king, the son of Dharma, and Kṛpa and the rest — what did those three do?

**11.1.3**  I have heard of Aśvatthāman's deed and of the mutual curse they pronounced upon one another; tell me what happened afterward — what Saṃjaya then related.

**11.1.4**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: With his hundred sons slain, the king Dhṛtarāṣṭra was wretched, like a tree whose branches have been lopped away, consumed by grief for his sons —

**11.1.5**  fallen mute in brooding, overwhelmed by sorrow; and the deeply wise Saṃjaya, approaching the lord of earth, spoke these words.

**11.1.6**  Why do you grieve, great king? There is no help in grief. Eighteen akṣauhiṇīs have been destroyed, O lord of peoples; this earth, rich in treasure, is now emptied of men, utterly desolate.

**11.1.7**  Kings from every quarter, men of many lands, gathered together with your son, and all alike have gone to their destruction.

**11.1.8**  For your fathers, sons, and grandsons, your kinsmen and friends, and your elders, have the funeral rites performed, each in due order.

**11.1.9**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: Hearing that piteous speech, the once-unassailable king, stricken by the slaughter of his sons and grandsons, fell to the earth like a tree struck down by the wind.

**11.1.10**  Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: My sons slain, my counsellors slain, all my friends slain — surely I shall now wander this earth in misery.

**11.1.11**  What use, now, is life to me, bereft of kinsmen — like a bird worn out by age, its wings shorn away?

**11.1.12**  My kingdom taken, my friends slain, and my sight gone as well — I shall shine no more, O most wise one, like the sun whose rays have faded.

**11.1.13**  I did not heed the words of my well-wishers — of Jāmadagnya as he spoke, of the divine sage Nārada, nor of Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana.

**11.1.14**  Nor what Kṛṣṇa, for my good, declared in the midst of the assembly: 'Enough of this enmity, O king; let your son be restrained.'

**11.1.15**  Having failed to act upon that counsel, I, the dull-witted one, now burn with bitter remorse; for I shall hear no more the righteous words spoken by Bhīṣma.

**11.1.16**  Recalling Duryodhana bellowing like a bull, and hearing of the slaying of Duḥśāsana, of the overthrow of Karṇa, and of the eclipse of that sun which was Droṇa — my heart is torn asunder.

**11.1.17**  I cannot recall, Saṃjaya, any misdeed of mine in the past whose fruit it is that I, the deluded one, now reap here today.

**11.1.18**  Surely some wrong was done by me in former births, that the Ordainer has yoked me to deeds whose lot is sorrow.

**11.1.19**  The decline of my years, the loss of all my kinsmen, and the destruction of my friends and allies have come upon me through the working of fate. Who else in this world is a man more wretched than I?

**11.1.20**  Therefore let the Pāṇḍavas behold me this very day, firm in a stern vow, set upon the long road that lies open to the world of Brahman.

**11.1.21**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: As the lord of men lamented thus, dwelling on his many sorrows, Saṃjaya spoke words to dispel that grief.

**11.1.22**  Cast off your grief, O king. You have heard the settled truths of the Vedas and the various teachings of the scriptures from the elders, O best of kings — what the sages once said to Sṛñjaya when he was stricken with grief for his son.

**11.1.23**  When your son had given himself over to the arrogance born of youth, you did not heed the words of your friends as they spoke; nor did you, greedy and hungry for gain, pursue any true good of your own.

**11.1.24**  Your counsellors were Duḥśāsana and the wicked-souled Rādheya, the evil-natured Śakuni, the ill-minded Citrasena, and Śalya — by whom the whole world was turned into a thorn of affliction.

**11.1.25**  The word of Bhīṣma, the elder of the Kurus, and of Gāndhārī and Vidura was not heeded by that son of yours, O descendant of Bharata.

**11.1.26**  No righteousness was ever honored by him; crying always 'War! War!', he destroyed all the kṣatriyas and increased the glory of his foes.

**11.1.27**  And you yourself stood neutral, saying nothing that befitted the case; you who should have borne the yoke did not hold the burden level in the balance.

**11.1.28**  From the very outset a man must act as is fitting, so that he be not afflicted with remorse over a matter once it has passed beyond recall.

**11.1.29**  Through fondness for your son, O king, and a wish to do what pleased him, you have come to this remorse; you ought not to grieve.

**11.1.30**  He who, seeing only the honey, does not perceive the precipice beyond, is brought to ruin by his greed for the honey and grieves — even as you do.

**11.1.31**  Not by grieving does one gain wealth; not by grieving does one find happiness; not by grieving does one attain fortune; not by grieving does one win the supreme.

**11.1.32**  A man who, having himself kindled a fire, then wraps himself in cloth, and as he burns gives way to anguish of mind — such a one is no wise man.

**11.1.33**  It was by you and your son that this fire which is Pārtha was fanned by the wind of words, drenched with the clarified butter of greed, and set ablaze.

**11.1.34**  Into that blaze your sons fell like moths; consumed by the flame that was Keśava, they are gone — and you ought not to grieve for them.

**11.1.35**  And this face you bear, sullied with falling tears, O king — this the wise do not commend, for it is not sanctioned by the scriptures.

**11.1.36**  These tears, they say, scorch men like sparks of fire. Cast off your anguish through wisdom; uphold the self by the self.

**11.1.37**  Thus consoled by the great-souled Saṃjaya, Vidura in turn spoke further, with thoughtful counsel, O scorcher of foes.

**11.2.1**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: Then, gladdening that bull among men, the son of Vicitravīrya, with words like nectar, Vidura spoke — hear now what he said.

**11.2.2**  Vidura said: Rise up, O king! Why do you lie there? Uphold the self by the self. This is the appointed end of all that is fixed and moving and mortal.

**11.2.3**  All hoardings end in loss, all that rises ends in a fall; unions end in separation, and life itself ends in death.

**11.2.4**  When Yama drags off the brave man and the coward alike, O descendant of Bharata, then why should those kṣatriyas not have fought, O bull among kṣatriyas?

**11.2.5**  He who does not fight dies, and he who fights lives on; but when his hour has come, O great king, no one passes beyond it.

**11.2.6**  Nor ought you to grieve, O king, for these slain in battle; if the scriptures are our authority, they have attained the supreme goal.

**11.2.7**  All of them were men of sacred study, all had fulfilled their vows, and all fell facing the foe — what cause for lamentation is there in that?

**11.2.8**  Out of the unseen they came forth, and to the unseen they have returned again; they are not yours, nor are you theirs — what cause for lamentation is there?

**11.2.9**  Slain, one wins heaven; slaying, one wins glory. Both, indeed, are of great worth to us; there is no fruitlessness in battle.

**11.2.10**  Indra will ordain for them worlds that grant every wish; for these become the guests of Indra, O bull among men.

**11.2.11**  Not by sacrifices rich in gifts, nor by austerities, nor by sacred learning do mortals go to heaven as do heroes slain in battle.

**11.2.12**  Thousands of mothers and fathers, and hundreds of sons and wives, have been known in the rounds of existence — whose are they, or whose are we?

**11.2.13**  Thousands of occasions for grief and hundreds for fear assail the deluded man day after day, but not the wise.

**11.2.14**  To Time none is dear and none is hateful, O best of the Kurus; Time is nowhere neutral — Time drags all things away.

**11.2.15**  Life is fleeting, and so are beauty, youth, the hoarding of goods, health, and the company of loved ones; the wise man does not crave after these.

**11.2.16**  You ought not to grieve alone over a sorrow that belongs to a whole realm; though one might even perish by it, the calamity is not thereby turned back.

**11.2.17**  If one sees a way to act, let him counter the trouble without grieving; but the remedy for sorrow is not to brood upon it — for the sorrow that is dwelt upon does not depart, and instead grows the greater.

**11.2.18**  From union with the unwished-for and from separation from the dear, men of little understanding are yoked to sorrows of the mind.

**11.2.19**  This over which you grieve is neither gain nor righteousness nor happiness; the grief does not depart from the purpose of any deed, and through it one falls away from the threefold aim of life.

**11.2.20**  Attaining one richer state of wealth after another, men remain unsatisfied and are bewildered; but the wise come to contentment.

**11.2.21**  Mental suffering one should slay with wisdom, and bodily suffering with medicines; this is the power of knowledge — let one not in this become the equal of fools.

**11.2.22**  As a man lies down, the deed once done lies down with him; as he stands, it stands with him; and as he runs, it runs after him.

**11.2.23**  In whatever condition a man does a deed, good or evil, in that very condition he reaps the fruit thereof.

**11.3.1**  Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: By your well-spoken words, O most wise one, this grief of mine has passed away; yet I wish to hear your words once more, in their truth.

**11.3.2**  From contact with the unwished-for and from the loss of the cherished, how is it that the wise are here freed from sorrows of the mind?

**11.3.3**  Vidura said: From whatever source the mind is freed, whether from pain or from pleasure, from each such source the wise man, having gained peace, attains a happy state.

**11.3.4**  When pondered, all this is impermanent, O bull among men; the world is like the plantain tree — no pith of it is to be found.

**11.3.5**  The wise declare the bodies of mortals to be but dwellings; in time they are cast off, and the spirit alone is the abiding good.

**11.3.6**  As a man, casting off a garment whether worn out or not, chooses another garment, so are the bodies of embodied beings.

**11.3.7**  O son of Vicitravīrya, whether a sojourn be of sorrow or of joy, beings here attain it through the deeds they themselves have wrought.

**11.3.8**  By one's deeds are heaven, pleasure, and pain attained, O descendant of Bharata; and so each bears that burden, whether helplessly or of his own will.

**11.3.9**  And as an earthen vessel set upon the wheel may perish — some while still being shaped, or others just when made —

**11.3.10**  or when cut from the wheel, or being lifted off, or already taken down; while still moist, or dried, or being fired in the kiln —

**11.3.11**  or being drawn out of the kiln, or already taken up, or even while in use, O descendant of Bharata — so it is with the bodies of embodied beings.

**11.3.12**  Still in the womb, or newly born, or but a day old, or come to half a month, or grown to a month alone —

**11.3.13**  or come to a year, or to a second year, in the bloom of youth, in middle age, or in old age — a man perishes.

**11.3.14**  By the deeds of former lives beings come into being and pass away; in a world so ordered by necessity, to what end do you grieve?

**11.3.15**  And as in the water, O king, some creature roaming about in play may rise and may sink again, O lord of men —

**11.3.16**  even so, from the thicket of worldly existence, in the rising and the sinking, those of little understanding are bound by the reaping of their deeds and are tormented.

**11.3.17**  But those who are wise, established in truth, seeking the end of worldly existence, who know the way beings come together — they attain the supreme goal.

**11.4.1**  Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: How is the thicket of worldly existence to be understood, O best of speakers? This I wish to hear; tell me the truth of it, for I ask.

**11.4.2**  Vidura said: Hear, O lord, all the workings of beings from birth onward. At first the embryo dwells here in the fluid stage for a certain interval.

**11.4.3**  Then, when the fifth month has passed, it takes on flesh; and thereafter, with all its limbs complete, the child is born in due month.

**11.4.4**  It dwells amid filth, smeared with flesh and blood; and then, driven by the force of the wind, it turns feet upward and head downward.

**11.4.5**  Reaching the door of the womb, it meets with many torments, crushed in the passage of birth and bound to the deeds of its former lives.

**11.4.6**  Freed from that round of the womb, he beholds yet other afflictions; the seizing planets steal upon him as hounds upon a piece of meat.

**11.4.7**  Then, when the later season of his life has come, diseases too steal upon him as he lives, bound fast by his own deeds.

**11.4.8**  Bound by the nooses of the senses, made sick by the sweetness of attachments, calamities of every kind beset him, O lord of men; and bound the tighter by them, he attains no satisfaction at all.

**11.4.9**  He does not so much as perceive that the realm of Yama has now drawn near; and dragged off by Yama's messengers, in due time he goes to his death.

**11.4.10**  Whatever pleasing or unpleasing words were spoken in his presence when he had lost his speech — he heeds them not, even as by his own self he binds his own self ever more.

**11.4.11**  Ah, how the world is deceived and made the thrall of greed! Maddened by greed, by wrath, and by pride, it does not come to know its own self.

**11.4.12**  He delights in his high birth and reviles the low-born; puffed up with the arrogance of wealth, he heaps scorn upon the poor.

**11.4.13**  He calls others fools, yet looks not upon himself; he flings instruction at all others, but has no wish to school his own self.

**11.4.14**  Whoever, in this unstable world of the living, abides from his very birth in the keeping of dharma — he shall attain the highest goal.

**11.4.15**  Thus having understood all things, whoever follows after the truth gains the path that leads to liberation, O lord of men.

**11.5.1**  Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: This thicket of dharma that is pursued through the intellect — expound to me at length, in its fullness, this whole path of wisdom.

**11.5.2**  Vidura said: On this I shall instruct you, having first bowed to the Self-Existent, telling how the supreme seers describe the thicket of worldly existence.

**11.5.3**  A certain brāhmaṇa, faring on within this vast round of existence, came, it is said, to a great and impassable forest, thronged with beasts that feed on flesh.

**11.5.4**  Hemmed in on every side by creatures shaped like lions, tigers, and elephants — most fearsome, great-jawed devourers — it struck terror even into Death himself.

**11.5.5**  Beholding this, his heart fell into the utmost dread; his hair stood on end, and his whole frame was thrown into agitation, O scorcher of foes.

**11.5.6**  Roaming that forest, running this way and that, he gazed about him in every direction, wondering where a refuge might be found.

**11.5.7**  Seeking some gap between those beasts, he fled on, oppressed by fear; yet he could win no distance away, nor break free of them.

**11.5.8**  Then he saw that the dreadful forest was hedged about on every side with a net, and was enfolded within the two arms of a most terrible woman.

**11.5.9**  That great forest was ringed about by five-headed serpents, towering like mountains, and by mighty trees that brushed against the sky.

**11.5.10**  And there in the midst of the forest was a well, all overgrown, hidden and encircled by creepers covered over with grass.

**11.5.11**  Into that concealed reservoir of water the brāhmaṇa fell, and there he hung, caught fast in the tangled mesh of creepers.

**11.5.12**  As a great jackfruit grows bound by its stalk, so did he hang there, his feet upward and his head below.

**11.5.13**  Then there too another affliction yet arose for him: at the rim about the mouth of the well he saw a mighty elephant.

**11.5.14**  Six-faced it was, dark and dappled, moving upon twelve feet, creeping on by degrees, wrapped about with creepers and trees.

**11.5.15**  And upon the boughs of that tree, clinging to its branches, were bees of many kinds, dreadful of form and fearful to behold, settled there, having gathered honey before, born of the hive.

**11.5.16**  Again and again they crave the honey, O bull of the Bharatas — honey so sweet to all creatures that not even a child is sated by it.

**11.5.17**  From that honey a stream flows down forever in many a trickle; and that man, hanging there, drinks of the stream without ceasing, yet his thirst is not stilled as he drinks on in his plight.

**11.5.18**  Unsated, he longs for it still, again and again; and yet, O king, no aversion to life was ever born in him.

**11.5.19**  There still the man's hope of life stands fixed, while black mice and white gnaw away at that very tree.

**11.5.20**  From the beasts of prey at the forest's impassable edge, from the most terrible woman, from the serpent at the bottom of the well, and from the elephant at its rim —

**11.5.21**  there is danger; and danger too from the falling of the tree, and the fifth from the mice; and the sixth great danger, they say, comes from the bees through his greed for honey.

**11.5.22**  Thus does he dwell there, cast into the ocean of worldly existence, and yet he comes to no weariness of his hope for life.

**11.6.1**  Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: Ah, surely it is a great misery — in such anguish does he dwell! How can there be any delight or contentment for him there, O best of speakers?

**11.6.2**  Where then is that place in which he dwells, hard pressed in the matter of dharma? And how might that man be released from that great peril?

**11.6.3**  Tell me all of this, that we may rightly strive thus; for a great compassion has arisen in me to deliver him.

**11.6.4**  Vidura said: This is a parable, O king, set forth by those who know the way of release, whereby a man finds a happy goal in the worlds beyond.

**11.6.5**  That great wilderness of which it speaks — that indeed is the round of existence; and this impassable forest is the thicket of worldly being.

**11.6.6**  Those beasts of prey that were told of are declared to be the diseases; and that woman of vast frame who stands over the place — her the wise call Old Age, destroyer of complexion and of beauty.

**11.6.7**  That well, O king, is the body of embodied beings; and the great serpent that dwells below within it is Time itself — the Ender of all creatures, who carries off every living thing.

**11.6.8**  And that creeper grown within the well, by whose tendrils the man hangs suspended — that is the hope of life in embodied beings.

**11.6.9**  That six-faced elephant which creeps about the tree at the rim of the well, O king, is held to be the year; its faces are the seasons, and its twelve feet are declared to be the months.

**11.6.10**  The mice that gnaw the tree away, ever at their work — those, the ponderers upon beings declare, are the nights and the days; and the bees there are proclaimed to be the desires.

**11.6.11**  Those many trickling streams that flow with the oozing honey — let them be known as the savors of desire, in which men sink and drown.

**11.6.12**  Those wise ones who thus have understood the turning of the wheel of existence — they indeed cut through the bonds of the wheel of existence.

**11.7.1**  Dhṛtarāṣṭra said: Ah, well has the tale been told by you who behold the truth! Yet greater still is my joy to go on hearing the nectar of your speech.

**11.7.2**  Vidura said: Hear once more; I shall expound the fullness of this path, hearing which the discerning are wholly freed from the rounds of existence.

**11.7.3**  Just as a man, O king, who has set out upon a long road, halts here and there from weariness, or makes his lodging for a while —

**11.7.4**  so, in the turning round of existence, in dwellings within the womb, O Bhārata, the foolish make their lodging, while the wise win release therefrom.

**11.7.5**  Therefore those versed in the scriptures call this very thing a road; and the thicket of worldly existence the thoughtful call a forest.

**11.7.6**  This is the world's revolving round for mortals, O bull of the Bharatas, for moving and unmoving things alike; therein the wise man should not be greedy.

**11.7.7**  The diseases of mortals, whether of the body or of the mind, whether seen or unseen — these are declared by the wise to be the beasts of prey.

**11.7.8**  Tormented by them without end and slain, O Bhārata, by those great beasts that are their own deeds, the dull of understanding are still not made afraid.

**11.7.9**  And even should a man be freed from those diseases, O king, Old Age that destroys all beauty closes about him in the end.

**11.7.10**  By sound, form, taste, and touch, and by manifold scents as well, he sinks into a great mire that has no foothold on any side.

**11.7.11**  Years, seasons, months, fortnights, days and nights, and their junctures, one after another plunder away his beauty and his span of life as well.

**11.7.12**  These are the treasure-stores of Time, which the dull of mind do not know; herein, they say, all creatures are inscribed by their deeds.

**11.7.13**  The body of beings they call the chariot; the inner being they call the charioteer; the senses they call the horses, and action and intellect the reins.

**11.7.14**  Whoever runs after the rushing speed of those galloping horses revolves, like a wheel, upon this wheel of worldly existence.

**11.7.15**  But he who reins them in by the intellect — that driver turns not back again; for this chariot, by which the dull of mind are bewildered, they call the chariot of Yama.

**11.7.16**  And such a one comes to that very thing which you have come to, O lord of men: the loss of kingdom, the loss of friends, and the loss of sons, O Bhārata.

**11.7.17**  This grief is but a craving renewed without end, O Bhārata; the good man, amid the greatest sorrows, should apply the medicine of sorrow.

**11.7.18**  Neither valor nor wealth, nor a friend, nor the company of well-wishers, sets one free from sorrow as does a self made steadfast in self-restraint.

**11.7.19**  Therefore, taking your stand upon friendliness and entering into good conduct, O Bhārata — self-control, renunciation, and vigilance: these three are the horses of Brahman.

**11.7.20**  He who stands upon the chariot of the mind, yoked with the reins of good conduct, casting off the fear of death, O king, goes to the world of Brahman.

**11.8.1**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: But hearing those words of Vidura, the best of the Kurus, consumed with grief for his sons, fell to the ground in a swoon.

**11.8.2**  Seeing him thus fallen senseless upon the earth, his kinsmen, and Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana, and the Kṣattṛ, Vidura,

**11.8.3**  and Saṃjaya, and other well-wishers, and the doorkeepers held in his esteem — with pleasantly cool water and with palm-leaf fans, O Bhārata,

**11.8.4**  touched his limbs with their hands and, fanning him with care, attended for a long while upon Dhṛtarāṣṭra brought to such a pass.

**11.8.5**  Then, after a long while, the lord of the earth regained his senses and lamented for a long time, overwhelmed by anguish for his sons.

**11.8.6**  Fie, indeed, upon this human estate, and upon the holding of possessions in human life, from which the sorrows arise that have their root therein, again and again!

**11.8.7**  In the loss of sons, in the loss of wealth, and of kinsmen and relations too, a most exceeding grief is met with, like poison and like fire, O mighty one.

**11.8.8**  By it the limbs are burned, by it understanding is destroyed; overcome by it, a man holds death itself to be a thing much to be desired.

**11.8.9**  This is the calamity that has come upon me through the reversal of my fortune; and this very thing I shall do this very day, O best of the twice-born.

**11.8.10**  Having spoken thus to his great-souled father, foremost among the knowers of Brahman, Dhṛtarāṣṭra grew bewildered and passed into the uttermost grief; and that king fell silent, sunk in brooding thought, O lord of the earth.

**11.8.11**  Hearing that speech of his, the lord Kṛṣṇa Dvaipāyana spoke these words to his son, who was consumed with grief for his own sons.

**11.8.12**  O mighty-armed Dhṛtarāṣṭra, hear what I shall say to you. Learned you are, and wise, and skilled alike in dharma and in worldly aims.

**11.8.13**  There is nothing worth knowing that is unknown to you, O scorcher of foes; for the impermanence of mortals you understand, beyond all doubt.

**11.8.14**  When the world of the living is unstable, and one's station unenduring, and life itself ends in death — why then do you grieve, O Bhārata?

**11.8.15**  Before your very eyes, O king of kings, the origin of this enmity arose; with your son made its instrument, it was brought about by the working of Time.

**11.8.16**  Since the slaughter of the Kurus was bound to come to pass, O king, why do you grieve for those heroes who have gone to the highest goal?

**11.8.17**  And the great-souled Vidura, knowing all this, O mighty-armed lord of men, strove with every effort toward peace.

**11.8.18**  Yet the course ordained by fate cannot be checked by any living being, however long one may strive against it: such is my conviction.

**11.8.19**  What task the gods had to accomplish I heard with my own ears; that I shall now relate to you fully, so that your firmness may return.

**11.8.20**  Once, having mastered all weariness, I went in haste to the assembly hall of Indra, and there I beheld the dwellers of heaven gathered together, and all the divine sages with Nārada at their head.

**11.8.21**  And there too, O lord of the earth, I saw the Earth herself, come into the presence of the gods upon an urgent errand.

**11.8.22**  Then the nourishing Earth, approaching the assembled gods, said: 'The task that you promised me in the abode of Brahmā, O most fortunate ones, let that now be swiftly accomplished.'

**11.8.23**  Hearing her words, Viṣṇu, to whom all the worlds bow down, smiled and spoke these words to the Earth in the council of the gods.

**11.8.24**  'The eldest of Dhṛtarāṣṭra's hundred sons, renowned by the name Duryodhana, he shall accomplish your task; and through that guardian of the earth you shall attain your purpose.'

**11.8.25**  'For his sake the protectors of the earth, gathered together at Kurukṣetra, smiters all, shall slay one another with mighty weapons.'

**11.8.26**  'Then, O goddess, your burden shall be destroyed in that battle; go swiftly to your own place, and uphold the worlds, O fair one.'

**11.8.27**  This son of yours, O king, was born for the destruction of the world, sprung from a portion of Kali in the womb of Gāndhārī.

**11.8.28**  Intolerant, fickle, prone to wrath, and hard to appease was he; and through the working of fate his brothers too were born of like nature.

**11.8.29**  His maternal uncle Śakuni, and Karṇa his dearest friend, and the kings allied with them, were all born for the destruction of the earth. This matter, O mighty-armed one, Nārada knows in its very truth.

**11.8.30**  By their own fault, O lord of the earth, your sons have perished; do not grieve for them, O king of kings, for there is no cause for grief.

**11.8.31**  For the Pāṇḍavas have offended in nothing, however small, O Bhārata; it was your evil-souled sons by whom this earth was brought to ruin.

**11.26.23**  Those whom the great birds and the vultures drag this way and that — even for them, by their own deeds, the worlds shall be won, O Yudhiṣṭhira.

**11.26.24**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: Thus addressed, the deeply wise Yudhiṣṭhira, son of Kuntī, gave command to Sudharman, to Dhaumya, to the charioteer Saṃjaya,

**11.26.25**  and to Vidura of great wisdom, and to Yuyutsu the Kaurava, and to the servants and charioteers headed by Indrasena, all of them together:

**11.26.26**  Let your honors have the funeral rites of these performed in full, so that no body shall perish unattended, as though it had no kin.

**11.26.27**  At the command of the king of righteousness, the steward Vidura, the charioteer Saṃjaya, Sudharman together with Dhaumya, and Indrasena and the rest,

**11.26.28**  gathering sandalwood and aloeswood and fragrant timber, clarified butter and oil and perfumes, and garments of fine linen,

**11.26.29**  and costly heaps of firewood, and the shattered chariots there, and weapons of every kind,

**11.26.30**  made the pyres with care and, calmly, by the rite prescribed in scripture, had the foremost of the kings cremated.

**11.26.31**  King Duryodhana and his brothers, more than a hundred of them, Śalya and King Śala, and Bhūriśravas as well,

**11.26.32**  and King Jayadratha and Abhimanyu, O Bhārata, the son of Duḥśāsana, and Lakṣmaṇa, and King Dhṛṣṭaketu,

**11.26.33**  Bṛhanta and Somadatta and more than a hundred of the Sṛñjayas, King Kṣemadhanvan, and Virāṭa and Drupada too,

**11.26.34**  Śikhaṇḍin the Pāñcāla and Dhṛṣṭadyumna son of Pṛṣata, and the valiant Yudhāmanyu and Uttamaujas as well,

**11.26.35**  the prince of Kosala and the sons of Draupadī, and Śakuni son of Subala, Acala and Vṛṣaka, and King Bhagadatta,

**11.26.36**  and the wrathful Karṇa, son of the Sun, together with his sons, and the Kekayas, those mighty archers, and the Trigartas, those great chariot-warriors,

**11.26.37**  and Ghaṭotkaca, lord of the Rākṣasas, the brother of Baka, and King Alambusa, and King Jalasaṃdha,

**11.26.38**  and other kings, O king, by the hundreds and the thousands — all these they had burned with blazing fires fed by streams of clarified butter.

**11.26.39**  For some of those great souls the rites for the fathers were performed; some were chanted over with hymns of the Sāman, and over others the mourners lamented.

**11.26.40**  With the sound of the Sāman and the Ṛc verses, and the weeping cries of the women, a swooning faintness came upon all beings in the night.

**11.26.41**  Those fires, smokeless, kindled and blazing, appeared as planets in the sky veiled by a thin haze of cloud.

**11.26.42**  And those who were without kin there, come together from many lands — gathering them all and heaping them in piles by the thousands,

**11.26.43**  Vidura, unhurried, having stacked them with abundant wood soaked in oil, had them burned at the command of the king of righteousness.

**11.26.44**  When he had had their rites performed, Yudhiṣṭhira, the king of the Kurus, set Dhṛtarāṣṭra before him and went toward the Gaṅgā.

**11.27.1**  Vaiśaṃpāyana said: They came to the Gaṅgā, auspicious, frequented by holy folk, full of pools, rich in banks, with broad marshlands and great forests,

**11.27.2**  and casting off their ornaments, their upper garments, and their headcloths, then for their fathers, grandsons, brothers, and kindred,

**11.27.3**  for their sons and elders and husbands, all the women of the Kurus, weeping and sorely afflicted, made the water-offering; and the knowers of the law performed the libation rites for their friends as well.

**11.27.4**  As the water-offering was made by the wives of the heroes for the heroes, the Gaṅgā came to have easy fords, and spread her waters wide again.

**11.27.5**  That bank of the Gaṅgā, vast as the great ocean, joyless and without festival, thronged with the wives of the heroes, shone in its mournful splendor.

**11.27.6**  Then Kuntī, O great king, suddenly worn by grief, weeping, spoke to her sons in a faltering voice:

**11.27.7**  That hero, the mighty archer, the leader of leaders of chariot-bands, who was slain in battle by Arjuna, marked with every sign of a hero —

**11.27.8**  he whom you, O Pāṇḍavas, thought a charioteer's son, Rādheya — he who shone in the midst of the host like the radiant lord of day,

**11.27.9**  who once fought against you all and your followers, who, leading on the whole army of Duryodhana, shone in his splendor,

**11.27.10**  to whose valor there is none equal on all the earth — true to his word, valiant, never fleeing in battle —

**11.27.11**  make the water-offering for that brother of yours, untiring in his deeds. For he was your elder brother, born to me of the Sun, that hero with earrings and armor, whose splendor was like the maker of day.

**11.27.12**  Hearing this unwelcome word of their mother, all the Pāṇḍavas grieved now for Karṇa, and became more stricken than before.

**11.27.13**  Then that tiger among men, the heroic Yudhiṣṭhira, son of Kuntī, said to his mother, hissing like a serpent:

**11.27.14**  He within whose arrow's flight none but Dhanaṃjaya could stand his ground — how was that god-begotten one of old a son of yours?

**11.27.15**  He by the might of whose arms we were scorched on every side—how could you have kept him concealed, like a fire wrapped in a garment? He whose terrible strength of arm the sons of Dhṛtarāṣṭra leaned upon for support—

**11.27.16**  No other chariot-warrior among warriors could withstand Karṇa, the son of Kuntī. He was our first-born brother, the foremost of all who bear weapons. How is it, lady, that you bore him first, him of wondrous valor?

**11.27.17**  Alas, by the concealing of this counsel of yours we are undone; for by the slaying of Karṇa we have been crushed, together with our kinsmen.

**11.27.18**  By the destruction of Abhimanyu, by the slaughter of the sons of Draupadī, by the ruin of the Pāñcālas, and by the fall of the Kurus—

**11.27.19**  this grief, a hundredfold greater than all of that, has touched me to the quick; for grieving over Karṇa I burn as though cast into a fire.

**11.27.20**  Nothing whatever would have been beyond our reach, even were it set in heaven, nor would this terrible carnage, which has brought the Kauravas to their end, ever have come to pass.

**11.27.21**  Having lamented thus at great length, O king, the King of Righteousness, Yudhiṣṭhira, wailing softly, the lord performed the water-offering for him.

**11.27.22**  Then on all sides the women and men who stood about there, gathered for that water-rite, suddenly broke into wailing.

**11.27.23**  Then the wise lord of the Kurus, Yudhiṣṭhira, out of love for his brother, had Karṇa's women brought forth with their retinue.

**11.27.24**  Then, together with them, that righteous-souled one performed the funeral rites; and having done so, his senses overwhelmed, he came up out of the waters of the Gaṅgā.
