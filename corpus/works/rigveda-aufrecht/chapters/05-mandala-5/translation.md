**5.1.1**  Agni has awakened through the fuel of the people, going forth to meet the Dawn as she approaches like a cow; the great ones like young birds spreading their wings, the rays stream upward toward the vault of heaven.

**5.1.2**  The Hotṛ has awakened to sacrifice to the gods; Agni, well-disposed and erect, has stood up at dawn. The blazing might of him, when kindled, has been seen; the great god has been freed from darkness.

**5.1.3**  When he takes the tether of the host, pure Agni adorns himself with pure rays of light; thereupon the dakṣiṇā, seeking the prize, is harnessed, and he who stands erect has drunk up the oblation with his tongues of flame.

**5.1.4**  The minds of those who devoutly seek Agni converge toward him as eyes converge toward the sun; when the two differently-formed Dawns set him in motion, the white steed is born at the forefront of the days.

**5.1.5**  He was born, the conqueror, at the head of the days, the ruddy one set among the fuels in the forests; Agni, the Hotṛ bearing seven treasures in house after house, most worthy of worship, has taken his seat.

**5.1.6**  Agni, the Hotṛ most worthy of worship, has sat down in the lap of the mother, fragrant in the world; the young seer, much-requested, possessing Ṛta, the upholder of the peoples — he burns in their midst.

**5.1.7**  Even now men invoke with reverences that inspired and able Agni, the Hotṛ at the rites — he who has stretched both world-halves with Ṛta; they ever groom the prize-winner with ghee.

**5.1.8**  He who belongs to the cleansing rite is groomed in his own home, praised by the seer, our beneficent guest; the bull of a thousand horns, whose strength is in that very power — O Agni, by your force you surpass all others.

**5.1.9**  Straightway, O Agni, you surpass all others, manifest to whomever you have become most dear; worthy of praise, of beautiful form, shining widely — the beloved guest of the clans of humankind.

**5.1.10**  The settled peoples bring tribute to you, O youngest Agni, both those nearby and those from afar; perceive the good will of him who offers most ardent praise — great and lofty is your shelter, O Agni, blessed and excellent.

**5.1.11**  Mount today the radiant chariot, O Agni, surrounded on all sides by those worthy of worship; knowing the broad mid-air and its pathways, bring hither the gods to eat the oblation.

**5.1.12**  We have spoken a word of homage to the wise and sacred seer, the bull and the mighty; Gaviṣṭhira has offered with reverence a hymn of praise into Agni — it has spread wide like a golden ornament in the sky.

**5.2.1**  The young mother carries the boy, clasped close, in secret; she does not give him to the father. The people do not diminish his force; they see him placed in front, hidden in the fire-stick.

**5.2.2**  What is this boy, O young one, that you carry in your womb? The great queen has given birth to him. For the embryo has grown through many autumns — I saw the newborn when the mother brought him forth.

**5.2.3**  From afar I saw him measuring out his weapons in the field — of golden teeth and pure appearance; dispensing immortality to us, mingling it — what can those who are without Indra and their praises do to me?

**5.2.4**  From the field I saw him moving at a distance, shining greatly like a herd in good shape; they did not grasp those — for he was born; those that were gray-haired become young women indeed.

**5.2.5**  Who would separate my darling from his cows — he whose herdsman had never been a stranger? Let those who seized him release him; the knowing one will drive the cattle back to us.

**5.2.6**  The enemies have hidden the king who clothes the people, the dweller among mortals; let the prayers of Atri release him — may the slanderers themselves become worthy of blame.

**5.2.7**  Even Śunaḥśepa, bound to the thousand-post yūpa, was released — for he prayed. Even so, O Agni, release from us these fetters; O Hotṛ, knowing one, sit down here now.

**5.2.8**  Being angered, the guardian of the divine ordinances spoke forth to me from afar — he turned away from me; Indra, the knowing one, saw you fully — instructed by him, O Agni, I have come.

**5.2.9**  Agni blazes with great light, making all things manifest by his greatness; he overcomes the godless illusions and those of ill approach; he sharpens his horns to pierce the demon.

**5.2.10**  And may the howlers of Agni be in the sky — his sharp weapons ready to slay the demon; even in his exultation his flames burst forth, and the godless obstructions do not withstand them.

**5.2.11**  This hymn of praise for you, O Tuvijāta, the inspired one, the wise craftsman, has fashioned like a chariot; if, O Agni, O god, you are pleased with it, may we win by means of it the sun-lit waters.

**5.2.12**  The bull of mighty neck, waxing great, the lordly one without enemies, brings wealth together. Thus the immortals have declared of this Agni: may he grant shelter to Manu who spreads the sacred grass, may he grant shelter to Manu who offers oblation.

**5.3.1**  You, O Agni, are born as Varuṇa when kindled; you become Mitra when blazing; in you are all the gods, O son of strength; you are Indra for the mortal who worships.

**5.3.2**  You become Aryaman when you bear the secret name of maidens, O self-nourishing one; they anoint Mitra, well-placed, as with rays of light, when you unite the married couple in one mind.

**5.3.3**  For your splendor the Maruts cleanse themselves, O Rudra, for your birth is beautiful and variegated; where Viṣṇu's highest step was placed, with that you protect the secret name of the cows.

**5.3.4**  By your splendor, O god of beautiful appearance, the gods holding many things attended the immortal; the eager ones, honoring the praises of Āyu, placed Agni as Hotṛ for the son of Manu.

**5.3.5**  No Hotṛ earlier than you, O Agni, is more worthy of sacrifice; no self-nourishing one surpasses you in poetic wisdom; and the clans whose guest you become — that one will win mortals by his sacrifice, O god.

**5.3.6**  May we, O Agni, aided by you, win, desiring wealth, awakened through the oblation; may we prevail in battle and in the contests of the days; may we, O son of strength, prevail over mortals with riches.

**5.3.7**  He who brings sin and transgression against us — let guilt rest upon the evil-speaking one; smite, O knowing Agni, this curse — him who injures us with duplicity.

**5.3.8**  The ancients at the dawning of this day sacrificed to you with oblations, making you their messenger, O god; when you go, O Agni, toward the assembly of riches, kindled by mortals with precious fuel — a god.

**5.3.9**  Protect the father, fight, O knowing son who bears you, O child of strength; when, O knowing Agni, will you look upon us? When will you, the knower of Ṛta, requite justly?

**5.3.10**  He who worships acquires many names, O bright Vasu, if you delight in that; will Agni, delighting in the god's strength and waxing great, win favor?

**5.3.11**  You indeed, O youngest Agni, carry the praiser across all difficulties; the thieves and the enemy peoples have been seen — those of unknown intent have become crooked.

**5.3.12**  These paths have come toward you, O Vasu, or that sin has been uttered; this Agni here does not harm us for the curse, nor, waxing great, does he give us over to injury.

**5.4.7**  May we serve you, O Agni, with recitations; may we serve you with oblations, O purifier, O auspiciously blazing one; send together for us riches containing all good things; place all treasures in us.

**5.4.8**  Be pleased, O Agni, with our rite, O son of strength, O dweller in three stations, with our oblation; may we be well-doers among the gods; protect us with your triple shelter.

**5.4.9**  O Jātavedas, carry us across all hard passages and all evils as a boat crosses a river; O Agni, praised with reverence like Atri, be the protector of our bodies.

**5.4.10**  I, a mortal, invoking you repeatedly, contemplating you the immortal with a praising heart — O Jātavedas, place fame in us; may I, O Agni, attain immortality through offspring.

**5.4.11**  For whom you make an easy world, O Jātavedas, O Agni, for the well-doer — that one attains with good fortune wealth rich in horses, in sons, in heroes, and in cattle.

**5.5.3**  Being invoked, O Agni, bring hither brilliant Indra, the dear one, on his pleasant chariots for our aid.

**5.5.7**  On the path of the wind, the two divine Hotṛs invoked by the son of Manu — come to this our sacrifice.

**5.5.8**  Iḷā, Sarasvatī, and great Mahī — three goddesses born of bliss — may they, unfailing, take their seat on the sacred grass.

**5.5.9**  O auspicious Tvaṣṭar, come hither — all-pervading nourishment in person; support us at every sacrifice.
