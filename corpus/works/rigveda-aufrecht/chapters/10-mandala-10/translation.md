**10.1.1**  Tall, he stood foremost at the dawns, risen up; having driven away the darkness he came with light. Agni, with his own bright, shining radiance, newborn, has filled all dwellings.

**10.1.2**  Born as the embryo of Heaven and Earth, O Agni, cherished and lovely among the plants, a wondrous child, crying aloud above his mothers, he passed beyond the darkness and the night-seasons.

**10.1.3**  Viṣṇu knowing well his highest station thus guards the third realm where he was born, great. When they fashioned their own nourishment from his mouth, those of one mind came thither to adore him.

**10.1.4**  Those who bore him with fuel, his mothers, move toward him grown strong with food, with offerings. He returns to them again in another form; thou art the Hotṛ among the human tribes.

**10.1.5**  The Hotṛ of the sacrifice, driver of the bright chariot, the banner of every rite, gleaming bright, matching in greatness every god and every god — I praise Agni, the honoured guest of men.

**10.1.6**  Wearing his beautiful, variegated garments, Agni stands at the navel of the earth. Ruddy, born at the station of Iḷā, as Purohita, O King, worship here the gods.

**10.1.7**  For thou, O Agni, ever stretchest out toward both Heaven and Earth as a son toward his two mothers. Go forward to those who laud thee, O youngest god; then bring the gods hither, O powerful one.

**10.2.1**  Gladden the gods who long for it, O youngest, knowing the seasons, O Lord of the Seasons, sacrifice here. Among the divine officiants, O Agni, thou art the most skilled in sacrifice among the Hotṛs.

**10.2.2**  Thou seekest the Hotṛ office and also the Potṛ office among men; thou art the Mandhātṛ, the giver of riches, keeper of holy Order. With Svāhā let us make our oblations; let divine Agni, worthy, sacrifice to the gods.

**10.2.3**  We have come upon the path of the gods; what we are able we will carry forward along it. Agni, the all-knowing, let him sacrifice, for he is the Hotṛ; it is he who orders the rites and the seasons.

**10.2.4**  If we have violated your ordinances, O gods, less knowing than those who know, Agni, all-knowing, fills all that up, he by whose seasonal rites he orders the gods.

**10.2.5**  What mortal men of simple mind and feeble understanding do not comprehend of the sacrifice — that Agni as Hotṛ, knowing the rites, most skilled in worship, understands, and he worships the gods in due order.

**10.2.6**  For the begetter begat thee as the splendid face and bright banner of all rites. Worship, bringing bounty over all lands, bringing desirable refreshments that strengthen, benefits for all peoples.

**10.2.7**  Thee whom Heaven and Earth begat, thee whom the Waters begat, thee whom Tvaṣṭṛ, the well-shaping craftsman, begat — knowing well the ancestral path, O Agni, blazing bright, shine forth resplendently.

**10.3.1**  The mighty Aratin, O King, blazing up, terrible in power, with skill, was seen in beauty. He shines forth conspicuously with his great radiance; driving away the dark one he moves toward the bright.

**10.3.2**  When by his form he overwhelmed the dark doe-antelope, begetting the daughter of the great Father, the glowing woman — holding up the sun's bright beam on high, Aratin shines forth with the treasures of heaven.

**10.3.3**  The auspicious one, accompanied by the auspicious one, has come; the lover approaches his sister from behind. Spreading out with his well-marked, brilliant days, Agni with his shining colors has stood facing the darkness.

**10.3.4**  His coursings, like the roarings of the great one, kindling the fire of the auspicious friend — of the praiseworthy bull, the great one who roars well — his flaming blazes, his night-gleams, shine in his going.

**10.3.5**  His blazes roar like winds as he shines, the great one who illumines beautiful days; he who with his greatest, sharpest, most playful, most lofty radiances reaches even the sky.

**10.3.6**  His gusts, of him who spreads out widely as he puffs and roars with his steeds — he who with his ancient gleaming, most divine, resonant radiances shines throughout as Aratin.

**10.3.7**  Carry the great one to us and sit with us, O Aratin, between the two young ones, Heaven and Earth. Let Agni, swift, with his swift, vigorous horses, come hither with vigor.

**10.4.1**  I worship thee, I send forth praise to thee, that thou mayst be worthy of honor in our invocations. Like a watering-place in the desert thou art, O Agni, for Pūru who desires to sacrifice, O ancient King.

**10.4.2**  Thee, whom men surround as cows approach a warm stall, O youngest one, thou art the messenger between the gods and mortals; thou roamest in between as the great one, through the shining realm.

**10.4.3**  As a mother cherishes her noble child and makes him thrive, tending him carefully, thou descendest swiftly from the bow, O eager one, and streakest forth like a loosened animal.

**10.4.4**  We are foolish and do not know, O wise Agni; thou thyself knowest thy own greatness well. He lies concealed, yet roams, eating with his tongue, and licks the young woman, being the lord of the folk.

**10.4.5**  He is born ever-new among the ancient ones; he stands in the forest grey-haired, smoke-bannered. Like a bull whose thirst is unquenched he moves forward; him whom men of one mind lead on.

**10.4.6**  Like thieves who hazard their bodies, the two who roam through the forest, have clothed themselves with ten ropes. Here, O Agni, is a newer hymn for thee; yoke thy chariot as it were with gleaming limbs.

**10.4.7**  May this prayer and this reverence and this song be ever a source of increase to thee, O Jātavedas. Guard for us, O Agni, our children and offspring; protect our bodies without ceasing.

**10.5.1**  The one ocean, foundation of riches, with many births, surveys us from our hearts. He clings to the udder in the lap of the hidden waters; the station of the bird is set in the midst of the fountain.

**10.5.2**  Clad in the same abode, the bulls have come together with the mares. The poets guard the station of Holy Order; they have placed the hidden names beyond.

**10.5.3**  The two, possessed of holy order and of magic power, hold together; having met, they begat the child and nourish it — unwinding with the mind even the thread of the moving and the stationary, the navel of all the world.

**10.5.4**  For the tracks of Holy Order attend upon the well-born one, upon the impulses that have come long ago, for vigor. Wearing their garment, the two worlds when praised increase together with ghee, with food, with sweetnesses.

**10.5.5**  Eager, knowing, he has loudly called the seven ruddy sisters and raised them up with honey to be seen. He who was born of old, spanning the mid-air, seeking, found the hiding-place of Pūṣan.

**10.5.6**  The poets fashioned seven boundaries; to one of these alone the wicked one approached. Standing in the nest of the highest of Āyu, at the branching of the paths, on the firm foundations.

**10.5.7**  Both the Non-existent and the Existent are in the highest firmament, in the birth of Dakṣa, in the lap of Aditi. Agni indeed is our firstborn of Holy Order, the bull and the cow in an earlier age of existence.

**10.6.1**  This is he in whose shelter and with whose aid the praiser kindles the fire and wins his wish. He who, surrounded, goes about the seers with his greatest radiances, the far-shining one.

**10.6.2**  He who, far-shining, shines with his radiances, Agni, with the gods, keeper of holy order, unceasing, he who has won friendship from his friends, not turning aside, like a swift steed.

**10.6.3**  He who commands all divine refreshment, who commands all life at the brightening of Dawn — in whose fire, all-sustaining, Ariṣṭaratha sets the oblations in mind, with his praises.

**10.6.4**  Increased by praises, delighting in the hymns, swift-flying he goes toward the gods. That joyful Hotṛ with his ladle, most skilled in sacrifice, Agni who mingles with them, draws the gods near.

**10.6.5**  Glorify with songs and reverences that gleaming Agni who trembles like Indra, whom the inspired seers praise with their thoughts — Jātavedas, wielder of the oblation-ladle, who bears the burden.

**10.6.6**  In whom all good things have assembled, as horses with swift steeds in the race — turn hither to us, O Agni, the aids that are most like those of Indra.

**10.6.7**  For indeed, O Agni, by thy greatness, sitting present, straightway when born thou wert fit to be invoked. The gods followed thy will in that; the primal helpers then grew strong.

**10.7.1**  Grant us, O Agni, welfare from sky and earth; grant us all life for the act of worship, O god. May we attend upon thy discernments, O wonder-worker; protect us, O god, with your broad protections.

**10.7.2**  These thoughts, O Agni, born for thee, praise the bounty with cows and horses. When a mortal follows after thy gift, O Vasu, bearing thy praises, O well-born one.

**10.7.3**  I think of Agni as a father, I think of Agni as a kinsman, I think of Agni as ever a brother and friend. I worship the face of great Agni — bright, adorable, dwelling in the sun in the sky.

**10.7.4**  Efficacious prayers that gain spoils are ours, O Agni, for him whom thou protectest at home, O everlasting Hotṛ. That keeper of holy order, red-horsed, rich in rain, may he have good fortune through his days and nights.

**10.7.5**  With day-gifts set, like a friend useful for service, the ancient ritual priest, the lusty lover of the rite — the descendants of Āyu with their arms generated Agni and seated him as Hotṛ among the peoples.

**10.7.6**  Sacrifice thou thyself to the gods in heaven, O god; what can the simple-minded, thoughtless one accomplish for thee? As thou hast sacrificed to the gods in the seasons, O god, so sacrifice, O well-born one, for thyself.

**10.7.7**  Be our protector, O Agni, and our guardian; be our strengthener of vigor and our nourisher. And grant us the gift of oblation-bearing, O greatly good one; protect us and our bodies without ceasing.

**10.8.1**  Agni moves forward with his great banner; the bull roars filling the two worlds. He has reached even the highest limits of the sky; the great one has grown mighty in the lap of the waters.

**10.8.2**  The crested bull, as the embryo, rejoiced; the calf, never resting, powerful, cried aloud. He, performing deeds surpassing those of the gods, goes first to his own abodes.

**10.8.3**  He who has seized upon the head of his two parents — at the rite the flood of the sun has been laid down. On his flight, the ruddy mares rooted in horses delight in the womb of holy order, in their own forms.

**10.8.4**  For, O Vasu, thou goest to the front of each dawn; thou wast the far-shining presence of the two Yamas. Thou settest seven stations for holy order, engendering Mitra for thine own self.

**10.8.5**  Thou hast become the eye of the great Ṛta, the guardian; thou hast become Varuṇa when thou seekest holy order. Thou hast become the Son of the Waters, O Jātavedas; thou hast become the messenger of him whose oblation thou dost favor.

**10.8.6**  Thou hast become the leader of the sacrifice and of the expanse, where thou art present with thy benevolent teams. Thou hast set thy head in heaven, winning the sun; O Agni, thou hast made thy tongue the bearer of the oblation.

**10.8.7**  Trita, within the cave, by the will of this one, seeking the intelligence, the tracks of the far Father, being helped, in the lap of his two parents, calling upon his kin, goes to fetch weapons.

**10.8.8**  Knowing the paternal weapons, spurred by Indra, the Waters' son fought against the enemy. Having slain the three-headed one of the seven rays, Trita released even the cattle of Tvaṣṭṛ's son.

**10.8.9**  Much did Indra surpass the rising might; the lord of the good overwhelmed the proud one. Even of Tvaṣṭṛ's son Viśvarūpa, while seizing his cattle, he cut away the three heads.

**10.9.1**  O Waters, you are truly sources of well-being; grant us strength and vigor for great joy and to behold the great.

**10.9.2**  Of your most auspicious essence grant us a share here, like eager mothers.

**10.9.3**  Let us hasten to you, for whose abode you quicken us; O Waters, generate us.

**10.9.4**  May the divine Waters be auspicious for our protection, for our drinking; may they flow on us for well-being and happiness.

**10.9.5**  Ruling over precious things, presiding over men — I ask the Waters for healing.

**10.9.6**  Within the waters Soma has declared to me all remedies, and Agni, who brings welfare to all.

**10.9.7**  O Waters, provide the healing balm, the protection for my body, so that I may long look upon the sun.

**10.9.8**  O Waters, carry away whatever evil is in me, whatever wrong I may have done, or whatever curse or falsehood.

**10.9.9**  I have gone today with the Waters; we have mingled with their essence. O Agni, rich in milk, come hither; unite me with splendor.

**10.10.1**  Yamī speaks: I would surely turn a friend toward friendship, though he has passed over many floods. The ordainer should establish us as the grandson's line of the Father, thinking of further offspring upon the earth.

**10.10.2**  Yama speaks: Thy friend does not wish for this friendship — that she who is of the same sign should become of a different form. The heroic sons of the great Asura, who uphold the sky, look out over the wide world.

**10.10.3**  Yamī speaks: Indeed the immortals desire this, that progeny of this one mortal might arise. Let thy mind be set in my mind; a husband should enter the body of his wife.

**10.10.4**  Yama speaks: What we have not done before, how should we do it now? We who speak of holy order — should we speak falsehood? Gandharva is in the waters and the watery maiden — she is our highest tie of kinship, she is our utmost bond.

**10.10.5**  Yamī speaks: Surely the divine Tvaṣṭṛ, Savitṛ the all-shaped, will make us husband and wife in the womb. None violates his ordinances; both Earth and Heaven know of this for us.

**10.10.6**  Yama speaks: Who knows of the first day? Who has seen it? Who would proclaim it here? Great is the ordinance of Mitra and Varuṇa. What would you say, O Yamī, to men with impunity?

**10.10.7**  Yamī speaks: The longing for Yama has come upon Yamī, to lie together in the same womb. As a wife opens her body to her husband — I would spread apart like the wheels of a chariot.

**10.10.8**  Yama speaks: These spies of the gods who wander here do not stand still, they do not close their eyes. Go quickly elsewhere, Yamī, not to me; spread apart with him like the wheels of a chariot.

**10.10.9**  Yamī speaks: With nights and with days she should serve him; let the eye of the sun open again and again. May Yamī, twin-born, related as kin to Yama, bear what is non-kin between Heaven and Earth.

**10.10.10**  Yama speaks: Later ages shall come when those of the same kin will do what is not kin. O fortunate one, stretch out thy arm to the bull; seek thyself another husband, not me.

**10.10.11**  Yamī speaks: What use is a brother if she remains without refuge? What use is a sister when Nirṛti comes upon her? Smitten by longing I speak out much of this: unite thy body with my body.

**10.10.12**  Yama speaks: I shall not unite my body with thy body. They call it evil when a man goes to his sister. Arrange for thyself pleasures with another, not me; thy brother, O fortunate one, does not desire this.

**10.10.13**  Yamī speaks: Alas, alas, O Yama — we have not found out either thy mind or thy heart. Surely some other woman clasps thee as it were a girth on a yoked horse, as a creeper embraces a tree.

**10.10.14**  Yama speaks: Let another clasp thee, Yamī, and let another clasp him as a creeper clasps a tree. Seek his mind, or let him seek thine; make a good compact together.

**10.11.1**  The bull-like one has milked out the milk-giving draughts of heaven for the bull-like one; the swift, inviolable son of Aditi. He knows all things even as Varuṇa knows through insight — let him, worthy of worship, perform sacrifice at the proper seasons.

**10.11.2**  May the Gandharva's consort and the water-maiden [Apsarās] guard my mind amid the roar of the stream. May Aditi establish us in the midst of the desired [realm]; our eldest brother, the foremost, shall proclaim it.

**10.11.3**  Verily, glorious, splendid and luminous was the dawn that shone for Manu, rich in light. When the eager ones, following the will of the willing one, brought forth Agni as hotar for the assembly.

**10.11.4**  Then the falcon, impelled [by the gods], brought hither that far-reaching, all-seeing drop [of Soma] to the sacrifice. When the Āryan clans chose the wondrous Agni as their hotar, then was wisdom born.

**10.11.5**  Thou art ever joyful, O Agni, and flourishest like barley; the pious man's sacrifice proceeds well through thy priestly invocations. When the inspired sage, after his toil, brings thee the hymn-worthy praise, thou approachest [him] with abundant food-prizes won.

**10.11.6**  The lover stirs up the two parents [Heaven and Earth] and desires to offer to Bhaga; the beloved one hastens, longing in his heart. The carrier [Agni] speaks, the active one is alert; the mighty asura [Agni] grows strong, trembling with inspired thought.

**10.11.7**  That mortal, O Agni, son of strength, who wins thy benevolence — he is heard beyond all others. Bearing food and borne along on horses, he, brilliant and mighty, adorns his days.

**10.11.8**  When, O Agni, this divine assembly gathers among the gods, worthy of worship, O worshipful one — and when thou distributest the treasures according to thy nature — may our share here be rich in wealth.

**10.11.9**  Hear us, O Agni, in the assembly, in the gathering-place; yoke thy chariot swift as the immortal. Bring hither to us Heaven and Earth, divine children; may thou never depart from the gods' company here.

**10.12.1**  Heaven and Earth, the primal pair, are renowned through Order [ṛta] and are truthful of speech. When the god [Agni], making mortals ready for sacrifice, sat down as hotar, proceeding forward, guarding his own vital force.

**10.12.2**  The god who surpasses the gods through Order — first knowing all, bear our oblation. Smoke-bannered, blazing bright with fuel, the joyful hotar, eternal, most skilled in speech and sacrifice.

**10.12.3**  The divine immortal [Agni] is the self-yoker; when born from the cow [fire-sticks], they sustained the two broad [worlds]. All the gods followed after that sacrificial formula of thine when the bright [cow of heaven] milked out the divine ghee.

**10.12.4**  I praise you two, O ghee-streaming waters, for your increase; O Heaven and Earth, O Rodasī — hear me. In the days when the heavens brought the vital breath's dispensation, may the two parents [Heaven and Earth] sharpen us here with sweetness.

**10.12.5**  What has our king taken? In what have we transgressed his ordinance? Who knows fully? Even Mitra, though invoked, [may be] cheated among gods — yet may the call go forth; there still is prize-wealth.

**10.12.6**  Here let him reconsider the name of the immortal [Agni] — that which, of equal mark, takes manifold forms. He who meditates well on Yama's [order] — protect him, O tall Agni, without ceasing in vigilance.

**10.12.7**  In whom the gods rejoice at the assembly, holding [their seats] in the dwelling of Vivasvat — in the sun they placed the light; in the moon the nights; the two luminaries circle unceasingly.

**10.12.8**  In that hidden thought wherein the gods wander together — we, for our part, know it not. May Mitra here [declare us] sinless, and Aditi; may divine Savitṛ speak to Varuṇa on our behalf.

**10.12.9**  Hear us, O Agni, in the assembly, in the gathering-place; yoke thy chariot swift as the immortal. Bring hither to us Heaven and Earth, divine children; may thou never depart from the gods' company here.

**10.13.1**  I yoke you two with the ancient sacred word, with reverences; may the hymn go forth like the sage's [chant] along its path. May all the sons of the immortal hear, even those who have taken up their stations in the divine realms.

**10.13.2**  When you two came forth as the two striving [like a pair of twins], men devoted to the gods brought oblations to you. Take your own place, you who know it well — be a good seat for Indu [Soma] among us.

**10.13.3**  I have mounted the five-footed [gāyatrī]; I follow the four-footed [triṣṭubh] according to sacred law. By the syllable I measure this [speech]; at the navel of Order [ṛta] I purify it together.

**10.13.4**  For the gods he [Yama] chose death; for progeny he did not choose immortality. They made Bṛhaspati the sacrificial ṛṣi; Yama yielded up his beloved body [to death].

**10.13.5**  The seven [streams] flow for the young Marut-accompanied one; the sons, for the father, have set Order [ṛta] in motion. Both [Heaven and Earth] reign over both aspects [of existence]; both strive and both nourish all.

**10.14.1**  Honour with oblation Yama, the king, son of Vivasvat — who has gone forth along the mighty slopes, and has explored the path for many, the gathering-place of peoples.

**10.14.2**  Yama first discovered the way for us; this pasture cannot be taken away. Where our ancient Fathers went forth, there, born along that same path, all beings follow their own paths.

**10.14.3**  Mātali grew strong with the Kavyas; Yama with the Aṅgirases; Bṛhaspati with those who chant the Ṛk. Those whom the gods exalted and those who exalted the gods — some rejoice with svāhā, others with svadhā.

**10.14.4**  Come, O Yama, sit upon this sacred grass together with the Aṅgiras Fathers, in accord with them. May the seer-proclaimed mantras bring thee hither; with this oblation, O king, be gladdened.

**10.14.5**  Come hither, O Yama, with the Aṅgirases worthy of worship, of varied forms; rejoice here. I call Vivasvat, thy father — having sat down on the sacred grass at this sacrifice.

**10.14.6**  The Aṅgirases are our Fathers — the nine-fold, the Atharvans, the Bhṛgus, the Soma-drinkers. May we abide in the good favour of those worthy of sacrifice, in their auspicious benevolence.

**10.14.7**  Go forth, go forth on the ancient paths along which our ancient Fathers departed. There thou shalt behold the two kings rejoicing in the oblation — Yama and the god Varuṇa.

**10.14.8**  Unite with the Fathers, unite with Yama and with [the reward of] sacrifices performed and pious works, in the highest heaven. Leaving behind all imperfection, return again to home; unite with thy body, radiant.

**10.14.9**  Depart, withdraw, and disperse from this [place] — the Fathers have made this world for him. With days, with waters, with nights illuminated — Yama bestows a resting-place upon him.

**10.14.10**  Run past the two dogs of Saramā, the four-eyed, brindled ones, by the good path. Then approach the gracious Fathers who feast together with Yama in joy.

**10.14.11**  Thy two dogs, O Yama — the guardians, four-eyed, watchers of the road, observers of men — commit this man to their keeping, O king; grant him welfare and freedom from disease.

**10.14.12**  Wide-nostrilled, life-drinking, fig-tree-coloured, the two messengers of Yama roam among men. May those two grant us today here to see the sun again — may they restore vital breath and blessings.

**10.14.13**  Press Soma for Yama, pour the oblation for Yama. The sacrifice goes to Yama, well-prepared, with Agni as its messenger.

**10.14.14**  Pour the ghee-rich oblation for Yama and step forward. May he bring us [the blessing] among the gods — long life for us to live.

**10.14.15**  Pour the sweetest oblation-offering for Yama the king. This homage [goes] to the ancient ṛṣis, the first-born, the first path-makers.

**10.14.16**  By the three Soma-pressings he [Yama] flies [encompasses] the six wide [worlds]; one alone is the Vast. Triṣṭubh, Gāyatrī — all the metres are established in Yama.

**10.15.1**  Let the lower Fathers rise up, and the higher, and the middle Soma-drinking Fathers. Those who have gone to life, the harmless, knowing Order — may those Fathers aid us at our invocations.

**10.15.2**  Let there be homage today to the Fathers, those who departed first and those who departed later. Those settled in the earthly realm of dust, or those now among well-ordered communities.

**10.15.3**  I have found the gracious Fathers, and Viṣṇu's grandson and his stride. Those who sit upon the sacred grass, who share by svadhā in the pressed [Soma] — may they come most swiftly here.

**10.15.4**  O Fathers seated on the sacred grass, come hither to us with your help; enjoy these oblations we have made for you. Come with the most blessed aid; then grant us health and freedom from affliction.

**10.15.5**  The Soma-drinking Fathers have been called upon, at the dear sacred-grass resting places. Let them come, let them hear here; let them speak and protect us.

**10.15.6**  Bending the right knee, sitting down, praise this sacrifice, all of you. O Fathers, harm us not for any sin that we, being but men, may have committed against you.

**10.15.7**  Sitting in the lap of the ruddy [dawn clouds], bestow wealth upon the mortal who gives. O Fathers, pass on to his sons the riches that are his; establish here for him vigour and strength.

**10.15.8**  Those ancient Soma-drinking Fathers of ours, the most excellent, who followed the Soma-drinking [rite] — may Yama, rejoicing with them, eat the oblations at his pleasure, himself eager with the eager ones.

**10.15.9**  Those who thirsted [for the divine], striving toward the gods, knowing the priestly arts, shaping hymns of praise with [sacred] songs — O Agni, come hither with the gracious [Fathers], with the true, wise, cauldron-seated Fathers.

**10.15.10**  Those who are true, eaters of oblation, drinkers of oblation, riding the same chariot with Indra and the gods — O Agni, come with a thousand praised by the gods, with the later and the earlier Fathers seated at the cauldron.

**10.15.11**  O Fathers tasted by Agni, come hither; well-guided, take your seats at each seat. Eat the oblations offered on the sacred grass; then bestow upon us wealth with all heroes.

**10.15.12**  Thou, O Agni, invoked as Jātavedas, having made the oblations fragrant, didst carry them [to the Fathers]. Thou gavest [them] to the Fathers by the svadhā [call]; they ate — do thou, O god, eat the presented oblations.

**10.15.13**  Those Fathers who are here and those who are not here, those whom we know and those whom we do not know — thou, O Jātavedas, knowest how many they are; enjoy the well-performed sacrifice with the svadhā-offerings.

**10.15.14**  Those [Fathers] burned by Agni, those not burned by Agni, who rejoice in the midst of heaven with the svadhā oblation — with those, O self-ruling one, fashion this vital dispensation for the body according to [thy] will.

**10.16.1**  O Agni, do not burn him entirely, do not scorch him; do not consume his skin nor his body. When thou hast made him done [cooked], O Jātavedas, then send him forth to the Fathers.

**10.191.1**  Together, altogether you kindle all things, O bull, O Agni, O Lord; on the seat of Iḷā you are kindled together — bring to us all good things.

**10.191.2**  Come together, speak together; let your minds be in agreement. As the gods of old, in concord, partook of their appointed share — so may you.

**10.191.3**  Common is the counsel, common the assembly, common the mind, and joined together the intention of these; with a common counsel I address you all; with a common oblation I make the offering to you.

**10.191.4**  Common be your purpose; common your hearts; let your mind be as one — so that all may go well for you together.
