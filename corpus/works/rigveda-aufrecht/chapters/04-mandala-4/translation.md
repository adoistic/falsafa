**4.1.1**  Thee, O Agni, the gods of one accord have ever sent as their divine messenger — by their will they sent thee forth. The immortal one established among mortals, the divine and all-knowing Ādeva — they generated thee, the all-wise Pracetas, the divine Pracetas.

**4.1.2**  O Agni, bring hither thy brother Varuṇa together with the gods, by thy goodwill — him who is fond of sacrifice, the eldest, who delights in sacrifice. Bring the truth-bearing Āditya, upholder of peoples, the king who upholds the tribes.

**4.1.3**  As a friend, turn thy friend hither, as a wheel on the chariot-track hastens swiftly — hasten toward us, O wondrous one. O Agni, find grace for us together with Varuṇa, among the Maruts of universal splendor. Grant bliss to our offspring and strength, O blazing one — grant us welfare, O wondrous one, grant us welfare.

**4.1.4**  Thou, O Agni, knowing the wrath of god Varuṇa, shouldst turn it away from us. Thou who art most worthy of worship, best of conveyors, ever blazing — drive away all hatreds from us.

**4.1.5**  Be thou, O Agni, our lowest and nearest aid at the break of this dawn. Worship Varuṇa on our behalf and bestow grace — come to us with clemency and be easy to invoke.

**4.1.6**  The most excellent and most wonderful vision of this blessed god among mortals — pure like clarified butter heated up, like the munificence of the inviolable cow, desirable and divine.

**4.1.7**  Threefold are those supreme, true, and desirable births of divine Agni. He comes enclosed within the limitless, blazing, bright, noble, and radiantly gleaming.

**4.1.8**  He is the messenger who pervades every dwelling, the Hotṛ with a golden chariot and a tongue of copper, with red horses, beautiful in form, of great light — ever joyous, the assembly of which is like one rich in food.

**4.1.9**  Awakening men — this bond of sacrifice — they lead him forth by great thongs. Achieving his goal among the houses of his worshiper, the divine one attained partnership with the mortal.

**4.1.10**  May Agni, knowing the way, lead us toward that treasure appointed by the gods. That which all the immortals fashioned by thought, that which Father Dyaus, the begetter, made true and watered.

**4.1.11**  He was born first in the households, at the base of great heaven, in the womb of this aerial space — footless and headless, hiding at the extremities, stretching within the nest of the Bull.

**4.1.12**  First among those worthy of praise he arose from the womb of Ṛta, in the nest of the Bull — desirable, youthful, beautiful, and of great radiance. Seven beloved ones generated him for the Bull.

**4.1.13**  Here our fathers, the human ones, pressed forward seeking after Ṛta. Calling upon the Dawns, they drove up the radiant cows locked within the stone-walled pen, the well-milking ones within the cavern.

**4.1.14**  Having shattered the rock they polished it; others among them declared this to all around. Without restraint they sang in praise of the work; finding the light they shaped it with their thoughts.

**4.1.15**  With cow-seeking mind they surrounded the mountain that was hard, compact, and enclosing the cows. With divine speech the men, the Uśijas, opened up the firm cattle-pen full of kine.

**4.1.16**  They first thought of the name of the cow; they found the three-times-seven highest names of the mother. Knowing these, the companies sang after them; the glorious red Dawn-cow became manifest.

**4.1.17**  Darkness was dispelled, heaven shone; the light of the divine Dawns arose. The Sun stood upon the broad meadows, seeing the straight and the crooked among mortals.

**4.1.18**  Thereupon, awakening, they beheld each other; thereupon they held the heaven-given treasure. May all the gods in all the dwellings — O Mitra, O Varuṇa — let truth be in our thought.

**4.1.19**  I would speak praise toward blazing Agni, the Hotṛ, the all-sustaining, the most worthy of worship — whose bright udder the cows did not consume, whose pressed Soma was not drunk, like that poured around the filter.

**4.1.20**  Aditi of all the worshipable beings, guest of all human beings — may Agni, winning the favor of the gods, become gracious to us, the all-knowing Jātavedas.

**4.2.1**  He who, the immortal truth-bearer, was established as the divine messenger among the gods — Agni, the Hotṛ, most worthy of worship, set among mortals to blaze magnificently, to be stirred with oblations.

**4.2.2**  Here today, O son of power, born between the two kindred, O Agni — thou goest as a lofty messenger having yoked the straight-membered, bull-like, bright horses.

**4.2.3**  Those exceeding, exhilarating, red, and ghee-dripping steeds I deem the swiftest by mind — belonging to Ṛta. Yoked with the red ones thou goest between you gods and the clans of mortals.

**4.2.4**  Bring Aryaman, Varuṇa, Mitra among these, Indra, Viṣṇu, the Maruts, and the Aśvins. O Agni, with good horses, fine chariot, and excellent gifts, do convey them to the people of good oblations.

**4.2.5**  O Agni, let our sacrifice be possessed of kine, possessed of sheep, possessed of horses, friend to heroes, ever imperishable. O mighty one, may this wealth be rich in refreshment, in offspring, long-lasting, broad-based, and fit for the assembly.

**4.2.6**  He who brings thee fuel perspiring, or scorches his head on account of thee — become thou his mighty guardian, O Agni, and protect him from every evil-doer.

**4.2.7**  He who brings thee food — even food desired — sharpening, arousing the joyous guest; the god-seeking one who kindles thee in his home — in him may wealth be steadfast and may he be a giver.

**4.2.8**  He who praises thee at evening or at dawn, or who, possessing oblations, makes thee a beloved offering — like a horse with golden trappings in its own stable, carry that worshiper beyond distress.

**4.2.9**  He who makes offering to thee, O immortal Agni, and performs service to thee with ladle extended — may he not be separated from wealth when he has striven; let no distress from the evil one encircle him.

**4.2.10**  He whose sacrifice thou, O Agni, hast enjoyed — the god bestowing well-placed boons on the mortal — may his Hotṛ-service be pleasing, O most youthful; may we be those whose worshiper prospers.

**4.2.11**  O all-knowing one, discern wisdom and unwisdom, separating mortals as if separating the clean from the crooked. For our prosperity and good offspring, O god, grant us bounty and Aditi, and protect us.

**4.2.12**  The undeceivable seers have instructed the seer, holding him firmly in the houses of life. Therefore, O Agni, mayst thou — visible — see those wondrous things with thy feet and thine own noble steps.

**4.2.13**  Thou, O Agni, art a good guide to the lauding poet who presses Soma and worships — O most youthful, thou who art dear to peoples. Bring a wide and shining treasure to the striving one for his protection.

**4.2.14**  For when we have done this by thy aid, O Agni, with feet, hands, and bodies — like those who equip a chariot through the effort of both arms — the clear-minded ones, eagerly seeking, have grasped the truth.

**4.2.15**  Then may we seven inspired ones be born first from the mother Dawn, as creators of men. May we become sons of Heaven, Aṅgirases, and burst open the wealth-laden mountain while we shine.

**4.2.16**  Just as our fathers of old, O Agni, eagerly seeking Ṛta, went in purity to the inspiring thought — singing praises, shattering the earth, they drove forth the reddish cows.

**4.2.17**  Good workers of fine brilliance, desiring the gods, like iron, the gods blowing on their birth — shining, they magnified Agni and Indra, and came surrounding the vast cattle-pen.

**4.2.18**  As it looked upon the rich cattle-herd, when the mighty births of the gods appeared — even the Urvaśīs of mortals lamented, O noble one, for the upward course of life to grow.

**4.2.19**  We have become skilled workers through thy deeds; the dawns, shining forth, flowed with truth. Tending Agni, complete and widely brilliant, they polished the beautiful eye of the god.

**4.2.20**  These hymns, O Agni, O wise ordainer, we have spoken — enjoy them, O seer. Blaze up high, make us better; grant us great wealth, O thou of many gifts.

**4.3.1**  Seek ye the king of the sacrifice, Rudra, the Hotṛ, the true worshiper of the two worlds — Agni of golden form, before the crash of the unconscious thunder, for our protection.

**4.3.2**  This womb we have made for thee — as a wife, desirous and well-dressed, for her husband. Sit down here facing toward us, surrounded — these offerings face thee, O thou of beautiful radiance.

**4.3.3**  Pronounce the hymn for him who listens, the inviolate, the ordainer who sees with a hero's eyes, who is gracious — praise the immortal god, whom I, like a Soma-presser with pressing-stone, seek as the honey-squeezer.

**4.3.4**  Do thou, O Agni, O most kindly one, O wise truth-perceiver, be aware of this Ṛta. When shall thy hymns be those to be rejoiced in together? When shall thy friendships be in thy house?

**4.3.5**  O Agni, how wilt thou speak to Varuṇa? How dost thou reproach Heaven for our transgression? How wilt thou speak to the bounteous Mitra and to Earth? What wilt thou say to Aryaman, what to Bhaga?

**4.3.6**  O Agni, what wilt thou say, thriving in thy seats, to Vāta the wide-spread and beautiful? What wilt thou say to the all-wandering Nāsatya? What to Rudra who smites heroes, O Agni?

**4.3.7**  What wilt thou say to great Pūṣan, the nurturer? What to Rudra of beautiful face, who gives oblations? What wilt thou announce to wide-striding Viṣṇu? What, O Agni, to great Śarva?

**4.3.8**  Being questioned, what wilt thou say to the truth-bearing troop of Maruts, what to the great, mighty Sun? Speak back to swift Aditi duly, O Jātavedas, all-knowing one of heaven.

**4.3.9**  By Ṛta seek the fixed truth, the raw and the ripe together with the honey — of the cow, O Agni. Though black, she swelled with kindred milk by the white nourisher, this one.

**4.3.10**  For even the bull, anointed with Ṛta, masculine Agni, by the milk on his back, moved without trembling as life-sustainer; the bull milked the bright udder of Pṛśni.

**4.3.11**  Shattering the mountain by Ṛta, the Aṅgirases rejoiced together with the kine. The men of good fortune sat around the Dawn; the sun-world became manifest when Agni was kindled.

**4.3.12**  By Ṛta the divine, immortal, inviolate waters, with their honey-sweet floods, O Agni — like a prize horse in races, ever pressing forward to flow, they were ever ready.

**4.3.13**  Let us not always bear the deceitful man's burden; let us not injure the lodger or approach him. Let us not, O Agni, reap the debt of the dishonest brother; let us not share the cunning of a treacherous friend.

**4.3.14**  Protect us, O Agni, with thy protections, guarding us ceaselessly, O beautiful one, propitious. Kick back, smash the firm evil; slay the demon even though it has grown great.

**4.3.15**  Be gracious with these songs, O Agni — touch these prizes with hymns, O hero. And enjoy the prayers, O Aṅgiras; may thy praise, beloved of the gods, be kindled.

**4.3.16**  All these mysteries, O Agni, O ordainer, I have proclaimed to thee, the all-knowing — traditional sayings, secret words. I have sung poetic works fit for the seer, with thoughts, O inspired one, with hymns.

**4.4.1**  Make thy force broad as an extended track; go like a king powerful with his elephant. Raging along the fierce trail, be the archer — pierce the demons with thy hottest darts.

**4.4.2**  Thy circlings fly swiftly; blazing boldly, thou dost touch with thy tongue the burning things; O Agni, hurl the heat-sparks, and unconstrained, let loose firebrands in all directions.

**4.4.3**  Send forth thy scouts in all directions, swiftest one; be the unsupplanted guardian of this tribe. Whether the evil-speaker be far or near, O Agni, let no confusion dare to overpower thee.

**4.4.4**  Rise up, O Agni, spread thyself out and against them, O sharp-weaponed one — burn our enemies down. Whoever has created enmity against us, O enkindled one, burn him down below like dry tinder.

**4.4.5**  Stand upright, shoot past us, make the divine powers manifest, O Agni. Bend low the firm ones of the sorcerers; crush the related and unrelated enemies.

**4.4.6**  He knows thy favor, O most youthful, who has opened a path for the advancing brahman. To him may all the bright days of wealth and glories of the noble one shine through the doors.

**4.4.7**  May he, O Agni, be fortunate and bountiful who seeks to satisfy thee with regular oblation and with hymns in his own house and lifetime; may all bright days and his sacrifice be fulfilled for him.

**4.4.8**  I sing to call down thy favor; may this song be in accord with what thou desirest. With good horses and fine chariots may we honor thee; may thou sustain sovereignty in us through the days.

**4.4.9**  Here may much come near thee of thyself, shining in the evenings day by day. Playing, may we, well-disposed, worship thee, having attained the glories of the peoples.

**4.4.10**  He who comes to thee, O Agni, with good horses and fine gold, with a wealth-laden chariot — thou art his protector, thou art his friend — he who has continuously enjoyed thy hospitality.

**4.4.11**  I break the great kinship with words; that came to me from father Gotama. Do thou mark this our speech, O Hotṛ, most youthful, of pure will, O dweller in the house.

**4.4.12**  Unsleeping, swift, auspicious, unwearied, undevoured, untired — let those guardians, O Agni, sitting together toward the same goal, protect us, O wise one.

**4.4.13**  Those guardians of Māmateya — thine, O Agni — watching, they protected from evil, keeping the blind at bay. The all-knowing one preserved them, the doers of good; the enemies, though wishing to harm, did not deceive them.

**4.4.14**  Strengthened by thee, protected by thee, may we attain prizes by thy guidance. Pacify both praises, O thou of true nature — do this henceforth, O shameless one.

**4.4.15**  With this fuel, O Agni, we would worship thee — take hold of the praise being recited. Burn the sorcerers; protect us from the demons; O great Mitra, from the blame of the treacherous slanderer.

**4.5.1**  Together and in accord, how shall we make offering to Vaiśvānara, the bounteous, to Agni of great light? He has supported the bank like a prop with his undiminished, great vehicle.

**4.5.2**  Do not despise him who gave this gift to me, the mortal — the self-empowered god. Unto the simple one, the clever, immortal, all-knowing Vaiśvānara, most manly, young Agni, made it known.

**4.5.3**  Having the great, doubly strong Sāman-chant, sharp-edged, thousand-seeded, a mighty bull — knowing the hidden footprint of the cow, Agni spoke the inspiring thought to me.

**4.5.4**  Agni of sharp fangs, of excellent gifts, shall devour them with his hottest flame — those who infringe upon the domain of Varuṇa and the cherished, firm, perceiving laws of Mitra.

**4.5.5**  Like brotherless women straying, like women hostile to their husbands and of ill ways — being wicked, untruthful, and false, they have found this deep place.

**4.5.6**  O Agni, this thought of mine for the purifier, the lover — do not put it as a heavy burden. Thou hast boldly placed the great, deep, young back — nourish it with refreshment seven-fold.

**4.5.7**  May that same purifying thought, through the same will, attain to him from the same level.

**4.5.8**  What is to be uttered of this my speech? They speak of the hidden, the secret laid within. As they cover the outlet of the cows, the bird protects the dear, foremost station of its form.

**4.5.9**  This verily is the great, mighty face — that the primal cow accompanies the dawn-cows. Blazing over the station of Ṛta, the swiftly moving one perceived the swiftly flying one hidden.

**4.5.10**  Then the shining one sat beside his parents, following the beautiful secret of speckled Pṛśni. Near the supreme station of the mother-cow sat the tongue of the bull, of the blazing and exalted one.

**4.5.11**  Being questioned with reverence I speak the truth, O Jātavedas, by thy command if this be so: thou rulest over all that exists, that which is in heaven and that which is wealth upon earth.

**4.5.12**  What is our wealth in this, what indeed is the treasure? Declare it to us, O all-knowing Jātavedas. The highest point of the hidden path — of that empty station we did not come to the source.

**4.5.13**  What is the boundary, what the guiding marks? How shall we swift ones reach that which is beautiful, as if a prize? When shall the divine wives of the immortal, the Dawns, be stretched out with the color of the Sun?

**4.5.14**  With dustless, trifling, obvious, trembling, unsatisfied speech — what do they say here to thee, O Agni? Let the unarmed ones sit and follow their own ways.

**4.5.15**  For the beauty of this enkindled bull, the face of good Vasu shone in the house. Clothed in brightness, of beautiful form to behold — like a dwelling with wealth he gleamed, of many gifts.

**4.6.1**  Stand erect, O Hotṛ Agni, at our sacrifice, more worthy of worship in the divine assembly. For thou dost surpass all thought, and thou dost even overcome the wisdom of the wise ordainer.

**4.6.2**  The blameless Hotṛ Agni, the joyous, all-knowing one has taken his seat among the tribes. He has raised his light on high like Savitṛ; like a post he has propped the smoke up to heaven.

**4.6.3**  Wearing the good burnished one, the gift-giving, ghee-dripping one, the divine gathering moving to the right — the well-aimed, well-fitted one anoints the new-born, rafter-like sacrifice-post.

**4.6.4**  When the sacred grass is strewn and Agni is kindled, the Adhvaryu stands erect, delighting. Agni the Hotṛ, like a herdsman, goes around thrice, moving, wearing them from of old.

**4.6.5**  The Hotṛ Agni, measured in gait, goes around of himself, joyous, speaking sweetly, truth-bearing. His flames run like prize horses; all beings tremble when he blazes forth.

**4.6.6**  Auspicious is thy appearance, O Agni of beautiful face, though thy nature is terrible and varied. Neither does the darkness veil thy blazing light, nor do the scattered sparks put any taint upon thy body.

**4.6.7**  None has checked his conquest; neither father nor mother could hold him when he is desired. Then, like Mitra, well-settled and purifying, Agni shone among the human tribes.

**4.6.8**  Twice-five sisters dwelling together gave birth to Agni among the human tribes — him who wakens at dawn, sharp-toothed like Atharvan's tooth, bright, well-breathing, keen like an axe.

**4.6.9**  Thy golden-dripping, reddish steeds, O Agni, straight-going and well-going — the ruddy, bull-like, straight-membered, wondrous ones — they called to the divine gathering.

**4.6.10**  Those resistless, tireless, fierce, shining, roaming flames of thine, O Agni, march toward their goal — like hawks in service, with great noise, like the Marut-troop.

**4.6.11**  The prayer has been fashioned for thee, O enkindled one; he recites the hymn and worships, he has distributed. The Uśijas, the desiring ones, reverently placed Agni the Hotṛ of men, proclaiming the praise of life.

**4.7.1**  He was here established first by the establishers, the Hotṛ most worthy of worship at sacrifices, to be praised — him whom the Bhṛgus of Apnavāna made resound in the forests, the many-formed, the mighty one, in every tribe.

**4.7.2**  O Agni, when shall thy divine perception come hereafter in succession? Then mortals have seized upon thee, the praiseworthy one, among the tribes.

**4.7.3**  Looking upon the truth-bearing, all-knowing one as at the star-studded sky — the one who will complete all sacrifices, in every house.

**4.7.4**  The swift messenger of Vivasvat who surpasses all peoples — the descendants of Āyu, the Bhṛgavāṇas, seized the beacon, in every tribe.

**4.7.5**  Him, the perceiving Hotṛ, they have seated in unbroken succession — the joyous, purely-blazing, most worthy of worship one, with his seven stations.

**4.7.6**  Him, wrapped in eternal mothers, set in the wood, not touching the ground — the many-formed one, hidden in the secret place, well-known, and sought from somewhere.

**4.7.7**  When the gods made sport in the same udder of the two companions, at the station of Ṛta — great Agni, the truth-bearer, invited with oblations by reverence, is ever the bird of the sacrifice.

**4.7.8**  Knowing the missions of the bird of the sacrifice, perceiving both extremities of the two worlds together — thou goest as messenger from of old, wearing them — more knowing are the ascents of heaven.

**4.7.9**  Thy black trail and the bright light in front — the moving flame, indeed the one form of forms; when the inciting ones establish the embryo, thou art straightway born and immediately art the messenger.

**4.7.10**  The strength of him newly born became visible when the wind blows following his flame. He sharpens his sharp tongue in the dry lands; he splits even firm foods with his jaws.

**4.7.11**  Since he grew on dry foods with the dry — young Agni makes a swift messenger of the swift.

**4.8.1**  With song I adorn the messenger, the all-knowing conveyor of oblations, the immortal, the most worthy of worship.

**4.8.2**  For he knows the treasure-trove of wealth, the great ascent to heaven; he will convey the gods hither.

**4.8.3**  He, the divine one, knows the bowing of the gods, for the truth-seeker in his house; he gives even beloved goods.

**4.8.4**  He is the Hotṛ and he perceiving the duty of the messenger, goes between, knowing the ascent to heaven.

**4.8.5**  May we be among those who have presented to Agni with gift-giving oblations — those who kindle him and make him thrive.

**4.8.6**  They, having won both wealth and heroic strength, became renowned — those who placed their service in Agni.

**4.8.7**  May riches much-desired come to us day by day; may prizes be roused to us.

**4.8.8**  He, the inspired one of the peoples, with the might of men, pierces through like an arrow.

**4.9.1**  O Agni, be gracious — thou art mighty, thou who comest to the god-seeking person who approaches the sacred grass to sit upon it.

**4.9.2**  Hard to overcome among human tribes, the immortal protector — may he become the messenger of all.

**4.9.3**  He is led around the dwelling, the joyous Hotṛ, at the celestial ceremonies; and he sits down as the Potṛ.

**4.9.4**  And Agni is the divine lady's companion at the sacrifice, and the lord of the house in the home; and he sits down as the Brahman-priest.

**4.9.5**  For thou art the speaker on behalf of those performing the sacrifice, and of the people; and of the oblations of humans.

**4.9.6**  He fulfills the duty of messenger for him whose sacrifice thou hast enjoyed — to carry the mortal's oblation.

**4.9.7**  Enjoy our sacrifice, our worship, O Aṅgiras; hear our call.

**4.9.8**  May thy hard-to-conquer chariot encompass us on all sides — that with which thou protectest the worshiper.

**4.10.1**  O Agni, today let us attain through songs that will-power touching the heart, auspicious as a horse — may we thrive through thy invocations.

**4.10.2**  For, O Agni, thou hast become the charioteer of the auspicious will-power, of the good discernment, of the great truth.

**4.10.3**  With these songs be turned toward us, O Agni — like the sun-light, with all thy faces, well-disposed.

**4.10.4**  Praising thee today with these songs, O Agni, may we make offerings. Thy vigors thunder forth like those of heaven.

**4.10.5**  Thy most pleasant sight, O Agni, whether now by day or now by night, shines near like a golden ornament of beauty.

**4.10.6**  Thy body is like purified ghee, spotless, pure gold — that of thine shines like a golden ornament, O self-empowered one.

**4.10.7**  Even an established hatred thou drivest away from the mortal, O Agni — from the worshiper here in this way, O truth-bearer.

**4.10.8**  May friendships with thee as a brother be auspicious to us, O Agni, among the gods. May that be our navel, in the seat, in the same udder.

**4.11.1**  O Agni, O son of power, thy face shines auspiciously near, like that of the sun. Bright to see, it is seen even at night — undaunted, beautiful in form, the food of sight.

**4.11.2**  Blow up for the singer his thought, O Agni, piercing the void with trembling, as thou art praised, O thou born mightily. What thou hast won together with all the gods, O bright one — grant us that, O greatly great one, and abundant thought.

**4.11.3**  From thee, O Agni, poems arise, from thee inspired thoughts, from thee come hymns of achievement. From thee comes wealth of varied beauty — such is the understanding for the worshiping mortal.

**4.11.4**  From thee the prize-horse, the burden-bearing, heaven-flying, victory-making one is born, of true vigor.

**4.11.5**  Thee, O immortal Agni, the mortals desiring the gods worship first among the divine — the god of the joyous tongue, yoked to enmity — with thoughts they serve the householder, the lord of the house, the blameless one.

**4.11.6**  Far from us keep ignorance, far from us distress, far from us every ill thought — so thou dost guard. Auspicious at night, O son of power, O Agni — whom even a god accompanies with welfare.

**4.12.1**  He who kindles thee, O Agni, with ladle extended, who prepares thy food thrice on the same day — may he be distinguished with glories, eminent, through thy power, O all-knowing Jātavedas.

**4.12.2**  He who brings thee fuel toiling, O great Agni, tending thy mighty face — enkindling thee, meeting evening and dawn, thriving with wealth, he is accompanied as he slays the enemies.

**4.12.3**  Agni rules over great sovereignty, Agni over the highest prize and wealth. The most youthful, self-empowered one bestows treasure upon the worshiper — for the mortal repeatedly.

**4.12.4**  Whatever sin we have committed against thee in many places through thoughtlessness, O most youthful — make us free of sin like Aditi; scatter our transgressions in all directions, O Agni.

**4.12.5**  Even from great sin, O Agni, confront it — from the wide expanse of both gods and mortals. May thy friends never always be harmed; grant welfare to our offspring and progeny.

**4.12.6**  As the worshipful Vasus freed even the wild cow bound by its leg — even so free us from distress, O Agni; extend our life further and further.

**4.13.1**  Agni beheld the vanguard of the shining Dawns, benevolent, giving treasure. Come, O Aśvins, to the dwelling of the good-doers; the divine Sun rises with his light.

**4.13.2**  The divine Savitṛ has raised his light on high, tossing the drop like a hero seeking cattle. Varuṇa and Mitra follow his law when they cause the Sun to rise in the heavens.

**4.13.3**  Him whom they made to separate from darkness — the firm abode, not wearying, for their goal — that Sun, the seven young tawny mares carry, the spy of the whole world.
