**7.1.1**  Men generate the praiseworthy Agni by the friction of two fire-sticks, letting them fall from their hands — him who is visible from afar, the householder, the Atharvan.

**7.1.2**  The Vasus have kindled that Agni within, well-visible, for protection from every quarter — he who is ever capable and constant in the house.

**7.1.3**  Blazing forth, O Agni, shine before our dwellings with unceasing flame, O youngest one; to you, ever and again, the prizes of the contest come.

**7.1.4**  Your fires, excellent beyond all other fires, have blazed forth brilliantly, rich in heroes — there where the well-born men are assembled together.

**7.1.5**  Grant us, O Agni, through our prayer, wealth rich in heroes, a praiseworthy progeny, O mighty one — wealth which no malevolent sorcerer can overcome.

**7.1.6**  To him, the highly capable one, the youthful woman, bearing oblations, ghee-laden, comes by night and at dawn; and Aramatī, seeking wealth, comes near to him.

**7.1.7**  Burn away, O Agni, all hostilities — with those heats wherewith you burned up the Jarūtha; chase away soundlessly the disease that afflicts us.

**7.1.8**  He who kindles your flame, O Agni — O Vasiṣṭha, O bright one, shining, purifying — may you be here with us through these praises.

**7.1.9**  Those mortal men, heroes following the fathers' way, who have shared in your flame in many places — may you be here, well-disposed, with us through these same praises.

**7.1.10**  Let these heroic men, in the slaying of Vṛtra, overcome all godless sorceries — those who admire my praiseworthy prayer.

**7.1.11**  May we not sit helpless among men, O Agni; may there be no childlessness and no herolessness around you; grant us houses and homes full of progeny, O householder.

**7.1.12**  The horse-possessing sacrifice ever approaches him: grant us an abode rich in progeny and in good offspring, grown great by its own birth and its remainder.

**7.1.13**  Protect us, O Agni, from the unpleasant demon; protect us from the trickery of the evil-minded, harm-seeking one; allied with you, may we overcome those who attack us.

**7.1.14**  May that Agni surpass all other fires — where the prize-winning son, strong-handed, gathers the thousand-footed, undying syllables.

**7.1.15**  That is the Agni who protects his kindler from the assailant, who rescues the sacrificer from distress — around whom the well-born heroes gather.

**7.1.16**  This is that Agni, invoked in many places, whom the lord, bearing oblations, kindles together; around whom the hotr-priest goes in the sacrificial rites.

**7.1.17**  Into you, O Agni, may we pour many oblations, sovereign and constant; we two making both the bridal pair go to the wedding feast.

**7.1.18**  These most eagerly desired oblations, O Agni — carry them as the undying one toward the gathering of gods; may the sweet-smelling ones come back to us.

**7.1.19**  Deliver us not, O Agni, to herolessness; not to ill-clad poverty; not to hunger; not to demons, O lord of ṛta; mislead us not in the house nor in the forest.

**7.1.20**  Proclaim my prayers now, O Agni; pour them forth, O god, for the generous lords; may we stand in your gift; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.1.21**  You, O Agni, are pleasant to invoke, gladsome to behold; shine brightly, O son of strength, with fair light; may no son ever be lost from us who cling to you; may no manly hero perish from among us.

**7.1.22**  O Agni, in these god-kindled fires, proclaim not our ill-maintenance; may no evil thought of yours, however slight, O son of strength, destroy us.

**7.1.23**  That mortal is wealthy, O Agni of fair face, who pours oblations to you, the immortal; he places in the deity a treasure of goods — to him the wise patron comes asking his wish.

**7.1.24**  O Agni, knowing the great prosperity, bring ample wealth to the patrons; whereby, O mighty one, we may rejoice with undiminished life and fine heroes.

**7.1.25**  Proclaim my prayers now, O Agni; pour them forth, O god, for the generous lords; may we stand in your gift; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.2.1**  Be pleased, O Agni, with our kindling-wood today; blaze high, sending forth the majestic smoke of worship; touch the back of heaven with your crests and spread out together with the rays of the sun.

**7.2.2**  We will praise with our sacrifices the greatness of Narāśaṃsa of the adorable ones — those gods, of good wisdom, pure, who are the layers of prayer, who savour both kinds of oblations.

**7.2.3**  We would always magnify for the sacrifice him who is praiseworthy, the mighty, highly capable lord, the truthful messenger between heaven and earth — Agni kindled by Manu after the manner of Manu.

**7.2.4**  The worshippers, carrying the offerings, kneel and strew the sacred grass in the fire with reverence; O adhvaryu-priests, cleanse the oblation while pouring — the spotted one whose back is anointed with ghee.

**7.2.5**  Wise in prayer, desiring the gods, the worshippers have thrown open the doors at the gathering of deities, eager as charioteers; the two (Dawn and Night), like two mothers licking their young, have anointed him who is one at the assemblies.

**7.2.6**  And the two great divine women — Dawn and Night — like a good-milking cow yielding milk: may those two wealthy, often-invoked, adorable ones who sit upon the sacred grass come near for our well-being.

**7.2.7**  Two inspired crafters — so I think — at human sacrifices do we invoke you both, O Jātavedas pair, to worship; make our sacrifice stand upright among the invocations; win for us the desirable things among the gods.

**7.2.8**  May Bhāratī with the Bhāratī goddesses, Iḷā with gods and men, and Agni, and Sarasvatī with the Sārasvatī beings come hither — may the three goddesses sit upon this sacred grass.

**7.2.9**  Pour that nourishing sap upon us, O god Tvaṣṭṛ, granting freely; from which is born the heroic, capable man of action, well-skilled, who works the pressing stones and desires the gods.

**7.2.10**  O Lord of the Forest, release the gods; Agni the sacrificer will prepare the oblation; he himself, the true hotr-priest, shall sacrifice as he knows the births of the gods.

**7.2.11**  Come hither, O Agni, blazing, together with Indra and the swift gods on one chariot; may Aditi of fair sons be seated on the sacred grass; Svāhā! — may the immortal gods rejoice.

**7.3.1**  Together, make Agni your god, together with the Agni flames — the most adorable, the divine messenger of the sacrifice — he who among mortals is the firm upholder of ṛta, of fiery forehead, ghee-fed, the purifier.

**7.3.2**  He snorts like a horse at pasture, straining forward, when he steps forth from his great enclosure; then the wind fans his flame after it, and your track is black.

**7.3.3**  When your ageless flames go forth as the new-born bull, O Agni, the reddish smoke ascends toward the sky; for you, the messenger, go to the gods.

**7.3.4**  Your blazing force has spread across the earth; when it crumples dry vegetation with its jaws, your attack goes forth like a loosed army; you separate the grain with your tongue like a wondrous being.

**7.3.5**  Him, the youngest, they groom at night and at dawn like a swift horse — the men who have sharpened him; the guest kindled in his own place blazes forth, the flame of the bull who receives the oblation.

**7.3.6**  Your face, of fair visage, is beautiful to behold when you blaze brilliantly at close range like golden ornament; your power surges like the thunder of heaven — show your brilliance, resplendent as the sun.

**7.3.7**  As we would offer to Agni with Svāhā, surrounded by ghee-laden oblations — with those unlimited great powers, O Agni, protect us with a hundred iron fortresses.

**7.3.8**  Or those unassailable gifts you have for the worshipper, or those words of praise with which you rescue him who has heroes — with those, O son of strength, O Jātavedas, protect us, our patrons and our praisers.

**7.3.9**  When like a pure axe he went forth shining, glowing with his own splendid body — he who was born, longed for, from the two mothers, the wise, purifying one for the worship of the gods.

**7.3.10**  Illuminate these auspicious things for us, O Agni; may we attain to clear-minded wisdom; may all these be for those who praise and hymn you; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.4.1**  Bring forth for the bright, shining one, oblation and well-purified prayer for Agni — he who, moving among them with his knowledge, goes between all divine and human generations.

**7.4.2**  Let him be the wise Agni, ever young, since the youngest was born from his mother; he who draws the forests together, consuming the clean food, who eats up great quantities of food forthwith.

**7.4.3**  In the assembly and army of this god whom the mortals have grasped as the white one — he who has declared the human grip to be difficult of abode: Agni has shone forth for Āyu.

**7.4.4**  This Agni, the poet, the all-knowing among the ignorant, the immortal, has been placed among mortals; may he not lead us astray here, O mighty one; may we always be well-disposed toward you.

**7.4.5**  He who has settled into the god-made womb — for by his wisdom Agni has crossed over to the immortals — him, the all-nourishing embryo, the herbs, the trees, and the earth bear.

**7.4.6**  For Agni is master of great immortality, master of giving wealth and excellent heroism; may we not sit around you, O mighty one, without heroes, without cattle, without soma.

**7.4.7**  May we be lords of the possessions to be distributed, of the spontaneous wealth, of the eternal riches; O Agni, there is no residue born of another kind — do not lead astray the path of the mindless one.

**7.4.8**  The foreign one, though pleasant, born of another womb, is not truly to be grasped — it must be thought over with the mind; yet even he returns to his home again; may the new prize-winner come to us victorious.

**7.4.9**  Protect us, O Agni, from the assailant; protect us, O mighty one, from disgrace; may the dusty path converge toward you, and with it the thousandfold wealth worthy of longing.

**7.4.10**  Illuminate these auspicious things for us, O Agni; may we attain to clear-minded wisdom; may all these be for those who praise and hymn you; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.5.1**  Bring forth your song to Agni the mighty, the pillar of heaven and earth; Vaiśvānara who has grown great in the lap of all the immortals, with those who keep vigil.

**7.5.2**  Agni is stationed in heaven and on earth; he is the leader of rivers, the bull of the standing waters; Vaiśvānara, grown great with his excellence, shines upon the human peoples.

**7.5.3**  Out of fear of you the dark peoples came, abandoning their unequal food; O Vaiśvānara, when you shone for Pūru, blazing, bursting open the enemy cities, O Agni.

**7.5.4**  Your threefold ordinance, O Vaiśvānara, O Agni, is followed by both earth and heaven; you have stretched out both world-halves with your light, blazing with your unceasing flame.

**7.5.5**  You, O Agni, the tawny, yearning hymns follow — the rushing, ghee-laden songs — calling you lord of the peoples, charioteer of wealth, Vaiśvānara, beacon of the dawns and of the days.

**7.5.6**  The Vasus have established lordly power in you; the great Mitra-friends have enjoyed your wisdom; you drove the Dasyus from their homes, O Agni, generating broad light for the Ārya.

**7.5.7**  Being born in the highest heaven, you at once guard the path like Vāyu; striding forth to generate the worlds, O Jātavedas, you bestow on offspring.

**7.5.8**  Direct that luminous refreshment toward us, O Agni Vaiśvānara, O Jātavedas — by which you fatten the bounty, O all-desired one, and give wide fame to the mortal worshipper.

**7.5.9**  Yoke for our patrons that abundant, glorious, prize-giving wealth, O Agni; O Vaiśvānara, grant us great shelter together with the Rudras and Vasus, O Agni.

**7.6.1**  I proclaim and praise the glory of the universal king, the mighty lord, the one to be extolled among the peoples — the mighty deeds like those of Indra; worshipping, I speak praise of the wood-Agni.

**7.6.2**  The seer, the beacon, the nourisher, the light of the rock — they urge forward the auspicious sovereignty of both worlds; with songs like those of the city-breaker I seek the ancient great ordinances of Agni.

**7.6.3**  The Paṇis, the knotted ones, the mumbling, the faithless, the ungrowing, the unsacrificing — Agni has repeatedly driven those Dasyus away; he made the former the latter, the non-sacrificers.

**7.6.4**  I praise that Agni, the mightiest in his deeds, who by his powers made the revelling ones in the western darkness into eastern ones — him who is lord of wealth, unbowed, who tames the attackers.

**7.6.5**  He who bent down the fortifications with his weapons, who made the dawns into consorts of the noble — that youthful Agni of Nahuṣa's people, by his powers, made the clans bring tribute.

**7.6.6**  All peoples rest in his protection, seeking his goodwill with their customs; Agni Vaiśvānara, the best of both worlds, has seated himself in the lap of the father and mother.

**7.6.7**  The god Vaiśvānara has taken the deep treasures at the rising of the sun; Agni has taken from the near sea and from the far, from heaven and from earth.

**7.7.1**  I urge forward for you even the powerful god Agni with reverences, as one urges a swift horse; be our knowing messenger of the sacrifice, O measuring one who is found among the gods through his own self.

**7.7.2**  Come, O Agni, along your own paths, joyful, enjoying the friendship of the gods; making the back of earth resound with your powers, you gladly burn up all the forests with your jaws.

**7.7.3**  The eastward-directed sacrifice, the well-laid sacred grass — Agni the invoked one pleases these, as a hotr-priest; calling upon the two all-desired mothers, O youngest, kindly one, from whom you were born.

**7.7.4**  The wise, discerning men of understanding instantly produce the expert charioteer at the sacrifice; Agni, the lord of the clans, has been placed in the house — joyous, honey-voiced, master of ṛta.

**7.7.5**  The chosen carrier has sat down, the one who has come — Agni the brahman-priest, the ordainer, who sits among men; both heaven and earth nourish him, and the hotr-priest sacrifices to him, the all-desired.

**7.7.6**  These have surpassed all with their glories — those manly ones who have fashioned the prayer and the choicest thing; who, hearkening, move the clans forward, who have meditated upon this truth of mine.

**7.7.7**  Now we Vasiṣṭhas implore you, O Agni, as lord of wealth, O son of strength; carry refreshment to the praisers and the generous patrons; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.8.1**  The king is kindled together by the people with reverences, he whose face has been offered ghee; the men press together to propitiate him with oblations — Agni has shone at the forefront of the dawns.

**7.8.2**  He has been recognized as exceedingly great — the hotr-priest, the joyful, youthful Agni of Manu; spreading out light across the earth, with the dark-rimmed wheel he has swelled among the herbs.

**7.8.3**  With what will you clothe us in excellent praise, O Agni? What self-power will you grant when sung? When shall we be lords, O good giver, winning wealth that is hard to overcome and perfect?

**7.8.4**  This Agni of Bharata is proclaimed everywhere, shining with great brilliance like the sun; he who has stood over Pūru in battles, the divine guest, blazing, has shone forth.

**7.8.5**  Let the many invocations be real in you; be well-disposed with all your flames; even when praised, O Agni, you are heard when you are hymned; increase yourself by your own body, O well-born one.

**7.8.6**  May this word, hundredfold and thousandfold, rise up doubly great for Agni; that it may be auspicious for the praisers and the household, shining, disease-removing, demon-slaying.

**7.8.7**  Now we Vasiṣṭhas implore you, O Agni, as lord of wealth, O son of strength; carry refreshment to the praisers and the generous patrons; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.9.1**  The wooer has awoken from the lap of the dawns — the joyful hotr-priest, the most poetic, the purifier; he sets the beacon of both kinds of beings and the oblations for the gods, wealth for the pious.

**7.9.2**  He is the wise one who bursts open the doors of the Paṇis, purifying the richly nourishing sun-beam for us; the joyful hotr-priest, the domestic one of the clans, is seen through the darkness of the nights.

**7.9.3**  The faultless seer, Aditi, Vivasvat, the well-spoken Mitra, the auspicious guest to us — Agni of brilliant radiance shines at the fore of the dawns; the embryo of the waters has entered into the plants.

**7.9.4**  The praiseworthy one for you, Jātavedas has blazed undimmed through the generations of Manu; he who shines with beautiful radiance — the cows have recognized him as he is kindled.

**7.9.5**  O Agni, go on your divine embassy, unharmed, toward the gods with the brahman-made band; sacrifice to Sarasvatī, the Maruts, the Aśvins, the waters, and all the gods for the bestowal of treasure.

**7.9.6**  Vasiṣṭha, kindling you, O Agni, strikes away Jarūtha; worship the bountiful goddess for wealth; of many kinds of ritual, O Jātavedas, grow old; preserve us evermore with blessings.

**7.10.1**  Like the lover of the dawn, he has reached his broad force, flashing, shining, blazing; the bull, the golden one, the pure, shines with his light; he who urges on prayers has stirred the eager ones.

**7.10.2**  Like the sun at the dawning of the days, he has shone forth; the prayer-devotees spread the sacrifice like a thought; Agni the god, knowing all births, runs as the messenger, devoted to the gods, the most winning.

**7.10.3**  The songs and prayers, desiring the gods, go to Agni begging for wealth — to him who is beautiful to behold, of fair visage, of good form, the bearer of oblations, the unwearying one of mankind.

**7.10.4**  O Agni, bring Indra together with the Vasus, bring the great Rudra together with the Rudras; bring Aditi with the Ādityas, she who belongs to all peoples; bring Bṛhaspati with the ṛk-singers, the all-desired one.

**7.10.5**  The clans praise at the sacrificial rites Agni the joyful hotr-priest, loved by the devout, the youngest; for he has become the lord of nights and of riches, the unwearied messenger for the worship of the gods.

**7.11.1**  Great art thou, the presiding spirit of the sacrifice; without thee the immortals do not rejoice. Come hither on the same chariot with all the gods, O Agni; sit here as Hotṛ, foremost of all.

**7.11.2**  Thee, the swift messenger, do the oblation-bearing men always invoke for embassy; he upon whose sacrificial grass thou, O Agni, hast sat down with the gods — for him the days become auspicious.

**7.11.3**  Thrice in the night do they discern the treasures stored within thee for the mortal worshipper. Like Manu, O Agni, worship the gods here for us; be our messenger and protector against malediction.

**7.11.4**  Agni is lord of the great sacrifice; Agni is lord of every oblation that has been made. The Vasus indeed delight in his will; therefore the gods appointed him the bearer of oblations.

**7.11.5**  O Agni, bring hither the gods to partake of the oblation; let those whose chief is Indra rejoice here. Place this sacrifice among the gods in heaven. Do ye ever guard us with all blessings.

**7.12.1**  With great reverence we have come to the youngest, who blazes when kindled in his own dwelling, the brilliantly shining one who fills the two wide worlds, who is well-fed with oblations, turned toward all sides.

**7.12.2**  He who overpowers all evils by his greatness — Agni, Jātavedas, is praised in the house. May he guard us from sin and reproach, both us who hymn him and our bountiful patrons.

**7.12.3**  Thou art Varuṇa and also Mitra, O Agni; the Vasiṣṭhas strengthen thee with their hymns. May the readily won treasures be in thee. Do ye ever guard us with all blessings.

**7.13.1**  Bring forth the prayer and meditation for Agni, the all-purifying, the thought-sustaining, the slayer of Asuras; being gladdened on the sacrificial grass, like an offering brought, for Vaiśvānara, the marshaller of thoughts.

**7.13.2**  Thou, O Agni, blazing with thy flame, hast filled both worlds at thy birth. Thou hast freed the gods from malediction, O Vaiśvānara Jātavedas, by thy greatness.

**7.13.3**  When, O Agni, thou wast born, thou surveyedst all the worlds, moving freely like a herdsman among cattle, wandering everywhere. O Vaiśvānara, find the path for the brahman. Do ye ever guard us with all blessings.

**7.14.1**  With fuel, with invocations of the gods, with oblations to Jātavedas the divine, to the bright-flaming one — reverently we would serve Agni with our gifts.

**7.14.2**  We would tend thee, O Agni, with fuel; we would serve thee with good praise, O adorable one; we, O Hotṛ of the sacrifice, with ghee; we, O god, with oblation, O thou of auspicious flame.

**7.14.3**  Come to us with the gods at the invocation of the gods, O Agni, delighting in the vaṣaṭ-call. May we be givers to thee, the divine. Do ye ever guard us with all blessings.

**7.15.1**  Into the mouth of the bountiful, the approachable one, pour the oblation — he who is to us the nearest helper.

**7.15.2**  He who has sat down over the five tribes in house after house — the wise, the householder, the ever-young.

**7.15.3**  May Agni guard our wealth and household folk on every side, and also guard us from distress.

**7.15.4**  I have fashioned a new hymn for Agni, the falcon of the sky — will he win treasure for us?

**7.15.5**  His glories are desirable to behold, like wealth with abundance of heroes, as he blazes at the head of the sacrifice.

**7.15.6**  May Agni, the most skilled in worship, the bearer of oblations, come to this vaṣaṭ-call and be pleased with our songs.

**7.15.7**  Thee, the lord of the settlement, the brilliant one, O god, we would fix in our hearts; O Agni, thou who art invoked, bringer of good heroes.

**7.15.8**  Shine through the nights and through the days; through thee may we be possessed of good fire. Thou art rich in heroes, thou desirest us.

**7.15.9**  To thee do the inspired men come with their prayers, striving for gain — unto thee, the thousand-streamed, moving near.

**7.15.10**  Agni, the bright-flaming, the immortal, drives away the demons; the pure, the purifying one, he is worthy of praise.

**7.15.11**  May he, the lord, the offspring of strength, bring us riches; and may Bhaga grant the desirable gift.

**7.15.12**  Do thou, O Agni, grant fame with good heroes; and the god Savitṛ, Bhaga, and Diti give what is desirable.

**7.15.13**  O Agni, O god, protect us from distress, ward off the one who injures us; burn him, ageless, with thy most scorching flames.

**7.15.14**  Then the great one comes to us, unconquerable, for the protection of men; be thou a fortress with a hundred arms.

**7.15.15**  Protect us from sin, O thou undeceivable, from him who seeks to harm, at the fall of evening and of morning, by day and by night.

**7.16.1**  With this reverence I call Agni to you, the offspring of strength, the dearest, the most wakeful, the herald of the sacrifice, the messenger of all, the immortal.

**7.16.2**  He yokes the red steeds of universal bounty; he runs swiftly when well-fed with oblations. The sacrifice is well-praised and well-laid; the divine bestowal of riches comes to men.

**7.16.3**  His flame has risen up as he, the bountiful, is fed with oblations; the red smoke-columns that touch the sky rise up. The men kindle Agni together.

**7.16.4**  Thee, the most glorious, we make our messenger; bring the gods hither to the feast. O offspring of strength, grant all that mortals enjoy; give us that which we implore of thee.

**7.16.5**  Thou, O Agni, art our householder; thou art the Hotṛ at our rite; thou art the Potṛ, O thou who holdest all desirable things, O wise one; worship and seek the desirable.

**7.16.6**  Make a jewel for the sacrificer, O thou of good will; for thou art the giver of jewels. Set in order every priest at the rite, O thou of good praise, and him too who is capable.

**7.16.7**  In thee, O Agni, well fed with oblations, may the patrons be dear — the guiders, the bountiful ones among the people, who share out broad pastures and kine.

**7.16.8**  Those in whose house Iḍā with ghee-filled hands sits down at dawn — save them, O mighty one, from treachery and reproach; grant us shelter, O thou of far-famed might.

**7.16.9**  He, the carrier with his sweet tongue, the knower, with his mouth — O Agni, bring riches to us through the bountiful patrons, and set in order the gift of oblations.

**7.16.10**  Those who give horse-wealth and great gifts with desire for fame — save them from sin with those who ferry across; be thou a hundred fortresses for them, O most youthful.

**7.16.11**  The god, the giver of wealth, desires a full vessel for you. Either pour it out, or fill it up; then indeed the god will hear you.

**7.16.12**  Him, the Hotṛ of the rite, the wise one, the carrier — the gods have made him so. Agni places a jewel and heroic wealth for the worshipper, for the man who gives.

**7.17.1**  O Agni, be thou well-kindled with good fuel, and let the sacrificial grass be widely strewn.

**7.17.2**  And let the willing doors open wide, and here bring hither the willing gods.

**7.17.3**  O Agni, go forward with the oblation, worship the gods; make the sacrifice proceed well, O Jātavedas.

**7.17.5**  Win all desirable things, O wise one; may our wishes be fulfilled today.

**7.17.6**  Thee they have appointed as bearer of oblations, O Agni, the gods have set thee, the offspring of strength.

**7.17.7**  May we be givers to thee, the divine; distribute for us great jewels as thou movest.

**7.18.1**  In thee, O Indra, even our fathers won all good things for the praisers; in thee are the good milch cows, in thee indeed the horses; thou art the most bountiful with wealth for the godly man.

**7.18.2**  For thou dwellest with thy wives like a king, enlightening the days as a wise seer. Adorn our hymns, O bountiful one, with kine and horses; sharpen us who strive toward thee for riches.

**7.18.3**  These joyful, god-seeking songs have come to thee here competing. May the path of thy riches come toward us; may we be in thy good favor, O Indra, in thy shelter.
