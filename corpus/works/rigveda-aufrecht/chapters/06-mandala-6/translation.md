**6.1.1**  For thou, O Agni, wast the first to take thought of this hymn, and becamest the wondrous Hotṛ. Thou, O bull, hast made thy strength hard to overcome, thy might for every might, to be with every might.

**6.1.2**  Then the Hotṛ, most worthy of worship, sat down in the seat of Iḷā, driving refreshment forward and being praised. Men devoted to the gods followed thee, the first, with minds fixed on great wealth.

**6.1.3**  Like a herd following one who goes with many cattle, the wakeful ones followed the wealth in thee. Agni, the shining, the visible, the great, endowed with fat, shining every day.

**6.1.4**  Seeking with reverence the footstep of the god, the glory-seeking ones obtained unfading fame. They assumed the sacred names; at thy auspicious appearance they made merry.

**6.1.5**  The settlements on earth magnify thee; the riches of the people on both sides magnify thee. Thou becamest a protector to be remembered at the crossing; father and mother art thou always to human beings.

**6.1.6**  Worthy of worship, he is dear in the settlements; Agni the Hotṛ, the joyous, the most worship-worthy, has sat down. We would sit with bent knees before thee who shinest in the house, with homage.

**6.1.7**  Thee, O Agni, the new one, we implore, we who have good thoughts and seek thy grace, longing for the gods. Thou, O Agni, hast led the clans, shining with the great light of heaven.

**6.1.8**  The seer of the clans, lord of the enduring peoples, the impeller, the bull of the human tribes — him who urges forward, who drives on refreshment, the purifying, resplendent Agni, the adorable bestower of riches.

**6.1.9**  O Agni, that mortal has sacrificed and toiled, who has attained to thee with fuel and gift of oblation — whoever knows the offering round about with reverence, all desires that one obtains, aided by thee.

**6.1.10**  For thee in return we would do service greatly, for the great one, with homages, O Agni, with fuel and with oblations. O son of strength, we who know, with hymns and songs of praise, would come to thy auspicious gracious thought.

**6.1.11**  Thou who hast stretched out both world-halves with thy brilliance, and who art the rescuer that seeks fame through praises — with great, vigorous, shining prizes, O Agni, shine forth more widely upon us.

**6.1.12**  Always kindle for us, O Vasu, manly gifts; much cattle for our offspring and our descendants. May many refreshments, great, far from evil, auspicious glories of good renown be ours.

**6.1.13**  O Agni, O king, through thee the many, many riches and the abundant wealth: may I obtain thy store of goods. For many desirable things are in thee, O much-desired Agni; wealth for the worshipper is in thee, O king.

**6.2.1**  For thou, O Agni, rulest over glory as Mitra over a principality. Thou, O all-beholding Vasu, nourishest fame like nourishment.

**6.2.2**  Thee verily do the tribes implore with sacrifices and with hymns. To thee comes the vigorous one, the wolf-free, cleaving through the air, all-beholding.

**6.2.3**  With united desire, the men of heaven kindle thee, the banner of sacrifice — when that human folk, longing for thy grace, offers oblation at the rite.

**6.2.4**  The mortal who prospers through thought for thee, O liberal giver, and who toils — he crosses with the help of the great heaven, even as one crosses enmity and distress.

**6.2.5**  The mortal who with fuel attains the sharpened oblation to thee — O Agni, he nourishes a dwelling full of abundance, for a hundred autumns.

**6.2.6**  Thy fearsome smoke rolls up and is spread bright in the sky. Like the sun with its brilliance, thou shinest gracefully, O Pāvaka.

**6.2.7**  Verily thou art to be praised in the settlements, thou art our beloved guest — delightful, to be cherished as a homestead, to be saved as a son.

**6.2.8**  For by wisdom in the trough thou art anointed, O Agni, a vigorous one capable of deeds — like a house with its own realm going everywhere, like a swift horse easy to call, a young one.

**6.2.9**  Thou, O Agni, dost consume even those unshaken things, like a beast in a pasture. For, O ageless one, the skillful ones cut the forests that are thy abode.

**6.2.10**  For thou goest as Hotṛ of those performing the rite, in the house of the clans. Grant prosperity, O lord of the clan; be pleased with the oblation, O Aṅgiras.

**6.2.11**  O Agni, friend-great one, tell the gods of the good will of the two world-halves toward us. Bring welfare, a good dwelling for the men of heaven; may we cross over hatreds, distresses, and evils — may we cross them, cross them with thy protection.

**6.3.1**  O Agni, he will dwell who is the guardian of Order, born of Order; he who is devoted to the gods and yearning for thee attains wide light. That mortal whom thou, O god, together with Mitra, and Varuṇa in unison, guardest from trouble with thy sharp power.

**6.3.2**  He has sacrificed with sacrifices, toiled with labors, and presented gifts to Agni of the prospering gate. Thus indeed neither dishonor among the glorious, nor distress, nor arrogance overtakes that mortal.

**6.3.3**  His appearance is sinless like the sun, fearsome when his flaming thought comes. His host does not neigh; yet this forest-dweller, pleasant in any dwelling, cannot be restrained by night.

**6.3.4**  His great form moves even where sharp, blazing with its mouth like a horse tossing its jaws. Lolling his tongue as he goes, like an axe he is about to set fire to the wood, like a metal-worker he makes it run.

**6.3.5**  Like a shooter he has set himself in place, ready to shoot; he sharpens his gleam like the edge of iron. He who has a wonderful banner, the fire-stick that cannot be contained by night, like a bird swiftly alighting with swift flight of wings.

**6.3.6**  He, the great friend, covers himself against the dawns like a singer, and glitters with his flame. The ruddy immortal one who by night, the ruddy one who by day guides men — the immortal ruddy one who by day guides men.

**6.3.7**  His worshipper bellows forth newness like the sky; the bull, the glittering one, has bellowed in the plants. He who goes with glowing course like sunlight in his path fills both world-halves with wealth, making them good consorts.

**6.3.8**  He who flashes with his own energies like lightning, whether with refreshments or with united rays — or who has fashioned the troop of the Maruts, blazed like a skillful artisan, fierce and impetuous.

**6.4.1**  As thou, O Hotṛ, hast sacrificed to the gods with the sacrifices of Manu, O son of strength — so do thou today, O Agni, sacrifice to those same gods for us equally, desiring the desiring ones.

**6.4.2**  May Agni, the far-shining, worthy of praise, perceptible like the morning sky, grant us comfort who knows all things. He who is all-living, immortal among mortals, who rises at dawn, who is a guest — Jātavedas.

**6.4.3**  The skies wonder at his mightiness; he wears radiances like the bright sun. He who ageless and purifying fills all things, who shatters even the ancient things of the rock.

**6.4.4**  For the praiseworthy one, the son, art thou; the fire-worshipper: Agni by birth has made food his seat. Be thou an energy-winner and grant us energy; like a king who subdues the enemy, mayst thou dwell within us without wolves.

**6.4.5**  He who keeps sharpening his teeth and consumes the obstructing food; like Vāyu he surpasses all nights as sovereign. May we overcome the adversaries of thy commanded ones — he moves around like a swift horse falling and flying.

**6.4.6**  Like the sun with its luminous rays, O Agni, thou hast stretched out both world-halves with brilliance. The brilliant night-flame guides around the darknesses; like Auśija, flying on his path of flame.

**6.4.7**  Thee we have chosen as the most joyful with rays of song — hear us greatly, O Agni. With strength, like Indra; the most manly ones fill thee as a divinity, like Vāyu, with bounty.

**6.4.8**  Now, O Agni, with the wolf-free paths of welfare, go before us for riches; lead us across distress. Bestow that blessing on the generous patrons who praise thee; may we rejoice a hundred winters with good heroes.

**6.5.1**  I call for you the youthful son of strength, the truthful of speech, youngest in thoughts — he who fills riches as the wise one, the all-desirable one, the much-desired, the blameless.

**6.5.2**  In thee are the treasures, O Hotṛ of the great banner; the adorable ones have stirred at evening and at morning. In thee as in earth all the worlds, all good fortunes, have been placed — in thee the purifying one.

**6.5.3**  Thou hast sat of old in these settlements; by wisdom thou becamest the charioteer of desirable things. From there, O Jātavedas, thou who knowest, fillest the worshipper with riches, in succession one after another.

**6.5.4**  Whoever from afar attacks us, O Agni, whoever among those near, O great friend, would harm us — with thy own ageless bulls, O most burning one, burn him, thou who hast burning power.

**6.5.5**  He who has served thee with sacrifice, with fuel, with hymns and praises, O son of strength — that one among mortals shines, O immortal wise one, with wealth, with glory, with fame.

**6.5.6**  Do that swiftly when set in motion, O Agni; drive away the rivals with strength, O powerful one. What is praised by thee by days and night with words — be pleased with that, with the singer's hymn that resounds.

**6.5.7**  May we obtain that desire, O Agni, with thy help; O giver of riches, may we obtain wealth with good heroes. May we obtain vigour while winning vigour; may we obtain thine ageless and undying glory.

**6.6.1**  He who desires with a new hymn, seeking a path toward the son of strength, with sacrifice — goes to the wood-cleaving one, dark-hued by night, the shining divine Hotṛ who delights in the feast.

**6.6.2**  The youngest, the white one, the thunderer standing in the luminous sphere, resounding with ageless ones — he, the purifier, the most abundant Agni, follows all the wide and many things consuming them.

**6.6.3**  Thy flames driven by wind, O Agni, move hither and thither, the bright ones of the pure. The immensely anointed, divine, nine-fold ones overpower the forests, boldly breaking them.

**6.6.4**  Thy bright, pure, shining ones — like horses let loose — shear the earth. Then thy whirling brightness shines widely, as thou passest over the ridge of the dappled one.

**6.6.5**  Then the tongue of the bull flies forward like a thunderbolt hurled, fighting among the cows. Like the attack of a hero, the conquest of Agni — the fearsome, hard to restrain — devours the forests.

**6.6.6**  With thy radiance thou hast boldly stretched out the earthly expanses of the great piercing one. Drive away the fearful things with thy forces; wishing to win, consume the rivals.

**6.6.7**  O brilliant one, O brilliant-dominioned one — the most brilliant, the giver of strength — grant us the brilliant, inspiring wealth, great, with many heroes; join to the singer who praises thee with brilliant praises.

**6.7.1**  The gods generated in the mouth the vessel: Agni Vaiśvānara, born in truth, the head of heaven, the fire-stick of earth — the wise one, the universal king, the guest of peoples.

**6.7.2**  The gods celebrated in unison the great invocation: the navel of sacrifices, the seat of riches — Vaiśvānara, the charioteer of rites, the banner of sacrifice, they generated.

**6.7.3**  From thee, O Agni, the inspired sage is born who wins prizes; from thee come heroes conquering enemies. O Vaiśvānara, O king, place in us the desirable riches.

**6.7.4**  All the immortal gods celebrate thee at thy birth, as a young child. Through thy designs, O Vaiśvānara, they came to immortality — when thou didst shine for both parents.

**6.7.5**  O Vaiśvānara, O Agni, none has ever defied those great ordinances of thine — when at birth in the lap of both parents thou didst find the banner for the courses of the days.

**6.7.6**  The ridges of heaven have been measured out by the eye of Vaiśvānara, by the banner of the immortal. On the very summit of that one, above all the worlds, seven streams have spread like branches.

**6.7.7**  He who of good insight measured out the realms of air, Vaiśvānara the wise who measured out the luminous regions of heaven — who has spread around all the worlds, the unwearied herdsman, the guardian of the immortal.

**6.8.1**  Now will I proclaim the might of the vigorous, ruddy, bountiful one in the assembly — a fresh, pure thought for Vaiśvānara; as Soma streams purified, so it flows beautifully to Agni.

**6.8.2**  Being born in the highest heaven, Agni, the guardian of holy ordinances, watched over the cosmic laws. Of good understanding, Vaiśvānara measured out the mid-air and with his greatness touched the vault of heaven.

**6.8.3**  The wondrous Mitra-like one propped apart the two world-halves; causing them to vibrate within, he expelled darkness with his light. He spread the two cosmic bowls apart as if they were skins; Vaiśvānara assumed universal virile power.

**6.8.4**  The great ones grasped him in the lap of the waters; the peoples stood around their praiseworthy king. Mātariśvan, as messenger of Vivasvat, brought Agni Vaiśvānara hither from the distant region.

**6.8.5**  O Agni, age after age bestow ever-fresh, glorious wealth on those who praise thee in the assembly. Like a wheel's rim, O ageless king, cut down the sinner below as fire cuts down a tree with its blaze.

**6.8.6**  O Agni, maintain among our patrons the ageless, hero-rich sovereignty. May we, O Vaiśvānara, O Agni, win the prize of hundreds and thousands by thy aid.

**6.8.7**  With thy inviolable guardians, O thou worshipped one, protect our patrons, O thou who hast three stations. Guard us who have offered, and the company of givers, O Agni Vaiśvānara; carry them forward as thou art praised.

**6.9.1**  The dark day and the bright day revolve in succession, perceived by all beings through the two realms of space. Agni Vaiśvānara, being born like a king, pierced through the darknesses with his light.

**6.9.2**  I know not the warp, I know not the woof, nor what it is they weave in contest, those going to the conflict. Whose son is he here who shall speak what needs saying — one greater speaks with one who is lesser, his father.

**6.9.3**  He indeed knows the warp, he knows the woof; he speaks what needs saying in due season — he who perceived this, guardian of immortality, moving below yet seeing beyond with another.

**6.9.4**  He is the first Hotṛ; behold this immortal light among mortals. He was born firm and settled here, the immortal one, waxing great in his own body.

**6.9.5**  The fixed light is set on high for seeing; the mind, swiftest of things, flies within it. All the gods, of one mind and purpose, go forth rightly toward that single will.

**6.9.6**  My ears fly open, my eye flies open; this light which was implanted in my heart blazes forth. My mind wanders far in thought — what shall I say? What shall I now contemplate?

**6.9.7**  All the gods bowed down in fear when thou, O Agni, didst stand in the darkness. May Vaiśvānara, the immortal, guard us; may he, the immortal, guard us.

**6.10.1**  Set before you in the sacrifice, at the rite going forward, the gladsome, heavenly Agni of pure praise. First with hymns — for he, the far-shining Jātavedas, will make our sacrifices auspicious.

**6.10.2**  O brilliantly faced Hotṛ, O Agni, kindled with fire among men, we honour him with the praise-song. The thoughts purified for him flow pure like clarified butter, like a shout of power as Mamatā would offer.

**6.10.3**  He has grown fat with fame among mortals, the wise man who has offered to Agni with hymns. The bright-flaming one bestows upon him wondrous aids for the winning of a cattle-pen.

**6.10.4**  He who on being born filled the two vast worlds with his far-seen radiance, making the dark path bright — even through the mighty surge of night's darkness the purifying one was seen by his flame.

**6.10.5**  Now, O Agni, with thy many-prize-bringing aids bestow wealth upon us and upon our patrons — those who surpass others in generosity and fame and in the power of heroes over men.

**6.10.6**  O Agni, rejoicing, give delight to this sacrifice which the seated oblation-offerer presents to thee. Among the Bharadvājas thou dost receive pure praise — grant the heroes their portion of vigorous sustenance.

**6.10.7**  Drive apart the two hatreds, increase Iḷā; may we rejoice for a hundred winters, rich in heroes.

**6.11.1**  Sacrifice, O Hotṛ, urged and best fitted to sacrifice, O Agni; urge like the impulse of the Maruts. Turn hither Mitra-Varuṇa, the Nāsatyas, and Heaven and Earth for the hotraship.

**6.11.2**  Thou art our most gladdening Hotṛ, not deceitful, a god within the assembly among mortals. With thy purifying tongue, O bearer Agni, sacrifice with thy own body.

**6.11.3**  For truly the most nourishing wisdom craves in thee to proclaim the birth of gods for him who praises to sacrifice. Most inspired of the Aṅgirases, when the wise singer at the rite chants the sweet meter.

**6.11.4**  Blazing brightly, O far-shining, fully pure Agni, sacrifice across the wide two worlds. Him whom the five peoples, having offered oblations with reverence, anoint as Āyu, easy to please.

**6.11.5**  I now strew the sacred grass with reverence for Agni; the ladle rich with ghee and pure praise is put in motion. The seat is established in the dwelling on earth; the sacrifice is set in place like an eye in the sun.

**6.11.6**  Favour us, O brilliantly faced Hotṛ, O Agni kindled with the divine fires. Grasping the wealth of riches, O son of strength, may we pass beyond the fence of sin.

**6.12.1**  In the midst of the dwelling, the ruling Hotṛ on the sacred grass, Agni, who pushes forward, is to be worshipped by the two worlds. He, the son of strength and guardian of cosmic order, has stretched his flame afar like the sun.

**6.12.2**  In thee, the fully pure, the adorable — sacrifice, O king; let Heaven now worship as if fulfilling completeness. Settled in three stations, swiftly like a runner's stride, sacrifice the human offerings and rich gifts.

**6.12.3**  He whose wheel blazes most sharply in the forest, who shines like one growing strong on the path striking forward — the non-deceiving, swift one is conscious in his very self, the immortal in the lowest plants.

**6.12.4**  Thus with our powers like those of heroes, Agni Jātavedas is to be praised at home. Consuming the wooden fuel, conquering by wisdom, not a lazy bull — like a father he is to be won over by sacrifices.

**6.12.5**  His flames win admiration when he freely cleaves and courses along the broad earth. He who at once breaks loose, free-running, swifter than swift — like a debt-free wanderer he shines across the desert.

**6.12.6**  So thou, O Agni, kindled with all thy fires, come to our aid against him who oppresses us. Thou seekest riches and driv'st away misfortune; may we rejoice for a hundred winters, rich in heroes.

**6.13.1**  From thee, O blessed Agni, all blessings spread like the branches of trees. At thy swift call come riches, prizes, the conquest of adversaries, the rain of heaven, and the stream of waters worthy of praise.

**6.13.2**  Like Bhaga thou dost bestow treasure for our desire; like one going everywhere thou dost rule with wondrous brightness. O Agni, thou art like Mitra of the great cosmic order, the distributor of abundant lovely wealth, O god.

**6.13.3**  He, the true lord, slays Vṛtra with his might; O Agni, the wise one carries off the prize from the miser. He whom thou, O all-knowing one, born of cosmic order, dost spur with wealth, along with the son of the waters.

**6.13.4**  The mortal who has reached thy keen edge, O son of strength, through songs, hymns, and sacrifices — he, O god Agni, assumes all desirable things; he rules with riches.

**6.13.5**  Those of wide renown and hero-rich — for heroes, O Agni, O son of strength, grant fulfilment. Thou dost make abundant cattle by thy might, and food — to the wolf, to the gracious one, to the niggard.

**6.13.6**  O eloquent one, O son of strength, flying forth for us, O Agni, grant us offspring and progeny, and the prize for us. May we attain fulfilment with all our praises; may we rejoice for a hundred winters, rich in heroes.

**6.14.1**  The mortal who has cherished service to Agni with his devotions — truly that ancient one will choose refreshment for his protection.

**6.14.2**  For Agni is the all-knowing, Agni is the most ordaining Ṛṣi. The peoples of Manu invoke Agni as Hotṛ in sacrifices.

**6.14.3**  For men of different kinds strive for thy aid, O Agni, for wealth from the lord. The Āyus overcome the Dasyu with their observances, pressing upon the one who holds no vows.

**6.14.4**  Agni gives the hero, the true lord, who overcomes in battle the waters, at whose strength the foes tremble with fear at the mere sight.

**6.14.5**  For Agni, the god knowing all, rescues the mortal from reproach. He is mighty; the wealth of him who holds Agni unchecked is unchecked in the prizes.

**6.14.6**  O great friend of Mitra, O god Agni, speak to the gods for goodwill of the two worlds. Bring welfare and happy dwelling to men under heaven; may we cross over hostilities, sins, and ill ways — may we cross them, may we cross them with thy help.

**6.15.1**  Glorify with song this your guest who wakes at dawn, the lord of all peoples. He discerns heaven by birth; the pure one perpetually consumes, like an embryo, what is immovable.

**6.15.2**  Him whom the Bhṛgus well-placed, worthy of praise with his flame upward, set in the lord of the forest as Mitra — thou, O wondrous one, being well pleased with Vītahavya, dost magnify thyself day by day with praises.

**6.15.3**  Be thou the uninjured increaser of strength, O lord, overcoming the uttermost of adversaries. O son of strength, extend wide shelter among mortals to Vītahavya and the wide-famed Bharadvāja.

**6.15.4**  Glorify with pure praises your blazing guest, the heavenly man Agni, the Hotṛ of Manu, the one of auspicious rite — like an inspired singer of celestial speech, the god who carries oblations, the messenger.

**6.15.5**  He who by the grace of his purifying, inspiring flame shone on the earth like the radiance of the dawn — pressing forward in the path of Etaśa at the battle, he who in the blaze is ageless and unsated.

**6.15.6**  Kindle fire upon fire for you with fuel, serve your dear and ever-dear guest. Seek ye with songs the immortal one; for the god among gods wins the desirable thing — the god among gods wins our service.

**6.15.7**  With fuel and with song I praise the kindled Agni, the pure, the purifying, firm in front at the rite. The inspired Hotṛ, much-desired, not deceiving, the poet — we seek with goodwill Jātavedas.

**6.15.8**  Thee, O immortal Agni, age after age they have established as their messenger, the bearer of oblations, the guardian worthy of praise. Gods and mortals alike, with reverence, have settled the ever-wakeful, omnipresent lord of the people.

**6.15.9**  Adorning thyself, O Agni, following the ordinances of both sides, thou travellest as the messenger of the gods through the two spaces. When we embrace thy thought and thy benevolence, then be thou our triple-sheltering, auspicious one.

**6.15.10**  We who know little would worship him of fair face, beautiful to see, well-formed, yet knowing more than the wise. Knowing all ordinances, may he sacrifice; may Agni carry forth the oblation and declare it among the immortals.

**6.15.11**  Him thou protectest and thou ferryest across, O Agni, who has reached out to thee, the inspired one, O hero, with his devotion — or the keen edge of the sacrifice, or the arousal — him thou fillest with strength and wealth.

**6.15.12**  Protect us, O Agni, from him who would injure us; protect us, O strong one, from blame. May the nourishing drink come to thee in full abundance; may the thousandfold, coveted wealth come to thee fully.

**6.15.13**  Agni is the Hotṛ, the householder, he is the king; Jātavedas knows all births. He who is most deserving of worship among gods and among mortals — may that guardian of cosmic order sacrifice for us.

**6.15.14**  O Agni, what thou dost today for the people in the rite — O Hotṛ of pure flame, for thou art the sacrificer — thou dost sacrifice by cosmic order in thy greatness, when thou existest widely. O youngest one, bear the oblations that are thine today.

**6.15.15**  For the well-set offerings are to be seen; let the two worlds establish thee to sacrifice. Aid us, O generous one, in winning the prize, O Agni; may we cross over all misfortunes — may we cross them, may we cross them with thy help.

**6.15.16**  O Agni of fair countenance, with all the gods, sit first on the woolly seat. Lead the nest-like, ghee-filled sacrifice rightly to Savitr and the sacrificer.

**6.15.17**  This is that Agni whom the inspired priests churn as Atharvan did — him whom they brought, eager and unwearied, from the dark cows.

**6.15.18**  Be born for the gods' enjoyment, for complete welfare, for prosperity. Bring hither the immortal gods, the increasers of cosmic order; touch the sacrifice among the gods.

**6.15.19**  We have kindled thee great, O Agni, O householder of men, with fuel. May our domestic rites not be impeded; sharpen us with thy keen brilliance.

**6.16.1**  Thou, O Agni, art the Hotṛ of all sacrifices, established by the gods among humankind.

**6.16.2**  Do thou at the rite worship the great ones with thy gladdening tongues; bring the gods hither and sacrifice.

**6.16.3**  For thou dost know the paths and the roads to the gods in their fullness, O Agni of good understanding at the sacrifices.

**6.16.4**  Bharata invoked thee then a second time with the swift ones for blessings; he worshipped the one worthy of worship at the sacrifices.

**6.16.5**  Thou, the many desirable things, give to Divodāsa who presses the Soma, to Bharadvāja who presents the offering.

**6.16.6**  O immortal messenger, bring hither the divine folk, hearing the perfect praise of the inspired singer.

**6.16.7**  Thee, O Agni, the mortals of devout mind invoke as the god at the sacrifices for the gods' enjoyment.

**6.16.8**  The bountiful ones set forth thy visible form and thy will; may all the desiring ones take delight in it.

**6.16.9**  Thou, the Hotṛ established by Manu, the bearer in thy mouth, who knowest most — O Agni, worship the peoples of heaven.

**6.16.10**  O Agni, come hither, praised, for the feasting, for the gift of oblations; sit down as Hotṛ on the sacred grass.

**6.16.11**  Thee, O Aṅgiras, we increase with fuel-sticks and with ghee; blaze mightily, O youngest one.

**6.16.12**  Thou, O god, winnest for us wide, praiseworthy, vast heroic glory, O Agni.

**6.16.13**  Thee, O Agni, Atharvan churned out from the lotus, from the head of every eulogist.

**6.16.14**  Thee, the Ṛṣi Dadhyaṅ, son of Atharvan, kindled — thee the slayer of Vṛtra, the breaker of strongholds.

**6.16.15**  Thee, the bull of the paths, the most destroyer of the Dasyu, the winner of spoils in battle after battle, Pāthya kindled.

**6.16.16**  Come now; I would speak other words to thee, O Agni. With these drops of Soma grow great.

**6.16.17**  Wherever thou dost place thy mind and the higher power, there thou makest thy seat.

**6.74.2**  O Soma-Rudra, drive away in all directions the disease that has entered our house; drive dissolution and misfortune far away from us — may there be prosperity and great renown for us.

**6.74.3**  O Soma-Rudra, place all these medicines for us in our bodies; release, loosen from us whatever sin has been committed that is bound in our bodies.

**6.74.4**  O Soma-Rudra, of sharp weapons, of sharp missiles, most benevolent — be gracious to us here; release us from Varuṇa's snare, guard us, being well-disposed toward us.

**6.75.1**  His countenance becomes like a thunder-cloud when the armoured warrior goes into the lap of battles; conquer you with an unwounded body — may the greatness of the armour protect you.

**6.75.2**  With the bow may we win cattle, with the bow may we win battles, with the bow may we conquer the fierce conflicts; the bow renders the enemy's desire impotent — with the bow may we win all the regions.

**6.75.3**  As if about to speak, she whispers into the ear, embracing her dear friend — the bowstring stretched upon the bow twangs like a woman at the battle, carrying one across.

**6.75.4**  These two that move forward like a woman toward the battle-ground — may they, coming together, hold him on their lap as a mother holds her son; may these two bowstring-ends pierce the enemies, shooting, scattering the foes.

**6.75.5**  Father of many, many are his sons — the quiver makes a sound when it goes down into battle; bound on the back, it conquers all conflicts and the assembled arrays when the archer is urged forward.

**6.75.6**  Standing in the chariot, the skilled charioteer leads the horses forward whithersoever he desires; praise the greatness of the reins — the lines draw back the mind from behind.

**6.75.7**  The bull-handed horses make fierce battle-cries along with the chariots, striving for victory; stepping forward with their fore-hooves, they destroy the enemies, crushing the foes without retreat.

**6.75.8**  The chariot's burden — that is its name, the oblation — where the weapon is laid, where its armour is; there may we be seated beside the mighty chariot, always well-disposed.

**6.75.9**  Sweet to sit with, fathers, strength-givers, having taken refuge in hardship, powerful and deep; with brilliant armies, mighty in arrows, unyielding, rich in real heroes, broad, overcoming hosts.

**6.75.10**  Brāhmaṇas, fathers, Soma-drinkers — may Heaven and Earth be auspicious and unharming to us; may Pūṣan, increaser of holy order, guard us from evil — may no evil-speaker have power over us.

**6.75.11**  A bird's feather clothes it, the animal's tooth is hers — bound with cow's sinew she flies when launched; where men run together and apart, there may the arrows grant shelter to us.

**6.75.12**  O Arrow, ward off from us; may our body become stone; may Soma speak for us, may Aditi grant us shelter.

**6.75.13**  She strikes against their backs, touches their hindquarters — O horse-whip of the discerning one, urge the horses forward in the battles.

**6.75.14**  Like a serpent with its coils it winds around the arm, the bowstring repelling the dart; the arm-guard knowing all the ways of motion — may this man guard that man on all sides.

**6.75.15**  She that is painted red, with the head of the roebuck, and whose mouth is of iron — to this divine arrow, born of the seed of Parjanya, great homage.

**6.75.16**  Released, fly forth, O shaft, sharpened by Brahman prayer; fly to the enemies, strike among them — do not leave any one of these alive.

**6.75.17**  Where the arrows fly together like boys with unbound hair — there may Brahmaṇaspati grant us shelter, may Aditi grant us shelter, grant us shelter always.

**6.75.18**  I cover your vital spots with armour; may King Soma clothe you about with immortality; may Varuṇa make broad space for you, broad and wider still — may the gods rejoice following you as you conquer.

**6.75.19**  He who desires to slay us, whether one of our own or a stranger — may all the gods confound him; the sacred prayer is my innermost armour.
