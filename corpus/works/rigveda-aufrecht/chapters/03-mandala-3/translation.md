**3.1.1**  O Agni, Soma's mighty one, you bear me — you have been made the conveyor at the sacrifice for worship. Shining toward the gods you harness the pressing-stone; for peace, O Agni, be pleased with your own body.

**3.1.2**  We have directed the sacrifice eastward; let our song increase while we honour Agni with fuel and reverence. The wise ones who have ruled the assemblies of heaven have sought a path even to the mighty one for the Gṛtsamadas.

**3.1.3**  The wise one of pure skill, kinsman of heaven by birth and of earth, has established the seat of joy. The gods found Agni, the visible one, dwelling within the waters, amid the work of the sister-streams.

**3.1.4**  The seven great rivers nurtured the blessed one, born white and ruddy in his greatness. The divine mares ran around Agni at his birth, as though around a new-born child, glorying in his beauty.

**3.1.5**  Spreading his domain with bright limbs, purifying wisdom with the strainers of the seers, clothing himself in flame, measuring out the life-span of the waters — he fashions their great splendours, full and unfailing.

**3.1.6**  The mighty ones of heaven, inviolate and fireless, have wandered about not crying out, coming to rest. Here the seven voices, ever young and of one womb, have held together the single embryo.

**3.1.7**  His forms, all gathered together and all-shaped, are spread in the womb of ghee at the flowing of the sweet liquors. There the swelling cows have stood; the two great mothers of the wondrous one have met together.

**3.1.8**  Shining brown, the son of strength has blazed forth, bearing bright and vigorous forms. The streams of honey and of ghee drip down where the bull has grown through his poetic power.

**3.1.9**  By his birth he found even the udder of the father; he poured forth his streams and loosed the milk-cows. Moving in secret with his benevolent companions, through the great powers of heaven, he was no longer hidden in the cave.

**3.1.10**  He bore the embryo of both father and mother; alone he sucked the many swelling ones. The two co-wives of the gleaming bull, kindred to each other, both human — protect them for him.

**3.1.11**  In wide space the great one, unimpeded, has grown, for many glorious waters have come together in Agni. The householder has lain in the womb of Ṛta; Agni rests in the work of the kindred sister-streams.

**3.1.12**  Brown like a hawk in the encounter of the great ones, brilliant Agni, wishing to be seen, comes to his son. He who begot the ruddy cows — the great young Agni, embryo of the waters, most manly — has risen up.

**3.1.13**  The auspicious one has engendered the visible embryo of the waters and the forests and the herbs, of manifold forms. Even the gods came together in mind to honour the most admirable one, the mighty one just born.

**3.1.14**  Great are the bright rays that follow brilliant Agni like lightnings; grown large as if in a cave, within his own seat, in the wide, unbounded expanse they milk for him the immortal draught.

**3.1.15**  The sacrificer praises you with oblations and eagerly praises your friendship and your favour. Grant protection to the singer together with the gods, and guard us with your domestic forces.

**3.1.16**  O Agni of excellent guidance, may those who dwell near you, holding all wealth, impelling with good seed and fame, may we prevail over the godless ones who make war.

**3.1.17**  O Agni, knowing all poetic wisdom, you have become the brilliant beacon of the gods. As the householder you have set men to face the task; you go as the charioteer after the gods, accomplishing your aim.

**3.1.18**  The immortal has sat down as king in the dwelling of mortals, accomplishing the affairs of the assembly. With face glistening with ghee, Agni has shone out broadly, knowing all things worthy of the sage.

**3.1.19**  Come to us, great one, swift-moving with your benevolent friendships, with your great aids. Grant us abundant and ample wealth, a share of fair speech and renown.

**3.1.20**  These, O Agni, are your ancient births; I have proclaimed new ones for the primordial. Great pressings have been set in motion for the bull; birth after birth the Knower-of-all-births has been established.

**3.1.21**  Birth after birth the Knower-of-all-births is established; he is kindled unceasing by the Viśvāmitras. May we be in the auspicious favour and fair benevolence of that god worthy of worship.

**3.1.22**  O mighty one, place this our sacrifice among the gods, O Agni of good resolve, bestowing gifts. Do you send forth our great nourishments, O Hotar; O Agni, win great wealth for us by sacrifice.

**3.1.23**  O Agni, accomplish for the worshipper Iḷā of many wonders, the ever-abiding winning of the cow. May there be for us a son and offspring rich in descendants; O Agni, may that favour of yours be upon us.

**3.2.1**  For Vaiśvānara who augments the Truth, for Agni, we proclaim wisdom like clarified butter; the axe fashions both the Hotar of man and the praising singer, as a chariot is built with insight.

**3.2.2**  By his birth he illumined both world-halves; he became the praiseworthy son of his two mothers. Agni, the carrier of oblations, unaging, delightful in food, hard to deceive, guest of the peoples, rich in light.

**3.2.3**  By the will and skill of the prevailing one, in the ordering of things, the gods engendered Agni with their thoughts. I invoke the great, shining one with radiance and light, as one who is about to win a prize like a swift horse.

**3.2.4**  Wishing to win, we choose the chosen, the excellent prize of the joyful one, the boldly-won, song-worthy; the gift of the Bhṛgus, the zealous one, of poet's wisdom, Agni shining with his heavenly flame.

**3.2.5**  The peoples here have set Agni in the fore for their well-being, the winner of prizes and fame, they who have strewn the sacred grass; with ladles stretched out they place the brilliantly gleaming, all-divine, Rudra-like Agni, the fulfiller of sacrificial purpose and of works.

**3.2.6**  O Hotar of pure flame, the men who have strewn the sacred grass at sacrifices move around your dwelling. O Agni, those who seek your service attend to that which they may obtain; bestow wealth upon them.

**3.2.7**  When the working waters supported him at his birth, he filled both world-halves and the great sun. That seer, delightful in food, is led about for the sacrifice like a swift horse intent on winning the prize.

**3.2.8**  Honour the giver of oblations and performer of sacrifice; serve the Knower-of-all-births who dwells in the house. The charioteer of great Ṛta, all-pervading, Agni has become the Purohita of the gods.

**3.2.9**  The zealous, immortal ones have purified the three fuel-loads of the wide-ranging great Agni. Of these three, they gave one for the enjoyment of mortals; the other two have gone to their kindred abode.

**3.2.10**  The human refreshments made the seer and lord of the clans into a hatchet-like sharpness for their aim. He goes flickering up hills and into valleys; he has maintained the embryo in these worlds.

**3.2.11**  Born knowing, he pleases those in whose bellies he dwells; the bull-like one roars magnificently like a lion. Vaiśvānara of wide radiance, immortal, distributes goods and precious things to the worshipper.

**3.2.12**  Vaiśvānara has ascended to the vault of heaven, to the back of the sky, rejoicing with holy praises. Wakeful, like the sun of old, generating wealth for the creature, he traverses the same course.

**3.2.13**  We implore the law-abiding, sacrificeable, inspired, worthy-of-praise one, whom Mātariśvan established with his celestial dwelling — the radiant Agni of golden hair, the well-illumined one, for ever-fresh well-being.

**3.2.14**  With reverence we implore the pure one, vigorous in his course, beholder of the sun, beacon stationed in the bright space of heaven, waking at dawn — Agni the summit of the sky, whom none may resist, the greatly mighty.

**3.2.15**  We always implore for wealth the joyful Hotar, the pure one of single purpose, the household god, worthy of praise, beholding all peoples — visible as a bright chariot for beauty, installed by Manu.

**3.3.1**  The inspired ones have placed their precious offerings for Vaiśvānara of wide radiance, for a path among the foundations. For Agni, the immortal, serves the gods, and has not violated the eternal laws.

**3.3.2**  The wondrous messenger travels between both worlds; the Hotar, the Purohita of man, is seated. Rich in wisdom, sent by the gods through heavenly days, Agni adorns the great abode.

**3.3.3**  The inspired ones magnify with their thoughts Agni, the beacon of sacrifices, the accomplisher of the assembly. In him upon whom the poets have joined their songs of praise, the sacrificer delights in favours.

**3.3.4**  Agni, the lord, father of sacrifices, the measurescape and the knowledge of those who praise — the seer of many forms has entered both worlds, a favourite of many; he is glorified with his abodes.

**3.3.5**  The gleaming Agni of gleaming chariot, whose vow is golden, Vaiśvānara who dwells in the waters and finds the light, penetrating swiftly and surrounded with mighty powers, fiery — the gods have placed the beautifully splendid one here.

**3.3.6**  Agni, along with the gods and all mortal beings, fashions by thought the sacrifice of many ornaments. The swift householder travels within, accomplishing his aims as charioteer, averting all attacks.

**3.3.7**  O Agni, thrive with excellent offspring through the life-spans; swell with strength; kindle our nourishments. Set the branches in motion, vigilant great one; you are the eager one and clear-minded of the gods and of the seers.

**3.3.8**  The men always extol with reverence and impulses to increase the lord of the clan, the great one, the guest, the guide of thoughts, the zealous one of the praisers, the insightful Knower-of-all-births of the rites.

**3.3.9**  The broadly-shining god, joyful, has become Agni of great chariot with his might, going beyond all habitations. May we adorn his vows as those who bring him many riches, with our good prayers in the house.

**3.3.10**  O Vaiśvānara, I delight in your abodes by which you became the discerning finder of light. Being born, you filled all worlds and both spheres; O Agni, you are all-encompassing of yourself.

**3.3.11**  The seer alone has emitted a great stream from the wondrous powers of Vaiśvānara through his own artistry. Agni was born magnifying both parents, heaven and earth, rich in seed.

**3.4.1**  Fuel-stave by fuel-stave, be kindly wakeful for us; flame by flame, bestow favour along with wealth. O god, bring the gods here for the sacrifice; as their friend you shall worship your friends with a good mind, O Agni.

**3.4.2**  The one whom the gods — Varuṇa, Mitra, Agni — honour three times on each day: make this sweet sacrifice of ours, which Tanūnapāt and Ghee-womb have distributed, a success.

**3.4.3**  The all-chosen sacred thought goes forth to honour the Hotar as the first for worship. To approach the bull with homages and praise him — so summoned and most sacrificing, let him worship the gods.

**3.4.4**  Your path at the sacrifice has been made upright; the flames have departed upward, reaching the realms. Or the Hotar has been set down at the navel of heaven; we shall strew the sacred grass wide enough for the gods.

**3.4.5**  Choosing the seven priestly functions with the mind, driving all things forward going by the Truth, the many born of man-like appearance among the assemblies have spread out through this sacrifice.

**3.4.6**  As the two Dawns, glorying and nearby, smile with their diverse forms, may Mitra and Varuṇa, and Indra with his Maruts, or the great ones, be delighted with us.

**3.4.7**  I have set in place the two divine Hotars; the seven refreshments exult with their own power. Proclaiming the Truth, they speak only the Truth, meditating on the ordinance as its guardians.

**3.4.8**  Let Bhāratī with the Bhāratīs, Iḷā in accord with gods and men, Agni, and Sarasvatī together with the Sarasvatīs — let these three goddesses sit on this sacred grass.

**3.4.9**  O divine Tvaṣṭar, bestow upon us that nourishing, life-sustaining vigour, freely giving; from which a hero is born, active in work, of good skill, who works the pressing-stones and desires the gods.

**3.4.10**  O Lord of the forest, release the gods; Agni the sacrificer sets the oblation flowing. He alone as Hotar, truer than others, worships as one who knows the origins of the gods.

**3.4.11**  Come hither, O Agni, kindled, in the same chariot as swift Indra and the gods. Let Aditi of good sons sit on the sacred grass; let the immortal gods be gladdened with Svāhā.

**3.5.1**  Agni, alert as the Dawns, the inspired seer, woke and became aware of the paths of the seers. Kindled with wide brilliance by the god-fearing, the conveyor has opened the doors of darkness.

**3.5.2**  Agni has indeed grown through the praises of the singers, with their songs and lauds worthy of homage. Delighting in many visions of the Truth, the messenger has shone forth together with the breaking of the Dawns.

**3.5.3**  Agni has been placed in human clans — the embryo of the waters, like Mitra, accomplishing by the Truth. The desirable and worshipful one has stood upon the ridge; the inspired one has become worthy of offerings of the prayers.

**3.5.4**  Agni becomes Mitra when kindled; the Hotar, the Knower-of-all-births, is Varuṇa. The vigorous householder as Adhvaryu is Mitra; he is Mitra of the rivers and of the mountains.

**3.5.5**  He guards the dear foremost step of the thievish bird; the great one guards the course of the sun. Agni, sublime, guards at the navel the seven-headed one; he guards the highest seat of the gods.

**3.5.6**  The skillful Ṛbhu has made the praiseworthy and lovely name; the god knows all the guidance-signs. He has lain on the skin of the grain, the ghee-rich step of the bird — that Agni guards without wavering.

**3.5.7**  Agni, desirous, has taken his place in the ghee-rich womb, widely extending and longed-for. Blazing, pure, sublime, and purifying, he renews the two mothers again and again.

**3.5.8**  Newly born, he has swelled with the plants when the wombs nourish with ghee. Like waters shining down a slope, Agni blazes in the lap of the parents, keeping them safe.

**3.5.9**  Praised, the great one has blazed upward, kindled with fuel, at the summit of heaven and the navel of the earth. Agni is Mitra, worthy of praise; as Mātariśvan the messenger he shall bring the gods for worship.

**3.5.10**  Sublime Agni, being the highest among the shining ones, has propped up the vault of heaven with his fuel. When Mātariśvan kindled from the Bhṛgus the hidden carrier of oblations.

**3.5.11**  O Agni, accomplish for the worshipper Iḷā of many wonders, the ever-abiding winning of the cow. May there be for us a son and offspring rich in descendants; O Agni, may that favour of yours be upon us.

**3.6.1**  The Kārus, being celebrated with thoughts, lead forward toward the divine as god-seekers. The prize-bearing, hymn-strong one goes eastward carrying oblation; the ghee-pouring one goes south to Agni.

**3.6.2**  Being born, you filled both worlds, and then left them to be enjoyed; now, O adorable one, let your seven-tongued conveyors be celebrated with your greatness of heaven and earth.

**3.6.3**  Heaven and earth, worthy of worship, set you down as Hotar for the house. When human tribes, desiring the gods, rich in offerings, invoke the bright flame.

**3.6.4**  The great one, fixed, has sat down firmly in his common seat, between great heaven and earth, being praised. In the lap sit the two co-wives, unaging and immortal, milk-cows of the far-ranging one who milk together.

**3.6.5**  Your vows, O Agni, are great and mighty; by your will you have stretched out both world-halves. You became the messenger at birth; you are the leader, O bull, of human beings.

**3.6.6**  Harness Ṛta's two long-maned, yoke-fitting, ghee-dripping red steeds to the chariot-pole. Then bring all the gods, O divine Jātavedas, make the sacrifice well-performed.

**3.6.7**  From heaven itself your flames shine out; you illumine many dawns as they blaze forth. When you, O Agni, glow with longing in the forests, the gods marvel at the joyful Hotar.

**3.6.8**  Whether gods exult in the wide atmosphere, or are in the bright space of heaven, or those of easy invocation, worthy of worship who are helpers — all like chariot horses have come to you, O Agni.

**3.6.9**  Come hither, O Agni, in the same chariot with these, or in separate chariots — for the horses are many. Bring the thirty-three gods with their wives in proper order; be gladdened.

**3.6.10**  He is the Hotar for whom even the two broad worlds speak praise for the increase of every sacrifice. Both stand firm, fair and eastward like a rite — the two truth-containing ones in the truth of the one born of Truth.

**3.6.11**  O Agni, accomplish for the worshipper Iḷā of many wonders, the ever-abiding winning of the cow. May there be for us a son and offspring rich in descendants; O Agni, may that favour of yours be upon us.

**3.7.1**  The seven voices have climbed up and entered the two mothers of the white-backed one's stream. The two all-encircling parents travel together and pour forth long life for worship.

**3.7.2**  The divine milk-cows, the horses of the bull, sky-nourishing, bearing sweetness, have stood at their station. Around you, at rest in the seat of Ṛta, one cow walks the path.

**3.7.3**  They climbed up, becoming easily yoked; the knowing master, finder of riches. The blue-backed one kindled the stream of the desert plant; the many-formed one has made them blaze.

**3.7.4**  The vigorous ones carry the great work of Tvaṣṭar, imperishable and holding firm. Flashing through his limbs in the common seat, he has entered the two worlds as if they were one.

**3.7.5**  They know the delight of the ruddy bull; they rejoice at the command of the tawny one. Shining with the lights of heaven, brilliantly shining — their glorious, great song is Iḷā.

**3.7.6**  From the two great parents, following the call, they have led the strength. The bull has laid its sheath on the night; it has swelled following the praiser's own abode.

**3.7.7**  Seven inspired ones with five Adhvaryus guard the beloved hidden step of the bird. The ageless bulls exult going forward; the gods have followed the divine ordinances.

**3.7.8**  I have set in place the two divine Hotars foremost; the seven refreshments exult with their own power. Proclaiming the Truth, they speak only the Truth, meditating on the ordinance as its guardians.

**3.7.9**  Many rays well-steered for the great swift steed, for the brilliant bull, are being bullish. O divine Hotar, more gladdening and discerning, bring the great gods and both worlds here.

**3.7.10**  Wealthy, of fair speech, with good beacons, the offering-rich ones have shone brilliantly at the Dawns. But even the sin that has been done, O Agni, do thou remit by the greatness of the earth.

**3.7.11**  O Agni, accomplish for the worshipper Iḷā of many wonders, the ever-abiding winning of the cow. May there be for us a son and offspring rich in descendants; O Agni, may that favour of yours be upon us.

**3.8.1**  God-fearing men anoint you at the sacrifice with divine sweetness, O Lord of the forest — whether you stand erect, placing wealth here, or whether your resting-place is in this your mother's lap.

**3.8.2**  Leaning in front of the kindled one, winning unaging and excellent manly strength with sacred speech — pushing poverty far from us, rise up for great good fortune.

**3.8.3**  Rise up, O Lord of the forest, upon the height of earth. Being well-trimmed, bestow vigour on the one who carries the sacrifice.

**3.8.4**  The youth, well-clad and adorned, has come; he becomes more excellent as he is born. The steadfast seers who are of good insight, who desire the gods with their mind, lead him up.

**3.8.5**  Born, he is being born in the goodly daylight of the days, growing in the contest in the assembly. The steadfast ones purify their works with wisdom; the inspired one, devoted to the gods, raises the voice.

**3.8.6**  The posts which the god-fearing men have fixed, O Lord of the forest, or which the axe has hewn — may the gods, self-luminous, established in them, bestow upon us a treasure rich in offspring.

**3.8.7**  Those cut down and set in the earth with ladles extended — may they, the accomplishers of the sacred ground, obtain for us among the gods the desired prize.

**3.8.8**  The Ādityas, Rudras, Vasus, of fair guidance, heaven and earth, the atmosphere — may the gods of one accord protect the sacrifice; may they raise high the beacon of the rite.

**3.8.9**  Like geese moving in a line, white-clad and self-luminous, they have come to us. Led up forward by the seers, the gods are going to the realm of the gods.

**3.8.10**  The self-luminous ones with their capitals, like horns among the horned, are seen on the earth. Listening to the call of the praisers, may they aid us in the winning of battles.

**3.8.11**  O Lord of the forest, grow with a hundred branches; may we grow with a thousand branches. May this whetted axe that has shaped you bring great good fortune.

**3.9.1**  We, mortals as friends, have chosen you, the divine, for our aid — Grandson of the Waters, the blessed, the clearly-blazing, the easily-victorious, the faultless.

**3.9.2**  Longing for the forests, you went and entered the mother waters; O Agni, your return cannot be forgotten — that, being far away, you were present here.

**3.9.3**  You have borne even what is sharp and bitter, and still you are well-disposed. Some go forward and forward; others sit around — those on whose friendship you depend.

**3.9.4**  He who had gone past many failures, past those who persistently hated him — the wise and guileless ones found him, established in the waters like a lion.

**3.9.5**  Mātariśvan led this Agni, who was thus hidden as though fleeing on his own, from afar — churned for the gods from the distance.

**3.9.6**  Mortals have seized you, O carrier of oblations, for the gods — O youngest, by your will you oversee all human sacrifices.

**3.9.7**  That your wondrous power is pleasing even to the simple one — when, O Agni, the animals sit around you, kindled on the dark night.

**3.9.8**  Pour the oblation to the self-performing sacrificer, the fierce one of pure flame. Attend with offerings to the swift messenger, the quick, ancient, praiseworthy, helpful deity.

**3.9.9**  Three hundred, three thousand, and thirty-nine gods have served Agni. They sprinkled him with ghees and strewed the sacred grass for him; then at last they seated him as Hotar.

**3.10.1**  You, O Agni, the thoughtful mortals kindle at the sacrifice — the universal king of peoples, the divine one.

**3.10.2**  You, O Agni, they invoke at sacrifices as Hotar and seasonal priest. O guardian of the Truth, blaze in your own house.

**3.10.3**  He who pays homage to you, O Knower-of-all-births, with fuel — that man, O Agni, obtains excellent manly strength; he flourishes.

**3.10.4**  That Agni, the beacon of the rites, shall come with the gods, anointed with seven Hotars, to him who holds the oblation.

**3.10.5**  Carry forth to the Hotar's office the mighty ancient speech for Agni, who bears the lights of the seers and the seer-gift.

**3.10.6**  May our songs increase Agni, from whom the lauded one is born — the visible one, for great vigour and wealth.

**3.10.7**  O Agni, most skilled at the rite, worship the gods for the god-fearing man. The joyful Hotar, you shine beyond the failures.

**3.10.8**  O purifier, blaze out for us luminously; for us be there excellent heroic strength. Be the nearest to the praisers for their well-being.

**3.10.9**  The inspired, greatly-praising ones, vigilant, kindle you — the immortal carrier of oblations who grows with might.

**3.11.1**  Agni is the Hotar and Purohita of the rite, the all-seeing one. He knows the sacrifice in proper order.

**3.11.2**  He is the immortal carrier of oblations, the eager messenger delightful in food — Agni is set in motion by wisdom.

**3.11.3**  Agni is aware through wisdom; he is the first and foremost beacon of the sacrifice. For his boat crosses to the goal.

**3.11.4**  The gods have made Agni, the son of strength, the Knower-of-all-births praised of old, their conveyor.

**3.11.5**  Agni is the inviolable forerunner of human clans — the chariot always swift and ever new.

**3.11.6**  Agni, unimpeded, who has overcome all challengers, the will of the gods — Agni of the most widespread fame.

**3.11.7**  The mortal who has sacrificed wins the gifts and the chariot-load — the abode of the one of pure flame.

**3.11.8**  We, inspired ones, may reach with our prayers all the well-placed gifts of Agni, the Knower-of-all-births.

**3.11.9**  O Agni, we shall win all desirable things in the contest; the gods have roused themselves in you.

**3.12.1**  O Indra and Agni, come with songs to the pressed soma, to the excellent one of the clouds. You who are sent by prayer — drink of this.

**3.12.3**  With the swiftness of the sacrifice I choose Indra and Agni as shelter of the seer. Let the two of them here be satisfied with the soma.

**3.12.4**  I invoke the two most effective, the slayers of Vṛtra, the two who always conquer and are never defeated — Indra and Agni, the most winning of prizes.

**3.12.5**  The singers who know the hymns, who praise with lauds, praise the two of you. I choose refreshment from Indra and Agni.

**3.12.7**  The prayers go forward and around the works of Indra and Agni, following the paths of the Truth.

**3.12.8**  O Indra and Agni, your mighty common seats and gifts — what prize of the water-filling is placed within you both.

**3.12.9**  O Indra and Agni, you adorn the shining spaces of heaven in the prizes. That heroic power of yours is clearly visible.

**3.13.1**  Sing forth the highest praise to the divine Agni; may the most skilled sacrificer come to us with the gods and sit on the sacred grass.

**3.13.2**  The law-abiding one whose both worlds and aids accompany his skill — those who have offerings invoke him; those seeking to win invoke him for protection.

**3.13.3**  He is the guide of these inspired ones and of their sacrifices — for it is truly so. Honour that Agni who is the giver and winner of bounty.

**3.13.4**  Let Agni grant us the most beneficent shelters for our enjoyment — from which refreshing wealth comes to us from heaven, from the sky, from the waters.

**3.13.5**  The reciters kindle with his auspicious thoughts the unrivalled blazing Agni, the Hotar and lord of the clans.

**3.13.6**  Moreover, O priest, be beneficent to us in our lauds, O most invoked of gods. O Agni, who grows with the Maruts, most winning of thousands, blaze out auspiciously for us.

**3.60.6**  O Indra, together with the Ṛbhus and the prize-winners, rejoice here in us at this pressing by your power, O much-praised one. These daily observances have been set in order for you through the ordinances of the gods and of Manu.

**3.60.7**  O Indra, together with the Ṛbhus and the prize-winners, seeking prizes, come here to the sacrificial hymn of the singer. With a hundred swift banners, O guide of thousands, come to the oblation-pouring of this sacrifice.

**3.61.1**  O Uṣas, possessing prize-wealth, prize-winning, all-knowing — be pleased with the hymn of the singer, O bountiful one. O ancient goddess, ever-young, full of insight, you walk according to the eternal ordinance, O all-desired one.

**3.61.2**  O Uṣas, immortal goddess, shine forth — you who travel in a bright chariot, bringing pleasant truths. Let the well-guided steeds bear you, golden-hued, broad in splendor.

**3.61.3**  O Uṣas, facing all beings of the universe, you stand high as the banner of immortality. Moving toward the same goal, roll forward like the wheel of a new (wagon).

**3.61.4**  Gathering like threads from a spindle, O bountiful Dawn, the consort of the sun goes forth. Generating light, the fortunate one of beautiful works has spread herself from the edge of the sky to the earth.

**3.61.5**  Toward the shining goddess Uṣas bring forward your well-fashioned hymn of praise with homage. The honey-placing one has stretched her power aloft in the sky; the radiant spaces have blazed forth, delightful to behold.

**3.61.6**  The guardian of Order was awakened by the rays of the sky; the rich one has stood brilliantly on both world-halves. O Agni, you who beg, you approach lovely wealth — the far-coming, brightly shining Uṣas.

**3.61.7**  From the foundation of cosmic Order, urging on the Dawns, the bull has entered the mighty heaven-and-earth. The great magic wisdom of Mitra and Varuṇa has spread its light in many places like the moon.

**3.62.1**  These Bhṛgus, meditating upon you, have become (powerful) like missiles driven by you two. Where, O Indra and Varuṇa, is that glory of yours by which you ever bring gain to your friends?

**3.62.2**  This one, most numerous of your worshippers, rich in wealth, calls out again and again for your help. O Indra and Varuṇa, united with the Maruts, hear my invocation from heaven and earth.

**3.62.3**  May that treasure be ours, O Indra and Varuṇa; may that wealth of all heroes be ours, O Maruts. May the protective goddesses shelter us with their shelters; may Bhāratī (Sarasvatī), as Hotrā, (favor) us with her bounties.

**3.62.4**  O Bṛhaspati, lord of all the gods, be pleased with our offerings. Bestow precious gifts upon the pious worshipper.

**3.62.5**  Worship Bṛhaspati the pure with hymns at the sacrifices. He has shown forth matchless strength.

**3.62.6**  Bṛhaspati the bull of the peoples, all-formed, undeceivable — him we choose as most excellent.

**3.62.7**  O Pūṣan, O radiant god, this newest excellent praise-song is recited by us for you.

**3.62.8**  Be pleased with this speech of mine that seeks the prize; promote (my) thought — as a bridegroom (desires) a maiden.

**3.62.9**  He who looks out over all the worlds and surveys them together — may that Pūṣan be our protector.

**3.62.10**  We meditate upon that most excellent splendor of divine Savitṛ — may he impel our thoughts.

**3.62.11**  We, seeking the prize with (our) insight, implore the divine gift of Savitṛ (and) of Bhaga.

**3.62.12**  The inspired men, urged by prayer, worship divine Savitṛ with well-formed sacrifices and with homage.

**3.62.13**  Soma goes, knowing the way; he proceeds to the purified (dwelling) of the gods. He takes his seat in the womb of cosmic Order.

**3.62.14**  May Soma make nourishing foods, free from disease, for us — both for the two-footed and the four-footed creature.

**3.62.15**  Increasing our life-span, overwhelming our adversaries, may Soma take his seat in the common gathering-place.

**3.62.16**  O Mitra and Varuṇa, sprinkle our pasture with ghee; O you of good wisdom, bedew the realms with sweetness.

**3.62.17**  O widely praised ones, growing great through homage, you two reign by the greatness of (your) power. With your longest (reach), O pure in vow.

**3.62.18**  Being praised by Jamadagni, sit in the womb of cosmic Order. Drink the Soma, O you who strengthen Order.
