**8.1.1**  Praise nothing else, O friends, and suffer no harm; be a praiser of Indra the bull, attending him at the pressing; again and again recite the lauds.

**8.1.2**  Like a bull who charges without bend, like an ox bearing the plough-yoke unwearied — he who dispels enmity, who wins all over, most munificent, the ally of both sides.

**8.1.3**  Though these many peoples here invoke you diversely for help, let this our prayer, O Indra, be yours; may it be your strengthening through all the days.

**8.1.4**  The inspired poets of the people, the bards of noble rank, spread their hymns to you, O Maghavan; advance upon the many-formed prize, bring the booty nearest to us for our aid.

**8.1.5**  Not even for a great price would I give you away, O wielder of the stone; not for a thousand, not for ten thousand, O thunderbolt-bearer, not for a hundred, O hundred-gifted one.

**8.1.6**  You are more to me than a father, than a brother who withholds; even my mother you and wealth gratify equally, O Vasu, for the sake of riches and bounty.

**8.1.7**  Where are you, where do you go? Your mind wanders to many places. You are roused, O warrior, O destroyer of citadels — the Gāyatrā metres have gone forth.

**8.1.8**  Sing the Gāyatrā hymn to him, to him who has been quickened, the fort-destroyer; with these very metres may the thunderer come to sit upon the barhis of Kāṇva and may he smash the forts.

**8.1.9**  Those steeds of yours that are in tens, those in hundreds, those in thousands, your bulls that run swiftly — with those come quickly to us.

**8.1.10**  Today I call forth for the Gāyatrā-trembling one the cow that yields savours; Indra is the good milch-cow, another, the food of broad stream, well fashioned.

**8.1.11**  When the sun urged on Etaśa the fleet, winged like the wind, and the hundred-powered Indra conveyed Kutsa the son of Arjuna, tracking the undefeated Gandharva.

**8.1.12**  He who even without prayer, before the collarbone could be pierced, is a joiner of joints, the Maghavan of many treasures, who repairs what has gone wrong.

**8.1.13**  May we not be like strangers, O Indra, remote from you, like abandoned forests; hard to satisfy, we, the stone-wielder's people, thought of wealth.

**8.1.14**  We pondered, lacking swift horses and lacking fodder, O Vṛtra-slayer; but once with your great bounty, O hero, may we exult following your laud.

**8.1.15**  If Indra would hearken to our laud, may the swift soma-drops that have flowed through the filter gladden him, him who grows mighty for Tugrya's descendants.

**8.1.16**  Come today, urged on to this joint praise, come from your friend's side; may the praise of the bountiful ones go before you — for you I desire excellent laud.

**8.1.17**  The pressers press the soma with stones; rinse it in the waters; men have clothed it as with a robe of cowhide and have drawn it from the flanks.

**8.1.18**  Whether from the earth or from the broad sky's luminous realm above, grow great with your body through this hymn of mine; O wise one, fill what has been born.

**8.1.19**  Press out for Indra the most intoxicating, most excellent soma; may the mighty one satiate himself with it, with every thought urging it like a racer for the prize.

**8.1.20**  Let me not make you weary, O Indra, always begging with my songs over the soma; you who rage like a wild beast at the pressings — who would not beg the sovereign lord?

**8.1.21**  The fierce rapture impelled by exhilaration, with fierce might, the overcomer of all, the one shaken from ecstasy — for in rapture he gives to us.

**8.1.22**  The god grants many desirable things to the mortal who has offered; he gives to the one who presses soma and to one who praises — praised by all, unharmed in renown.

**8.1.23**  Come, O Indra, rejoice with your brilliant bounty, O god; fill your broad belly with the soma draughts together with your drinking companions, as a lake fills up.

**8.1.24**  A thousand, a hundred, harnessed to your golden chariot — let the long-maned bays yoked by prayer, O Indra, bring you here to drink the soma.

**8.1.25**  On your golden chariot let the two tawny coursers with peacock-feathered tails and white backs convey you here for drinking the sweet exhilarating juice.

**8.1.26**  Drink of this, O hymn-lover, as the first drinker drinks the pressed soma; this offering of the refined, flavoursome juice is pleasant, fitted for exhilaration.

**8.1.27**  He who is alone in his wonder-deeds, great and fierce in his ordinances — may that broad-jawed one come; he will not stay behind; coming, he does not avoid the call.

**8.1.28**  You crushed with blows the roving fort of Śuṣṇa; you followed the light then, when you, O Indra, became one to be called upon in the second pressing also.

**8.1.29**  For me at sunrise, for me at midday of the sky, for me at the forward journey even in the dark season, O Vasu, have the lauds turned to you.

**8.1.30**  Praise, do praise — these are your most bountiful ones, O you of the patrons' gifts: Ninditāśva, Prапаthī, Paramajyā, Magha — thus, O Medhyātithi.

**8.1.31**  When in faith I mounted the chariot of Vananvat's horses, then is the wealth of the beautiful known to him who is the cattle-rich descendant of Yadu.

**8.1.32**  He who has bestowed upon me the bright bay horse together with golden trappings — may this chariot of Āsaṅga, resounding with good fortune, excel all things.

**8.1.33**  Then Plāyogi surpassed others; Āsaṅga, O Agni, with ten thousands; ten bright bulls he gave me, gleaming, they stood forth from the pond as if from a lake.

**8.1.34**  Before him is seen the broad thigh hanging down without bone; many a woman, beholding it, has said: 'You bear, O noble one, a splendid prize.'

**8.2.1**  This pressed soma-juice, O Vasu — drink of it; fill full your belly; we have poured it out for you without fear.

**8.2.2**  Shaken by men, pressed with stones, filtered through the wool of sheep, purified — like a horse washed in the rivers.

**8.2.3**  Like barley sweetened with milk, we have prepared it for you, O Indra, at this joint celebration.

**8.2.4**  Indra alone is the soma-drinker; Indra alone drinks the pressed juice, the all-living; he stands between gods and mortals.

**8.2.5**  Neither the bright one, nor the evil-wisher, nor the satisfied can turn away the wide-ranging one, the good-hearted Indra.

**8.2.6**  When others hunt after him as a herd hunts a wild beast — with cows away from us — they approach him with milch-cows.

**8.2.7**  Let three soma-pressings of the god Indra be made, of him who drinks the pressed juice in his own dwelling.

**8.2.8**  Three vessels trickle down; the three bowls are well full, all upon the same support.

**8.2.9**  Pure you are, established in many places, mixed with milk in the middle, most exhilarating with curds — for the hero.

**8.2.10**  These sharp soma-draughts, O Indra, pressed here among us, bright, call for the admixture of milk.

**8.2.11**  Mix the admixture and the cake offering; O Indra, prepare this soma — for I hear that you are wealthy.

**8.2.12**  Having drunk in their hearts they fight, like madmen with spirituous drink; they shout like a naked udder.

**8.2.13**  The praiser of such a wealthy patron of prizes would himself be wealthy; go forward then, O bay-horsed one of renown.

**8.2.14**  Even a laud being recited — the unfriendly man of no gifts does not heed it; nor does he heed the Gāyatrā being sung.

**8.2.15**  O Indra, do not hand us over to the mocker, do not hand us over to the arrogant one; give of your bounty, O powerful one, with your powers.

**8.2.16**  We indeed, for this very purpose, the Kāṇvas your friends wishing you near, laud you with hymns.

**8.2.17**  Nothing else was gained in the newest works of the craftsman, O thunderer; it is your laud alone that has been desired.

**8.2.18**  The gods desire the one who presses soma; they do not long for sleep; the unwearied ones go to the feast of joy.

**8.2.19**  Come forth now with prizes; do not be wrathful toward us, like a great man with a young wife.

**8.2.20**  Let not the ill-willed one do evil to us today at evening from afar, like an ill-favoured son-in-law.

**8.2.21**  For we know the bountiful good will and the three-fold-born mind of this heroic one.

**8.2.22**  Pour in with the Kāṇvas present — for we know nothing more glorious than the hundred-aided one of might.

**8.2.23**  Press for Indra, O presser, the excellent soma, for the hero, for the mighty; bring it for the manly one to drink.

**8.2.24**  He who is most knowing in unmoved exploits, who gives to the singers the horse-rich prize, to the praisers the prize rich in cattle.

**8.2.25**  Wondrous, ever more wondrous, O pressers, hasten with the intoxicating soma for the hero, for the champion.

**8.2.26**  The Vṛtra-slayer will drink the pressed soma; he will come, not distant from us; the hundred-aided one is restrained.

**8.2.27**  Let the two bays yoked by prayer convey here the bountiful, the friend renowned through hymns, him who delights in songs.

**8.2.28**  Sweet soma-draughts await you — come! Prepared soma-draughts await you — come! O broad-jawed, sage-like, mighty one, come here to this joint rejoicing.

**8.2.29**  And the praises that magnify you for great bounty and heroic might, O Indra — they magnify the doer of deeds.

**8.2.30**  The songs and lauds directed to you, O hymn-bearer — those altogether have taken on powers.

**8.2.31**  Thus he, roaring aloud, the prize-winner, the sole thunderbolt-holder, from of old unscathed, distributes.

**8.2.32**  Indra the Vṛtra-slayer with his right hand, the much-invoked, the much-praised — great through his great powers.

**8.2.33**  In him all peoples, and the bold deeds and the courses — they all indeed follow him, the gladdener, the generous.

**8.2.34**  He, Indra, has done all these things, he who is heard beyond all — the prize-bestower for the bountiful.

**8.2.35**  The bearer-forward of the prize-seeking chariot, whom he protects even from afar — the lord of wealth, he is the carrier.

**8.2.36**  The inspirer who gains with swift horses, the hero who slays Vṛtra with his allies, the true protector of the one who serves.

**8.2.37**  Worship him, O Priyamedhas, worship Indra with a gathered mind; may he who is the true drinker of soma be here with the soma-draughts.

**8.2.38**  Sing, O Kāṇvas, of him who has song-fame, the lord of truth, eager for renown, far-stretching — the prize-winner.

**8.2.39**  He who even without a word of praise gave cattle to his friends, the mighty companion of men — those who have leaned their desire upon him.

**8.2.40**  Thus, O stone-wielder, you went to the devout Kāṇva Medhyātithi, having become a ram, going towards him.

**8.2.41**  Instruct Vibhindhu to give four times ten thousand and eight thousand besides.

**8.2.42**  And those two who grow with milk — may the Naptyas of Raṇa give no harm; we have invoked them for procreation.

**8.3.1**  Drink of the pressed, flavoured juice; rejoice, O Indra, in the cow-rich one; be our companion in joint rejoicing for our increase; may your thoughts protect us.

**8.3.2**  May we be in your favour, prize-winners; let not our foe overthrow us; protect us, O you of brilliant might, with your aids; guide us into your blessings.

**8.3.3**  May these words of mine magnify you, O lord of many goods; the purifying, bright, inspired ones have sung around you with their lauds.

**8.3.4**  This one, made powerful with a thousand sages, has spread like the ocean; true is that greatness of his — I extol his might, which reigns supreme among the inspired at the sacrifices.

**8.3.5**  Indra truly at the assembly of gods, Indra at the proceeding sacrifice, Indra we call in the battle for the gaining of the forest's prize, Indra for the winning of wealth.

**8.3.6**  Indra spread out the two worlds with his greatness and might; Indra made the sun shine; in Indra all living beings have found their order; in Indra the pressed soma-drops.

**8.3.7**  O Indra, the descendants of Āyu who drink first, the Ṛbhus together in accord have resounded with lauds; the Rudras praise the ancient one.

**8.3.8**  By this has Indra grown strong in his bull-like might through the rapture of the pressed soma with Viṣṇu; today the descendants of Āyu praise that greatness of his as of old.

**8.3.9**  I seek from you that heroic strength, that prayer for the ancient design — with which you helped the Yatis and Bhṛgu when the prize was set, and with which you protected Praskaṇva.

**8.3.10**  With which you released the great waters into the ocean — that is your bull-like might, O Indra; that greatness of his is immediately unattainable, which the wide earth resounds with.

**8.3.11**  Be able for us, O Indra, since I seek from you the wealth and heroic strength; be able for the prize the first to be desired, be able for the laud, O ancient one.

**8.3.12**  Be able for us — for you protected our ancient understanding, O Indra, of those who desire the prize; be able as you protected Ruśama, Śyāvaka, Kṛpa, O Indra, and Svarṇara.

**8.3.13**  What mortal now, swifter than linseed plants, shall praise him newly? For those who sing his Indra-might and glory have never truly attained to his greatness.

**8.3.14**  When do those who praise and order the divine, when does the seer, what inspired one, convey it? When, O Maghavan Indra, do you come to the call of the soma-presser? When do you come to the praiser?

**8.3.15**  Up go these most honey-sweet words, the lauds are set in motion — the prize-winners with imperishable aids, like chariots rushing for the prize.

**8.3.16**  Like the Kāṇvas, like the Bhṛgus, like the suns, they have all attained to what has been obtained; the Āyus, the Priyamedhas, magnifying Indra with their lauds, have sung out.

**8.3.17**  Yoke your two bays, O Indra, best Vṛtra-slayer, from afar; come hither, O Maghavan, for the soma-drinking, O fierce one, come with your two tall steeds.

**8.3.18**  For these Kāṇvas have cried out to you with prayer, the inspired ones for the winning of the sacrifice; so you, O Maghavan Indra, O hymn-lover, hear our call like a beloved one.

**8.3.19**  You drove Vṛtra out from the great bows, O Indra; you drove out the magic of Arbuda the beast; you drove out the cows from the mountain.

**8.3.20**  The fires blazed forth, the sun blazed forth, the Indra-belonging essence of soma blazed forth; you pressed down the great serpent from the air — that, O Indra, is your manly power.

**8.3.21**  Him whom Indra, the Maruts, Pākasthāman, Kaurayāṇa gave me — the most splendid of all in his own nature, running as if in heaven.

**8.3.22**  Pākasthāmā gave me Rohita, the well-yoked one filling the girth-strap — the awakener of riches.

**8.3.23**  For whom ten other carriers bear back the yoke-shaft — like a bird to the home of Tugrya.

**8.3.24**  The self of the father, the body is the garment, the strength-giving ointment — Rohita's fourth one, Pākasthāman, the Bhoja, the giver, I have spoken of.

**8.4.1**  Whether eastward, westward, northward or southward, O Indra, you are called by men — you are universally wide-spread, born for men, for the Ānavas you are abundant strength, for the Turvaśas.

**8.4.2**  Whether with Rumā, Ruśama, Śyāvaka, or Kṛpa you rejoice together, O Indra — the Kāṇvas, the bearers of lauds, draw you with their prayers; O Indra come.

**8.4.3**  As a wild bull thirsty makes its way to a waterless pool's edge, come quickly at our invitation, at the forward journey; among the Kāṇvas drink in full companionship.

**8.4.4**  May the soma-drops gladden you, O Maghavan Indra, for the giving of bounty to the presser; from there you drank soma pressed in the bowl — that greatest strength you have taken on.

**8.4.5**  He put forth strength with strength, broke anger with might; all your enemies, O young Indra, have bowed down like trees.

**8.4.6**  Like one accompanied by a thousand, he who has brought near praise of you the youthful warrior — he makes a son surpassing, with heroic strength; he prospers with words of homage.

**8.4.7**  Let us not fear, let us not toil, in your friendship, O fierce one; great and worthy of sight is your deed, O bull — may we see Turvaśa and Yadu.

**8.4.8**  He roars following the left flank like a bull; the giver's gift does not wane; the milch-cows mixed with honey by the bees — come quickly, run, drink.

**8.4.9**  Your friend, O Indra, is rich in horses, rich in chariots, well-formed, rich in cattle; he accompanies the feast of meat always; the moon-bright one goes to the assembly.

**8.4.10**  Come like a thirsty antelope to the watering-place; drink the soma following the wish; having drunk, O Maghavan, day by day you take on the strongest might.

**8.4.11**  Make the soma flow, O Adhvaryu — Indra thirsts; now the two bull-strong bays have been yoked and the Vṛtra-slayer has come.

**8.4.12**  Even the worshipping man himself reckons where you satisfy yourself with soma; this food of yours, fit companion, well sprinkled — come for it, run, drink.

**8.4.13**  O Adhvaryus, standing in the chariot, press the soma for Indra; those pressing for the offering-performing sacrifice look toward the bright one above.

**8.4.14**  Both eager bull-strong bays will convey Indra to the waters; may the seven steeds lovely at the rite convey you hither at the pressing.

**8.4.15**  We choose Pūṣan for alliance, the lord of many goods; O mighty Indra, much-invoked, give of your bounty for our thought, for the strength to win wealth, O liberator.

**8.4.16**  Sharpen us like a razor on a whetstone, O liberator of riches; give to us — in you is that wealth of the morning cow, easily found, which you impel toward mortal man.

**8.4.17**  I reach out to you, O Pūṣan, to straighten myself; I reach out to the praiser, O shining one; I do not reach for what is remote — for that, O Vasu, I praise the Sāman of the Pajras.

**8.4.18**  Cattle have gone somewhere to the pasture, O shining immortal of permanent treasure; O Pūṣan, be our protector, be favorable, most bountiful for the winning of the prize.

**8.4.19**  Substantial bounty with a hundred horses at the heavenly attacks of Kuruṅga, in the gifts of the splendid, fortunate king — among the Turvaśas we have thought of it.

**8.4.20**  Through the thoughts of the prize-winning Kāṇva and the heaven-assailing Priyamedhas, the seer drove off sixty thousand impeccable ones, drove off herds of cattle.

**8.4.21**  Even the trees have resounded at my approach; they allotted me cattle abundantly, they allotted me horses abundantly.

**8.5.1**  As if from afar, when truly the ruddy-hued one shone out, it spread its beam all across the universe.

**8.5.2**  O wonder-working Aśvins, you two accompany the Dawn with a manly, mind-yoked chariot of broad splendour.

**8.5.3**  O Vājinīvasū Aśvins, the lauds have faced toward you two; I, the messenger, have readied speech.

**8.5.4**  We Kāṇvas praise the Aśvins, who are dear to many for our aid, who delight many, who have many goods.

**8.5.5**  Most bountiful, most prize-winning, urging onward, lords of beauty — you will come to the house of the devout one.

**8.5.6**  For the god-devoted, devout one, sprinkle the cattle-pasture with good intelligence and irresistible help, with ghee.

**8.5.7**  Let our laud come running swiftly with swift eagles; come, O Aśvins, with your horses.

**8.5.8**  With those with which you two circle around the three remote realms, all the luminous spaces of heaven, and the three watches of night.

**8.5.9**  And for us the cow-rich refreshments and the gifts — O you who know the day, loosen the bonds on the path to the winning.

**8.5.10**  Bring to us, O Aśvins, wealth rich in cattle, rich in heroes, rich in good chariots — bring refreshments rich in horses.

**8.5.11**  Grown great, lords of beauty, wondrous ones with golden paths — drink the soma-sweetness.

**8.5.12**  O Vājinīvasū, to us and to the bountiful patrons of wide extent, bring shelter that cannot be deceived.

**8.5.13**  Do settle down the prayer of the people, come swiftly with the youngest speed; do not pause at another place.

**8.5.14**  Drink of this, you two Aśvins, of this pleasant intoxication, O attentive ones, of the honey bestowed.

**8.5.15**  Bring to us the wealth of hundreds, of thousands, of great abundance, all-nourishing.

**8.5.16**  In many places indeed the wise men, the praisers, call upon you two, O heroes; come, O Aśvins, with the singers.

**8.5.17**  The people who have strewn the sacred grass, who bear oblations, who are prepared — they invoke you two, O Aśvins.

**8.5.18**  This laud of ours today is the most carrying and most intimate; may it be for you two, O Aśvins.

**8.5.19**  The leather bag of honey placed upon the chariot-seat — from that drink, O Aśvins.

**8.5.20**  With that, O Vājinīvasū, bring rich refreshments for our cattle, for our offspring, for our wellbeing, for our cow.

**8.5.21**  And the heavenly refreshments and the rivers — O you who know the day, you pour them down like opening a door.

**8.5.22**  When did Tugra's son find you two, O heroes, abandoned in the ocean, when your chariot flew to pieces?

**8.5.23**  You Nāsatyas honour again and again with aids Kāṇva, restored and settled in his house.

**8.5.24**  Come with those newer, excellent aids when I call upon you two, O Vṛṣaṇvasū Aśvins.

**8.5.25**  As you helped Kāṇva, Priyamedha who was nearby praised, Atri, Śiñjāra — so, O Aśvins.

**8.5.26**  As in the contest for the prize you helped Aṁśu among cows, Agastya — as Sobhari in the races.

**8.5.27**  To this extent, O Vṛṣaṇvasū Aśvins, or even more — while praising we seek your favour.

**8.5.28**  O Aśvins, you have mounted the chariot with golden seat, with golden reins, sky-touching.

**8.5.29**  Your pole is golden, the shaft is golden, the axle is golden; both wheels are golden.

**8.5.30**  With that, O Vājinīvasū, come to us from far away; come to this excellent praise of mine.

**8.5.31**  Bring from afar, O Aśvins, eating the many divine, immortal refreshments.

**8.5.32**  Come to us, O Aśvins, with glory, with renown, with riches; O Nāsatyas, you of many splendours.

**8.5.33**  Let the winged ones, the birds sprinkled with drops, convey you both here to the people whose rite is good.

**8.5.34**  Your chariot that rolls with the refreshment singing along — the wheel does not obstruct it.

**8.5.35**  With a golden chariot, with swift-footed horses — O Nāsatyas, swift in thought.

**8.5.36**  You two refresh the wakeful wild beast, O Vṛṣaṇvasū; mix refreshment and wealth for us.

**8.5.37**  May you two Aśvins know the new acquisitions of mine, as when Kaśu the Caidya gave a hundred camels and ten thousand cattle.

**8.5.38**  He who has given me ten gold-gleaming kings — the peoples around him, the subjects of the Caidya, pressed down to the ground, wrapped in hides on all sides.

**8.6.1**  Great is Indra who through might, like Parjanya swelling with rain, has been magnified through the lauds of Vatsa.

**8.6.2**  When the bearers, the inspired ones, carry the offspring of the Ṛta forward with the carrying of the Ṛta, who nourish the offspring of the Ṛta.

**8.6.3**  When the Kāṇvas fashioned Indra with their lauds as the accomplisher of the sacrifice, the kindred declared the weapon.

**8.6.4**  To his wrath all peoples, all clans, have bowed, as rivers to the ocean.

**8.6.5**  This might of his was blazing when he rolled both together — Indra rolled the two worlds like a hide.

**8.6.6**  He split the head of the roaring Vṛtra with his hundred-jointed thunderbolt, with the bull-like weapon.

**8.6.7**  These thoughts at the head of the streams of the inspired we have praised forward, shining like the blaze of Agni.

**8.6.8**  When the thoughts that are in hiding blaze forth of their own accord, the Kāṇvas do so through the stream of the Ṛta.

**8.6.9**  May we attain to that, O Indra — the wealth rich in cattle, rich in horses; may we bring prayer forward for the ancient design.

**8.6.10**  I indeed have grasped the wisdom of the Ṛta from the father; I was born like the sun.

**8.6.11**  With ancient thought I adorn the hymns as Kāṇva does — with which Indra took on his energy.

**8.6.12**  The seers who have not praised you, O Indra, and those who have praised you — grow great for me, O well-praised one.

**8.6.13**  When his wrath surged forward, breaking Vṛtra joint by joint, he set the waters moving toward the ocean.

**8.6.14**  You struck down the bullying Śuṣṇa with the thunderbolt, O Indra, against the Dasyu; for you, O bull, O fierce one, are famed as such.

**8.6.15**  The heavens have not encompassed Indra in his might, nor the mid-spaces the thunderbolt-holder, nor the earths.

**8.6.16**  He who lay blocking your great waters, O Indra, as they were being held firm — you thrust him down with your feet.

**8.6.17**  He who seized these two great worlds, both together — him, O Indra, you have hidden in darkness.
