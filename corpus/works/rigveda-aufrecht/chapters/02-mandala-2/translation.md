**2.1.1**  You, O Agni, are born from the heavens; you, the swiftly blazing one, from the waters; you from the rock; you from the forests; you from the plants; you, O lord of men, are born pure.

**2.1.2**  Yours, O Agni, is the Hotṛ-office; yours the seasonal Potṛ-office; yours the Neṣṭṛ-office; you, engaged in the rite, are the Āgnīdhra. Yours is the Praśāstṛ-function; you perform the Adhvaryu duties; and you are both the Brahman-priest and the master of our house.

**2.1.3**  You, O Agni, are Indra, the bull of the righteous; you are Viṣṇu of the wide stride, worthy of reverence. You are Brahmā, the finder of wealth, O lord of prayer; you, the upholder, preside with abundant wisdom.

**2.1.4**  You, O Agni, are King Varuṇa who holds firm his ordinances; you become Mitra, the wonderful, worthy of praise. You are Aryaman, the lord of the righteous, at whose table all share; you are Aṁśa, O god, the apportioner at the assembly.

**2.1.5**  You, O Agni, are Tvaṣṭṛ granting heroic offspring to the worshiper; yours are the wives of the gods — of Mitra's greatness is the kinship. You swiftly hasten to bestow wealth in fine horses; you are the host of men, O Purūvasu, rich in many goods.

**2.1.6**  You, O Agni, are Rudra, the mighty Asura of the great sky; you command the troop of the Maruts, the nourishing ones. You travel with the ruddy winds as your auspicious companions; you are Pūṣan who guards the worshiper by your very nature.

**2.1.7**  You, O Agni, are the giver of wealth to the one who worships fitly; you are the god Savitṛ, the bestower of treasures. You are Bhaga, O lord of men, and you command wealth; you are the guardian in the house of whoever has honored you.

**2.1.8**  You, O Agni, the tribes worship as the lord of the house in their home; the peoples adore you as their king who bestows favor well. You preside over all things, O bright-faced one; you face a thousand hundreds and tens.

**2.1.9**  You, O Agni, do men worship as a father with oblations; they seek you as a brother for their comfort, you who shine in your body. You become a son to one who has honored you; you, a kind and unchecked friend, are their protector.

**2.1.10**  You, O Agni, are the Ṛbhu at the summit, worthy of homage; you command the wealth of nourishing sustenance. You shine forth, O Dakṣa, for the giving of gifts; you are the all-directing one, the extender of the sacrifice.

**2.1.11**  You, O Agni, are Aditi to the worshiper, O god; you as Bhāratī, the invocation-goddess, grow through praise. You are Iḷā of the hundred winters, skilled in action; you are Sarasvatī, O lord of wealth, the slayer of Vṛtra.

**2.1.12**  You, O Agni, well-nourished, are the most exalted bird; in your desirable color is the beauty of sight. You are the great, forward-advancing food-strength; you are the abundant, all-pervading, wide wealth.

**2.1.13**  You, O Agni, have the Ādityas made their mouth; the pure ones have made you their tongue, O seer. The gift-seekers follow you at the sacrifices; in you the gods eat the offered oblation.

**2.1.14**  In you, O Agni, all the immortal, un-treacherous gods eat the offered oblation with open mouth. Through you mortals relish the libation; you, the pure one, were born as the embryo of the plants.

**2.1.15**  You are with them all and you face them all, O Agni, well-born; and you, O god, are far-surpassing. For here, in your greatness, the strengthening force pervades, and both the world-halves, heaven and earth, follow you.

**2.1.16**  The generous lords who send forth for the praisers the gift headed by cattle and adorned with horses — O Agni, lead both us and them to better fortune. May we speak great words at the assembly, possessed of heroes.

**2.2.1**  Make Jātavedas grow through the sacrifice; worship Agni with oblation, with your whole being, with hymn — him the enkindled, the one of good cheer, whose men are noble, the heavenly Hotṛ, enthroned at the forepost in the assemblies.

**2.2.2**  Toward you the nights and dawns have lowed, O Agni, like milch-cows toward their calves at their resting-places. Like heaven's hub you illumine the generations of men; you shine through the nights, O much-desired one, ever watchful.

**2.2.3**  Him of wondrous deeds the gods have set down as the hub at the base of the atmospheric realm, the charioteer between heaven and earth — Agni bright-flaming as a car worthy of knowledge, praiseworthy among settlements like Mitra.

**2.2.4**  Him sprinkling in the atmosphere, in his own house, shining like the moon, bright-gleaming, in the curve they have placed — the offspring of the speckled one who illumines with his eyes, like a guardian at the ford, following both peoples.

**2.2.5**  Let that Hotṛ encompass the whole sacrifice; him too men adorn with oblations and song. Golden-jawed, he roars as he grows among the kindlings, illumining the two world-halves as heaven does with stars.

**2.2.6**  Enkindled, bringing wealth, shine for us as an auspicious boon; bestow and cause wealth to blaze among us. Make the two world-halves favorable to us, O Agni; be pleased with the oblations of man, O god.

**2.2.7**  Give us, O Agni, the great gift worth thousands; increase like a door that yields strength, O widely famed one. Make heaven and earth favorable to us through prayer; shine brightly like the sun at the dawns.

**2.2.8**  Enkindled, following the pleasant nights in order, may he shine like the sun with his red-gleaming light. Agni with his invocations is the well-sacrificing one of men, the king of the peoples, the beloved guest for the mortal.

**2.2.9**  Thus among the immortals, O primal Agni, has our thought been nourished in the great heavens, among human beings — like a milch-cow yielding for the singer at the assemblies, of herself driving forth the hundredfold, manifold vigor.

**2.2.10**  May we, O Agni, illumine the peoples — whether through a swift horse or through heroic strength or through prayer. May our glory above the five peoples shine high and unassailable like the unconquerable sun.

**2.2.11**  Be aware of us — you, the powerful, the praiseworthy, in whom the well-born generous ones are active — the sacrifice, O Agni, which the vigorous ones approach, shining perpetually on their offspring in their own house.

**2.2.12**  May we both — the praisers and the generous, O Jātavedas, O Agni — abide in your shelter. Grant us the abundant, many-gleaming wealth of cattle, with offspring and good progeny.

**2.2.13**  The generous lords who send forth for the praisers the gift headed by cattle and adorned with horses — O Agni, lead both us and them to better fortune. May we speak great words at the assembly, possessed of heroes.

**2.3.1**  Agni, enkindled, set in the earth, has stood facing all the worlds. The Hotṛ, the purifier, from of old of good intelligence — may Agni, the god worthy of the gods, sacrifice to the gods.

**2.3.2**  Narāśaṁsa, adorning all the dwellings, radiant in his own light against the three heights of heaven — let him, sprinkling with ghee, with willing mind, moistening the oblation, anoint the gods at the head of the sacrifice.

**2.3.3**  Praised, O Agni, with mind, being worthy of us, sacrifice to the gods today, going before mankind. Bring hither the unshaken host of Maruts and Indra; men, worship the one who is seated on the sacrificial grass.

**2.3.4**  The divine sacrificial grass, growing, filled with heroes, spread out for wealth, easily borne on this altar, anointed with ghee — let the Vasus, the Viśve Devāḥ, the Ādityas, the worthy of sacrifice, sit upon it.

**2.3.5**  Let the divine Doors, being called upon, spread wide, well-approached with reverences; wide-extending, let them spread out, ever-young, purifying the glorious color abounding in heroes.

**2.3.6**  Performing their good deeds, moistened as by ancient rains, Dawn and Night delight like two weavers at their work. Weaving together the stretched thread, facing each other — let them adorn the sacrifice with milk, well-yielding.

**2.3.7**  The two divine Hotṛ-priests, the first, the more knowing, sacrifice straightforwardly, the more wondrous with their hymn. Anointing one another, sacrificing to the gods in due season, at the navel of the earth, upon the three ridges.

**2.3.8**  Sarasvatī, perfecting our prayer, and Iḷā the goddess, and Bhāratī all-pervading — these three goddesses, sitting down, guard this unbroken refuge with their own nature.

**2.3.9**  Of reddish form, well-supporting, food-giving — an obedient hero desiring the gods is born. May Tvaṣṭṛ loosen the navel of progeny in us; then may the path of the gods also reach us.

**2.3.10**  The tree of the forest, releasing it, comes to the fore; Agni advances the oblation with his thoughts. Knowing its threefold anointing, may the divine slaughterer lead the oblation to the gods.

**2.3.11**  Ghee is sprinkled; ghee is his womb; he is established in ghee; ghee is his very dwelling. Bring hither in order; delight yourself; carry the oblation consecrated with Svāhā, O bull.

**2.4.1**  I call for you the brilliantly shining one, the well-lauded one, Agni the guest of the peoples, of fine gifts — he who has become beloved like Mitra, Jātavedas the god among godless folk.

**2.4.2**  Worshiping him, they placed him a second time at the dwelling-place of the waters; the Bhṛgus placed him among the peoples of Āyu. May this one, the swift-horsed Agni, the hub of the gods, encompass all the beings.

**2.4.3**  The gods placed beloved Agni among the human peoples, as they would settle a friend who would dwell among them. He illumines with flame those who desire him — he who is worthy of skill in the house of the generous worshiper.

**2.4.4**  Delightful is his nourishment like a possession of one's own; the power of his advancing skill is a joy to see. He who thrusts out his tongue among the plants, tossing like a racehorse, whirling through his obstacles.

**2.4.5**  When the forest-eaters, as they marvel at my might, bend their color to the Uśij-fires — he is known in the delightful realm by his variegated brightness, he who, though aged, becomes instantly young again.

**2.4.6**  He who, like one thirsting, shines among the forests; along the path he has roared like a chariot-horse. The scorching one of black trail has become conscious, smiling like heaven with its clouds.

**2.4.7**  He who has stood up and encompassed the wide expanse — a beast going of his own will without a herdsman. Agni the flame-possessor, heating the withered plants, causing a black trembling, has not tasted the earth.

**2.4.8**  Now I recite the thought for you in the third assembly, meditating upon your former helping. Bestow on us, O Agni, the great wealth that gathers heroes, the nourishing strength, with good offspring.

**2.4.9**  Just as, O Agni, the Gṛtsamadas through you conquered in secret and overwhelmed the adversaries — possessed of heroes, overcoming hostility — bestow that same vitality from the generous ones upon the singer.

**2.5.1**  The conscious Hotṛ has been born, a father for the fathers' aid. Offering excellent wealth, may we, the vigorous, be able to attain the rein.

**2.5.2**  In him who is the leader of the sacrifice, seven cords are stretched; the Potṛ, as a man of the gods, drives the eighth — the divine one; he pervades all that.

**2.5.3**  He has indeed sustained it; verily, when the Brahman-priest has spoken the holy words after him, the wheel-rim has encompassed all poetic wisdom as the felly of a wheel.

**2.5.4**  For the pure Praśāstṛ together with the pure, knowing its fixed ordinances, was born with his power of will; he mounts up following them like branches.

**2.5.5**  The milch-cows of Āyu attend the color of this Neṣṭṛ. Perhaps the sisters who have come to this — will they bring a choice gift from the three?

**2.5.6**  When the sister approaches the mother bringing ghee to where she stands, the Adhvaryu rejoices at their coming like barley at rain.

**2.5.7**  Let the seasonal priest make heaven for his own heaven-seeker; the laud and the sacrifice — we have been adequate; we have given; we have won.

**2.5.8**  As a knowing one may do what is fit for all the adorable gods — this sacrifice which we have made, O Agni, let it also be in you.

**2.6.1**  Accept for me, O Agni, this kindling-wood, accept this drawing-near; and listen to these songs.

**2.6.2**  With this, O Agni, for your worshiper — O grandson of strength, desired of horses — with this well-spoken hymn, O well-born one.

**2.6.3**  You who are pleased by song and longing for wealth, O giver of wealth — may we, reverencing you with hymns, pay you homage.

**2.6.4**  Be aware of us — O sage, O bountiful one, O lord of wealth, O giver of wealth — drive away from us all enmities.

**2.6.5**  May he give us rain from the sky; may he give us strength that meets no opposition; may he give us thousandfold refreshments.

**2.6.6**  O youngest, for the praiser who seeks your aid — come as our messenger with our hymn, O most sacrificing Hotṛ.

**2.6.7**  For, O Agni, you travel between the two births, O knowing, O seer — as a friendly messenger among the people.

**2.6.8**  You who know, fill us too; O knowing one, sacrifice in order; and sit down on this sacrificial grass.

**2.7.1**  O youngest, O Agni of the Bharata-clan, bring the most excellent, radiant wealth — O Vasu, wealth longed for by many.

**2.7.2**  Let no niggardliness of the divine or the mortal master us; carry us across that also, and across enmity.

**2.7.3**  And through you may we, like rivers in flood, wade across all enmities.

**2.7.4**  O Agni, pure, purifying, worthy of praise — you shine forth greatly; you are invoked with ladles of ghee.

**2.7.5**  You, O Agni of the Bharata-clan, are ours, invoked with cows and bulls and eight-footed victims.

**2.7.6**  Whose food is dry wood, whose libation is ghee — the ancient, deserving, desirable Hotṛ, the wondrous son of strength.

**2.8.1**  As if hastening for prizes — now praise the harnessing of Agni's cars, of the most glorious, the bountiful one.

**2.8.2**  He who is of good guidance for the worshiper, the undecaying, wearing out the enemy — of beautiful aspect, invoked with oblations.

**2.8.3**  He who, in his beauty, is praised in the houses at dusk and dawn — whose ordinance is never diminished.

**2.8.4**  He who, like the sun, shines brightly with his beam, gleaming with his flame — anointed with undecaying ones all around.

**2.8.5**  After Atri — the self-ruling Agni — the praises have increased; he has assumed all glories.

**2.8.6**  With the aids of Agni, Indra, Soma, and the gods, may we, remaining unharmed, keep company and overcome those who battle against us.

**2.9.1**  The Hotṛ, perceiving, fierce, blazing, skilled, has sat down in the Hotṛ's seat — Agni, whose ordinance is undeceived, whose insight is excellent, most excellent, carrier of a thousand, pure-tongued.

**2.9.2**  You are the messenger; you are our protector across; you, O bull, are the guide to better things. Be the unwavering, shining guardian, O Agni, of our offspring and the continuation of our persons.

**2.9.3**  May we worship you in your supreme birth, O Agni; may we worship you with praises in the lower dwelling. The womb from which you have arisen — that I sacrifice to; into you when enkindled the oblations have been poured.

**2.9.4**  O Agni, sacrifice with the oblation, O most sacrificing; readily accept the gift-offering of largess. For you are the lord of riches among riches; you are the thinker of the bright word.

**2.9.5**  Neither part of your wealth diminishes, O wondrous one, being born day by day. Make the singer nourishing, O Agni; make him the lord of wealth with good offspring.

**2.9.6**  Most beneficent with your array of praisers, the most sacrificing to the gods, auspicious to us — undeceived guardian and our protector across, O Agni, shine brightly and wealth-bestowing.

**2.10.1**  Agni, oft-invoked, the first, at the step of the father-like Iḷā, enkindled among mankind — clothed in beauty, the immortal, the all-wise, worthy of being cleansed, seeking glory — he is the victorious one.

**2.10.2**  May Agni of brilliant radiance hear my call with all his hymns — immortal, all-wise. The dark ones and the red carry his car; he has made a wheel bearing Dawn's overcoming — he who is all-supporting.

**2.10.3**  They generated the well-born one in the one lying open; Agni became the embryo in the manifoldly-adorned ones. Even in the rough one he dwells, enveloped by no one, the all-wise, with his great nocturnal powers.

**2.10.4**  I anoint Agni with the ghee-oblation — dwelling opposite all the worlds, broad across, great in body, widest, mighty in food, fierce to behold.

**2.10.5**  I anoint him facing all around from all sides; with a demon-free mind may he relish it. Agni of manly splendor, of desirable color, roaring without being touched in his body.

**2.10.6**  Let him be known for his share with overwhelming choice; may we, as messengers of yours, speak after the manner of Manu. I invoke Agni who is complete, with the ladle and speech — the honey-mixed one — with praise.

**2.11.1**  Hear the call, O Indra; may we not be harmed; may we be yours for the giving of wealth. For these streams, flowing like rivers, seeking riches, increase you with their strength.

**2.11.2**  Release the great waters, O Indra, those which swelled but were penned around by the serpent, O hero, the many. He who grew great with praises, he struck down even the immortal-thinking Dāsa.

**2.11.3**  In the praises, O hero, in which you have delighted, O Indra, and in the Rudra-hymns — for you these bright ones, as you rejoice in them, hasten forward like winds.

**2.11.4**  Increasing your bright strength, placing the bright thunderbolt in your two arms — bright are you, O Indra, grown great, making the Dāsa-peoples subject to us with the sun.

**2.11.5**  The hidden one, the secret one, the concealed, shrouded in the waters, the magical one dwelling there — even him who had propped up the waters and the sky — you slew the serpent, O hero, with your heroic power.

**2.11.6**  Let us laud your former great deeds, O Indra, and let us laud the new ones accomplished. Let us laud the thunderbolt longing in your arms; let us laud the two bays, the banners of the sun.

**2.11.7**  Your two prize-winning bays, O Indra, have struck out the self-poured heavenly mead. The earth spread apart equally; even the mountain stood still about to quake.

**2.11.8**  The mountain lay still without swerving; bellowing with its mother-waters, it contracted. In the far distance they increased the voice, spreading out the pipe sent by Indra.

**2.11.9**  Indra has kicked out the great Vṛtra lying prone — the magical one. Both world-halves trembled in fear at the thunderbolt of this roaring bull.

**2.11.10**  The thunderbolt of this bull bellowed out — what the inhuman one can overwhelm the human. Having drunk the pressed Soma, he brought down the magic of the magical Dānava.

**2.11.11**  Drink and drink again, O Indra, O hero; may the exhilarating pressed ones exhilarate you. Let those who fill your flanks increase you; thus the pressed drink of the citizen has helped Indra.

**2.11.12**  In you, O Indra, we have become inspired seers; seeking your grace, we would gain the thought with truth. May we who seek your help hold fast to your praise; may we at once be yours for the giving of riches.

**2.11.13**  May we be of those, O Indra, who, with your help, seek your aid, increasing your strength. O god, bestow on us the wealth abounding in heroes — that most vigorous gift for which we have longed.

**2.11.14**  Grant us dwelling; grant us friendship; grant us, O Indra, the Marut-host. Those who rejoice together, who are exhilarated — the winds guard the foremost leader.

**2.11.15**  Let those in whom you, exhilarated, having drunk, have been satisfied pass through, O Indra, O firm one. You have increased us well in battles as conquerors, with great hymns to the sky.

**2.11.16**  Those great conquerors of yours, or those who have sought your favor with praises — they, strewing the sacrificial grass as in a homestead, protected by you, O Indra, have truly reached the prize.

**2.11.17**  Among the mighty, O hero — rejoicing — drink the Soma at the three Kadrū vessels, O Indra. Having caused your beards to tremble with delight, come with your two bays to drink of the pressed drink.

**2.11.18**  Assume the might, O hero, with which you broke down Vṛtra, Dānu, and Aurṇavābha. You revealed the light for the Ārya; the Dasyu was made to sit on his left side, O Indra.

**2.11.19**  May we be of those who, with your help, crossing over, overcome all rivals and Dasyus for the Ārya. You have subjected to us for the friendship of Trita that Tvaṣṭra-born thing of all forms.

**2.11.20**  Growing great on the pressed, exhilarating drink of Trita, he has struck down the myriad serpents. Indra with the Aṅgirases has turned the sun like a wheel; he has cloven Vala.

**2.11.21**  Now may that bountiful right hand of yours, O Indra, yield to your singer the choicest gift. Grant aid to the praisers; let our share not be consumed. May we speak great words at the assembly, possessed of heroes.

**2.12.1**  He who, as soon as born, was the first thinker, the god who encompassed the gods with his power of will — from whose might the two world-halves trembled in fear at the greatness of his manly force — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.2**  He who made firm the trembling earth; he who stilled the mountains when they were enraged; he who measured out the atmosphere more widely; he who propped up the sky — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.3**  He who, having slain the serpent, released the seven rivers; who drove out the cows having opened Vala's lair; who begot fire between the two stones — the victorious one in battles — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.4**  By whom these all-moving things were made; who has placed the dark Dāsa-color below, hiding it; who, like a gambler winning, has taken up the mark and the Ārya's abundance — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.5**  Of whom they ask in awe "Where is he?" and they even say of him "He does not exist" — yet he diminishes the Ārya's riches like a dice-player's throw. Place faith in him — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.6**  He who is the encourager of the feeble, of the emaciated; of the Brahman-singer calling out in distress; of the one whose pressing-stones are yoked — the fine-jawed protector of the pressed Soma — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.7**  Whose are the horses in every direction, whose the cattle, whose the settlements, whose all the chariots; who begot the sun, who the dawn, who is the leader of the waters — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.8**  Whom the two opposing battle-cries invoke — the far and the near, both enemies — having mounted the same chariot, they call upon him separately — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.9**  Without whom the peoples do not conquer; whom those who fight invoke for help; who has become the measure of all; who shakes the unshakable — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.10**  Who slew with his weapon those who were repeatedly committing great sin, those who thought nothing of it; who yields not one measure to the arrogant; who is the slayer of the Dasyu — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.11**  Who found Śambara dwelling in the mountains in the fortieth autumn; who slew the serpent growing in strength, Dānu lying asleep — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.12**  He who, seven-rayed, the mighty bull, released the seven rivers to flow; who, thunderbolt-armed, kicked away Rauhinī as she was ascending to the sky — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.13**  Even heaven and earth bow down to him; even the mountains fear his might. He who drinks Soma, who is intent, whose arms hold the thunderbolt, who is thunderbolt-handed — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.14**  Who helps the presser, who helps the cooker, who helps the reciter, who helps the toiling one with his aid; whose growing power is prayer, whose is the Soma, whose is this bounty — he, O people, is Indra.

**2.12.15**  You truly shatter the prize even for the unwilling presser and cooker — yes, you are true. May we, always dear to you, O Indra, possessed of heroes, speak forth at the assembly.

**2.13.1**  She who is the birth-season, his mother — around her waters he was swiftly born, in whom he grows. That which became swollen, brimming with milk: the first-pressed nectar of the soma-stalk — that is praiseworthy.

**2.13.2**  Together they come, bearing milk in abundance, bearing nourishment for universal enjoyment. One road runs downward along the slopes — he who first made these things, thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.3**  The one speaks after, whatever he gives; the other, fashioning forms, moves by means of water. All things endure the impulse of the one — he who first made these things, thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.4**  They sit distributing nourishment to creatures, like wealth upon the back of one who waxes great as he comes. With unsatiated fangs he eats the father's food — he who first made these things, thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.5**  Then thou madest the earth to be seen by heaven — thou who, slaying the serpent of the torrents, didst open the paths. The gods begat thee, like a mighty steed, to be hymned with praises and with waters — thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.6**  Thou who dispensest nourishment and growth, who hast milked out honey-sweetness from the moist and dry alike — thou layest up treasure in the realm of the sun; thou alone art lord of all — thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.7**  Thou who, by ordinance, didst hold up the blossoming and the fertile streams at their bestowing, who didst create unequal beings blazing from heaven, the wide wide-spreading on every side — thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.8**  Thou who didst bring down Nārmarī together with his wealth, for the slaying and for the gift of Dāsaveśa — the mouth encompassed by the strength-bestowing one, and even today, O much-doer, thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.9**  Whose hundred, even ten, eating together at once — at the summons of one alone didst thou indeed spur them forth. Thou didst bind the Dasyus without a rope for Dabhīti's sake; thou becamest most helpful — thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.10**  All things followed the blockings of his manly power; they gave him treasure, they assigned wealth to the effective one. Thou didst prop up six expanses, five visible ones; thou becamest the encompassing beyond — thou art praiseworthy.

**2.13.11**  Well worth proclaiming is thy heroic valour, O hero, whereby with a single will thou findest goods. The might of Jātuṣṭhira the powerful dost thou increase — all that thou hast done, together with Indra, is praiseworthy.

**2.40.1**  O Soma and Pūṣan, generators of riches, generators of heaven, generators of earth — the two guardians born of all existence — the gods made you the navel of immortality.

**2.40.2**  These two gods as they are being born show favor; let these two conceal the undesired darknesses. Through these two — Soma and Pūṣan — Indra generated the ripe (cattle) within the raw (stones), among the ruddy cows (of dawn).

**2.40.3**  O Soma and Pūṣan, the measure of the atmosphere is the seven-wheeled chariot that pervades all space — turning in opposite directions, yoked by the mind: that chariot with five reins, O ye two bulls, you impel.

**2.40.4**  One has made his seat on high in heaven; the other (moves) above the earth in mid-air. Let those two release for us the navel of abundant bounty, the richly-nourishing pouring-forth of riches, upon us.

**2.40.5**  One has engendered all the worlds; the other moves beholding everything. O Soma and Pūṣan, strengthen my thought; through you both may we conquer all opposing hosts.

**2.40.6**  May Pūṣan, all-pervading, quicken (our) thought; may Soma, lord of riches, establish wealth. May the divine, unassailable Aditi protect (us); may we speak the great (word) in the assembly, endowed with good heroes.

**2.41.1**  O Vāyu, come hither with those thousandfold chariots of yours, drawn by your team, to drink the soma.

**2.41.2**  O Vāyu, with your team come hither — this bright (soma) is poured out for you. You shall be one who goes to the house of the soma-presser.

**2.41.3**  Of the bright (soma) mixed today with milk — O Indra and Vāyu, with your teams, come hither, O ye two heroes, and drink.

**2.41.4**  O Mitra and Varuṇa, increasers of truth (ṛta) — this soma is pressed out for you both. Hear my invocation here.

**2.41.5**  The two kings without treachery are seated in the firm, highest assembly — the hall with a thousand pillars.

**2.41.6**  Those two universal sovereigns, for whom ghee is poured, the two Ādityas, lords of the gift (dānu) — they associate without deviation.

**2.41.7**  O Nāsatyas, O Aśvins — come hither with (gifts of) cattle, with horses. O Rudra (sons of Rudra), bring the wealth worthy of heroes.

**2.41.8**  That no malicious mortal enemy from without or from within might do harm to you, O ye two whose wealth is the bull (Vṛṣaṇvasū — the Aśvins).

**2.41.9**  O Aśvins, bring us here the wealth of tawny aspect, O ye attentive ones, that finds wide space.

**2.41.10**  Indra, truly, has shaken off great terror from here; for he is the firm one, the all-seeing.

**2.41.11**  And may Indra be gracious to us; let no evil overtake us from behind; may blessedness be before us.

**2.41.12**  May Indra make (us) fearless on every side, on all quarters — the all-seeing conqueror of enemies.

**2.41.13**  O all-gods (Viśvedevāḥ), come hither; hear this invocation of mine; sit down upon this barhis-grass.

**2.41.14**  This intoxicating (soma), pungent and sweet, for you in the rites of the Śunahotras — drink this desirable (draught).

**2.41.15**  The Marut-hosts with Indra as their chief, the gods with Pūṣan as their gift-giver — all of you, hear my invocation.

**2.41.16**  O Sarasvatī, best of mothers, best of rivers, best of goddesses — we are as though without praise; O mother, make praise for us.

**2.41.17**  In you, O Sarasvatī divine, all life-forces are sheltered. Find joy in the rites of the Śunahotras; O goddess, shine upon our progeny.

**2.41.18**  Be pleased, O Sarasvatī possessed of sustenance (Vājinīvatī), with this brahman-prayer — the devotion that the Gṛtsamadas, O guardian of truth (ṛtāvari), dear to the gods, offer up to you.

**2.41.19**  We choose only you two, the two beneficent ones presiding over the sacrifice, to go forth — and Agni the bearer of oblations.

**2.41.20**  May Heaven and Earth today direct this successful, sky-touching sacrifice among the gods.

**2.41.21**  In your lap, O ye two without treachery, let the divine and holy powers sit here today for the drinking of soma.

**2.42.1**  Crying again and again, announcing (yourself) by your birth-cry, you set your voice in motion like a rower his boat. Be you of auspicious omen, O bird; may no universal misfortune find you.

**2.42.2**  Let not the hawk slay you, nor the eagle; let not the armed warrior-archer find you. Crying again and again in the direction of the ancestors, speak here as an auspicious, good-word-speaking one.

**2.42.3**  Cry down (your omen) from the south upon our houses, auspicious and good-word-speaking, O bird (śakunta). Let no thief gain power over us, no wicked one; may we speak the great (word) in the assembly, endowed with good heroes.

**2.43.1**  The śakunta-birds (kārus) sing circling sunwise; the birds speak in their proper season. It (the bird) utters both voices as though a sāman-singer — it reigns over the gāyatrī and the triṣṭubh in succession.

**2.43.2**  Like an Udgātṛ, O bird, you sing the sāman; like a brahman's son you recite at the soma-pressings. Like a powerful bull (going) to the nursing cows, proclaim blessings to us from every side, O bird; proclaim what is auspicious to us from all directions, O bird.

**2.43.3**  You who are speaking, O bird, speak blessings; even sitting in silence, discern well-being for us. When you speak as you fly up, like the karkarī (calling bird) — may we speak the great (word) in the assembly, endowed with good heroes.
