Lecture XI.My purpose in this Lecture is, first, the description of the special characteristics of judicial legislation as regards its relation to public opinion; and, next, the illustration, by a particular example,—namely, the changes in the law as to married women’s property,—of the way in which judge-made law may determine the course and character of parliamentary legislation.
