**17.1.1**  Truth, the vast, cosmic order fierce, consecration, austerity, sacred knowledge, and sacrifice — these uphold the Earth. She, the mistress of what has been and what shall be, may Earth make a wide world for us, unobstructed, in the midst of humankind.

**17.1.2**  She who bears the upland slopes, the plains, and the level stretches, the many-powered herbs — may Earth spread wide for us, may she prosper us.

**17.1.3**  She in whom the ocean, the river, and the waters dwell; she in whom the gods discovered the untruth; she who sustains in manifold ways the living breath that stirs — may that Earth bestow upon us cattle, horses, and food.

**17.1.4**  She upon whom the ancestors of old moved about; she upon whom the gods turned back the asuras; she in whom all this living, stirring universe dwells — may that Earth grant us the first draft of life.

**17.1.5**  She in whom the four directions of Earth exist; she in whom food and cultivated peoples arose — the station of cattle, horses, and birds — may Earth grant us fortune and vital radiance.

**17.1.6**  The all-sustaining, wealth-holding, steady foundation, golden-breasted resting place of the world — bearing Vaiśvānara, the universal fire, Earth, whose bull is Indra, may she bestow wealth upon us.

**17.1.7**  She on whom the gathering waters flow in their rounds, pouring day and night without ceasing — may that Earth, abundant-streamed, milk nourishment for us, and also sprinkle us with vital radiance.

**17.1.8**  She whom the gods guard, ever-wakeful, at all times, the Earth, the wide one, without negligence — may she milk sweet, dear nourishment for us, and also sprinkle us with vital radiance.

**17.1.9**  She who was the primordial waters above the ocean; whom the wise have traced through their visionary powers; she whose heart, in the highest sky, is wrapped in truth, the immortal essence of Earth — may that Earth place brilliance and strength in my realm for me.

**17.1.10**  She whom the two Aśvins measured out, upon whom Viṣṇu strode; she whom Indra, lord of power, made enemy-free for himself — may that Earth release milk for us as a mother for her son.

**17.2.1**  Your hills, your mountains, your snow-bearing ranges, your forests, O Earth — may it be gentle for us. I stand upon the tawny, black, reddish, all-formed, firm Earth, protected by Indra — unconquered, unslain, unwounded, I take my stand upon the Earth.

**17.2.2**  What is your center, O Earth, what is your navel, and those bodily energies that have arisen from you — set us there, purify us.

**17.2.3**  Earth is the mother, I am the son of Earth; Parjanya is the father — may he fill us with nourishment.

**17.2.4**  She on whose surface they set up the altar, on whom the all-skilled ones spread out the sacrifice; she on whose surface the wooden stakes are measured out, and the bright oblations ascend before — may that growing Earth increase us.

**17.2.5**  Whoever hates us, O Earth, whoever attacks us, whoever with his mind intends to slay us — deliver that one to us, O Earth, as you have done in former times.

**17.2.6**  Born from you, mortals move about on you; you sustain the two-footed and the four-footed. In you, O Earth, are these five races of humans, for whom the rising sun stretches out the immortal light with its rays to mortal beings.

**17.2.7**  May all those offspring be milked for us completely; O Earth, place the sweetness of speech in me.

**17.2.8**  The mother of all, of herbs, the firm Earth, the wide one, held firm by cosmic law — may we ever walk in her, benign and gentle, all our days.

**17.2.9**  Great is your assembly place; you have become great; great is the speed, your trembling, your quaking. Great Indra guards you with his might — O Earth, make us glorious in sight, as though shining with gold.

**17.2.10**  Fire is in the Earth, fire is in the herbs; the waters carry fire, fire is in the stones. Fire is within human beings; fires are in cattle and horses.

**17.3.1**  Fire blazes from the sky; from divine Fire comes the broad atmosphere. Mortals kindle Fire, the carrier of oblations, the lover of clarified butter.

**17.3.2**  Earth, the abode of fire, dark-kneed — may she not make me shrunken, wasted, or without brilliance. On the Earth they pour out the sacrifice, the duly prepared oblation, for the gods. On the Earth mortals live, sustained by food according to their nature. May that Earth grant us breath and long life; may Earth not make us bitten by old age.

**17.3.3**  That fragrance of yours, O Earth, which has arisen, which the herbs carry, which the waters carry, which the gandharvas and apsarases have shared, which befits a cow and a horse — with that make us fragrant; let no one hate us.

**17.3.4**  That fragrance of yours which entered the lotus, which was gathered at the wedding of Sūryā — the immortals, O Earth, took the fragrance first. With that make us fragrant; let no one hate us.

**17.3.5**  That lustre and splendor of yours, O Earth, which is in men and in women, which is in brides and in bridegrooms, which is in cattle and horses, in wild beasts, and in elephants — that vital radiance which is in a maiden, O Earth — with that unite us; let no one hate us.

**17.3.6**  Rock, Earth, stone, sand — the Earth that has been gathered together and held firm; she on whose surface the forest trees stand firm forever — to that golden-breasted, steadily held Earth we address our words.

**17.3.7**  Rising, sitting, standing, walking about — may we not stumble upon the Earth with our right and left feet.

**17.3.8**  I address the wide Earth, the patient Earth, the ground swelling with sacred power, bearing vigor, nourishment, and a share of food — upon you, O Earth, upon clarified butter, I take my seat.

**17.3.9**  May pure waters flow over my body; whoever my foe is in what is not dear to me, him I lay low. With the purifying strainer I purify the Earth.

**17.3.10**  Bearing treasures in many hidden places — gems, gold — may Earth bestow wealth on us. The goddess, wealth-giving, graciously bestowing, of good mind, may she grant us riches.

**17.4.1**  O Earth, may your eastern directions, your northern ones, those below you and those behind — may they all be pleasant for me as I travel; may I not fall in the world wherein I take my dwelling.

**17.4.2**  Push me not from behind, push me not from the front — O Earth, grant me well-being and breadth; drive away slaughter; let those who lie in ambush not find me.

**17.4.3**  As far as I look upon you, O Earth, with the shining sun, so long may my sight not be extinguished — year after year, ever higher.

**17.4.4**  Whatever I dig from you, O Earth, as a medicinal herb — may that grow back again; may I not pierce your vital parts, O one whom many search; may I not wound your heart.

**17.4.5**  When lying down I turn upon my right or my left side, O Earth; when we stretch out on our backs, lying facing you with our spines — do not harm us then, O Earth, you who bestow healing on all things.

**17.4.6**  Summer is yours, O Earth, and the rains, autumn, early winter, the cold season, and spring — your seasons are ordained year by year; may Earth with day and night yield nourishment to us.

**17.4.7**  She who drives away the serpent and searches out all things, in whom the fires dwell within the waters — she who drives off the Dasyus who scoff at the gods, Earth who chose Indra as Vṛtra did not choose him, bearing him up for the mighty, the virile bull — may he point us toward the wealth from the Earth that we desire.

**17.4.8**  May Bhaga set things in motion; may Indra go as the leader of the herd; she upon whom stand the sacrificial hall and the offering-shed, upon whom the post is fixed, upon whom the brahmins chant with Ṛgvedic hymns, those who know the Yajurveda with Sāman, upon whom the ritual priests are yoked to press soma for Indra to drink — may that Earth, well-disposed, place us graciously on the southern side at the sacrifice.

**17.4.9**  She upon whom the ancient world-making seers sang forth the cows, the seven wise ones together through sacrifice, ascetic rite, and the extended soma session — may she bestow on us cattle of every kind; may Savitṛ grant me long life.

**17.4.10**  She upon whom are food, rice, and barley, upon whom these five peoples dwell — to Earth, whose lord is Parjanya, let there be homage; let rain increase.

**17.5.1**  She upon whom mortals, the people of the village settlements, sing and dance; upon whom the battle-cry is raised, upon whom the war-drum sounds — may that Earth drive away our rivals; may she bring low the one who hates us.

**17.5.2**  She upon whom the divine-built cities stand, upon whose field they display their power — may Prajāpati make this Earth, who bears all creatures in her womb, delightful for us in every direction.

**17.5.3**  Earth bearing in many ways a people of diverse speech and diverse customs, each as if at home — like a steady cow that does not kick, may she yield us a thousand streams of wealth.

**17.5.4**  Whatever serpent, scorpion, sharp-fanged, frost-roused, buzzing worm lies in a crevice — the worm that stirs in the swampy ground in the rainy season, O Earth — may that crawler not creep upon us; and whatever is auspicious in it, with that be gracious to us.

**17.5.5**  Your roads that lead people in many directions, the track of the chariot, and the paths along which the mind travels — upon which both the good and the wicked walk — may we conquer on that path, free from foes, free from thieves.

**17.5.6**  Bearing the sluggish (or the soft?), heavy-bearing, enduring the dissolution of both good and evil — Earth, in accord with the boar, opens herself wide for the wild boar.

**17.5.7**  Your forest-dwelling animals, the beasts set loose in the woods, lions and tigers that roam man-eating — O Earth, drive away from here the hyena, the wolf, the evil omen, the she-bear, and the demon.

**17.5.8**  Gandharvas and Apsarases, those who wander about, the kimīdins — Piśācas, all the Rākṣasas — O Earth, drive all of them away from us.

**17.5.9**  She to whom winged two-footed creatures fly — swans, eagles, birds, and fowl; upon whom the wind, Mātariśvan, moves, raising dust-clouds and shaking the trees; the flame that blows along the forward and the inward current of the wind — she upon whom the black and the red are gathered together, upon whom day and night are ordained, spread out over the Earth.

**17.5.10**  Earth, enveloped and re-enveloped by the rain — may she sustain us with blessing in each beloved dwelling-place and resting-place.

**17.6.1**  Sky and Earth — this expanse — and the midspace have given me room; Fire, the Sun, the Waters, and all the gods together have bestowed wisdom upon me.

**17.6.2**  I am the prevailing one, named the Higher, here on Earth; I am the overpowerer, the all-conqueror, ever victorious in every direction.

**17.6.3**  O goddess, when you first spread out there in front, sent forth by the gods, you pervaded greatness — well-being entered into you at that very moment; you arranged the four directions.

**17.6.4**  In the villages, in the wildernesses, in the assembly halls that are upon the Earth — among them, O goddess Earth, may I rise up with honey-sweet speech.

**17.6.5**  What I speak, may I speak it sweetly; what I see, may the soma-pressers purify me; I am lustrous, I am impelled — those whom I strike down are the agitators.

**17.6.6**  Like a horse she has milked the dust — she scattered that people who oppressed the Earth when she was born; the gracious one, guardian of the world, embracing the forest trees and the herbs.

**17.6.7**  Peaceful, fragrant, pleasant, her udder filled with sweet drink, milk-laden — may Earth speak over us; may the Earth speak together with her milk.

**17.6.8**  O Mother Earth, set me down well-established in blessing; you who are in accord with Heaven — set me in splendor, in prosperity.

**17.6.9**  She whom Viśvakarmā sought out with an oblation, in whom the fires dwell within the waters — the vessel of enjoyment was laid hidden in a secret place; it became manifest with its riches, possessed of motherly powers.

**17.6.10**  You are the scattering-ground of peoples, Aditi, yielding all desires, of all forms; whatever is deficient in you, Prajāpati, in accord with his creatures, fills that up for you.

**17.7.1**  In which limb does his austerity abide? In which limb is his cosmic order established? Where do his vow and his faith stand? In which limb is his truth firmly founded?

**17.7.2**  Where is the sacred power, where do the waters stand? In which limb are the directions established? Along which limb do day and night, in mutual accord, move together as one?

**17.7.3**  In which limb does his earth stand? In which limb does the atmosphere stand? In which limb is heaven set in place? In which limb stands what is beyond the sky?

**17.7.4**  From which limb does his fire blaze? From which limb does Mātariśvan blow? From which limb does the moon measure out, measuring the great limb of the Pillar?

**17.7.5**  Striving to reach where does fire blaze upward? Striving to reach where does Mātariśvan blow? Where do the encompassing forces approach in their striving? Tell us that Pillar — which one precisely is it?

**17.7.6**  Striving to reach where do the two young women of different forms, day and night, run in mutual accord? Where do the waters approach in their striving? — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.7.7**  Where do the fortnights go? Where do the months go, in accord together with the year? Where the seasons go, where the seasonal moments go — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.7.8**  He upon whose branches Prajāpati upheld all the worlds — tell us that Pillar, which one precisely is it?

**17.7.9**  What is the highest, what is the lowest, and what is the middle, all-formed — which Prajāpati created — how far did the Pillar enter there? What did not enter it, where did that come to be?

**17.7.10**  How far did the Pillar enter into what has come to be? How much of what is yet to be lies latent within it? The one limb that it made thousandfold — how far did the Pillar enter there?

**17.8.1**  Where the worlds and the treasuries are, where people know the sacred power to be, where non-being and being are within — tell us that Pillar, which one precisely is it?

**17.8.2**  Where austerity, having gone forth beyond, holds up the higher cosmic order; where vow and faith, sacred power and the waters are gathered together — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.8.3**  In which earth, atmosphere, and heaven are set aloft; where fire, the moon, the sun, and wind stand fixed in place — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.8.4**  He in whose limb all thirty-three gods are gathered together — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.8.5**  Where the seers who brought things into being, the Ṛc verses, the Sāman, the Yajus, and the great earth — the solitary seer in whom they are all fixed — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.8.6**  Whose four directions stand as arteries stretched to their reach; in whom sacrifice has gone forth beyond — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.8.7**  Where immortality and death and the Person are gathered together; whose arteries are the ocean — tell us that Pillar, which one precisely is it?

**17.8.8**  Those who know the sacred power in the Person, they truly know the Pillar. He who knows Parameṣṭhin, and he who knows Prajāpati — those who know the eldest Brahman, they truly know the Pillar.

**17.8.9**  Whose head is Vaiśvānara, whose eyes the Aṅgirases became; whose limbs are the demonic forces — tell us that Pillar, which one precisely is it?

**17.8.10**  Whose mouth they call the sacred word, and whose tongue the honey-whip; whose udder they call the Virāj — [that Pillar, tell us which one precisely it is].

**17.9.1**  From whom they carved the Ṛc verses, from whom they scraped out the Yajus; whose body-hairs are the metres — tell us that Pillar, which one precisely is it?

**17.9.2**  People know the branch of non-being as if it were the highest support; yet those of lesser insight who worship his branch consider it to be being.

**17.9.3**  Where the Ādityas, the Rudras, and the Vasus are gathered together — tell us that Pillar, which one precisely is it?

**17.9.4**  Whose treasure the thirty-three gods always guard — who today knows that treasure which the gods protect?

**17.9.5**  Where the gods who know the sacred power worship the eldest Brahman — he who truly knows that Brahman, the knowers of Brahman know him.

**17.9.6**  Those gods named the Bṛhant ones were born from non-being; that one limb of the Pillar, further-knowing people call non-being.

**17.9.7**  Where the Pillar, bringing forth, set the ancient tradition in motion — that one limb of the Pillar those who follow the ancient way truly know.

**17.9.8**  Where the thirty-three gods took their portions as limbs and body-parts — those very thirty-three gods certain knowers of the sacred power truly know.

**17.9.9**  People know the Golden Embryo as the supreme, as the unexcellable risen one; the Pillar poured out that golden light in the beginning, within the world.

**17.9.10**  In the Pillar are the worlds; in the Pillar is austerity; upon the Pillar is cosmic order established. We know you, the Pillar, directly — in Indra all is gathered together.

**17.10.1**  In Indra are the worlds, in Indra is heat, in Indra is cosmic order established. We know you as Indra directly — in the Skambha all is gathered together.

**17.10.2**  I call out name by name, before the sun, before the dawn. That which first came into being — together with it, sovereignty departed; beyond that there is no being whatsoever.

**17.10.3**  He whose measure is the earth, whose belly is the atmosphere — who made heaven his head — to that greatest Brahman, homage.

**17.10.4**  He whose eye is the sun and whose moon is ever renewed — who made fire his mouth — to that greatest Brahman, homage.

**17.10.5**  He whose in-breath and out-breath is the wind, whose eyes became the Aṅgirases — who made the directions his cognizing faculty — to that greatest Brahman, homage.

**17.10.6**  He who was born from toil and austerity and pervaded all the worlds — who made Soma his alone — to that greatest Brahman, homage.

**17.10.7**  The Skambha upheld the earth and yonder heaven; the Skambha upheld the vast atmosphere. The Skambha upheld the six broad directions; the Skambha entered into this entire world of beings.

**17.10.8**  Why does the wind never cease? Why does the mind not rest? Why do the waters, ever seeking truth, keep moving on forever?

**17.10.9**  A great Yakṣa in the midst of the world, suffused with austerity upon the surface of the waters — upon it all the gods whatsoever take their refuge, like branches around the trunk of a tree.

**17.10.10**  He to whom with both hands and both feet, with voice, with hearing, with sight — to whom the gods ever offer tribute, the bounded to the boundless — tell me of that Skambha: who indeed is he?

**17.11.1**  From him darkness has been struck away; he has turned back from evil. All three lights that exist in Prajāpati — all are in him.

**17.11.2**  He who knows the golden reed standing in the waters — that one is truly the hidden Prajāpati.

**17.11.3**  No one surpasses Prajāpati, no one surpasses Parameṣṭhin. And yet there too our attention was turned, where the eldest passed beyond the beyond.

**17.11.4**  That truly exists beyond the earth; that truly exists beyond the sky. The worlds are strung upon it, and upon it these creatures are woven.

**17.12.1**  You who lick inside the pot, O ill-looking seeker of misplaced things — O Uruṇḍa, O Abhicaṅkrama, O Vakmaka, O Bastavāsini — we know all your names together, O she-demons. As many as are born among you, so many shall perish from here, O Sadānvāḥ.

**17.12.2**  Karṇā, Dṛṣadrathāmahā, Urukī, Keśinī, Kraku, Khaḍurī, Ambarīṣyā — we destroy these Sadānvāḥ she-demons.

**17.12.3**  Paṇḍugirā, Phāladatī, she who swallows without proper incantation — all of you we declare harmful; perish from here, O Sadānvāḥ.

**17.12.4**  Those she-demons who devour one who is lying down, who seek by night the sick man, and who lick from the mouth and hand of a sleeping person — accustomed to gnawing with their teeth — those we drive away from here.

**17.12.5**  The Sadānvāḥ she-demons who come flying and scattering, smelling of goat — I will pierce them with the Bhaṇvā sword, as a bull with sharp horns.

**17.12.6**  O Sadānvāḥ, O progeny of Sādānvī — both women and men together. With strength, powerfully, with might, I crush and smite the Rākṣasas.

**17.12.7**  The four-fanged ones, the jar-testicled ones, the long-haired ones, the blood-faced ones, those smelling of bottle-gourd, the rats — ill-named ones, we destroy you.

**17.12.8**  Born in the thicket, upon the tender one who weeps and wails from you — all of you ill-named ones together with your offspring, we destroy, O Sadānvāḥ.

**17.12.9**  Those whose offspring cry out within the clefts, and who blaze in the forest, who lie down upon the trees — [the remainder of the verse is lost in the manuscript].

**17.12.10**  Those who roam when storm-clouds rise, along with rain and lightning, who quickly seek out dwellings — all of you ill-named ones together with your offspring, we destroy, O Sadānvāḥ.

**17.13.1**  Those [demon-women] who arise from grain, from a field that has been sown, from what has been threshed — the fierce ones that attack what has been done, let those sadānvāḥ perish from here.

**17.13.2**  Those [women-demons] who take human form, who always have an evil stench, ever rough, with scattered feet — drive them away with the thunderbolt, expel them, O lord of Śacī!

**17.13.3**  Rise up! Run away! There is no shelter for you here. Indra will shatter together all your embryos and eggs at once.

**17.13.4**  Indra, strike down the thick-shelled one; crush the ill-fated kuha; the greed-demon, the one reeking of dung-smoke — we destroy these sadānvāḥ.

**17.13.5**  Why do they brew the naked-caller and the goat-snatcher who slays? The sharp-prodding viṭiṭiṅga-demons — we destroy those sadānvāḥ.

**17.13.6**  You bhaṇva-sadānvā demons subdue whose brew? Make the broth bitter! And when you are sated with the liquor — then you urinate out the surā.

**17.13.7**  After drinking of her [brew], one vomits and then lies down with a splitting head-pain. Those that corrupt the shared meal — we destroy those sadānvāḥ.

**17.13.8**  Let the foul-smelling dung-smoke demons go quickly to the trees! And let the evil-hearted arāyī-goblins stroke the house [to their ruin].

**17.13.9**  Among those [demons]: the solitary lurker, the bent-horned one, the forest-screamer, the sole laugher, the chattering one — all your names together, O bhaṇvā-demons, we know them. As many as have been born among you, let that many perish from here, O sadānvāḥ.

**17.13.10**  I drive forward the herb endowed with power, this victorious [plant] that overcomes poison, the herb that slays the sadānvāḥ — we speak it forth for victory.

**17.14.1**  O ill-seen one, terrible-eyed, naked bhaṇvā, sadānvā — the rushing, blazing, pure fire [is here] — what do you want here, arāyī? Do not come near my smoke! I press you down, O sadānvā.

**17.14.2**  The solitary heron-like one, the solitary digger — what do they seek, lurking by the fire? The ill-named arāyī-demons that wander at night seeking women in childbed — we drive those away from here.

**17.14.3**  The boaster, the fire-croucher, the dancing one full of restless curiosity, the granary-haunter, the chariot-breaker, the three-humped ones born from the threshing floor — we drive those away from here.

**17.14.4**  Those disheveled, maddened, broad-chested, terrible-eyed ones — some sit as if stretching out the heads of others, O sadānvā-demons. O Bṛhaspati, hurl their embryos far away!

**17.14.5**  Those whose odor of many forms circles a person on the path — let Agni Jātavedas overpower and drive away those sadānvāḥ from here.

**17.14.6**  Those who wish to devour a person being cremated, [who prey upon] the vacant [body] on the fire — you are called bhaṇvā niṣkuṣṭā ('the winnowed one'); we drive those sadānvāḥ away from here with the tip of the fist.

**17.14.7**  Those accustomed to the fire-pit who eat dry ash, like mares and she-asses — we destroy those sadānvāḥ.

**17.14.8**  Those sadānvāḥ who devour fetuses one by one at every morśa, who are accustomed to the body of a woman like red cows [attached to their spot] — we drive those away from here.

**17.14.9**  Those sadānvāḥ who arise from the ancestral realm, born of Indra, coming to us — we send those back again, as if returning a load that was brought.

**17.14.10**  Eaters of the raw, eaters of the cruel, eaters of what smells of no fire — eat that corpse carried off to the beyond, O sadānvāḥ! A bare corpse is your proper fare; what indeed do you seek in houses?

**17.15.1**  Those sadānvāḥ who are girls, who are old women, who are young women — let all go as kurūṭinī-demons, as the cowless outcast clan; let the arāyī-goblin creep away in awe from here.

**17.15.2**  Let Rudra loose against them his fury that slays wayfarers; let the archer smite them with lightning and the faultless thunderbolt. O Śakra, do not spare them; O Indra, shatter the bhaṇvā-demons!

**17.15.3**  Those gluttonous ones who devour boys — some — and old men — let Indra the Vṛtra-slayer destroy them, the god who repels all demons from all sides.

**17.15.4**  Those who are slave-women of the asuras and those made [to harm] humans as well — let both kinds go far away, beyond the distance of ninety [river-crossings] by boat.

**17.15.5**  Those whose din when gathered sounds like the growling of wolves — those that offer pracaṅkaśā ['the harsh snatcher'] as a gift-in-return — we destroy those sadānvāḥ.

**17.15.6**  The yakṣa-spirits that are stirred up at evening from wherever they station themselves to roam the night — let Agni, overpowering them all, repel those demons from all sides.

**17.15.7**  Those ṛkṣīkā-demons, the egg-laying kalīlā-ones, the pulīkā-beings born in the waters — one guardian alone knows their origin. Those sadānvāḥ who have been born — we drive them away from here.

**17.15.8**  The heavy-shadowed one, the one borne upward, the child-devourer, the echo-maker, the over-milker, the wanderer, the cotton-tufted one — all these granddaughters of Caṇḍa among the sadānvāḥ we destroy.

**17.15.9**  The loudly speaking one, the name-caller, the thundering one, the claw-footed one, the protruding-toothed one, the noseless one — we destroy these sadānvāḥ.
