**5.1.1**  Homage to the tawny-armed one, the fierce one born in the Sindhu — whoever pays homage to her, he departs away from this man's house.

**5.1.2**  Depart from our houses, depart from the calf-rope! You will entangle yourself right here — come down from the kitchen.

**5.1.3**  Hā, O mother, O Suhūtalā! And also, O Sāmanantamā! Your son who is spotted-armed — make him your sāman; and also your daughter, your granddaughter — and you yourself become reconciled.

**5.1.4**  Let Bhūtapati drive them out, let Indra drive out the mind-afflicting Sadānvās! May he who is seated at the foundation of the house stand over them with his thunderbolt.

**5.1.5**  Depart hence, O Sadānvās, not harming this house! Let the milch-cow who stands here remain, along with the draft-ox and the heifer.

**5.1.6**  You who roam as one who overpowers, like a bull that has prevailed — we address you as the Sadānvā-slayer, for the purpose of victory.

**5.1.7**  Overpower our malevolent foe, overpower the one arrayed in battle against us! Overpower all the rākṣasas — you are the overpowering one, O plant!

**5.1.8**  You overpower tigers, you overpower lions with teeth on both jaws; even bees that make honey — you overpower them, O plant!

**5.2.1**  This sovereign lady goes forward to the Father, to the first-born, to those who stand firm on the earth. For him they prepare this radiant, serpent-like gharma-vessel of the first nourishing draught.

**5.2.2**  Brahman, born first in the east, the seer Vena illumined it beyond the limits with brilliant light. Its supreme stations are those of the foundation — it opened wide the womb of what exists and of what does not exist.

**5.2.3**  He who was born knowing the kinship of this — the god speaks forth all births; Brahman seized Brahman up from the midst, rising from low to high, and self-power stood forth.

**5.2.4**  The great one propped apart the vast realm when born; a second time, heaven and the earthly atmosphere. Arising from the foundation by birth to the foremost position — Bṛhaspati is his deity, the universal sovereign.

**5.2.5**  Now that cow-domain drives forward the greatness of this great, primal deity. He was born thus, together with the many, and indeed he shall be unwavering from the former half.

**5.2.6**  For he, standing by cosmic order in heaven and on earth, props apart the great atmospheric spaces, the two realms. When the radiant day was born from the light, may the luminous, unstained ones shine forth.

**5.2.7**  Thus Atharvā-Bṛhaspati addressed the all-divine Father with homage: "You are the begettor of the universe, you sustain first — the wise poet, self-powerful, not betraying the gods."

**5.2.8**  He who approaches the foremost with his head, with might — the gods worship Bṛhaspati: he split Vala apart, he shatters foes with a crash, bellowing he conquered the cows, the sun, and the waters.

**5.3.1**  That sun has flown up, the widely-seen, the slayer of the unseen. Rising, may he smite with his rays — rising he rendered them devoid of potency.

**5.3.2**  That sun has set, the universally-seen, the slayer of the unseen. Setting, may he smite with his rays — setting he rendered them devoid of potency.

**5.3.3**  Both those that are seen and those that are unseen — those active in both modes here — I have seized their name; let them all perish together.

**5.3.4**  O herb, slayer of the unseen, of unmeasured power, victorious over poison — O Cukākaṇi, you were born to slay the seen ones; slay them by species.

**5.3.5**  Slay the chief of the unseen ones, of the serpents that roam in vain! Crush all species of worms as a thresher crushes barley.

**5.3.6**  Both the stinger and the creeper, and that one who is the eleventh male — O Cukākaṇi, cut them all down as a man with an axe cuts a tree.

**5.3.7**  Cut them down together, O Cukākaṇi, as a man with an axe cuts a tree! Burn all species of worms together as fire burns dry grass.

**5.3.8**  Agni, standing at the post, blazing, full of splendor — let him torment all species of worms! O Jātavedas, allied with Bṛhaspati, smite the unseen ones as a millstone smites black beans.

**5.4.1**  O Agni, may splendor be mine at the invocations; kindled by thee, may we nourish our bodies. May the four quarters bow down to me; with thee as overseer may we conquer armies.

**5.4.2**  O Agni, thrusting back the fury of our foes, thou art our herdsman — guard us on every side. Let the ill-willed foes turn away, wide awake though they be; may the mind of these wicked ones be scattered in many ways.

**5.4.3**  May all the gods be at my invocation — the Maruts with Indra, Viṣṇu, Agni. May the wide intermediate space be mine; in this my desire, may Vāyu blow favorably for me.

**5.4.4**  May they sacrifice to me; may what is dear to me be true in purpose, the intention of my mind. Into no sin whatsoever shall I fall; may all the gods protect me here.

**5.4.5**  May the gods sacrifice wealth unto me; mine shall be the blessing, mine the invocation of the gods. May the divine priests win this for us; may we be unharmed in body, well-heroied.

**5.4.6**  O divine, wide six realms, make broad space for us; all ye gods, rejoice here. May we not be bereft of offspring, not of wealth; may we not be delivered to the hater, O King Soma.

**5.4.7**  May the wide-ranging great one extend shelter to us in this contest, the much-invoked one of abundant food. O bay-horsed Indra, be gracious to us for our offspring; harm us not, deliver us not over.

**5.4.8**  Dhātṛ, Vidhātṛ, the lord of the world; divine Savitṛ, conqueror of enemies; Bṛhaspati, Indra, Agni, and both Aśvins — may these gods protect the sacrificer from Nirṛti.

**5.4.9**  Call Indra hitherward, here beyond, for the victory of the conqueror. Let him be ours alone; from here let him work heroic power.

**5.4.10**  We call Indra hitherward from yonder — him who wins cattle, wins wealth, wins horses. Be pleased with this our sacrifice at the invocation; O bay-horsed one, we make thee our ally.

**5.4.11**  Indra the protector, Indra the helper, easily invoked in every call, Indra the hero — let us invoke the mighty one, the much-invoked Indra; may Indra the bountiful guard our well-being.

**5.4.12**  The three goddesses bestow great shelter upon me, and also prosperity upon my offspring and body. May the clans of one mind be well-disposed toward me; may the ancestral field acknowledge us.

**5.4.13**  He who with Śakra's fury as Indra's enemy seeks to slay us — smite thou him, O Vṛtra-slayer; bring wealth hither to us.

**5.4.14**  Those who curse us — let them come to naught; by Indra and Agni we repel them. May the Ādityas and Rudras who touch the heights make me the fierce overseer, the supreme sovereign.

**5.5.2**  The atmosphere is the sacrificial cow; she bears Vāyu as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.3**  Heaven is the sacrificial cow; she bears Sūrya as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.4**  The Ṛg-veda is the sacrificial cow; she bears the Sāman as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.5**  The sacrificial fee (dakṣiṇā) is the sacrificial cow; she bears the Yajña as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.6**  The Viś (the common people) is the sacrificial cow; she bears the Kṣatriya as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.7**  Speech (Vāc) is the sacrificial cow; she bears the Supreme Being (Parameṣṭhin) as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.8**  The cow-sacrifice (vaśā) itself is the sacrificial cow; she bears the Rājanya as her embryo. [Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.]

**5.5.9**  The Sāman is the sacrificial cow; she bears the year (Saṃvatsara) as her embryo. Protect this one; to thee is the oblation due, to thee reverence, to it svāhā.

**5.6.1**  Seven suns have entered the sky; the one endowed with the southern path follows them along their way. All of them together pour down clarified butter upon him, milking out nourishment without stumbling.

**5.6.2**  While shining down below, Sūrya destroys the disease of the Dāsas by scorching thoroughly. He who has his wings spread upward rises, following the great courses.

**5.6.3**  By his upward shining we know the Pitṛs; by his thorough scorching, we know the Dasyus. Those other suns that move in secret following their own courses — they too we know.

**5.6.4**  Heaven goes along with these peoples; five others dwell higher still, beyond the sky. Into that vast heaven, brahman entered fully; he who truly knows them has crossed over to the farther shore.

**5.6.5**  He who gives, he who causes gifts to be given, or he who with faith deposits treasure — may Yama, the son of Vivasvat, king, protect all those treasures.

**5.6.6**  May the cheats who steal the sacrificial fees from the right-side donors not learn of these treasures. By an easy path King Yama leads all such transgressors away.

**5.6.7**  By the path by which Vaivasvata Yama the king went hence — by that very path may Agni Vaiśvānara lead us, knowing the way, the path-maker, the herdsman of all.

**5.6.8**  The great light is placed within mortals, by which the gods crossed over their enemies. By that bridge may we all cross over — by the Vaiśvānara light that dispels disease, O gods.

**5.6.9**  Upward we rise, beholding the higher light beyond the darkness. We have reached the divine Sūrya among the gods, the highest light.

**5.6.10**  Āroka, Bhrāja, Paṭara, Pataṅga, Svarṇara, the luminous Vibhāsa — all of them pour down clarified butter upon him, milking out nourishment without stumbling.

**5.7.1**  Let the quarters of the sky, cloud-bearing, fly up together; let the clouds driven by the wind come together. Let the roaring waters of the great bull, cloud-laden, resounding, refresh the earth.

**5.7.2**  The all-pervading wind surveys the clouds; let the rushing torrents of waters rise up in every direction. Let the streams of rain magnify the earth; let the plants of every form spring up in their diversity.

**5.7.3**  Thunder forth, O Parjanya, shake the ocean with your roar; saturate the earth with your milk. Through you let the rain be sent forth in abundance; let the lean cow seeking fodder come home.

**5.7.4**  Stir up, O Maruts, from the ocean; let the blazing hymns send the clouds flying upward. The mighty, bounteous ones pour forth rain; let the plants be attended by the essences of the waters.

**5.7.5**  Let the Marut-hosts sing out to you, O Parjanya, thundering one by one in their distinct voices. Let the streams of the raining rain pour forth over the earth.

**5.7.6**  Let the bounteous ones flow together — the springs and the serpentine water-courses. Let the winds of the raining rain blow forth along the earth.

**5.7.7**  Wind, lightning, cloud, and rain — let the bounteous ones come together. Swell forth, fill out; pour together with milk upon the earth.

**5.7.8**  Agni, united with the bodies of the waters, who became the lord of the plants — may Jātavedas, the All-knower, win rain for us, the breath of life, the immortal drink, from heaven, for our offspring.

**5.7.9**  Let lightning flash in every direction; let the winds blow from all quarters. Let the clouds, set in motion by the Maruts, rain down upon the earth.

**5.7.10**  Prajāpati, stirring up the waters from the primordial flood, from the ocean, agitates the sea. Let the seed of the virile horse swell forth; come hither, our father, the mighty Asura, pouring down the waters with this thunder.

**5.7.11**  Let the gurgling whirlpools of the waters breathe; send down the waters below. Let the frogs with their speckled arms speak out along the pools.

**5.7.12**  Having lain dormant for a year, like Brahmaṇas who observe their vow, the frogs have spoken forth, quickened into voice by Parjanya.

**5.7.13**  Speak up, O little frog; speak the rain aloud, O tāduri-frog. Float in the middle of the pond, spreading out your four limbs.

**5.7.14**  Draw up the great treasury and sprinkle it; let there be lightning, let the wind blow. Let them extend the sacrifice released in manifold ways; let the plants be filled with gladness.

**5.8.1**  As wide as heaven and earth in their expanse, as far as the seven rivers extend — the utterance that corrupts the poison I have now spoken forth from here.

**5.8.2**  Suparṇa, the winged Garutmān, first carried you, O poison. He neither brought you up nor tasted you, and yet he became its father.

**5.8.3**  That poison which the five-fingered one placed upon you, even upon the curved part of the bow — from the arms of Apaskambha I have spoken the poison forth.

**5.8.4**  From the arrow-shaft I have spoken the poison forth, and from the collyrium, and from the quiver likewise. From the tail-fin, from the horn, from the kurmal plant — I have spoken the poison forth.

**5.8.5**  Sapless is your dart, the arrow-shaft, and likewise sapless is your poison. And of the sapless tree, your bow is utterly devoid of potency.

**5.8.6**  Those who ground it, those who smeared it on, those who shot it, those who let it fly — may they all be castrated. The poison-mountain is made castrate.

**5.8.7**  Castrated are your diggers; castrated you yourself are, O herb. Castrated is that mountain, that hill, from which this poison was born.

**5.8.8**  This water — let it ward off what must be warded off; it has been brought from the realm of Varuṇa. There, where amṛta has been poured in, that has rendered the poison sapless.

**5.9.1**  With the khadira-wood comb, with the water therein, and with the teeth of the kaṅkata-comb — with that which is the very poison of the poison, I have destroyed the evil ones.

**5.9.2**  With the kiti-plant of a hundred joints, with the thousand-eyed hide, with sharp digging-spades we drive out the sadānvā-demons.

**5.9.3**  You are might, O mighty one; drive the Kaṇva-demons away hence, to the beyond. These that have just now arrived and those seizure-demons that were here before.

**5.9.4**  Let them not remain thus, let them not remain here. A head like a pot, like one with horns — O Bṛhaspati, come here, sharp-horned, looking down upon the sadānvā-demons.

**5.9.5**  Your churning-rods have now been shattered; your milk-pail has been split. The cow has given to you today, O Kaṇva-demon. Withdraw; I crush what is lower.

**5.9.6**  Those that stand at the spindle, those at the wicker-frame, those that swing upon the swing, and those that are dreadful — those that harm the embryos: all those evil ones I have destroyed.

**5.9.7**  Those that dwell in garments and those that haunt distant places — the dark-blue ones, the tawny ones, and the red ones — those that harm the embryos: all those evil ones I have destroyed.

**5.9.8**  Those that draw in and those that draw out — bind up his breath. Let all the ill-named ones assemble together and leave not a remnant of this man.

**5.10.1**  This one that has been struck by the pestle, ground by the millstone, infused with poison — Agni burns, the sky burns; burn thou likewise, O surā.

**5.10.2**  Those who cause the sprout to grow declared thee poison; let the poison flow down into the jar. Thy raw state is poison, O surā; set in the hand thou art poison — become [thou] placed-back-as-poison.

**5.10.3**  Let the grain be a lion for thee, the boiled rice a tiger; let the kneading-board be a spotted serpent — flow together [as if] into the heart of a wolf.

**5.10.4**  This one that has been fermented in the vessel, dripping with fresh grass, crackling with residue — born of the fury of a boar, crush the one who lies with feet upturned.

**5.10.5**  Swelling up, causing to fall, dust-tawny, crackling with residue — born of fury dug up from the ground: what is behind, make thou that to be in front.

**5.10.6**  Let thy straining be poison, O surā; let there be blood in thy vessel. Let them churn quivers and bows away from thee, one from another.

**5.10.7**  These mortal drinkers walk about blood-smeared, drinking poison, made powerful by thee, O surā. Some, struck down, still incite others to fight — proclaim that very greatness of surā.

**5.10.8**  Strike them down with force, O plant with running sap; intoxicate them for combat. Let the surā-drinker, with garments torn, with forearms broken, with head shattered, encounter [his fate] — running with thy stream.

**5.10.9**  Drink the poison-infused [brew] in great excitement, mixed with raw flesh, blended with blood. The one whose hand has been cut off wanders through the village, bartering manifold deeds of hostile slaying.

**5.10.10**  I lift up the sword-bearing, arrow-bearing [power] from the hundred. Intoxicate, thoroughly intoxicate them; cause them to rear up like a serpent; let them not leave one another.

**5.11.1**  May Agni consent for thee; may Varuṇa consent for thee. From that let a son be born to thee; let him, clad in armor, fight among the cattle.

**5.11.2**  Know this, O Vāyu; know this, O Indra [and] Bṛhaspati. We perform this ointment-rite for the obtaining of a son, [this rite] that causes the birth of a male.

**5.11.3**  That by which this [womb] has been obstructed, on account of which thou dost not obtain a son — from that sin may Indra and Agni protect thee day by day.

**5.11.4**  May the Atharvans, the Aṅgirasas, all the gods who strengthen cosmic order — may they hear my invocation today, for the obtaining of a son for her.

**5.11.5**  Indrāṇī, Varuṇānī, Sinīvālī, and also Aditi — Varutrī, the fierce one among the consorts of the gods: let her point out a son for thee today.

**5.11.7**  Those whose names I have grasped and those whose names I have not remembered — all those gods, assembling together, shall grant thee a son full of vitality.

**5.12.1**  This bull, Madhavāna, was born from the honey-rich [plants]; let him indeed lie down in thy womb — potent is [the power] becoming a male.

**5.13.1**  Auspicious, together with auspicious ones, full of vigor, unite with thy body, O Jātavedas! Bearing a jewel, well-disposed, from the front — I scatter thee forth from the houses for radiance.

**5.13.2**  On earth the hot-pot is set firm; in the atmosphere, resting upon heaven. May Heaven protect him on every side — he who cooks thee, O rice-oblation.

**5.13.3**  Those who have set the ocean in motion, and the Sindhu, those who have moved the atmosphere, the earth, and the sky — the gods who travel upon the same chariot as the wind — him, the cooker of the rice-oblation, those gods reach here.

**5.13.4**  By the Ṛc-verse is the pot set upon the fire; by the Sāman-chant the rice-oblation is cooked. This I deem to be the Soma-shoot — this oblation is for all the gods, the Vaiśvadeva offering.

**5.13.5**  What has clung — in the mortar, in the pestle, in the winnowing fan, on the ground, in the cooking-pot — the drops that have splashed, the washings that have been made — all that I fill up for thee with the sacred formula.

**5.13.6**  Rise upward! Do not scatter; dispel the intervening murk. Crossing over all the Rākṣasas, mount thou into heaven.

**5.13.7**  Swift for us, become one that knows no obstacle; let this be established together with the sacred formulas — in harmony with earth, in harmony with Agni, in harmony with the rays of the sun, in harmony with the works of the gods.

**5.14.1**  Thou art the face of prosperity, the ray of truth; thy high-sounding fame reaches heaven. Let the two oblation-makers rise up; worship the gods well; cast away the pressings.

**5.14.2**  The divine waters are the makers of the sacrifice; the resplendent goddesses are the makers of the oblation. The rice-oblation in a single vessel is measured out equal to the Agniṣṭoma rite.

**5.14.3**  Thou art the Gāyatrī metre, the carrier of the oblation; as the deity Agni thou art fully kindled. Flowing in a thousand streams, in the world of the well-doer, with a back of clarified butter — thou art immortal.

**5.14.4**  Let the two Parameṣṭhins — Tapas (Austerity) and Satya (Truth) — partake of the rice-oblation. By those two, the heavenly world is brought here; therefore thou art called the overlord.

**5.14.5**  Thou art of wide-going song, measured by the breath of Vāyu. Strike down contemptuous words from me; may I be freed from wrongdoing.

**5.14.6**  Drive away the Rākṣasas with splendor; honor the oblation for the gods. Thou art all-pervading and wide-spreading.

**5.14.7**  Like the high-flying Suparṇa, fly up to heaven; make that dear one dear to the gods; deliver me to the ṛṣis. Let the pure partake of the pure; may the well-doers drink the honey.

**5.14.8**  Two kinds of gods have come to our sacrifice — those whom the rice-oblation pleases and those by whom it is invited. Let the Ādityās and the Aṅgirasas partake of this heavenly oblation, sitting down in due season.

**5.15.1**  The first portion of the beestings, of milk, of clarified butter, of food — this we gather together. This share we send forth to those who eat the unhurled oblation; let the divine gods accept that oblation of ours.

**5.15.2**  Some eat the poured oblation; others eat the unpoured oblation; both together move about the Vaiśvadeva sacrificial offering. Let them, going in harmony, rejoice here; find delight in nourishment and strength for the sacrificer.

**5.15.3**  Let neither Bhava nor Śarva smite these cattle; let no rough-maned disease seize our calves. Those already born and those still within the womb — unharmed, O Agni, let them take hold of the teat.

**5.15.4**  These cattle, bringing forth, fruitful — let them be of one mind with the women. May the gods mingle abundance into them; their calves I unite with life-force and fat.

**5.15.5**  May the women and the cows conceive; may Viṣṇu fashion the womb aright. Receiving the seed of the bull, the impetuous draught-bull moves after the cow in heat.

**5.15.6**  The offered first-portion injures nothing at all; make the Soma-honey according to your wish. Let those who eat the unhurled oblation lead the sacrifice well; may increase of wealth attend the sacrificer.

**5.15.7**  Let the rivers enter into thy footprints, O Earth; let the plants shoot up; let the refreshments be spurred on. Strike the ocean to its ridge, O Maruts of Parjanya; let the auspicious grain ripen; let the world rejoice.

**5.15.8**  The seven Ṛṣis, seven are their seats, ten are the finger-like ladles of the Aśvins, five are the prizes of the course. May the breath, the diffused breath, the mind, the intention, and the divine Speech — knowing it all — carry the oblation to the gods.

**5.15.9**  Those worms that are visible and those that are invisible, and those that are lean — the heads of all these I cut off with a sword; and then their calves I unite with life-force and fat.

**5.16.1**  May Heaven and Earth together milk this sacrifice; may Mātariśvan, the Purifier, do so from the fore. May Tvaṣṭṛ, Vāyu together with Soma, and Vāta milk it out, unflinching.

**5.16.2**  I heat the hot-pot with a stream of ambrosia; I present the oblation to Savitṛ for the gods. May the gods eat the bright, well-cooked oblation, while the offerer pours into the womb of immortality.

**5.16.3**  We have caused the oblation cooked on Agni's fire to send up its fragrance; mount the back of the immortal abode. O forest-lords, strew the sacred grass; make it all around sweet as honey, rich as ghee.

**5.16.4**  Whatever disease-demon lurks in the waters, I appease him for you; I anoint this pasture-land with nourishing strength. I make the suckling milk uncontaminated for you; drinking life-breath, come back to the herd.

**5.16.5**  May the sons of Iḍā and of the friendly cows suck their milk unharmed, without injury. May the crops be well-tended according to the seasons; may Iryā the herdsman and Vāyu guard them.

**5.16.6**  Drink this ghee in due measure—it is secretly placed, deposited among human beings. All you gods, and Vaiśvadeva in Agni, rejoice in the oblation, each according to his share.

**5.36.1**  The fetters belonging to Varuṇa and those belonging to Nirṛti, the fetters of trees and of plants, those earthly ones that have arisen from the earth — may they not harm you; may welfare be yours.

**5.36.2**  The fetters that are in the atmosphere, those that are in the sky, those scattered throughout food that bind in manifold ways, those that have arisen from among cattle — may they not harm you; may welfare be yours.

**5.36.3**  The human bond with which men curse, or the priestly rite of the fathers which one undertakes — Varuṇa who comes as the proximate one, may he not harm you; may welfare be yours.

**5.36.4**  Whether you plunged into the waters with intent, or whether you laid hold of the fire when it was blazing — whatever falsehood you spoke, knowing or unknowing, may that not harm you; may welfare be yours.
