**16.1.1**  Homage to Antaka, to Mṛtyu — let this man remain here together with his vital breath. In the portion of Sūrya, in the realm of immortality, may your in-breath and out-breath take their delight here.

**16.1.2**  Bhaga has seized him upward, the radiant Soma has seized him upward; the Maruts, the gods, have raised him up — Indra and Agni have raised him for well-being.

**16.1.3**  Here is your vital force, here your breath, here your life-span, here your mind. With the divine word we bear you up out of the nooses of Nirṛti.

**16.1.4**  Rise up and do not fall down; loosening the foot-fetter of Death, do not sever yourself from this world, from the sight of Agni and Sūrya.

**16.1.5**  May Vāyu, Mātariśvan, blow for you; may the waters rain down their immortal gifts upon you; may Sūrya warm your body with well-being. Let Death be compassionate toward you — do not perish.

**16.1.6**  Your going is upward, O man, not a going-down; may your living make your vitality complete. Mount this immortal, pleasant chariot, and then in old age speak forth at the assembly.

**16.1.7**  Let not your mind go thither; let it not be lost. Do not stray from the living in intoxication; do not follow the Pitṛs. May all the gods protect you here.

**16.1.8**  Do not fix your gaze upon those who have gone, those who lead to the far realm. Ascend, come from darkness to light — we take hold of your hand.

**16.1.9**  Śyāma and Śabala, the two dogs of Yama, the road-guardians sent forth — come hither; do not stare away, do not stand there with your mind turned backward.

**16.1.10**  Do not travel this path — it is dreadful, the one by which they lead those who went before; so I declare it. Darkness lies yonder, O man; do not fall forward upon it. Fear lies beyond, but safety lies this side of you.

**16.2.1**  May the fires that dwell within the waters guard you; may that fire which men kindle guard you. May Vaiśvānara, Jātavedas, guard you. May the heavenly one not scorch you along with the lightning.

**16.2.2**  May Earth guard you, may Dyaus guard you; may Sūrya and Candramās guard you. Let the flesh-eater not assail you — keep away from Saṃkasuka from afar.

**16.2.3**  May the mid-air with its divine weapon guard you; may Awareness and Counter-awareness guard you. May the Sleepless One and the Ever-wakeful guard you; may the Watcher guard you, and the Vigilant.

**16.2.4**  Let them guard you, let them protect you, let them shield you from distress. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**16.2.5**  For the living, in the community, may Vāyu, Indra, Dhātṛ, the protecting Savitṛ establish you. Let not the breath forsake your strength — we call after your vital spirit.

**16.2.6**  Let neither the jaw nor the snap of teeth find you; let not the darkness; let not the tongue of the sacrificial grass cause your destruction — how could you perish? May the Ādityas and Vasus raise you up; may Indra and Agni raise you for well-being.

**16.2.7**  O gods, let this man be here only; let him not go from here to yonder. We ferry this one across from Death with a thousandfold heroic power.

**16.2.8**  I have ferried you across from Death; may the life-sustaining ones blow strength into you. Let not the disheveled-haired woman weep over you; let not she who wails with grief lament for you.

**16.2.9**  I have fetched you back; I have found you. You have returned, renewed. With every limb intact, all your sight, and all your life-span I have found for you.

**16.2.10**  Heaven has seized you upward, and Earth, and Prajāpati has seized you upward. The plants whose queen is Soma have ferried you across from Death.

**16.2.11**  As far as your light extended, darkness has retreated from you. Away from you we blow Death, Nirṛti, and wasting disease.

**16.3.1**  Grasp hold of this hearing of immortality; may your old age be unbroken. I bring back your vital force and your life-span. Do not go to the dust, to darkness — do not perish.

**16.3.2**  Come back to the light of the living, to the world; I carry you to a hundred autumns. I make your life-span ever longer and more extended. Let the nooses of Death, the curses, be released.

**16.3.3**  From Vāyu I have recovered your breath; from Sūrya I have recovered your sight all around. Whatever mind is yours, in you I hold it steady. Awaken with all your limbs; speak, converse with your tongue.

**16.3.4**  With the breath of bipeds and quadrupeds I blow upon you as upon a fire just kindled. Homage to your eye, O Mṛtyu; I have paid homage to your breath.

**16.3.5**  Let this one live; let him not die — we stir this one back into life. I make the healing remedy for him. O Mṛtyu, do not slay this man.

**16.3.6**  The Jīvalā, the Naghāriṣā, the living herb — the protecting, enduring, powerful Arundhatī — these I invoke here for his inviolate well-being.

**16.3.7**  Speak favorably; do not seize him — release this one. Belonging to you entirely, let him who lives all his years be here. O Bhava and Śarva, show mercy, grant shelter; thrust evil away and bestow life.

**16.3.8**  May the weapon of the gods enfold you protectively. I ferry you across; I have carried you over from the dust of Death. Driving the flesh-eating fire away from afar, I place a boundary around you for the sake of life.

**16.3.9**  O Mṛtyu, speak favorably for this one; be gracious to him — let him go up from here. Uninjured, with all his limbs intact, well-formed, let him attain the enjoyment of the self through old age for a hundred years.

**16.3.10**  O Mṛtyu, the dusty downward path of yours — I have turned it away from him. Guarding this man from that path, we make the brahman a coat of armor for him.

**16.4.1**  I make for you breath and vital breath; let old age and death make long life and well-being. The messengers of Yama dispatched by Vaivasvata, all those who roam about — I drive them far away.

**16.4.2**  Far away — malice, dissolution, the seizing demon beyond, flesh-devouring Piśācas; whatever evil being there is, every Rakṣas — that we smite away like darkness.

**16.4.3**  From immortal Agni Jātavedas, long-lived and cognizant of all births, I take breath for you, as for Manu — so that you may be unharmed and immortal in fellowship. This I do for you; may this be fulfilled for you.

**16.4.4**  May Heaven and Earth be auspicious to you, resting upon you without scorching heat, shining with splendour. May the sun warm you auspiciously; may the wind blow auspiciously upon your heart. May the heavenly, milk-rich waters flow auspiciously over you; may the plants be auspicious to you.

**16.4.5**  I have lifted you up from the lower region toward the higher earth. There let both Ādityas — Sun and Moon together — guard you.

**16.4.6**  The garment you put on for wearing, the girdle you fasten about yourself — we make that auspicious for your body; may it be unharmful in contact with you.

**16.4.7**  When, with a razor of fine keenness, the barber shaves off hair and beard — making the face beautiful, let it not rob this one of life.

**16.4.8**  Auspicious to you be rice and barley — not causing weakness, not causing oppression. These two repel disease; these two release from distress.

**16.4.9**  Whatever you eat, whatever you drink — the grain, the produce of tillage, the milk — what is to be eaten and what is not to be eaten: all food I make free of poison for you.

**16.4.10**  To both Day and Night together we entrust you. From the greedy devouring demons — protect this one of ours on all sides.

**16.5.1**  May there be for you a hundred, ten thousand years of life; two ages, three, four. May Indra and Agni, may all the gods consent to this without anger.

**16.5.2**  For autumn, for winter, for spring, for summer we entrust you. May the rainy seasons be gentle for you — those in which the plants flourish.

**16.5.3**  Death rules the two-footed; Death rules the four-footed. From that Death, lord of cattle, I lift you up. May he not slay you.

**16.5.4**  Unharmed, you shall not die; you shall not die — fear not. Truly, they do not perish there; they do not go to the lower realm of dust.

**16.5.5**  Truly everything lives there — cow, horse, man, beast — where this sacred formula is performed as a protective enclosure for life, for bliss.

**16.5.6**  May it guard you on all sides from the sorcery of kindred folk, from those of equal standing. Be one who does not wither; be immortal, one who outlives; may your vital forces not abandon your body.

**16.5.7**  May all the gods sustain you with immortality; may Paśupati be your advocate. May Savitṛ make you free of sickness; may breath enter you for the sake of life.

**16.5.8**  The hundred-and-one deaths, the fatal nights that are not to be outlived — from those let the gods release you, above and beyond Agni Vaiśvānara.

**16.6.1**  I kindle the strong slayer of Rakṣases; I approach the shelter of the most widely spread Friend. Agni, whetted and kindled with rites — may he protect us from harm by day, by night.

**16.6.2**  Touch with your flame those who grow strong with iron fangs, O Jātavedas, when kindled. With your tongue seize the fool-gods; having consumed the flesh-eaters, close your mouth upon them.

**16.6.3**  O bearer of both kinds, apply your fangs — fierce and whetted — upon the lower and the upper. And roam through the atmosphere, O Agni; crush the sorcerers with your jaws; fall upon them.

**16.6.4**  O Agni, pierce the skin of the sorcerer; may the fierce thunderbolt strike him down with heat. Shatter his joints, O Jātavedas; the flesh-eating fire, hungering for raw flesh, picks him apart.

**16.6.5**  O Agni, bending your arrows with sacrificial rites, smearing your shafts with speech and with thunderbolts — with those, pierce the sorcerers in their hearts; shatter the arms of those who oppose us.

**16.6.6**  Wherever you now see him, O Jātavedas, O Agni — standing still or moving about — or a sorcerer flying through the atmosphere: pierce him through with darkness, wholly, you who are whetted.

**16.6.7**  And shatter the sorcerer with your spears when seized, O Jātavedas; and when he is about to fly. Strike first, O blazing Agni; may the raw-flesh-eating female spirits, shrieking, devour him.

**16.6.8**  Declare here, O Agni, which one he is — the sorcerer who hears this. Seize him, O youngest one, with fuel; surrender him to the sight of the man-watching eye.

**16.6.9**  With your keen eye, O Agni, guard the sacrifice; lead it forward eastward to the Vasus, O wise one. Blazing against the fierce Rakṣases — may the sorcerers not deceive you, O watcher of men.

**16.6.10**  O watcher of men, look upon the Rakṣas among the settlements; shatter his three foremost points. Shatter his ribs, O Agni, with your heat; cleave the root of the sorcerer into three.

**16.7.1**  Thrice let the yātudhāna come within thy snare; he who smites the truth with falsehood, O Agni — him, crackling with thy flame, O Jātavedas, cut down before the eyes of him who praises thee.

**16.7.2**  What curse the two together hurl today, O Agni, what harsh word the chanters generate — the arrow that is born from wrath and from the mind: with that pierce the yātudhānas in the heart.

**16.7.3**  Smite away the yātudhānas with thy heat; smite away the rakṣas with thy blaze, O Agni; smite away the foolish demons with thy flame; smite away those who would drink up the vital force as thou blazest forth.

**16.7.4**  Let the gods today cut off the crooked one; let the hurled curses fly back upon him; let the arrows strike the word-thief in the vital spot; let the yātudhāna enter thy noose from every side.

**16.7.5**  From of old, O Agni, thou dost crush the yātudhānas; the rakṣases have never conquered thee in battles. Burn down the flesh-eating fools along with their companions; let them not escape from thy divine weapon.

**16.7.6**  The yātudhāna who defiles himself with human flesh, who does so with the flesh of horse or cattle beast, who carries off the milk of the inviolable cow, O Agni — hew off the heads of those with thy blaze.

**16.7.7**  Whoever, gazing upon men, is a yātudhāna and would drink the year-long milk of the dappled cow, or the colostrum — whoever among them would satisfy himself therewith, O Agni, pierce that one head-on with thy flame in the vital spot.

**16.7.8**  Let the yātudhānas drink the poison of the cows; let those evil ones be cut down for Aditi. Let the god Savitā give them over utterly; let them forfeit their share in the plants.

**16.7.9**  Do thou, O Agni, guard us from below and from above, from behind, and protect us from in front. Let those ageless, most scorching flames of thine, blazing, burn the evil-proclaimer.

**16.7.10**  From behind and from before, from below and from above, guard us with the poet's wisdom, O Agni the poet. As a friend guards a friend against old age, O Agni, thou art immortal — guard us mortals.

**16.8.1**  That eye, O Agni, fix upon the chanter — the hoof-smashing one with which thou seest the yātudhāna. Like Atharvan, with the divine light, burn down completely that unwise one who harms the truth.

**16.8.2**  Around thee, O Agni, we place our thought as a stronghold, O wise one, O powerful one — bold-hued, day by day the slayer of the Bhaṅgurāvat demons.

**16.8.3**  Agni blazes forth with great light; by his greatness he makes all things manifest. He overcomes the divine māyās and the evil ones; he sharpens his two horns to pierce the rakṣas.

**16.8.4**  Agni drives away the rakṣases, brilliant in radiance, immortal; pure, cleansing, worthy of invocation.

**16.8.5**  O Agni, protect us from affliction; ward off, O god, those who would harm us. Ageless, burn them with thy most scorching flames.

**16.8.6**  Those two ageless horns of thine, O Jātavedas, sharp-gleaming, whetted by sacred utterance — with those two pierce through the kimīdin who is hard-hearted, who would destroy, the yātudhāna who faces thee, O Jātavedas.

**16.8.7**  Burn together the Bhaṅgurāvat rakṣas with thy poison, O Indra; O Agni, with thy brilliant radiance, with flames whose tips are burning hot.

**16.8.8**  Burn down, O Agni, the yātudhāna and the kimīdin as a pair. I whet thee with hymns; be vigilant, O wise one, undeceived.

**16.8.9**  Smite down, O Agni, the blaze with thy fiery wrath, from every direction. Shatter the strength, the heroic power, of the yātudhāna rakṣas.

**16.8.10**  Crush down utterly this one who dwells on the path, O Indra, O guardian, with the fools who destroy yātudhānas. Let these two yātudhānas as a pair, O Agni, lie dead and scattered in every direction, slain by thy blaze.

**16.8.11**  Let Bṛhaspati guard us from behind and from the north, from below against the evil-doer. Let Indra guard us from in front and from the middle; let him, as a friend, make things more spacious for his friends.

**16.9.1**  O Indra and Soma, scorch the rakṣas, press it down, cast it down, O ye two bulls who increase darkness. Smite it away, the senseless one; burn it utterly, smite it dead and thrust it away; sharpen yourselves against the devourers.

**16.9.2**  O Indra and Soma, let burning heat go against the evil-proclaimer, against evil, like a cauldron full of fire. Lay irresistible hatred upon the brahman-hater, the flesh-eater, the fierce-eyed kimīdin.

**16.9.3**  O Indra and Soma, pierce the evildoers through into the pit, into the darkness that has no handhold. So that not one of them may rise up from there again — let that be your power, full of wrath and might.

**16.9.4**  O Indra and Soma, hurl down from heaven your stone-blows, heated in fire. With your burning, ageless weapons of death, pin the devourers in their ribs; let them go voiceless.

**16.9.5**  O Indra and Soma, hurl the death-bolt from heaven; bring the crusher from the earth upon the evil-proclaimer. Hew out the solar timber from the mountains with which you two crush down the waxing rakṣas.

**16.9.6**  O Indra and Soma, let this thought encompass you on all sides like a girth around prize steeds. The oblation-invocation I send forth to you with insight — invigorate these sacred formulations, as a king invigorates his men.

**16.9.7**  Remember it back with your vigorous courses — slain, the rakṣas of treachery, the Bhaṅgurāvat demon. O Indra and Soma, let there be no easy passage for the evildoer, for that deceitful one who would ever assail me.

**16.9.8**  Whoever denounces me as I go about with a guileless mind, denounces me with false words — may he be seized like water by a gleaming vessel; may the speaker of falsehood be shown to be a speaker of falsehood, O Indra.

**16.9.9**  Those who scatter naive speech about with their courses, or who corrupt what is auspicious with their practised powers — let Soma give them over to the serpent, or let him place them in the lap of Nirṛti.

**16.9.10**  Whoever would harm the sap of our food, O Agni — of horses, of cattle, of bodies — let that enemy, that thief, that committer of theft, go forth diminished; let him be abandoned here with his body and his progeny.

**16.10.1**  May he be cut off — body and offspring — and may all three earths lie beneath him. His glory withers away, O gods, whoever seeks to harm me by day and whoever seeks to harm me by night.

**16.10.2**  To the person of discernment it is well known: the words of truth and falsehood touch each other. Of these two, whichever is true, whichever is the more upright — that one Soma protects; the false he strikes down.

**16.10.3**  Soma does not drive forward the crooked, nor the kṣatriya who bears false office. He slays the rakṣas; he slays the speaker of falsehood — both lie in Indra's snare.

**16.10.4**  If indeed I am one who makes a god of falsehood, or if I invoke the gods in vain, O Agni — why then art thou angry with us, O Jātaveda? Let them who speak treachery cleave to Nirṛti without purpose.

**16.10.5**  May I die this very day if I am a yātudhāna, or if the breath of a man has been scorched by me. But let him who calls me a yātudhāna falsely be scattered apart with his ten heroes.

**16.10.6**  Whoever says to me 'thou art a sorcerer, a yātudhāna,' whoever, being a rakṣas himself, says 'I am pure' — may Indra smite him with a great blow; may he fall the lowest of all living creatures.

**16.10.7**  She who moves abroad at night like a screech-owl, concealing her treacherous body — may she fall down into endless pits; may the pressing-stones smite the rakṣas with their thunder.

**16.10.8**  Spread out, O Maruts; search through the settlements; seize the rakṣases, crush them together. Those who take the forms of birds and fly through the nights, or those who have set deception within the divine sacrifice — crush them.

**16.10.9**  Here they come flying, the dog-roving ones; the deceivers seek to deceive Indra, the undecievable. Śakra sharpens his weapon against the slanderers; now will he loose his thunderbolt against the sorcerers.

**16.10.10**  Roll forth the stone of heaven, O Indra; sharpen it well, O Maghavan, whetted by Soma. From the east, from the west, from below, from above — smite the rakṣases with the mountain.

**16.11.1**  Indra became Parāśara against the yātu-sorcerers, against those who churn away the oblation. Śakra indeed advances like an axe through a forest, splitting the rakṣases by the hundreds like vessels.

**16.11.2**  Slay the owl-sorcerer and the screech-owl-sorcerer; slay the dog-sorcerer and the kokay-bird-sorcerer; slay the eagle-sorcerer and the vulture-sorcerer — crush the rakṣas, O Indra, as with a pressing-stone.

**16.11.3**  Let no rakṣas, none possessed by yātu-sorcery, press upon us. Drive away the coupled Kimīdins. May the Earth protect us from earthly distress; may the Atmosphere protect us from the heavenly.

**16.11.4**  O Indra, smite the male yātudhāna and also the female who injures through sorcery. Let those with broken necks, the god-bereft, weep; may they not behold the sun as it rises.

**16.11.5**  Keep watch, keep vigil — O Indra and Soma, be awake! Hurl the weapon of death upon the rakṣases; hurl the thunderbolt upon the sorcerers.

**16.12.1**  The brown ones and the bright ones, the red ones and the dappled ones, the dark ones, the black plants — to all these healing herbs we now speak out.

**16.12.2**  May they protect this man from the yakṣma disease sent by the gods — those whose father is Dyaus, whose mother is Pṛthivī, and whose root has become the ocean.

**16.12.3**  The divine waters are foremost among the healing herbs. They have driven out the sin-laden yakṣma from your limb by limb.

**16.12.4**  I address the spreading plants, the clumping ones, the single-stemmed, the creeping herbs. I call you — the filamentous, the jointed, those with spreading branches — those plants that belong to all the gods, the potent, the life-sustaining for man.

**16.12.5**  By that power of yours, O herbs, which overpowers, by the virile force and strength that is in you — with that, release this man from this yakṣma disease.

**16.12.6**  I invoke Jīvalā, Naghāriṣā, the living herb, and Arundhatī the uplifting, the flowering one, the honey-sweet one.

**16.12.7**  May the wise, the knowing ones come here at my word, so that we may carry this man across, beyond misfortune.

**16.12.8**  The food of Agni, the embryo of the waters — those that grow anew as punarṇavās: may those steadfast, thousand-named healing plants be gathered and brought here.

**16.12.9**  The herbs that dwell in hollows, whose nature is water, the sharp-horned ones — shaking off all misfortune, they drive it away.

**16.12.10**  Those that release bonds, the wide-opening ones, the fierce ones that destroy poison, and those that destroy the balāsa affliction, those that destroy rakṣases, that neutralize kṛtyā witchcraft — may those herbs come here.

**16.13.1**  Benign be to you the herbs, the purchased ones, the mightier plants, the creepers that have been invoked with praise; may Sarasvatī, the eldest of the waters, protect our cattle, our horse, our man, our beast.

**16.13.2**  Honey-sweet the root, honey-sweet the tip of these plants has become; honey-sweet the middle of the creepers; honey-sweet their leaf, honey-sweet their flower — born of sweetness, they are a taste of immortality; they yield ghee and food for the herd of cattle led by the bull.

**16.13.3**  However many, however numerous these herbs that grow upon the earth — may they, with their thousand leaves, release us from the distress of death.

**16.13.4**  The tiger-amulet, the gem of plants, the guardian that wards off curses — may it drive far away from us all diseases and all demons.

**16.13.5**  As the roar of a lion, as the blazing of fire scatters what was gathered against the herbs — the wasting disease of cattle and of men, chased off by the plants, shall flow away like a navigable river into a river channel.

**16.13.6**  Loosed forth from Vaiśvānara, the all-consuming fire, the herbs spread themselves over the earth from here — they whose king is the lord of the forest.

**16.13.7**  The creepers that I know and those that I see with my eyes, those unknown ones that we may come to know, those in which we know healing to be gathered — may all herbs, complete and entire, heed my utterance.

**16.13.8**  The Āṅgirasa plants, the universal-healing creepers that grow on mountains and on level grounds — may those herbs, full of delight, benign, be a blessing to our heart.

**16.13.9**  The aśvattha, the darbha among the creepers, King Soma the immortal oblation; rice and barley are two healers, the two immortal sons of heaven.

**16.13.10**  Rise up, you herbs, as it thunders and roars — when Parjanya, rich in seed, waters you, O speckle-mothered ones.

**16.14.1**  By that immortal power we bring this man across to safety; and I make a remedy so that he may live a hundred years.

**16.14.2**  The boar knows the healing plant; the mongoose knows the medicinal herbs; whatever herbs the Gandharvas and the serpents know — let those herbs come here.

**16.14.3**  Those that the suparṇas know, the heavenly Āṅgirasa plants that the raghaṭa bird knows; those that the birds, the haṃsas know, and all the flying creatures; the herbs that the deer know — those I call hither to give him aid.

**16.14.4**  As many herbs as the inviolable cows graze upon, as many as the goats and sheep graze upon — of that many kinds of universal-healing herbs I bring a full measure toward you.

**16.14.5**  In as many herbs as human healers know a remedy — may herbs of that many kinds, brought to you, bestow shelter upon you.

**16.14.6**  Those that are in flower, those that have given birth, those bearing fruit, and those without fruit — like mothers uniting together, let them give milk to him for the sake of his unharmed wholeness.

**16.14.7**  I have raised you out of the five-fettered snare, and out of the ten-fettered snare as well; I have lifted you out of Yama's foot-fetter — with the herbs I have brought you safely across.

**16.15.1**  The first chariot belongs to Indra; the next chariot belongs to the gods; Varuṇa's is truly the third. The last chariot is that of the serpents — it has come near to the post; let it then strike.

**16.15.2**  The darbha grass is the flame, the tender shoot; it is the shield of the horse, the shield of the man; it is the cross-bar of the chariot — the venom of the serpents is made powerless; indeed it is a mighty ward.

**16.15.3**  O white one, strike down with your fore-hoof and with your hind-hoof alike; like a piece of wood that has been soaked in water, the venom of the serpents is made powerless — indeed it is a mighty ward.

**16.15.4**  Araṃghasa, having plunged and emerged again, spoke once more: like a piece of wood that has been soaked in water, the venom of the serpents is made powerless — indeed it is a mighty ward.

**16.15.5**  Paidva slays the kaṣarṇīla serpent; Paidva slays the white one and the dark one; Paidva has shattered the head of the ratharvyā, of the spotted snake.

**16.15.6**  O Paidva, go forth first; we shall follow after you. Drive the serpents away from the path along which we shall travel.

**16.153.5**  The brahmacārin, kindled with sacrificial fuel, wearing a black antelope skin, consecrated (dīkṣita), long-bearded — he goes straightway from the eastern to the western ocean, grasping the worlds, roaming about repeatedly.

**16.153.6**  The brahmacārin generating Brahman, the waters, the world, Prajāpati, Parameṣṭhin, and Virāj — having become an embryo in the womb of immortality, having become Indra, he crushed the asuras.

**16.153.7**  The brahmacārin, first of all, bore alms from this earth and from heaven. They who make Brahman and tend the (sacred fire) with fuel — upon those two all the worlds are strung.

**16.153.8**  The teacher fashioned the two firmaments — the two vast, deep (spaces) — the earth and the heaven. The brahmacārin guards both of them with austerity; upon those two the gods rejoice together in exhilaration.

**16.153.9**  One (treasure) is this side, another is beyond — two hidden hoards set in the hidden place of the brahmaṇa. The brahmacārin guards both of them with austerity; the one who knows makes that Brahman entire and sole.

**16.154.1**  One fire is on this side, below the back of heaven; another is here, above the earth — the two meet in the midspace between the two worlds. Upon them the firm rays are arrayed; the brahmacārī ascends to them by means of his austerity.

**16.154.2**  Ruddy and dark-necked, great-membered, roaring — he bore his seed along upon the earth. The brahmacārī pours seed upon her ridge; by that the four quarters live upon the earth.

**16.154.3**  Parjanya, Mṛtyu, Varuṇa, Soma, the herbs, and the milk — these were the mighty ones, the vigorous beings; by them they brought forth this heavenly light.

**16.154.4**  In Agni, in Sūrya, in the moon, in Mātariśvan, in the waters — the brahmacārī places the fuel-offering. Their flames move separately in the cloud; their clarified butter is Man, their horse is the rain.

**16.154.5**  He renders the clarified butter pure at home; having become the ācārya, whatever Varuṇa desired from Prajāpati — that the brahmacārī delivered; Mitra from his own self bestowed it.

**16.154.6**  The ācārya is the brahmacārī; the brahmacārī is Prajāpati. Prajāpati reigns supreme; Virāj became Indra, the sovereign.

**16.154.7**  By brahmacarya and austerity the king protects his realm. By brahmacarya the ācārya seeks out the brahmacārī.

**16.154.8**  By brahmacarya a maiden wins a young husband. The draft-ox, by brahmacarya, and the horse strive to obtain their fodder.

**16.154.9**  By brahmacarya and austerity the gods drove away Death. Indra indeed by brahmacarya brought forth the immortal heavenly light.

**16.154.10**  The herbs, what has been and what shall be, day and night, the forest trees, the year together with its seasons — these were born as brahmacārīs.

**16.155.1**  The cattle of the earth and of the sky, those of the forest and those of the village, and those without wings and those winged — these too were born as brahmacārīs.

**16.155.2**  All beings of Prajāpati, each separately, bear the vital breaths within themselves. Brahman, sustained in the brahmacārī, protects all of them.

**16.155.3**  The brahmacārī bears the blazing brahman; in him all the gods have assembled together. He, the snātaka — tawny and ruddy-brown — shines brilliantly upon the earth.

**16.155.4**  This, pressed out by the gods, unascended, moves resplendently. In him are all cattle, in him the sacrifices, in him food together with the divine powers.

**16.155.5**  Generating prāṇa and apāna, then vyāna, generating sight and hearing, O brahman, and intelligence — set in us the highest speech and glory.

**16.155.6**  Food, seed, blood, and the womb — shaping these, the brahmacārī stood performing austerity upon the back of the waters, glowing with heat in the ocean.
