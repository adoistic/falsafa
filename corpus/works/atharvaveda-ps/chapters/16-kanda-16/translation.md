**16.1.1**  Homage to Antaka, to Mṛtyu — let this man remain here together with his vital breath. In the portion of Sūrya, in the realm of immortality, may your in-breath and out-breath take their delight here.

**16.1.2**  Bhaga has seized him upward, the radiant Soma has seized him upward; the Maruts, the gods, have raised him up — Indra and Agni have raised him for well-being.

**16.1.3**  Here is your vital force, here your breath, here your life-span, here your mind. With the divine word we bear you up out of the nooses of Nirṛti.

**16.1.4**  Rise up and do not fall down; loosening the foot-fetter of Death, do not sever yourself from this world, from the sight of Agni and Sūrya.

**16.1.5**  May Vāyu, Mātariśvan, blow for you; may the waters rain down their immortal gifts upon you; may Sūrya warm your body with well-being. Let Death be compassionate toward you — do not perish.

**16.1.6**  Your going is upward, O man, not a going-down; may your living make your vitality complete. Mount this immortal, pleasant chariot, and then in old age speak forth at the assembly.

**16.1.7**  Let not your mind go thither; let it not be lost. Do not stray from the living in intoxication; do not follow the Pitṛs. May all the gods protect you here.

**16.1.8**  Do not fix your gaze upon those who have gone, those who lead to the far realm. Ascend, come from darkness to light — we take hold of your hand.

**16.1.9**  Śyāma and Śabala, the two dogs of Yama, the road-guardians sent forth — come hither; do not stare away, do not stand there with your mind turned backward.

**16.1.10**  Do not travel this path — it is dreadful, the one by which they lead those who went before; so I declare it. Darkness lies yonder, O man; do not fall forward upon it. Fear lies beyond, but safety lies this side of you.

**16.2.1**  May the fires that dwell within the waters guard you; may that fire which men kindle guard you. May Vaiśvānara, Jātavedas, guard you. May the heavenly one not scorch you along with the lightning.

**16.2.2**  May Earth guard you, may Dyaus guard you; may Sūrya and Candramās guard you. Let the flesh-eater not assail you — keep away from Saṃkasuka from afar.

**16.2.3**  May the mid-air with its divine weapon guard you; may Awareness and Counter-awareness guard you. May the Sleepless One and the Ever-wakeful guard you; may the Watcher guard you, and the Vigilant.

**16.2.4**  Let them guard you, let them protect you, let them shield you from distress. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**16.2.5**  For the living, in the community, may Vāyu, Indra, Dhātṛ, the protecting Savitṛ establish you. Let not the breath forsake your strength — we call after your vital spirit.

**16.2.6**  Let neither the jaw nor the snap of teeth find you; let not the darkness; let not the tongue of the sacrificial grass cause your destruction — how could you perish? May the Ādityas and Vasus raise you up; may Indra and Agni raise you for well-being.

**16.2.7**  O gods, let this man be here only; let him not go from here to yonder. We ferry this one across from Death with a thousandfold heroic power.

**16.2.8**  I have ferried you across from Death; may the life-sustaining ones blow strength into you. Let not the disheveled-haired woman weep over you; let not she who wails with grief lament for you.

**16.2.9**  I have fetched you back; I have found you. You have returned, renewed. With every limb intact, all your sight, and all your life-span I have found for you.

**16.2.10**  Heaven has seized you upward, and Earth, and Prajāpati has seized you upward. The plants whose queen is Soma have ferried you across from Death.

**16.2.11**  As far as your light extended, darkness has retreated from you. Away from you we blow Death, Nirṛti, and wasting disease.

**16.3.1**  Grasp hold of this hearing of immortality; may your old age be unbroken. I bring back your vital force and your life-span. Do not go to the dust, to darkness — do not perish.

**16.3.2**  Come back to the light of the living, to the world; I carry you to a hundred autumns. I make your life-span ever longer and more extended. Let the nooses of Death, the curses, be released.

**16.3.3**  From Vāyu I have recovered your breath; from Sūrya I have recovered your sight all around. Whatever mind is yours, in you I hold it steady. Awaken with all your limbs; speak, converse with your tongue.

**16.3.4**  With the breath of bipeds and quadrupeds I blow upon you as upon a fire just kindled. Homage to your eye, O Mṛtyu; I have paid homage to your breath.

**16.3.5**  Let this one live; let him not die — we stir this one back into life. I make the healing remedy for him. O Mṛtyu, do not slay this man.

**16.3.6**  The Jīvalā, the Naghāriṣā, the living herb — the protecting, enduring, powerful Arundhatī — these I invoke here for his inviolate well-being.

**16.3.7**  Speak favorably; do not seize him — release this one. Belonging to you entirely, let him who lives all his years be here. O Bhava and Śarva, show mercy, grant shelter; thrust evil away and bestow life.

**16.3.8**  May the weapon of the gods enfold you protectively. I ferry you across; I have carried you over from the dust of Death. Driving the flesh-eating fire away from afar, I place a boundary around you for the sake of life.

**16.3.9**  O Mṛtyu, speak favorably for this one; be gracious to him — let him go up from here. Uninjured, with all his limbs intact, well-formed, let him attain the enjoyment of the self through old age for a hundred years.

**16.3.10**  O Mṛtyu, the dusty downward path of yours — I have turned it away from him. Guarding this man from that path, we make the brahman a coat of armor for him.

**16.4.1**  I make for you breath and vital breath; let old age and death make long life and well-being. The messengers of Yama dispatched by Vaivasvata, all those who roam about — I drive them far away.

**16.4.2**  Far away — malice, dissolution, the seizing demon beyond, flesh-devouring Piśācas; whatever evil being there is, every Rakṣas — that we smite away like darkness.

**16.4.3**  From immortal Agni Jātavedas, long-lived and cognizant of all births, I take breath for you, as for Manu — so that you may be unharmed and immortal in fellowship. This I do for you; may this be fulfilled for you.

**16.4.4**  May Heaven and Earth be auspicious to you, resting upon you without scorching heat, shining with splendour. May the sun warm you auspiciously; may the wind blow auspiciously upon your heart. May the heavenly, milk-rich waters flow auspiciously over you; may the plants be auspicious to you.

**16.4.5**  I have lifted you up from the lower region toward the higher earth. There let both Ādityas — Sun and Moon together — guard you.

**16.4.6**  The garment you put on for wearing, the girdle you fasten about yourself — we make that auspicious for your body; may it be unharmful in contact with you.

**16.4.7**  When, with a razor of fine keenness, the barber shaves off hair and beard — making the face beautiful, let it not rob this one of life.

**16.4.8**  Auspicious to you be rice and barley — not causing weakness, not causing oppression. These two repel disease; these two release from distress.

**16.4.9**  Whatever you eat, whatever you drink — the grain, the produce of tillage, the milk — what is to be eaten and what is not to be eaten: all food I make free of poison for you.

**16.4.10**  To both Day and Night together we entrust you. From the greedy devouring demons — protect this one of ours on all sides.

**16.5.1**  May there be for you a hundred, ten thousand years of life; two ages, three, four. May Indra and Agni, may all the gods consent to this without anger.

**16.5.2**  For autumn, for winter, for spring, for summer we entrust you. May the rainy seasons be gentle for you — those in which the plants flourish.

**16.5.3**  Death rules the two-footed; Death rules the four-footed. From that Death, lord of cattle, I lift you up. May he not slay you.

**16.5.4**  Unharmed, you shall not die; you shall not die — fear not. Truly, they do not perish there; they do not go to the lower realm of dust.

**16.5.5**  Truly everything lives there — cow, horse, man, beast — where this sacred formula is performed as a protective enclosure for life, for bliss.

**16.5.6**  May it guard you on all sides from the sorcery of kindred folk, from those of equal standing. Be one who does not wither; be immortal, one who outlives; may your vital forces not abandon your body.

**16.5.7**  May all the gods sustain you with immortality; may Paśupati be your advocate. May Savitṛ make you free of sickness; may breath enter you for the sake of life.

**16.5.8**  The hundred-and-one deaths, the fatal nights that are not to be outlived — from those let the gods release you, above and beyond Agni Vaiśvānara.

**16.6.1**  I kindle the strong slayer of Rakṣases; I approach the shelter of the most widely spread Friend. Agni, whetted and kindled with rites — may he protect us from harm by day, by night.

**16.6.2**  Touch with your flame those who grow strong with iron fangs, O Jātavedas, when kindled. With your tongue seize the fool-gods; having consumed the flesh-eaters, close your mouth upon them.

**16.6.3**  O bearer of both kinds, apply your fangs — fierce and whetted — upon the lower and the upper. And roam through the atmosphere, O Agni; crush the sorcerers with your jaws; fall upon them.

**16.6.4**  O Agni, pierce the skin of the sorcerer; may the fierce thunderbolt strike him down with heat. Shatter his joints, O Jātavedas; the flesh-eating fire, hungering for raw flesh, picks him apart.

**16.6.5**  O Agni, bending your arrows with sacrificial rites, smearing your shafts with speech and with thunderbolts — with those, pierce the sorcerers in their hearts; shatter the arms of those who oppose us.

**16.6.6**  Wherever you now see him, O Jātavedas, O Agni — standing still or moving about — or a sorcerer flying through the atmosphere: pierce him through with darkness, wholly, you who are whetted.

**16.6.7**  And shatter the sorcerer with your spears when seized, O Jātavedas; and when he is about to fly. Strike first, O blazing Agni; may the raw-flesh-eating female spirits, shrieking, devour him.

**16.6.8**  Declare here, O Agni, which one he is — the sorcerer who hears this. Seize him, O youngest one, with fuel; surrender him to the sight of the man-watching eye.

**16.6.9**  With your keen eye, O Agni, guard the sacrifice; lead it forward eastward to the Vasus, O wise one. Blazing against the fierce Rakṣases — may the sorcerers not deceive you, O watcher of men.

**16.6.10**  O watcher of men, look upon the Rakṣas among the settlements; shatter his three foremost points. Shatter his ribs, O Agni, with your heat; cleave the root of the sorcerer into three.

**16.7.1**  Thrice let the yātudhāna come within thy snare; he who smites the truth with falsehood, O Agni — him, crackling with thy flame, O Jātavedas, cut down before the eyes of him who praises thee.

**16.7.2**  What curse the two together hurl today, O Agni, what harsh word the chanters generate — the arrow that is born from wrath and from the mind: with that pierce the yātudhānas in the heart.

**16.7.3**  Smite away the yātudhānas with thy heat; smite away the rakṣas with thy blaze, O Agni; smite away the foolish demons with thy flame; smite away those who would drink up the vital force as thou blazest forth.

**16.7.4**  Let the gods today cut off the crooked one; let the hurled curses fly back upon him; let the arrows strike the word-thief in the vital spot; let the yātudhāna enter thy noose from every side.

**16.7.5**  From of old, O Agni, thou dost crush the yātudhānas; the rakṣases have never conquered thee in battles. Burn down the flesh-eating fools along with their companions; let them not escape from thy divine weapon.

**16.7.6**  The yātudhāna who defiles himself with human flesh, who does so with the flesh of horse or cattle beast, who carries off the milk of the inviolable cow, O Agni — hew off the heads of those with thy blaze.

**16.7.7**  Whoever, gazing upon men, is a yātudhāna and would drink the year-long milk of the dappled cow, or the colostrum — whoever among them would satisfy himself therewith, O Agni, pierce that one head-on with thy flame in the vital spot.

**16.7.10**  From behind and from before, from below and from above, guard us with the poet's wisdom, O Agni the poet. As a friend guards a friend against old age, O Agni, thou art immortal — guard us mortals.
