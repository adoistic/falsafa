**3.1.1**  The kingdom has come to you together with lustre; advance eastward as lord of the clans, sole sovereign — reign widely! May all the regions summon you, O king; be here one worthy of approach, worthy of homage.

**3.1.2**  May the clans choose you for kingship; may these five divine regions choose you. Ascend to the crest, the crown of the kingdom; from there, O mighty one, distribute the good things.

**3.1.3**  Let those of common birth who bear oblations come toward you; Agni, the swift messenger, shall keep away hostility. May your wives and sons be well-disposed; O mighty one, may you behold abundant tribute.

**3.1.4**  The two Aśvins shall precede you, both Mitra and Varuṇa, the Viśve Devās, the Maruts — all shall summon you. May you stand paramount among those of common birth; in your own domain reign wide as Savitṛ.

**3.1.5**  Run forward from the uttermost distance; Heaven and Earth have become propitious for you. Thus has king Varuṇa spoken — he has summoned you; he calls you hither: come here now.

**3.1.6**  Go forth, O mortal, to Indra the Indra — the worshipful one, in accord with Varuṇa, summons you. He has called you to his own station; may he worship the gods and duly set the clans in order.

**3.1.7**  The prosperous pathways, manifold and varied in form, all assembling together, have made space for you. May all of them, in full accord, summon you here to the tenth station, O mighty one, be of good will and of your own choosing.

**3.1.8**  When with swift oblation we cause you to depart — here, then, may Indra make the clans, each one exclusively, tribute-bearing.

**3.2.1**  The remedy upon the head of the swift-rushing antelope — it has made that which is well-suited for hereditary disease fade away, having utterly destroyed the spreading sickness.

**3.2.2**  The antelope-bull has stridden over you with all four hooves. O horn, release and scatter whatever hereditary malady is pent up in the heart.

**3.2.3**  That which shines yonder like a four-sided roof — with that we destroy all your hereditary disease from your limbs.

**3.2.4**  The two auspicious stars named Vicṛtau have risen; may they loose the hereditary disease — its lowest snare and its highest.

**3.2.5**  The hereditary disease that has pervaded you from the pressing rite being performed — I know its remedy; I destroy the hereditary disease for you.

**3.2.6**  At the setting of the asterisms, at the setting also of the dawns — all sickness has departed from us; the hereditary malady has withdrawn.

**3.2.7**  The waters are indeed the healers; the waters are the chasers of disease. The waters are the remedy for all — may they release you from the hereditary disease.

**3.3.1**  The Aśvattha tree, a male born from a male, has sprung forth atop the Khadira tree. Let it smite my enemies — those whom I hate and those who hate me.

**3.3.2**  O Aśvattha, crush those enemies, the harassers who rage repeatedly. Come to me with Indra the Vṛtra-slayer, with Agni and with Varuṇa.

**3.3.3**  As you, O Aśvattha, crush those born before and those born after — so stand against and overwhelm those who battle against you.

**3.3.4**  As you, O Aśvattha, split asunder within the great ocean — so split my enemy's thoughts in all directions and overwhelm them.

**3.3.5**  You who roam as one that overcomes, like a bull that has conquered — O Aśvattha, with you we shall overpower our rivals.

**3.3.6**  Let Nirṛti bind them with the inescapable nooses of Death. O Aśvattha, my enemies — those whom I hate and those who hate me.

**3.3.7**  Let them drift downward and away, like a boat cut loose from its moorings. For those who have been driven off and expelled there is no return.

**3.3.8**  I drive them forth with mind, with intelligence, with sacred power. We drive them forth with the branch of the tree — of the Aśvattha.

**3.4.1**  Because, rushing forward, you cried out and roared as you struck — therefore you are called rivers (nadyaḥ); those are your names, O Sindhus.

**3.4.2**  Because, sent forth by Varuṇa, you leapt swiftly together toward the goal — Indra attained you as you strove; therefore, O Waters, follow his ordinance.

**3.4.3**  Flowing according to desire, he caused your hiccup to cease; Indra, O divine ones, held you back with his powers — therefore your name for the hiccup is vār (water).

**3.4.4**  One god came near to you as you flowed, drawing close; the great ones roared upward — therefore you are called udaka (water, the upward-roarer).

**3.4.5**  The divine Waters — it is ghee, the Waters declare; they carry Agni and Soma, those very Waters. The sharp sap of the honey-mixers has come to meet me; may it come to me together with vital breath and vital lustre.

**3.4.6**  Even now I see, or else I hear — the roar comes to me, the voice reaches my mouth. Having partaken, I deemed myself at that moment sharing in immortality, when the golden-hued Waters came and I was not yet sated.

**3.5.1**  May Agni, our messenger, go forth against our foes, burning back their curses and their malice; may he confound the minds of our adversaries and render them weaponless — Jātavedas, the Knower of creatures.

**3.5.2**  This Agni has bewildered all the thoughts that are in your hearts; he blows you away from your dwelling, he blows you forth from every side.

**3.5.3**  O Indra, confound their minds — draw their very intent hither; with the rushing blast of Agni and of Wind scatter them in all directions and destroy them.

**3.5.4**  Let their intentions scatter hence, and let their minds be bewildered; and whatever is today within their hearts — that, strike out and expel from them utterly.

**3.5.5**  Seizing their minds in utter bewilderment, O Apvā, go forth and grasp their limbs; advance, burn them inwardly with grief in their hearts, pierce our foes — those enemies fit to be seized — with darkness.

**3.5.6**  That army yonder of our adversaries, O Maruts, which advances against us contending with its might — hide it away with darkness and by the hostile rite, so that not one of them may know another.

**3.6.1**  May Agni the Omniscient go forth against our foes, burning back their curses and their malice; may he confound the army of our adversaries and render them weaponless — Jātavedas, the Knower of creatures.

**3.6.2**  You are fierce for such a deed, O Maruts — advance, show mercy, prevail together; the Vasus have shown mercy to those who sought their aid. For Agni, knowing all, shall go forth against their foes.

**3.6.3**  O Maghavan, against the hostile army of those who make themselves our enemies — both of you, O Indra, slayer of Vṛtra, and Agni, burn them back in retaliation.

**3.6.4**  Urged forward, Indra with his two tawny steeds goes downhill; your thunderbolt goes forth crushing the foes; strike those who face you, those alongside, those who flee — make all destruction a truth and reality for them.

**3.6.5**  O Indra, bring about confusion of the army against our foes; with the rushing blast of Agni and of Wind scatter them in all directions and destroy them.

**3.6.6**  May Indra confound their army; may the Maruts strike them down with might; may Agni seize their eyes — may the enemy host return defeated.

**3.7.1**  One hundred and one viṣkandhas are spread throughout the earth. Of all of them, let this be the viṣkandha-nullifier.

**3.7.2**  Of Karṣapha, of Viśapha — Dyauṣpitā (Sky-Father) is the father, Earth the mother. Just as the gods have acted against them, so act ye against them again likewise.

**3.7.3**  The sputum-free ones (the seers) upheld it; thus was it made by Manu. I render the viṣkandha castrate (vadhri), even as one strips the scrotum from the bulls of the herd.

**3.7.4**  In the tawny cord, the knot (khṛgala) which the wise ones bind — let those who tie knots render the renowned power of kābava castrate (vadhri).

**3.7.5**  By which (cord) you move about in pursuit of glory, as the gods with their asura-māyā — even as an ape (kapi) is the corrupter of dogs, so are the cord-tyers the corrupters of kābava.

**3.7.6**  For ruin indeed I shall shatter you, having defiled the kābava. Like swift chariots (driven off the field), you shall career away by means of my imprecations.

**3.8.1**  From Yama's realm you have arisen, O wise one, eager to yoke mortals to intoxicated women (pramadās). You journey alone on the same chariot, O Svapna, knowing (your nature), measuring out the womb of the Asura.

**3.8.2**  Bamba first saw you everywhere, before the night, at the birth of a certain day. From that time onward you arose over this (world), O Svapna, concealing yourself in an all-pervading form.

**3.8.3**  The great pressing-stone (bṛhan grāvā) turned from the Asuras toward the gods, desiring greatness. To that Svapna the thirty-three (gods), having won their way to heaven, granted lordship.

**3.8.4**  Neither the Pitṛs nor the gods know this babblement (jalpi) that wanders here in the midst. The Āptya-men placed Svapna in Trita Āptya; the Ādityans, instructed by Varuṇa.

**3.8.5**  Let the cruel deeds of the evildoer depart from him; let the doer of good live a holy life free from evil dream. You make heaven drunk with your highest kinsman; you are born from the tormented mind of one who suffers.

**3.8.6**  We know all your kindred from of old; we know, O Svapna, who here is your overlord. Protect our glory with glory here; go far away, depart to a distance from those who hate us.

**3.9.1**  O Mothers (Ambās), fair-faced ones, release (the waters) with their creatures; release what is truly to be known. I send forth messengers to slay this poison. The potent water (vār) — the sap-drained poison, the serpent-born (āheya) — the poison sap-drained, rendered non-poisonous.

**3.9.2**  I invoke Indra here, the soma-drinker, vigorous on both sides (ubhayāvin), to slay this poison. The potent water — the sap-drained poison, the serpent-born — the poison sap-drained, rendered non-poisonous.

**3.9.3**  I invoke fierce Varuṇa here together with the royal lords (rājanyās), to slay this poison. The potent water — the sap-drained poison, the serpent-born — the poison sap-drained, rendered non-poisonous.

**3.9.4**  I invoke Aditi here, daughter of the sun (sūraputrī), the maiden (kanīnikā), to slay this poison. The potent water — the sap-drained poison, the serpent-born — the poison sap-drained, rendered non-poisonous.

**3.9.5**  I invoke Bṛhaspati here, who is the purohita (domestic priest) of the gods, to slay this poison. The potent water — the sap-drained poison, the serpent-born — the poison sap-drained, rendered non-poisonous.

**3.9.6**  The āṇas, the cāṇas, and the caṇḍāmarkas — (I invoke them) to slay this poison. The potent water — the sap-drained poison, the serpent-born — the poison sap-drained, rendered non-poisonous.

**3.9.7**  Of the nine, of the ninety, of the festering poisons — of all of them I have seized the name. The poison whose sap has utterly departed: gone, removed, sap-drained is the poison.

**3.10.1**  Death is one, Yama is one, Śarva is one, the arrow-hurler is Bhava — may they fashion healing for us, away from the divine hosts.

**3.10.2**  May Yama restore us together with the Fathers; may Mitra and Varuṇa, Vāyu and Agni restore us; may the slayer of evil, the proclaimer of evil, restore us; may the goddess Nirṛti restore us once more.

**3.10.3**  The arrow that has flown dispatched by the gods, or that which has been released toward heat or toward the great power — may Soma the knowing drive it away from us, or the Fathers invoked by the gods, who behold mankind.

**3.10.4**  May the thousand-eyed immortal one, of manifold forms, protect here. In your friendship, O fierce one, may I come to no harm — you who are companion to the cow and companion to man.

**3.10.5**  The fury of yours, O Thousand-eyed one, that is poured round with poison — with that same fury be gracious to us; come to us as benign, as Śambhu.

**3.10.6**  May no world of mine fall under your wrath, O Thousand-eyed one, in your blazing fury. Go to him who hates us; strike down him whom we hate.

**3.11.1**  Those gods who stand in the eastern quarter, named Hetayas — their arrows are Agni. May they show us mercy, may they speak on our behalf. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**3.11.2**  Those gods who stand in the southern quarter, named Aviṣyavaḥ — their arrows are the Waters. May they show us mercy, may they speak on our behalf. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**3.11.3**  Those gods who stand in the western quarter, named Virājaḥ — their arrows are Kāma. May they show us mercy, may they speak on our behalf. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**3.11.4**  Those gods who stand in the northern quarter, named Pravidhyantaḥ — their arrows are Vāyu. May they show us mercy, may they speak on our behalf. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**3.11.5**  Those gods who stand in the fixed (nadir) quarter, named Vilimpāḥ — their arrows are Food. May they show us mercy, may they speak on our behalf. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**3.11.6**  Those gods who stand in the upper quarter, named Aviṣyantaḥ — their arrows are Rain. May they show us mercy, may they speak on our behalf. Homage to them; svāhā to them.

**3.12.1**  He who dwells within the Waters, he who dwells within Vṛtra, he who is in man, he who is in the stone — he who has entered into the herbs and into the trees of the forest: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.2**  He who dwells within Soma, he who dwells within cattle, he who has entered into birds and into wild beasts — he who has entered into two-footed creatures and into four-footed ones: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.3**  He who shares a chariot with Indra, Vaiśvānara the all-consuming one — him I invoke with repeated calls, the one who ever conquers in battles: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.4**  That god whom they call the all-devourer, whom they call Kāma, whom they call the giver and the receiver — he the wise, the mighty, the encompassing, the undeceivable: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.5**  Thee whom thirteen world-beings and five human ones recognized in mind as the hotar-priest — for the bearing of vital luster, for glory, for the one rich in true speech: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.6**  To him whose food is the bull, whose food is the cow, who bears Soma upon his back, the wise ordainer — to the eldest forms of Vaiśvānara: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.7**  Those who move in succession through heaven, earth, and atmosphere, those who pursue the lightning — those who dwell within the quarters and those who dwell within the wind: may this oblation be offered to those fires.

**3.12.8**  Bṛhaspati, Varuṇa, Mitra, Agni, the golden-handed Savitṛ, Indra — all the gods and the Aṅgirasas we invoke: may they appease this flesh-devouring fire.

**3.12.9**  Appeased is the flesh-devouring fire, and also the man-seeking one. And that which is the all-devourer — that flesh-eating fire I have wholly pacified.

**3.13.1**  Here has come the parṇa-amulet, powerful, crushing rivals by its might; the vigour of the gods, the sap of plants — may it invigorate sovereignty in me without faltering.

**3.13.2**  In me hold sovereignty, O parṇa-amulet; in me establish splendour; may I, in the overlordship of the realm, be allied with you and be supreme.

**3.13.3**  The prize, the dear hidden treasure, that the gods deposited in a forest tree — may Indra bestow that amulet upon me together with long life, to be worn.

**3.13.4**  The leaf of Soma has come hither with powerful force, given by Indra, in friendship by Varuṇa; I wear it, shining brilliantly in many ways, for long life, for a hundred autumns.

**3.13.5**  May the parṇa-amulet cling fast to me for my complete uninjuredness, so that I may be supreme over the human accumulation of riches.

**3.13.6**  May vital power return to me, may the self, wealth, and the brahman return; may the dhiṣṇya fires arrange themselves back in their proper stations, right here.

**3.13.7**  The carpenters, the chariot-makers, the blacksmiths, and the wise counsellors — subdue them all, O Parṇa; make my people into submissive attendants.

**3.13.8**  Let there be a submissive attendant whether Vaiśya, or Śūdra, or noble Ārya — subdue them all, O Parṇa; make my people into submissive attendants.

**3.14.1**  That by which you became a barren woman — that we destroy; this very thing we lay away far from you, elsewhere, at a distance.

**3.14.2**  May an embryo enter your womb, a male, like an arrow into a quiver; may a hero be born here — your son at ten months.

**3.14.3**  Bear a male child, a son; after him may another male be born; may you become a mother of sons — of those already born and those yet to be born.

**3.14.4**  With the good seeds that bulls engender, find a son for yourself with those; be a prolific cow, a milch-cow.

**3.14.5**  I make for you the Prajāpati rite; may the embryo enter your womb; find a son, O woman — one who shall be a blessing to you, and you in turn shall be a blessing to him.

**3.14.6**  Those divine plants whose father was Parjanya and whose mother was the Earth — may they, the goddesses, help you toward the finding of a son.

**3.14.7**  That bull who shall stir your womb together with his seed — may he pour into you offspring of long life, lasting a hundred autumns.

**3.15.1**  The plant that the great bull with the moist navel, Vāta, enclosed — from it I bring up for you the all-sustaining poison-neutralizer.

**3.15.2**  The plant that the boar rooted up in a single lotus-pond — from it I bring up for you the all-sustaining poison-neutralizer.

**3.15.3**  The plant that Aditi sowed as seed-sower upon a lotus-pond — from it I bring up for you the all-sustaining poison-neutralizer.

**3.15.4**  She whose nest is within the waters in the great ocean — from it I bring up for you the all-sustaining poison-neutralizer.

**3.15.5**  I bring up for you from the highest; from the lowest I bring up for you; from the middle-ones the middle one — I bring up that poison-neutralizer.

**3.15.6**  I seized, together I seized, both ends together I seized; I bring up the poison-neutralizer of heaven and earth.

**3.16.4**  The serpent was not as an embryo; the serpent struck the stone-cloud and rendered its venom impotent. By the brahman of the poison it came into being — from that, alive, you shall not escape.

**3.16.5**  The serpent is scorched, the serpent is utterly scorched, rendered juiceless, made powerless. By the brahman of the poison it came into being — from that, alive, you shall not escape.

**3.16.6**  I give back your venom to you — you have indeed reached your former step. You think yourself one who has bitten me; bitten by me, you shall not escape.

**3.17.1**  One hundred and one are the remedies; of them you are the mother, O herb. You flow down to the ocean, established upon the earth.

**3.17.2**  In her I know the remedy: the ten-headed, ten-tongued one. He who first gathered you — may he fare well, O herb; and he to whom the heroic physician gave you — may he be well.

**3.17.3**  May sight return again, may breath return again, may life's span return again to us. I have extracted you by the counter-rite; by the counter-rite I have extracted you.

**3.17.4**  I release you from the bond of the curse, and also from that which belongs to Varuṇa; and from the foot-fetter of Yama, from every transgression against the gods.

**3.17.5**  Well joined are the skull-plates of your head and of your heart — those who know them. The rising sun, Āditya, has caused the body's radiance to shine forth.

**3.17.6**  Flow forth from Himavat; your confluence is in the Sindhu. Let all those waters, gathering together, bestow sight and breath upon us.

**3.18.1**  May the elephant-splendour spread wide, the great glory that arose from the body of Aditi. All of that — this which is Savitṛ's — let all the gods together with Aditi grant to me.

**3.18.2**  Let Mitra and Varuṇa, and Indra and Rudra fashion it. Let the gods, sustainers of all, anoint me with that splendour.

**3.18.3**  That great radiance of yours, O Jātavedas, which blazes up when you are invoked — with that radiance today, O Agni, make me full of splendour.

**3.18.4**  By that splendour whereby the elephant came into being, whereby the king stands foremost among men, whereby the gods ascended heaven with their light — with that splendour, O Agni, unite me here.

**3.18.5**  As great as is the splendour of the sun and of the mighty elephant — that much splendour, O you two Aśvins garlanded with lotus-wreaths, create for me.

**3.18.6**  As far as the four cardinal directions, as far as the eye reaches in its range — let that vital power, that elephant-splendour, gather itself entirely within me.

**3.19.3**  Sharper than the edge of Agni's axe, and sharper still — sharper than the vajra of Indra are those for whom I am the purohita.

**3.19.4**  May they fall down, may they be brought low, those who assail Indra the bountiful overlord. I wound the enemies with brahman-power; I raise up my own men.

**3.19.5**  I sharpen the weapons of these men; I make their kingdom flourish with good heroes. May their kṣatra-power be ageless and conquering; may all the gods win over their fierce resolve.

**3.19.6**  Go forward, conquer, urged onward! I sharpen your weapons, O men. You of sharp arrows and powerful bows — strike down the weaklings; you of fierce weapons and mighty arms!

**3.20.1**  Right here I set firm the house, that it may stand in peace, sprinkled with ghee. O house, may we approach you — all heroes, good heroes, heroes unharmed.

**3.20.2**  Stand firm right here, O house — you who possess horses, who possess cattle, who possess pleasant speech; full of vigor, full of ghee, full of milk — rise up for great good fortune!

**3.20.3**  You are a support, O house, of great meter, storing good grain. May the calf low toward you, may the young child, may the milk-cows come streaming in at evening.

**3.20.4**  May Savitṛ, Vāyu, Agni, Tvaṣṭṛ, the hotar — knowing it — firmly establish this house. May the Maruts sprinkle it from their udder with ghee; may Bhaga, our king, extend our tillage.

**3.20.5**  O wife of Māna, sheltering and pleasant goddess — you were first established by the gods. Clothed in grass, benevolent, may you be; bestow on us, O auspicious one, wealth rich in good heroes.

**3.20.6**  Into you may the young boy come, and the calf together with the herd; into you may the jar of fermented grain and the pot of curds flow in.

**3.21.1**  Magnify this kṣatriya of mine, O Indra; make him the sole bull of the clans. Drive away all his enemies; subdue them for him in the coming contests.

**3.21.2**  May he be the lord of lords of wealth; may he be king, the lord of the clans. Place great splendors in him, O Indra; render his enemy devoid of splendor.

**3.21.3**  Allot to him a share in the village, in horses, in cattle; cut off from his share whoever is his enemy. May he be the highest king among the kṣatra-powers; O Indra, subdue all the foes for him.

**3.21.4**  May Heaven and Earth milk out abundant treasure for him, like a milk-cow yielding warm milk. May this king be dear to Indra, dear to the cows, to the plants, and to the waters.

**3.21.5**  I yoke for you Indra, who brings victory — by whom they conquer and are never conquered — who shall make you the sole bull of the peoples and the highest of human kings, O king.

**3.21.6**  May you be supreme; may all the others be lower — whoever, O king, are your adversaries. As sole bull, as the companion of Indra, as the victor, trample upon the great deeds of those who would be your enemies.

**3.22.1**  O horn-faced one, of Āṅgirasa descent, born of the gods, all-perceiving — born of heaven and earth, O Thousand-eyed one, be here for us.
