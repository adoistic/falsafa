**8.1.1**  How shall we speak to the Asura of heaven, how to the tawny Father of fierce might? O Varuṇa, having given the dappled cow as the southern fee, O Punarmagha, thou hast deemed it good in thy mind.

**8.1.2**  Not by desire alone do I become Punarmagha, having consulted whom I brought near this dappled cow. By what wisdom art thou greater than I, O Atharvan? By what birth art thou Jātavedas?

**8.1.3**  Truly I am profound in poetic wisdom; truly by my birth I am Jātavedas. Neither serf nor noble has measured the greatness of my vow which I shall uphold.

**8.1.4**  No other is more skilled in vision, no fashioner, no one more steadfast than thou, O Varuṇa, possessor of thine own power. For thou, indeed, knowest all births; even the crafty man fears thee.

**8.1.5**  For thou, O Varuṇa, possessor of thine own power, knowest all births, O thou before whom troops tremble. What is beyond yonder firmament? What is the lower beyond the lower, O unwearied one?

**8.1.6**  One thing is beyond yonder firmament; beyond the other lies yet another that is hard to see. This, knowing it, I declare to thee, O Varuṇa: let the Paṇis become speakers of what is beneath; let the defiled serfs creep low.

**8.1.7**  For thou, O Varuṇa, dost proclaim many blameable deeds among the Punarmagh ones. Do not, then, fall upon the Paṇi by so much; let not people call thee ungracious.

**8.1.8**  Let not people call me ungracious; again I give thee the dappled cow, O singer of praise. Come to me with all my hymn of praise among the folk, among the gods and among men, O sage.

**8.1.9**  I come to thy praises that grant increase; give me that which is yet ungiven. Thou art our seven-paced companion in yoke, our equal kinsman is Varuṇa, equal in birth.

**8.1.10**  I know indeed that which is our equal birth; I give thee what is yet ungiven. The god, life-sustaining, to the god who praises; the wise one of excellent insight to the wise one who lauds.

**8.1.11**  For thou, O Varuṇa, possessor of thine own power, didst beget Atharvan, the progenitor, the all-divine. Make for him a wide and laudable life span; thou art our friend and supreme kinsman.

**8.2.1**  My roar is like a bull, like thunder from the sky; with fierce speech I shall drive it away from thee. I have seized its essence with this my grip, as the sun rises from the darkness like a light.

**8.2.2**  Whatever waterless poison is thine, that of thine I have now seized with these means. I seize the middle, the highest, and also the lowest; by fear it shall not prevail, and now it is thine no more.

**8.2.3**  With strength I smite thy strength; with body I smite thy body. With poison I smite thy poison. O serpent, lie thou still; live not. Let the poison flow back and return upon thee.

**8.2.4**  Of the black serpent, of the Taimāta, of the brown one, of the waterless one, and of the wrath of the all-conquering — I loose the bowstring as of a fierce bow, as one unyokes chariots.

**8.2.5**  O Kailāta, O dappled one, O Upatṛṇya, O brown one — listen to me, O black ones, O Alīkas. Do not stand in the way of our friend's desire. Make yourselves heard; indeed, be pleased, O serpents.

**8.2.6**  Āliki and Vili are thy father and thy mother. We know thy kinsman on every side. Thou art made powerless — what wilt thou do?

**8.2.7**  Daughter of Urugūlā, born of the slavegirl of the black one — she who was crawling about spreading her rash, has now rendered the powerful serpents powerless.

**8.2.8**  The eared porcupine spoke, roaming at the foot of the mountain: 'Whatever burrowing creatures these are, their poison is the most powerless of all.'

**8.2.9**  Not Tābuvam, not Tābuvam — thou art not really Tābuvam. By the Tābuvam the poison is made powerless.

**8.2.10**  Not Tastuvam, not Tastuvam — thou art not really Tastuvam. By the Tastuvam the poison is made powerless.

**8.2.11**  May this herb render thy poison powerless, O serpent — the Trāyamāṇā, the overpowering one, the possessor of power, the prevailing one. It has surely expelled the poison from cow, from horse, from man.

**8.3.1**  O Āñjana with the three peaks, born from the Himavat — crush all the sorcerers and all the female demons of sorcery.

**8.3.2**  Thou art indeed a protector, a crusher of sorcerers, O Āñjana. Thou hast power over immortality, and thou art nourishment for the drink-offering.

**8.3.3**  Thou art the protection of men, the protection of cattle. For the protection of horses, of steeds, thou hast stood firm.

**8.3.4**  Protect me, protect what is dear to us, protect our wealth. Let not enmity cross us; let nothing afflict us.

**8.3.5**  No curse reaches him, no sorcery, no grief-causing spell. Viṣkandha does not afflict him who carries thee, O Āñjana.

**8.3.6**  From the spell cast through the mouth, from evil dreaming, from hereditary disease, from the curse, and from the evil eye, from its terrible gaze — protect us from that, O Āñjana.

**8.3.7**  Three are the servants of Āñjana: fever, the consumptive disease Balāsa, and the serpent. Highest among mountains, Trikakud is the name of thy father.

**8.3.8**  Thou art the pupil of Vṛtra's eye; thou art the two eyes of the mountain. Proclaimed by all the gods, thou art named Paridhi — the Encompasser.

**8.3.9**  The Gandharva Vaida knows thy name as a secret. From wherever thou art born, O Āñjana, come hither for the sake of our unharmed well-being.

**8.3.10**  Whether thou art Traikakuda or art called Yamunā — both thy names are auspicious; by both of them protect us, O Āñjana.

**8.3.11**  Into whatever limb by limb, joint by joint thou dost creep, O Āñjana — drive out the disease Yakṣma from that place, like the fierce serpent that strikes at the middle.

**8.3.12**  The surrounding attackers do not slay him; he does not fall among the fallen. Among men he does not die who carries thee, O Āñjana.

**8.3.13**  Knowing this, O Āñjana, I shall speak truly, not falsely. May I win the horse, the cow, the garment, and my own self, O thy man.

**8.6.1**  You see hither, you see back, you see away, you see — heaven, the atmosphere, and earth: all that, O goddess, you behold.

**8.6.2**  The three heavens, the three earths, and these six great quarters — by you may I behold all sorcerers, O divine herb.

**8.6.3**  You are the pupil of that divine Suparṇa [the celestial eagle]; you have climbed up to earth like a weary bride carried in a vehicle.

**8.6.4**  Her has the thousand-eyed god placed in my right hand; by her I see all that has come to be and all that shall be.

**8.6.5**  As the four-eyed dog, as the dark horse among steeds, as fire facing in every direction — so are you, O herb.

**8.6.6**  You are the eye of Kaśyapa, the four-eyed one of Śuni; do not conceal from me the piśāca moving like the sun through the clear sky.

**8.6.7**  Show me the yātudhānas, show me the yātudhānīs; show me the kimīdin standing as though just bathed.

**8.6.8**  I have seized the all-protecting [herb] from the yātudhāna and the kimīdin; by it I see everything — both the Śūdra and the Ārya.

**8.6.9**  As the sun and moon behold all beings — so do you behold them; may the evil-living one not approach here.

**8.6.10**  He who flies through the atmosphere, he who crawls along the earth, he who regards heaven as his refuge — make that piśāca visible.

**8.6.11**  Reveal your forms; do not conceal yourself — for you, O thousand-eyed one, see [me] as I come toward you.

**8.7.1**  The mokṣeja, the toda, the tumala, those that dwell on the road, and the spotted ones — O herb, render powerless the venom of all these serpents.

**8.7.2**  Of the aśvakranda, the vaṇḍa, the pṛdāku, and the gonasa — O herb, render powerless the venom of all the white-patched ones as well.

**8.7.3**  Of the dyāṃpāta, the gavaka, and the snake with the godhā-striped back — make this whole breed of the black ones harmless; grant us freedom from harm.

**8.7.4**  This breed of the svaja serpents — make that of the brown ones powerless. You are the remedy for every brown serpent here; you are the corrupter of venom.

**8.7.5**  Let the trāyamāṇā herb speak out against all the kings among serpents — the tiraścirājin, the black ones, and those that lie in ambush.

**8.7.6**  With might she overpowers the sorcerers; with might, the sorceresses. The power that has overpowered all of you — I have seized hold of its name.

**8.7.7**  Together with Paidva's power and with my fierce utterance — the blind serpents, the sṛjaya snakes, the śaphaka, and the rathavra: leaping upon all of them together with force, she has made them all devoid of venom.

**8.7.8**  The yellow one that is a vidradha of the asita serpent, and whatever else is a vidradha — she who is the submerger of the vidradhas, the purifier: she crushed them all.

**8.7.9**  The blue-throated one among the svajas, the tawny one among the svajas as well — O herb Arundhatī, you crush the speckle-tailed one.

**8.7.10**  Let not this man bitten by the serpent die; may his father's venom not be his. For I am bringing him this herb, Arundhatī.

**8.7.11**  This whole breed of the pṛdāku serpents — render it powerless, O jīvala. You are Indra's dear plant; you are here the corrupter of venom.

**8.8.1**  A hundred flow hither, a hundred flow yonder; a hundred are the shafts of Vṛtra. From those — O waters, flow apart and away.

**8.8.2**  Fluttering through the mid-space, they were born forth from the cloud. Golden-hued are those waters — may they be beneficent to your heart.

**8.8.3**  Benign be they to your heart; benign to the regions of your heart; benign to your liver and lungs; and benign also to your inner parts.

**8.8.4**  Whatever you have trembled with in your limbs, whatever in the head, whatever in the joints of the back — may the waters cleanse all of that away, as a craftsman planes the broken part of a cart.

**8.8.5**  Let heart be joined with heart, let the lobes of the lung be joined with the lobes. Release the seized limb with the waters; may the radiance of my heel come together whole.

**8.8.6**  The divine waters coming down from the mountains, flowing forth from the gods — whatever man they reach today, that man shall suffer no harm.

**8.8.7**  Beneficent to you be the Himāvat waters; beneficent too be the spring-fed waters. Beneficent to you be the ever-flowing waters; and beneficent too be the rain-waters.

**8.8.8**  Beneficent to you be the desert waters; beneficent too be the marshy waters. Beneficent to you be the well-dug waters; and those brought in jars — may they too be beneficent.

**8.8.9**  Cloudless, digging their own way, inspired, deep in their flowing — more healing than healers: thus do we address the waters directly.

**8.8.10**  By the divine waters, by the stream-flowing waters — by the waters that cleanse: O horses, become strong and swift!

**8.8.11**  Those waters are auspicious waters — waters that make one free of disease. Just as there is refreshment when one thirsts, so too are those waters the foremost remedy for you.

**8.9.1**  The powers gathered together have brahman as their chief; brahman in the beginning stretched out the highest heaven. Among all beings brahman was born first — who then is worthy to contend against brahman?

**8.9.2**  These heaven and earth are brahman; these seven rivers are brahman; all these Ādityas are brahman; the gods attend upon brahman.

**8.9.3**  The brahmaṇa speaks brahman; into brahman the night retreats. That which belongs to Savitṛ̥ is born of brahman; through brahman Agni shines forth.

**8.9.4**  The plants come to rest in brahman; the rains rain down as brahman. All this that is endowed with self — as far as the sun surveys it — that is brahman.

**8.9.5**  The hotr̥ priest is brahman, the sacrifice is brahman; the oblation-sticks are measured by brahman. The adhvaryu is born of brahman; through brahman the oblation is poured out.

**8.9.6**  The ladling-spoons laden with ghee are brahman; the altar is raised up through brahman. The threads of the sacrifice are brahman — the officiating priests who have prepared the oblation.

**8.9.7**  The bull of auspicious seed is brahman; the cows that produce the oblation are brahman. That chariot of the divine one which conquers while adorned with its own sound — it too is brahman.

**8.9.8**  Through brahman the plow is drawn; through brahman the chariot is yoked. Through brahman the two-footed man does not falter as he walks.

**8.9.9**  The seers were born of brahman; the royal warriors too are of brahman. This universe is brahman; all that is born is of brahman; the food that will be revealed is of brahman.

**8.9.10**  The śūdras who serve the royal warriors are brahman; brahman also are those who tax them. Their auspicious dwelling-place is brahman; through brahman are their assembly-sitters.

**8.9.11**  The fishermen are brahman, the slaves are brahman; these gamblers too are brahman. Woman and man, both born of brahman; women are brahman, and the weavers as well.

**8.9.12**  Brahman on the heights, brahman in the valleys; brahman in the enclosed spaces — the forest trees, the mountains are brahman, the plants are brahman. Brahman has stretched out through all this, between both heaven and earth.

**8.9.13**  Brahman alone became the highest, O Jātaveda; the thunderbolt-wielder shattered the great demon sorcerer. May Bhava and Śarva, dispatched by Māyā, release their fiery weapon against him for his destruction.

**8.13.1**  From the ocean a honey-sweet wave has risen up; in silence it has reached to immortality. The secret name of ghee that exists — it is the tongue of the gods, the navel of immortality.

**8.13.2**  We shall proclaim the name of ghee; at this sacrifice we sustain it with reverences. Let the brahman hear it as it is chanted: the four-horned wild bull lowed it forth.

**8.13.3**  Four are his horns, three are his feet, two are his heads, seven are his hands. Thrice-bound, the bull bellows aloud; the great god has entered into mortals.

**8.13.4**  Placed in three parts, hidden away by the Paṇis, the gods found the ghee within the cow. One part Indra engendered, one part Sūrya; one part they fashioned out by inspiration through the power of Svadhā.

**8.13.5**  These flow forth from the heart, from the inner ocean, possessed of a hundred enclosures — the enemy does not perceive them. I behold the streams of ghee; golden is the reed-grass in their midst.

**8.13.6**  They flow together aright like rivers, like milk-cows, purified inwardly by heart and mind. These waves of ghee rush forth like deer driven forward by the bowstring.

**8.13.7**  Like the roar that precedes the Sindhu, their rushing voices — the great ones fly like a wind that sweeps away. The stream of ghee, like a red prize-horse splitting the courses, swells with its waves.

**8.13.8**  Like fair women going to a tryst, radiant and smiling, they flow toward Agni. The streams of ghee rush to the kindling-sticks; delighting in them, Jātavedas rejoices.

**8.13.10**  Rush forward to the praise, to the contest for cattle; place auspicious treasures in us. Lead this sacrifice, O deities — the streams of ghee flow forth sweet as honey.

**8.13.11**  Upon your abode all existence is established — in the inner ocean, within the heart, within life. That wave of yours, sweet as honey, which has been brought from the face of the waters, from the confluence — may we attain it.

**8.14.1**  Two of differing form wander, each seeking its own goal; each in turn suckles the calf. In one he appears tawny, self-sustaining; in the other he is seen white, of brilliant radiance.

**8.14.2**  Ten young women, unwearied, who carry the embryo of Tvaṣṭṛ, lead it round — it of sharp edge, self-resplendent among peoples, shining brightly.

**8.14.3**  They adorn three of his births — one in the ocean, one in the sky, one in the waters. Following the eastward direction of earthly beings, he ruled the seasons and distributed them in due order.

**8.14.4**  Who among you has comprehended this secret? The calf has begotten the mothers through inherent powers. The embryo of the many active waters, resting in their lap — the great seer moves forth, self-sustaining.

**8.14.5**  Possessing them, the beautiful one grows — upright in the lap of the crooked ones, in the self-sustained. Both, fearing the one being born of Tvaṣṭṛ, facing westward, welcome the lion.

**8.14.6**  Both auspicious ones take pleasure in him, not proudly; neither the cows nor the calves stood near them with their tracks. He became the lord of the wise among the wise; they anoint him from the south with oblations.

**8.14.7**  He stretches both arms upward like Savitṛ, the terrible one straining at both flanks. He shoots up a bright garment from himself; he sheds the new garments from the mothers.

**8.14.8**  He makes a brilliant, supreme form when he mingles his dwelling with rays of light and waters. The seer-vision cleanses the foundation thoroughly; that has become the gathering of the divine host.

**8.14.9**  Broadly your course goes around the foundation, the brightly shining abode of the great bull. O Agni, kindled by all self-resplendent ones, guard us with your undeceivable guardians.

**8.14.10**  He makes the desert a stream, a path, a wave; with bright waves he reaches the earth. He bears all prizes within his womb; he moves within the newly delivered mothers.

**8.14.11**  Thus, O Agni, growing through the kindling-sticks, shine brilliantly upon us, O purifier, with splendor. May Mitra and Varuṇa magnify that for us, Aditi, Sindhu, Earth, and Heaven.

**8.15.1**  He who is of the lineage of Jamadagni here, he who is a Kauśika, he who is of the lineage of Atri, and he who is a Kaśyapa — the Bharadvājas, Gotamas, and those who are Vasiṣṭhas — to them we here declare the transgressions.

**8.15.2**  The Agastyas, Kaṇvas, Kutsas, Prasravaṇas, Virūpas, Gargas, Mudgalas, Yaskas, Śūnakas — the Saṃkṛtis, all brāhmaṇas who have not been wronged — to them we here declare the transgressions.

**8.15.3**  He who does not rise to help a brāhmaṇa in distress, whether through sloth, pride, or stubbornness — all the Viśve Devās are witnesses against him; may the guilt, the poison, converge upon him.

**8.15.4**  Whatever festering wounds of guilt belong to a brāhmaṇa, whatever evil deeds done in many ways — he who follows along when instructed places them within himself; thus the gods also cause them to lodge in him.

**8.15.5**  He shall not eat, shall not drink, shall not lie down, shall not kiss wife nor son — one instructed in brahman-guilt should simply rise up and stand; that is the path of cosmic order.

**8.15.6**  The middling great seers of the hundred-versed hymns, and whatever progeny of the lesser hymns is here — the births of the seers that we know — to them we here declare the transgressions.

**8.15.7**  Of the fifteen hundreds of brothers, thirty-three gods arose as pupils. When one was pierced, all were wounded; they found that guilt resting upon the brāhmaṇa.

**8.15.8**  He who did not bring this, he who did not rise, he who did not speak up in this matter — he acts treacherously against that brāhmaṇa; he who, though the brāhmaṇa was in distress, sought brotherhood among brāhmaṇas.

**8.15.9**  Rise up, O brāhmaṇas, speak together; let us demand back what was won — may it all return. May Indra and Agni, may all the Viśve Devās turn what was won back to me again.

**8.15.10**  He secures long life; he attends to progeny — the brāhmaṇa who, himself a brāhmaṇa, when called follows in the matter of guilt.

**8.15.11**  Neither Śarva smites his progeny, nor Rudra smites it, nor the thunderbolt — he, the brāhmaṇa, who truthfully speaks in the matter of a brāhmaṇa's guilt.

**8.15.12**  The drinking of Soma delights him, the one who sees into men, together with the pressing-stones — he who stripped away the darkness from the standing heart of a brāhmaṇa as the sun strips away darkness.

**8.15.13**  He who, rising up in the matter of a brāhmaṇa's guilt, departs seeking food — let both Heaven and Earth scorch him; then let him go the path of one who has not gone.

**8.19.1**  From clarified butter the goat came into being; from the quarters these rice-porridges arose. Bṛhaspati beheld them — he it was who became the five-porridge sacrifice.

**8.19.2**  Gather these porridges together in fourfold fashion, O Bṛhaspati. The goat, born from clarified butter, became the fifth among them.

**8.19.3**  By its smoke it reaches heaven; by its heat it reaches the atmosphere; by its glow it reaches the quarters — this goat, the five-porridge sacrifice.

**8.19.4**  Your mother, your father, your brothers, and those who are your own — let them all live upon it, having cooked the goat as the five-porridge offering.

**8.19.5**  Those of your forebears who have gone beyond, and those who are the more recent fathers — let a stream of clarified butter flow to them, hundredfold, flooding everywhere.

**8.19.6**  Those who always give, or those who cook the rice-porridge — they indeed dwell in a world higher than the realm of Yama.

**8.19.7**  The Atirātra does not attain it; the Ukthya does not attain it; nor does the Agniṣṭoma attain the goat, the five-porridge sacrifice.

**8.19.8**  It is measured equal to the Daśarātra; it is reckoned equivalent to the Dvādaśāha; the goat, the five-porridge sacrifice, is measured equal to the Dīrghasattra.

**8.19.9**  All that is lost from the sacrificial grass, all that perishes, all that a thief drives away — for those whose rite is the five-porridge sacrifice, it is restored in full.

**8.19.10**  She who, having known a first husband, then finds another — those two, cooking the goat as the five-porridge offering, shall not be separated.

**8.19.11**  The later husband of a twice-wed woman comes to share the same world with her, giving the goat as the five-porridge offering with the southern light.

**8.20.1**  May the Sun sprinkle me with radiance; may both the Aśvins sprinkle me. The Āditya rising high aloft — may he too sprinkle me with radiance.

**8.20.7**  As far as you, O divine Sun, look out upon the world as you rise — look upon me with that same radiance.

**8.20.9**  I am clothed about with Bhaga's bounty, with radiance, and with wealth, so that I may ever move glowing, brilliant like the sun.

**8.20.11**  With a golden wheel the house of Bhaga is covered over. I open it with sacred utterance — may that be granted to me by the Aśvins, granted to me by the lotus-wreathed ones.
