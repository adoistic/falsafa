**1.1.1**  May the divine Waters be auspicious to us for our succour, auspicious for us to drink; may they flow about us bringing well-being and ease.

**1.1.2**  O Waters, fill me full of healing medicine, a shelter for my body; and may I long behold the sun.

**1.1.3**  Soma declared to me that within the Waters are all remedies, and Agni who brings well-being to all.

**1.1.4**  Ruling over precious things, dwelling among the peoples — from the Waters I beg healing medicine.

**1.2.1**  The mothers — kinswomen who minister at the sacrifice — travel along their paths, mingling the milk with honey.

**1.2.2**  Those yonder that are near the sun, and those with whom the sun moves together — may they speed our sacrifice.

**1.2.3**  I call upon the divine Waters where our cows drink; an oblation is to be made to the rivers.

**1.2.4**  Within the Waters is immortality, within the Waters is medicine; and in the praises of the Waters, O horses, you become vigorous steeds.

**1.3.1**  We know the father of the arrow — Parjanya, of many sustaining powers; we know indeed its mother — Pṛthivī, sustainer of all.

**1.3.2**  Homage around us to the bowstring; may our body become stone-hard. O plant, grow mightier — drive away hostilities, sweep off malevolences.

**1.3.3**  When the cows, embracing the tree, do reverence to the quiverless, unquivering, skillfully-wrought arrow — drive the shooting shaft away from us, O Indra; may these herbs be auspicious to us.

**1.3.4**  May Indra's vajra smite away the demon; may the arrows shot from afar fly away from us.

**1.4.1**  We know your father, O arrow — Parjanya, rich in seed. By him I make wholeness for your body; may your discharge flow upon the earth; let what is bāl go outside.

**1.4.2**  By Indra, by Varuṇa, by Candra, and by Sūrya as well [is this healing accomplished].

**1.4.3**  Whatever has collected in the intestines, in the two flanks, whatever has gathered in the bladder — [may it flow out] (remainder lost).

**1.4.4**  I burst open your urinary passage, the obstruction, as [one opens] a channel. By that I make wholeness for your body; may your discharge flow upon the earth; let what is bāl go outside.

**1.4.5**  As an arrow flies away, released from the bow, so may your urine be released — outward, all of it, bāl.

**1.5.1**  Vaṣaṭ! O Pūṣan, may Aryamā — the skilled hotṛ, the Disposer — fashion a fine recitation for her. May the birth-giver flow forth; may the joints open wide for her giving birth.

**1.5.2**  The four directions and four quarter-directions, and those of the earth as well — the gods have brought the embryo together; may those very ones open it wide for her to give birth.

**1.5.3**  May she who is prepared open wide; we loosen the womb. Relax [it], O Sūṣaṇa; and you, O Viṣkala, let [the child] go free.

**1.5.4**  Not in the sinews, not in the joints, not in the hair, not in the nails — may the variegated membrane fall away, for a dog to devour the afterbirth.

**1.5.5**  Not in the flesh, not in the fat, not clinging to anything whatsoever — may the afterbirth fall down.

**1.6.1**  Those three-times-seven who go about bearing all forms — may Vācaspati place their strengths into my body today.

**1.6.2**  Come near to us, O Vācaspati, together with the divine mind. O lord of the breath-utterance, make it rest within me; within me alone — my body.

**1.6.3**  Stretch forth here in both directions, as two bow-tips by the bowstring. May Vācaspati hold [them] fast within me; within me alone — my body.

**1.6.4**  Vācaspati is invoked; I, the devotee of Vācaspati, am invoked. May I succeed together with my renown; may I not fail through my renown.

**1.7.1**  The divine Gandharva who is lord of the world, worthy of praise among the peoples with obeisance by the single-lived — thee, that one, I hymn with sacred utterance, O divine god; homage be to thee; thy dwelling-place is in the sky.

**1.7.2**  Touched by heaven, adorable, with skin of sunlight, he has driven off the fury of the divine wrath; gracious with obeisance by the single-lived — may the Gandharva, who is lord of the world, show favour.

**1.7.3**  The Gandharva too has indeed come together with these blameless Apsarases; they say their dwelling is the ocean, whence they go hither and thither at once.

**1.7.4**  O lightning-flash of the clouds, O star-born ones, you who attend upon the Gandharva Viśvāvasu — to those of you, O goddesses, I make obeisance.

**1.7.5**  Those who are weepers, dwellers in darkness, eager for dice-play, bewitchers of the mind — to those Apsarases, wives of the Gandharvas, I have paid homage.

**1.8.1**  From yonder mountain thou art drawn forth as a healing remedy; a remedy, an excellent remedy — whatever remedy I make of thee.

**1.8.2**  Indeed, indeed — the hundred remedies that are thine, and the thousand that are thine: of those thou art the highest, free from leaking away, free from disease.

**1.8.3**  This great red-blooded one has been lifted up from the earth; that is the remedy against discharge, and it has indeed destroyed disease.

**1.8.4**  The field-crickets carry up the remedy from the ocean; thou art the Atharvaṇa remedy for the wound, thou art the Atharvaṇa seat-of-disease.

**1.9.1**  This one is the Asura of the gods who rules on high — for the command of King Varuṇa is true; conquering over and beyond that with sacred utterance, I lead this man up out of the fierce wrath.

**1.9.2**  Homage to thee, O King Varuṇa, to thy wrath — for thou, O god, dost mark all transgression; a hundred, a thousand others I drive away; let this man of ours live to see out the years.

**1.9.3**  Whatever untruth thou hast spoken with thy tongue, much that is crooked — I release thee from Varuṇa, the king whose ordinance is truth.

**1.9.4**  I have released thee from Vaiśvānara, from beyond the great ocean-floods; speak forth, O fierce one, to thy kinsmen; fashion the sacred utterance and do homage for us.

**1.10.1**  Varuṇa has spoken in favor of lead; Agni attends upon lead. Indra bestowed lead upon me as a dispeller of disease.

**1.10.2**  This overpowers viṣkandha; this drives back those who devour. By means of this I shall overpower all things that are born of the piśācī-demon.

**1.10.3**  Those who arose at the new-moon night gleaming — the devourers: Agni is the fourth one, the slayer of sorcerers; may he protect us from them.

**1.10.4**  Whether you strike a horse, whether a cow, whether a man — we pierce you with lead, so that you shall be devoid of heroes against us.

**1.11.1**  With the all-encompassing amulet by which Indra has encompassed himself — with that, O Bṛhaspati, make this one revolve toward the kingdom.

**1.11.2**  Encompassed are the rivals, encompassed are those who are hostile to us; stand over the one who wars against us, over the one who does harm to us.

**1.11.3**  The god Savitṛ has encompassed you; Soma has encompassed you; all beings have encompassed you, so that you are the all-encompassing one.

**1.11.4**  That sun has risen; risen too is my word: that I shall be a slayer of enemies, without rivals, a killer of rivals.

**1.11.5**  A bull who annihilates rivals, who overpowers the kingdom, who overcomes all — that I may shine supreme over these heroes and over the people.

**1.12.1**  May this one grow old unto you alone, O Old Age; let not the other deaths harm him except you. As a mother with full love holds a son in her lap, may Mitra protect him from distress through friendship.

**1.12.2**  May Mitra and Varuṇa, devouring foes in accord, make for you death-by-old-age. That Agni, the invoking priest, knowing the ordinances, the god who is the generator of all, shall proclaim all things.

**1.12.3**  Heaven is your father, Earth is your mother — let them make for you old-age-death and long life, so that you may live a hundred winters in the lap of Aditi, guarded by in-breath and out-breath.

**1.12.4**  You rule over earthly cattle — those already born and those yet to be born. Let not the in-breath abandon him, nor the out-breath; let not friends slay him, nor enemies.

**1.13.1**  Given by Indra, directed by Varuṇa, fierce and set in motion by the Maruts, he has come to us. This one I deliver into your two hands, O Heaven-and-Earth — may he neither thirst nor hunger.

**1.13.2**  Grant him vital nourishment, O nourishment-rich one; grant him milk, O milk-rich one. Let Heaven-and-Earth bestow vital nourishment upon him; let all the gods, the Maruts, and the Waters bestow vital nourishment.

**1.13.3**  May the auspicious ones satisfy his heart; free from disease, let him move about here rejoicing. May the two Aśvins, assuming a form by their magic power, together clothed, drink this mixed drink.

**1.13.4**  May the purifying draught attend his drinking; may vital nourishment and self-sustaining power attend him — this is she. Indra, struck first, released her — she is that vital nourishment and ageless self-sustaining power for you. By her may you live through autumns radiant with vigor; may it not flow away from you — the healers have made it fast for you.

**1.14.1**  O All-Gods, protect this man; and you Ādityas, be wakeful over him. Let not a rival, nor one of different lineage reach him; let not the man-slaying blow reach him.

**1.14.2**  You who are gods, and those who are fathers and those who are sons — of one mind, hear this utterance of mine. I deliver this man over to all of you; may you lead him in well-being to old age.

**1.14.3**  You gods who dwell in heaven, who are on earth, who are in the atmosphere, in the plants, in the waters — make long life and old age his lot; ward off a hundred other deaths from him.

**1.14.4**  Those gods for whom there are fore-offerings and after-offerings, who have shares in the oblation poured and unpoured, for whom the five directions are apportioned among you — those who sit at the sacrificial session, I make them into protectors for this man.

**1.15.1**  I take hold of your good fortune, as a garland upon the head. Like a mountain with a great root, may you long sit among your fathers.

**1.15.2**  O Yama, king, this maiden is shaken loose as a bride for you. Let her be bound in the house of her mother, and also of her brother, and also of her father.

**1.15.3**  O king, this one is your guardian of emblems; we deliver this one over to you. May she long sit among the fathers; may it reach fully to the crown of her head.

**1.15.4**  By the sacred power of Asita, of Kaśyapa, and of Gaya — like female kin binding an inner treasure-sack, I bind up your good fortune.

**1.16.1**  You are born of the night, O herb — in the dark, in the black, in the deep-dark. O Rājanī, stain and color away this leprosy (kilāsa) and this greyness (palita).

**1.16.2**  Both the white-spot disease (kilāsa) and the greyness (palita) — drive them out and destroy them from here, O speckled one. Let your own (natural) color restore itself to you; expel the white patches far away.

**1.16.3**  Dark (asita) is your place of dissolution, dark (asita) is your abode. You are the dark-herb (asiknī), O plant — drive out and destroy this from here, O speckled one.

**1.16.4**  The white mark (śveta lakṣma) upon the skin — whether of leprosy (kilāsa) born from the bones, or born of the body — though wrought by a curse (brahmaṇā dūṣā), I have destroyed it utterly.

**1.17.1**  The first-born of the womb, the bull of the dawn-rays (usriya), the cloud-born, goes forth thundering with rain. May he, who moves straight and pierces, whose single vital power (ojas) he has spread threefold, be merciful to our bodies.

**1.17.2**  He who has lodged himself with blazing flame in every limb, who seized it — the one who seized the joints is also seized. I worship with oblation (havis) the joints and the inter-joints; him who abides in the heart and was born through the mind I worship.

**1.17.3**  I release him from headache and likewise from cough — him who has entered joint by joint. That (affliction) which is cloud-born, which is wind-born, and that parching fever (śuṣma) — let them attend to the forest trees and the mountains.

**1.17.4**  Auspicious (śam) be it for your upper limb, auspicious be it for your lower limb; auspicious be it for your back and your marrows — auspicious be it for your body.

**1.18.1**  May Mitra come near, ordering himself by the seasons, settling the earth with the dawn-cows (usriyā). May Varuṇa, Vāyu, and Agni bestow upon us that great kingdom (bṛhad rāṣṭra) worthy of being established.

**1.18.2**  May Dhātṛ, Mitra, Varuṇa, divine Agni, Indra, and Tvaṣṭṛ accept my words. I call upon the goddess Aditi, mother of heroes — may I stand foremost among those born of the same kind (sajāta).

**1.18.3**  With reverences I call upon Soma and Savitṛ, (and) all the gods, for my own supremacy (uttaratva). This Agni blazes on throughout the day, kindled by the companions of the same birth (sajāta) who do not contradict.

**1.18.4**  Remain here; do not go forward. Ārya, the herdsman and lord of prosperity (puṣṭapati), has driven you (here). Toward him, O desiring-women (kāminī), for his desire — may all the gods bring you (wives) together, here, under the sky.

**1.19.1**  May the Vasus establish wealth in this one — Indra, Tvaṣṭṛ, Varuṇa, Mitra, Agni; may the Ādityas and all the gods sustain him in the highest divine light.

**1.19.2**  For him let the gods grant splendor in the quarters — the sun, fire, and gold; shine forth with the superior brahman-power, making rivals lower than yourself.

**1.19.3**  O Jātavedas, by the same superior brahman-power with which you gathered the vital essences for Indra — by that, O Agni, increase this man here; bestow upon him the fullness of riches and sovereignty.

**1.19.4**  I carry off the sacrifice and the vigor of these foes, O Agni — the fullness of riches and their possessions; let the rivals be lower than us; O gods, establish this man in the highest light.

**1.20.1**  Let not the piercers find us, let not the smiting archers find us; O Indra, strike far from us the scattering volleys of arrows.

**1.20.2**  Let the arrows shot from all directions fall away from us — those discharged and those yet to be discharged; may gods, mortals, and seers pierce our enemies.

**1.20.3**  The enemy, whether known to us or unknown, who seeks to slay us — may Rudra with his arrows pierce those foes of ours.

**1.20.4**  Whether kinsman or stranger who plots harm against us, O Indra — let all the gods shatter him; the sacred formula is my innermost armor.

**1.21.1**  Not yet perished, falling away of themselves, more unreal than unreal things — more sapless than the sehora plant, more withered than salt: [thus depart, O apacit growths].

**1.21.2**  Fall away, O apacit growths, as a bird flies from its nest; may the Sun make the remedy, and may the Moon drive you away.

**1.21.3**  One is spotted, one is tawny, one is black, two are ruddy — I have seized the name of all of them; O ones that slay no hero, depart.

**1.21.4**  The unborn, the dark wanderer — the apacit will fly away; the glaur will fly away from here; that which dissolves shall perish.

**1.21.5**  Depart, O female apacit growths — Indra and Pūṣan have perceived you; depart from his neck, depart from his feet, O ones who estrange kinsmen.

**1.22.1**  To the four immortal Guardians of the Quarters (āśāpāla), the overseers of all beings — we would do honour with oblation.

**1.22.2**  Ye four divine Guardians of the Quarters who stand as sentinels — may they release us from the nooses of Nirṛti, from sin upon sin.

**1.22.3**  Thee who art without blemish I would honour with oblation; thee who art without wound I offer ghee. May the fourth divine Guardian of the Quarters bring great prosperity hither to us.

**1.22.4**  May there be welfare for our mother and likewise for our father; welfare for the cattle and for men. May all be of good being and well disposed toward us; may we look upon the sun for a long time.

**1.23.1**  O people, at the assembly a great brahman shall be proclaimed — one that is neither upon the earth nor in heaven, from which the plants draw breath.

**1.23.2**  Its abode is in the atmosphere, like the resting place of those who have grown weary. Wise and creative men know that as the foundation of this being.

**1.23.3**  When the two quivering worlds and the earth were fashioned — that [cosmic principle] is moist even today and always, revolving like the rim of a wheel about to split.

**1.23.4**  One [sphere] has enveloped the all; upon the other the all rests. To Heaven, omniscient, and to Earth I have made obeisance.

**1.24.1**  Let the rivers flow together; let the divine winds also flow together. With all those confluent streams I cause wealth to flow hither to me.

**1.24.2**  The confluences that flow together — of milk and of water — with all those confluent streams I cause wealth to flow hither to me.

**1.24.3**  The springs that flow together out of the rivers, ever perennial and unfailing — with all those confluent streams I cause wealth to flow hither to me.

**1.24.4**  Come hither, ye bearers of oblation; come hither, ye confluent ones as well. May every head of cattle come here; increase the riches of this man.

**1.38.1**  Of these three earths, the earth is indeed the highest among them. Upon the skin of these I have gathered together the healing herb.

**1.109.4**  O Soma and Rudra, place all remedies within us, within our bodies; loosen and release whatever sin is bound in the limbs, and whatever has become visible.

**1.110.1**  The impulse of ghee, together with the gods in union, nourishing throughout the year with oblation — may ear, eye, and breath be uncut for us; may we be uncut of lifespan and radiant vigor.

**1.110.2**  May breath call to us; we in turn invoke breath; earth has seized luster, the atmosphere luster; Soma and Bṛhaspati are the ordaining dispensers of luster.

**1.110.3**  Heaven and earth have become the twin repositories of luster; having seized luster, may we travel together along the earth. The cows, approaching with glory, attend the lord of the herd; having seized glory, may we travel together along the earth.

**1.110.4**  Build the cattle-pen, for he is your protector; stitch many broad coats of armor; make iron fortresses unassailable; let not your cup overflow — make it firm.

**1.111.1**  The wind blows downward, the sun burns downward; the inviolable cow milks downward — may your venom go downward and be spent.

**1.111.2**  The cows have settled in the cowpen, the calves at the tether; the waves of rivers have subsided, the vigor of the venom-spent serpents has subsided.

**1.111.3**  I have indeed grasped together the heads of serpents and their offspring; reaching the thousand-armed pool, I washed out the venom of the serpent.

**1.111.4**  For the swift and for the slow, for the widely-dwelling peoples as well — I make for you the remedy: the serpent-born poison rendered powerless.

**1.112.1**  These two thighs of mine, well-clad, I anoint with luster for myself; for I know the secret name of these two as few others know it.

**1.112.2**  Earth is filled full with luster; heaven is propped up by the sun; the splendor that we behold in the wind — that I direct down into my two thighs.

**1.112.3**  I know indeed your secret name, O conquering, unconquered pair; I pray for abundant offspring and a realm guarded by Indra.

**1.112.4**  Knowing your secret name, O Aśvins — sweet as the honey of the bee; like the eye of the sun for all living beings — hold offspring steadfast in me, hold wealth steadfast in me.

**1.112.5**  I spread out a hundred-snared net across the thighs and across the loins; whoever is bound in that binding — let him be impotent toward me.
