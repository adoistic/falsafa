**6.1.1**  That was the foremost among the worlds from which was born the Fierce One, blazing with virile might; born forthwith, he shatters enemies; all the helpers exult after him.

**6.1.2**  Swelling with power, abounding in strength, he places fear in the slave-foe; both the unclad and the clad bow down to thee, the champion, at the gift-offerings in the festal joys.

**6.1.3**  All helpers mix their will in thee when these come twice and thrice together. Sweeter than the sweet, blend that sweet one together; truly that honey — do thou cleave to it with honey.

**6.1.4**  For thus, as the winner of riches, the sages extol thee in battle after battle. More mighty, O Bold One, extend thyself firmly; may the ill-intentioned sorcerers not deceive thee.

**6.1.5**  Through thee we have prevailed in battles, beholding many things worthy of war. With my words I urge thy weapons forward; with sacred speech I strengthen thy pinions.

**6.1.6**  May I praise him of many forms, the Skilled One, the mightiest, the child of the Āptyas, lord among the Āptyas; with his strength he makes the seven rivers appear, overcoming multitudes of counterparts.

**6.1.7**  Thou dost establish both the nearer and the farther, him in whose dwelling thou hast protected with thy help; thou dost make the two swift-moving Mothers stand firm, and from there thou accomplishest manifold deeds.

**6.1.8**  These prayers Bṛhaddiva, the foremost, the sun-seeker, proclaims as might for Indra; the self-sovereign dwells in the great enclosure and has opened wide all his own doors.

**6.1.9**  Thus did Bṛhaddiva Atharvan, the great one, declare Indra himself to be his own body; the sisterly winds of Mātariśvan, stainless, both urge him on and increase him with strength.

**6.2.1**  Prospering, of exhilarating origin, radiant in manifestation, with immortal breath, nobly born, growing; with breath undeceived, shining — like a serpent Trita has upheld the three.

**6.2.2**  He who first settled in the ordinance — from thence he fashions manifold forms; the nourishing one first entered the womb, he who has heeded the unuttered word.

**6.2.3**  He whose heat poured forth from thy body, dripping gold, pure, following their own nature — here thou dost hold the immortal names; the robes, O master, are set in motion for us.

**6.2.4**  When these have proclaimed the ancient foremost thing, standing seat by seat on that which ages not — the Sage, as the two Mothers of vitality lick him, has measured the kinsman-bride's form as counterpart.

**6.2.5**  That great and broad ruling power — the Sage has fashioned it with his wisdom, O Yama, in reverence; when the converging ones press toward the earth, then the great wheel of obstruction has grown.

**6.2.6**  The seven sages have fashioned the seven bounds; towards one of them the sinful one has gone; yet thou who art immortal in breath, encompassed, advancing, O Asura, attained, goest along by thine own power.

**6.2.7**  The eldest life sets down its treasures, or the Sage, the giver, is attended by vigour; or when a son urges the kingly power against his parents, calling the eldest bound with blessing.

**6.2.8**  Look now upon those, O Varuṇa, who have turned back again at thy sacrifice; thou dost fashion forms — thou fillest one half with the other's power, and with half thou increasest thy vigour, O Unwildering.

**6.2.9**  We have magnified the powerful friend, Varuṇa the vigorous son of Aditi; we have spoken for him the words proclaimed by the Sage, to the two Worlds truthful in speech.

**6.3.1**  Who drove you forth from behind? Who dug out the paths in front, when you, the Waters urged on by Varuṇa, came hurrying?

**6.3.2**  Prajāpati released you; he dug out the paths in front; and he in turn released us from behind — released by him, we flow.

**6.3.3**  The purifying Waters flow in many ways, through all these worlds and through all the regions; may they purify us from sin and blame; may they release us from the lap of death and dissolution.

**6.3.4**  May the Waters, our mothers, refine us; may the clarified-butter purifiers purify us with ghee; for the divine ones carry away all defilement — from them indeed I come forth pure, cleansed.

**6.3.5**  The divine Waters, our mothers, intent on refining, dripping with ghee, have mingled together with honey; may those bright ones, the divine ones, bestow on us the whole span of life by night and by day.

**6.3.6**  Most watery of waters, most wealthy of the wealthy ones — may the Waters purify; may they purify us.

**6.3.7**  You, O Waters, hold the glory of heroes; you refine the pure ones; your great directions, the sub-regions separate, flow in motion.

**6.3.8**  You are the womb of Mitra and Varuṇa; you are the sweetest milk-cows of Soma; the god Indu takes up his dwelling in you, O divine ones; O Waters, gladden the brahman power and the kṣatra power.

**6.3.9**  Continually prevailing through these, we go pursuing the appeasers; the Waters are the refiners of all — those which the gods gave to Manu.

**6.3.10**  When they flow, the Waters purify; when they stand still, they become purely clean; I have found no blame in them, no defilement — from of old they have been mingled together with honey.

**6.3.11**  Golden-hued, pure, purifying, the Waters have strode forward, having cast away blame; a hundred of you, O divine ones, and a thousand purifying filters purify at the outpourings.

**6.3.12**  Such as they are, life-bestowing, they refine the brahman whose toes they touch, worthy of praise; those who make everything pure, who do not pass away — like cows, well-born, you stand with milk.

**6.3.13**  Release us from all defilements, O rivers, from whatever sins we have committed with our bodies; directed by Indra, urged on by Varuṇa, pour in honey, O Waters, into the ocean.

**6.4.1**  Night is your mother, the Sky your father, Aryamā is your grandfather. Śilācī indeed is your name; you are the sister of the gods.

**6.4.2**  Whoever drinks you lives; you protect the man. You are the upholder of the eternal ones, and the binder of the eternal ones.

**6.4.3**  Whatever has been done by a staff, or by an arrow, or by a wound with burning heat — for that you are the healer; Niṣkṛti indeed is your name.

**6.4.4**  Auspiciously you abide on the plakṣa, on the aśvattha, on the khadira, on the dhava; auspiciously on the nyagrodha tree. Come to us, O Arundhati!

**6.4.5**  Tree by tree you climb, like a maiden in her ardour. Victorious, standing firm against all, Saṃjayā indeed is your name.

**6.4.6**  O golden-hued young one, vigorous, with a hairy flank — O Lākṣā, you are the sister of the waters; the wind has become your very breath.

**6.4.7**  O golden-armed, blessed one, of the sun's colour, most beautiful in form — the expiation goes where it must go; expiate this man here.

**6.4.8**  Ghṛtācī by name is the illegitimate child; Ajababhru is your father. You are the blood of that dark horse of Yama — so are you infused.

**6.4.9**  Fallen together from the blood of the horse, she has clung to the leaf. You are a flowing, winged one; come to us, O Arundhati!

**6.4.10**  O Ghṛtācikā, who rides the wind-chariot, whose wings are lightning, O Arundhati — you go most readily to the afflicted; verily you shall carry him away from pain.

**6.4.11**  Whatever of you has been consumed by the Piśācas, let that swell up again for you. May Lākṣā, the all-healer, protect you together with the gods.

**6.5.1**  As Heaven and Earth do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.2**  As Wind and the Atmosphere do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.3**  As the Sun and the Moon do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.4**  As Day and Night do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.5**  As the cow and the bull do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.6**  As Mitra and Varuṇa do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.7**  As the brahman and the kṣatra do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.8**  As Indra and his sovereign power do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.9**  As the hero and heroic might do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.10**  As the in-breath and the out-breath do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.11**  As Death and Immortality do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.12**  As Truth and Untruth do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.5.13**  As what has been and what shall be do not fear and do not perish, even so, O my in-breath, do not fear; O my out-breath, do not fail.

**6.6.1**  Honey-sweet is my going forth, honey-sweet is my going onward; may I speak with a honey-sweet voice; may my two eyes be honeyed in appearance.

**6.6.2**  He who stretches out toward me, who has fallen at my feet — and he who has come back again to me — may his two eyes wither with longing.

**6.6.3**  A barren cow is your mother, O Madhu-substance; a potent bull-ox is your father. You are born from a milch-cow; being a king, you are most laden with sweetness.

**6.6.4**  The bull-like one possessed of virile power, soma-backed, dwelling in the sky — Indrāṇī brought forward the honey-substance first of all, for the sake of good fortune.

**6.6.5**  The honey-substance that feeds on food, the one with a dry mouth — for the heart, indeed. May it anoint me with sweetness, so that I may obtain what I desire.

**6.6.6**  The honey-substance, born of the river, is led among people like a horse. May it anoint me with sweetness, so that I may obtain what I desire.

**6.6.7**  May sweet Sinīvālī sprinkle me together with sweetness. May she anoint me with sweetness, so that I may obtain what I desire.

**6.6.8**  Just as the bees anoint honey upon honey, so do I anoint your mind upon this body of mine.

**6.6.9**  Whatever of your mind has gone away, whatever is bound here or elsewhere — that I turn back to you; within me I bind your mind.

**6.7.1**  Kaśyapa and Suparṇa both stood in the Marīci; Suparṇa looked about and saw the earth enveloped in the ocean.

**6.7.2**  That earth which Suparṇa beheld sunken within the great flood — thee, O Sūkara, by thy wizardry thou didst lift up thrice from the ocean.

**6.7.3**  This earth which thou, O Sūkara, didst bring up from the ocean by thy wizardry — she is the corrupter of poison; let her be the remedy for this one.

**6.7.4**  She who, unshaken, stands upon the truth, clad in gold — from her, the all-sustaining, I draw forth the poison-destroyer.

**6.7.5**  She of whom one says: "whose nest is this" — [so is she named by one who knows].

**6.7.6**  She of whose earth the crickets make a home for themselves — from her, the all-sustaining, I draw forth the poison-destroyer.

**6.7.7**  He who found thee, O Earth; he who lifted thee up, O Earth — I have seized upon the names of those two, O thou of a thousand streams.

**6.7.8**  The names which Indra the Vṛtra-slayer made for thee, O Earth — those names of thine, O tawny one, we know them fully; be thou the destroyer of poison!

**6.7.9**  Those that the Maruts fashioned for thee, those that the seven seers know, that which all the Ādityāḥ know — that Earth is the destroyer of poison.

**6.8.1**  Overpower the yātudhānas, overpower the yātudhānīs; overpower all the rākṣasas — thou art the overpowering one, O herb.

**6.8.2**  O overpowering one, O ever-victorious one — vanquishing, I am the superior. Moreover I am the conqueror; I, together with the ever-following ones, have conquered you.

**6.8.3**  Thou who movest as the overpowering one, like a bull that has conquered — destroyer of sadānvāḥ, destroyer of rākṣasas: thou, O herb, art terrible.

**6.8.4**  O Khelā with drooping breasts — flee hence, fly away from here! A head with horns like a bull here has not missed thee.

**6.8.5**  O thou of no pressing, O thou of many sons, O thou with no entrails, O great-bellied one — depart quickly from us along the road, O gravel-devourer, roam on!

**6.8.6**  Those misers who roam seeking the pressing of the fool — upon them, O Agni of the black track, sharp-horned, go goring!

**6.8.7**  That piece of wood which floats in the midst of the river, uninhabited by any person — grasp hold of that, O malevolent one, and go yonder beyond.

**6.8.8**  She whose birth-place the desired ones have traversed — the unseen worms, the scorching insects — to her, the consort of Balāsa, to the kuṣṭha-afflicting one, I make obeisance.

**6.8.9**  O daughter of Śarva, O wife of Bhava, O sister of the yātu-demons, O ever-following one — feed upon her sons and brothers there where we conduct thee.

**6.9.1**  This soma-stalk is pressed for the bull; for the bull it is taken up, fierce for the one who beholds men. The divine, manly one has cried aloud — great is the name of the bull, for such is his hump.

**6.9.2**  Be thou the equinox, O Viṣṇu — thou who art the lord of men for me. Moreover, even as Indra among the gods, let him proclaim himself among the peoples.

**6.9.3**  Let him proclaim himself among men, in concert with Indra the ally; and may Agni Vaiśvānara, the overcomer, confer upon thee the kingdom.

**6.9.4**  He whose share — this bull — is led around for Indra: let him smite enemies on the approach, and let him smite them on the departure.

**6.9.5**  Let him be the slayer of the enemy, the slayer of those who rage; let him be the leader of the clans, going at the fore among those who break forth.

**6.9.6**  Make this bull of Indra long-lived, full of brilliance, and also the overlord of the clans, the master of this earth.

**6.9.7**  He who is of fine horns, of fine hump, auspicious — he has seated himself upon the sacrificial grass; with the bull born of farmer-folk we offer worship.

**6.9.8**  Offering worship with the bull, as if with gentle ghee, we overcome with Indra all scorners and all battle-hosts.

**6.9.9**  The bull which the gods gave thee as thy exclusive share — with him strike down the Vṛtra-foes and smite the enemies in battle.

**6.9.10**  Smite the enemies, thrust aside the scorners — thou art arisen, endowed with virile power. We lead thee around as Indra's share; let our world be wide, free from adversaries.

**6.9.11**  O thou grown great with ghee, fed with ghee, of a thousand horns, well praised — O thou invoked with ghee, blaze forth!

**6.9.12**  Thou who, anointed with ghee, dost stand fierce for victory — do thou carry us through the straits and through the overpowering of battle-hosts.

**6.10.1**  The karkī cow, blessed and fortunate, wife of the bull, desirous of offspring, obedient, lowing — she who is in-calf and moves in season: a single donor gives her as one among a thousand.

**6.10.2**  She who bears the embryo and yields milk, she who, as the cow of all the gods (vaiśvadevī), is milked for the agnihotra — may that cow not harm us; she is the spouse of Varuṇa.

**6.10.3**  Toward the womb of the karkī cow the bulls move in one accord, eager to beget offspring. May she come to us as Aditi of all forms; the worlds cry out to her.

**6.10.4**  Sent forth by Prajāpati, full of seasonable vigor, the bulls resort to her for offspring. Let the virile bulls, bellowing, press upon her as she lows in desire — the seven-named lowing cow.

**6.10.6**  Self-standing, O lowing cow, you stand firm for the bull, facing Soma, Sūrya, and Agni. Come to me without harm, O lowing one; may the lord of cattle prosper the livestock for your giver.

**6.10.7**  Prosperous shall the giver be — he who gives the radiant cow as Agni's share; the winning steeds shall stimulate you, O prize-bearing one, in your dear abode, the heart, sweet with Soma.

**6.10.8**  I render homage while receiving her — to that fire which dwells within the lowing cow, and to the manly greatness and might that has arisen in her. May she be pleasant and gracious to my person.

**6.10.9**  The lowing cow has come; we receive her — the cow of nourishment together with the troop of males. Bearing strength and yielding ghee, may she bring thousandfold prosperity for my giver.
