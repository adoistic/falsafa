**18.1.1**  By truth the earth is upheld; by the sun the sky is upheld; by cosmic order (ṛta) the Ādityas stand firm; in heaven Soma is exalted.

**18.1.2**  By Soma the Ādityas are mighty; by Soma the great earth endures; therefore Soma is placed in the lap of these constellations.

**18.1.3**  One who has drunk thinks he has drunk Soma when they press the plant; the Soma that the brahmins know — of that no earthly being partakes.

**18.1.4**  Protected by covering rites, guarded by Bārhaté hymns, O Soma, you stand listening to the pressing-stones; no earthly being eats of you.

**18.1.5**  Thought was the bridal cushion, sight the anointing; heaven and earth were the casket when Sūryā went to her husband.

**18.1.6**  The Raibhī verse was the gift-accompanying (bride's) escort; the Nārāśaṃsī was the wooing chant; adorned with the Gāthā song, Sūryā's auspicious garment comes.

**18.1.7**  The praise-hymns (stoma) were the bridal supports; the meter was the headdress (kurīra); the Aśvins were Sūryā's suitors; Agni went as the vanguard.

**18.1.8**  Soma became the wooer of the bride; the Aśvins were both her suitors; when Sūryā was proclaimed in mind as destined for her lord, Savitṛ gave her away.

**18.1.9**  Mind was her chariot-body; heaven was its canopy; two bright bullocks were yoked when Sūryā went to her husband.

**18.1.10**  The two cows driven on by Ṛk and Sāman came as your Sāman-verses; your two wheels were hearing itself; in heaven the path moves ever to and fro.

**18.2.1**  Your two wheels are pure as you travel; the axle of the wind is driven; Sūryā mounted the mind-made chariot, going forth toward her lord.

**18.2.2**  The bridal procession of Sūryā moved forward, the one whom Savitṛ released; at the (asterism) Agha cattle are slaughtered; under the Phalgunīs she is carried in procession.

**18.2.3**  When you two Aśvins came questioning, with a three-wheeled vehicle for the bridal procession of Sūryā — where was your one wheel, where did you stand to guide?

**18.2.4**  When you two lords of prosperity came to choose Sūryā as bride, all the gods knew that of yours; Pūṣan chose you as fathers — as a son does.

**18.2.5**  Your two wheels, O Sūryā, the brahmins know in their proper seasons; but the one wheel that is hidden — only the truly devoted know that.

**18.2.6**  I release you from the snare of Varuṇa with which the gracious Savitṛ bound you; in the womb of cosmic order, in the world of the well-performed, may it be easy for you, O bride, together with your husband.

**18.2.7**  We worship Aryaman, the good kinsman, finder of a husband; may they release me from here and from there, as a cucumber from its stalk.

**18.2.8**  May they release me from here and from there, may he make her well-bound from that side; so that she, O Indra the bounteous, may be blessed with fine sons and good fortune.

**18.2.9**  May Bhaga lead you from here, taking your hand; may the Aśvins carry you forward in their chariot; go to the house, that you may be mistress of the house, a free woman who speaks at the assembly.

**18.2.10**  May what is dear to you here prosper for progeny; be wakeful in this house for the gārhapatya (householder's fire); unite your body with this husband; and then as an elder you shall speak at the assembly.

**18.3.1**  Remain here together, be not parted; enjoy a long life; playing with sons and grandsons, rejoicing in your own home.

**18.3.2**  These two youths roam east and west by their power, playing they go around the sacrifice; one surveys all the worlds; the other, assigning the seasons, is born ever new.

**18.3.3**  Born again and again he becomes new, the banner of the days; he goes to the fore of the dawns; apportioning the share to the gods as he comes, the moon extends long life.

**18.3.4**  Give away the śāmūlya garment to the brahmins; distribute the goods; this witchcraft, having become a footprint-tracker, enters the wife who enters the husband.

**18.3.5**  It becomes dark-red, the attachment of witchcraft is dissolved; her kinsmen prosper while the husband is bound in fetters.

**18.3.6**  The body becomes inauspicious, afflicted by that evil (charm); when the husband wraps his own limb with the bride's garment.

**18.3.7**  The tearing away, the cutting apart, and also the further trimming — look upon the forms of Sūryā's (garment); the brahmin purifies these.

**18.3.8**  This is harsh, bitter, hard like a hoof-paring, poisonous — not fit to eat; only that brahmin who knows Sūryā truly deserves to handle the bridal rites.

**18.3.9**  That brahmin carries away the soft, auspicious garment — he who studies the expiation rite by which the wife comes to no harm.

**18.3.10**  You two bring together abundant fortune; let them speak truth, roused by the truth; O Brahmaṇaspati, make this husband shine for her; and let the good provider speak this word beautifully.

**18.4.1**  Remain here; go not away — O cattle, multiply this one with offspring. Going beautifully, the ruddy cows radiant with soma-lustre: may all the gods fix your hearts here.

**18.4.2**  O cattle, enter this one together with your offspring; he does not diminish the share of the gods. Pūṣan and all the Maruts drive you for his sake; Dhātṛ and Savitṛ urge you on for him.

**18.4.3**  May the paths be thornless and straight by which our friends go to seek the bride for us. May Bhaga, may Aryaman, may Dhātṛ unite and send us forth with lustre.

**18.4.4**  With that by which the Aśvins anointed the hip of the great naked one, or with that of Sūrā — with that by which the dice were anointed — with that endow her with lustre.

**18.4.5**  Whatever lustre is laid in the dice, and whatever is placed in the Sūrā drink, whatever lustre is in cattle — O Aśvins, with that endow her with lustre.

**18.4.6**  He who shines without fuel in the midst of the waters, whom the inspired priests praise at sacrifices — O Son of Waters, grant the honey-sweet waters by which Indra, the mighty, grew strong.

**18.4.7**  This dazzling, body-blemishing seizer — I push it away. That which shines bright and auspicious in the flames — I raise that up.

**18.4.8**  May the brāhmaṇas bring hither the headdress for her; may the waters — not slayers of heroes — rise up. May she quickly go around Aryaman's fire; the father-in-law and brother-in-law await her.

**18.4.9**  Auspicious to you be the gold, and auspicious be the waters; auspicious be the union, auspicious the piercing of the yoke-pin. Auspicious to you be the waters purified a hundredfold; touch your body together with your husband in well-being.

**18.4.10**  Just as the bull has engendered the Sindhu's sovereignty over the rivers — so be you a sovereign queen, going home to your husband's house.

**18.5.1**  Be a sovereign queen among the fathers-in-law; be a sovereign queen over the mothers-in-law as well. Be a sovereign queen over the sisters-in-law; be a sovereign queen over the brothers-in-law too.

**18.5.2**  Those divine women who cut and wove, those who on both sides fixed the borders — may they clothe you to old age; put on this garment full of life.

**18.5.3**  They weep for a living man yet lead the ritual forth; the men have pondered the long procession onward. What boon they have stirred up here for the ancestors — that comfort be for husbands, an embrace for the bride.

**18.5.4**  A firm, gentle stone I hold for you for offspring, in the lap of divine Earth. Mount it, you who are praised and blessed with good heroes; may Savitṛ make your life long.

**18.5.5**  May the god Savitṛ take your hand; may king Soma make you rich in offspring. May Agni Jātavedas make the fortunate wife, together with her husband, live to old age.

**18.5.6**  I take your hand for good fortune, so that you may grow old with me as your husband. Bhaga, Aryaman, Savitṛ, Purandhi — the gods have given you to me for household mastery.

**18.5.7**  With that by which Agni took the right hand of this Earth — with that I take your hand; do not falter together with me.

**18.5.8**  Dhātṛ took your hand; Savitṛ took your hand. Bhaga took your hand; Aryaman took your hand. By sacred law you are wife — I am your householder.

**18.5.9**  May she be mine, my nurturer — Bṛhaspati has given you to me. Live together with me as husband, bearing offspring, for a hundred autumns.

**18.5.10**  Tvaṣṭṛ fashioned a garment for beauty, by the instruction of Bṛhaspati among the wise. With that, may Savitṛ and Bhaga clothe this woman for offspring, even as they clothed Sūryā.

**18.6.1**  May the houses enfold you with lustre and good fortune; let go of this luminous form — as Dawn in the sight of the sun.

**18.6.2**  Indra and Agni, Heaven and Earth, and Pūṣan, Mitra and Varuṇa, Bhaga, both Aśvins, Bṛhaspati, the Maruts, Brahman, and Soma — may they all increase this woman with offspring.

**18.6.3**  Bṛhaspati first arranged Sūryā's hair upon her head. With that we adorn this woman together with her husband, O Aśvins.

**18.6.4**  This is that form which the woman put on below — you wish to know the wife moving with her mind. I shall pursue her together with the Navagva companions; who, knowing this, has untied this snare?

**18.6.5**  I shall loose her form into myself — he who sees knows the nest of the mind. I do not consume a stolen thing; freed by the mind, loosening Varuṇa's noose of my own accord.

**18.6.6**  This Varuṇa's noose I loosen, with which Savitṛ the benevolent bound you. A wide world, an easy path — this I make for you, O bride, together with your husband.

**18.6.7**  Rise up; smite away the demon; place this woman in good fortune. Dhātṛ the wise has found the husband for her; may king Bhaga go before, knowing the way.

**18.6.8**  Bhaga has fashioned the four feet; Bhaga has fashioned the four lotuses. Tvaṣṭṛ has carved the leather thongs in the middle — may she be auspicious to us.

**18.6.9**  With this clarified butter as oblation — for offspring and the excellent, for cattle and for sight — let us kindle Agni fully together.

**18.6.10**  The bridal conveyance is fair with kiṃśuka flowers, all-formed, golden-hued, well-rolling, well-wheeled. Mount it, O Sūryā, into the world of immortality; make the bridal journey gentle for husbands.

**18.6.11**  Let not the post harm the girl on the god-made path. We make the divine gate of the house, and the bride's path, gentle.

**18.6.12**  Let brahman be yoked behind, brahman in front; brahman at the end, brahman in the middle, brahman on all sides. Reaching the god-city unassailed, benign and gentle, reign in the world of your husband.

**18.7.1**  For you they first led Sūryā forth together with the bridal procession; O Agni, now give back the bride to her husbands together with offspring.

**18.7.2**  Agni has given back the wife together with life and vital radiance; long-lived may be her husband — may he live a hundred autumns.

**18.7.3**  First Soma was your husband; then the Gandharva was your next lord; Agni was your third husband; the fourth is born of mortal man.

**18.7.4**  Soma gave her to the Gandharva; the Gandharva gave her to Agni; Agni gave both wealth and sons to me, and also gave this woman.

**18.7.5**  Good will has come to you two, O Vājinīvasus; O Aśvins, may desires find rest in your hearts. You two became guardians as a pair, as Lords of Welfare; may we attain the dear abodes of Aryaman.

**18.7.6**  Rejoicing with an auspicious mind, bestow wealth abundant in all heroes, worthy of praise; O Lords of Welfare, make easy the ford, well-accessible the watering-place; strike down evil thought from the most steadfast path.

**18.7.7**  Strike away the demons, strike away evil thought — O Lords of Welfare, having gone from here on this bridal journey; O Agni, knower of the field, going before the cows, conquering demons, thrust aside enemies from the front.

**18.7.8**  Whatever herbs, whatever rivers, whatever fields, whatever forests there are — let those guardians protect you, O bride rich in offspring, for your husband.

**18.7.9**  We have set forth on this easy path, bearing good fortune, where the hero suffers no harm and wins the wealth of others.

**18.7.10**  Hear this well from me, O men — by this blessing the master and mistress of the house attain happiness. Those Gandharvas and divine Apsarases who dwell today in these forest trees — may they be gentle to this bride; let them not harm the bridal procession as it is borne along.

**18.8.1**  Whatever diseases follow the people and accompany the gleaming bridal procession of the bride — may the gods worthy of sacrifice lead those diseases back to where they came from.

**18.8.2**  May those who roam about, who beset the master and mistress of the house, not find them; may they pass over what is hard to cross by an easy path; let the hostile ones run away.

**18.8.3**  I illuminate the bridal procession with sacred prayer, with the houses, with a non-terrible eye, with Mitra's gaze. Whatever is bound and all-formed in this one — may Savitā make that gentle for her husbands.

**18.8.4**  This woman has come home as an auspicious one; Dhātā has found this world for her. May Aryaman, Bhaga, both Aśvins, and Prajāpati increase her with offspring.

**18.8.5**  This woman has come to a fruitful field possessed of soul; O men, sow seed in this one. Bearing the mighty seed of the bull in her flanks, may she generate offspring for you.

**18.8.6**  Stand firm here like Virāj, like Viṣṇu, O Sarasvatī; may she spring forth like dūrvā grass for Indra; may she be in the good favor of Bhaga.

**18.8.7**  Let the wave beat on the yoke-pin; let the waters release the bonds. May the two blameless, sinless ones — not evildoers — approach prosperity.

**18.8.8**  With non-terrible eye, not harming the husband, gentle, prosperous, most pleasant, well-ruling in the houses — rich in offspring, bearing heroes, loving her husband's brothers — creep toward this householder's fire.

**18.8.9**  Not harming the husband's brothers, not harming the husband — be gentle, well-disposed toward the cattle, having good heroes; bearing heroes, loving the husband's brothers, gentle — may we prosper together with you being of good spirit.

**18.8.10**  Arise! Seeking what have you come here? I, the overpowering one, rebuke you — be gone from this house. O Nirṛti, seeker of the empty, you came here — arise, O Ill-will, fly away; do not linger here any longer.

**18.9.1**  When this bride has worshipped first the householder's fire, then, O woman, make obeisance to Sarasvatī and to the Fathers.

**18.9.2**  Bring this hide as a protective armor, to be spread beneath this woman; may she bring forth through Sinīvālī; may she be in the good favor of Bhaga.

**18.9.3**  Spread the balbaja grass upon the red skin; sitting down there, may she who has fine offspring worship this fire.

**18.9.4**  The balbaja grass that you lay down and the skin you spread beneath — upon that let her mount, she who has fine offspring, the maiden who wins a husband.

**18.9.5**  Mount the hide, sit near the fire — this god destroys all demons; O most auspicious one, sit near this fire as a joint wife; honor the gods here.

**18.9.6**  From this mother's lap may various forms of cattle be born into being; generate offspring here for this husband of yours; may this son of yours be excellent in seniority.

**18.9.7**  Most auspicious, the one who leads the household onward, most pleasant to the husband, a source of happiness to the father-in-law, gentle to the mother-in-law — may you enter these homes; may you be rich in offspring, living to old age.

**18.9.8**  This bride is most auspicious — come together and behold her; having given good fortune to her, depart with the misfortune.

**18.9.9**  Whatever young women of ill heart are here, and whatever old women too — they have indeed bestowed vital radiance upon her; now depart to your homes.

**18.9.10**  The bridal chariot spread with gold, bearing all forms — upon it Sūryā, daughter of Savitā, has mounted for great good fortune.

**18.10.1**  Ascend the bridal bed with glad spirit; beget offspring here for this husband. Awakening wisely like Indrāṇī, radiant and mighty, keep watch at dawn.

**18.10.2**  The gods at the first bestowed the wife upon him; they touched body with body together. Like the sun, O woman, all-formed in thy greatness, bearing offspring, be propitious to thy husband.

**18.10.3**  Rise up hence, for she is the wife of a husband; I entreat Viśvāvasu with reverence and songs. Seek a kinswoman who sits with the fathers and dwells in the dark — that is thy share by birth; know him as such.

**18.10.4**  The Apsarases who revel in common joy between the oblation-cart and the sun — go to them, to thy birthplace; Gandharva, I make obeisance to thee in due season.

**18.10.5**  We make obeisance to the mind of the Gandharva and to his wrath and to his eye. Viśvāvasu, I make obeisance to thee with prayer; pass beyond to the Apsarases and to the bride.

**18.10.6**  May we be well-minded with wealth; from here we have turned the Gandharva away. That god has gone to his highest abode; we have gone forward, extending our life-span.

**18.10.7**  Let the two parents unite in season; father and mother, be ye the source of seed. Let the young man cause the young woman to mount upon him; may you two make offspring here, rejoicing together.

**18.10.8**  May Prajāpati beget offspring for you two; may Aryaman anoint you both with day and night. She who is not ill-omened enters the husband's world; be thou auspicious to us among bipeds and quadrupeds.

**18.10.9**  O Pūṣan, impel for us that most auspicious one into whom men sow their seed, who spreads her thighs for us with desire, into whom, with desire, we would thrust the phallus.

**18.10.10**  Ascend her thigh, lay on thy hand, embrace thy wife with glad spirit. May you two beget offspring; may you flourish in wealth here; may Savitā grant long life to you both.

**18.11.1**  The bridal portion of the two brides that they gave us — the garment of the bride — that do you two, Bṛhaspati and Indra, grant together with favor to the brahmin.

**18.11.2**  Given by the gods together with beauty is this bridal garment of the bride. He who gives it to a discerning brahmin — he indeed smites the demons of the marriage-bed.

**18.11.3**  Awakening from the pleasant womb, laughing together, rejoicing in greatness — you two who have good cattle, good sons, a good home, may you cross over and live to see dawning mornings.

**18.11.4**  Wearing new garments, fragrant and well-clad, I have come forth alive to the dawning mornings. Like a bird from the egg I have freed myself from every sin.

**18.11.5**  O adorning Heaven-and-Earth, in whose realm is happiness, mighty in vow — may the seven flowing waters release us from distress.

**18.11.6**  To Sūryā, to the gods, to Mitra and to Varuṇa — to those who are the wise overseers of existence — to them I have made obeisance.

**18.11.7**  He who is shattered even apart from harm, torn before the collar-bones — the bounteous Indra, Purūravas, is the reuniter of joints, the restorer of what is wrenched asunder.

**18.11.8**  Let darkness depart away from us — whatever is dark blue, tawny, or red. The scorching, spotted she-demon — I release her onto this post.

**18.11.9**  As many sorceries as cling about us, as many nooses of King Varuṇa — those that harm growth and those that do not bring prosperity — all of them I release onto this post.

**18.11.10**  Whatever borders, whatever selvages there are, the warp-threads and the weft-threads — the garment woven by women: may that touch me pleasantly and bring me ease.

**18.13.1**  Whatever evil has been wrought by evildoers upon your offspring, your cattle, or what is established in your houses — may Agni and Savitṛ release you from that sin.

**18.13.2**  This woman speaks, scattering flower-blossoms: "May my husband have long life; may my fathers prosper."

**18.13.3**  O Indra, unite this couple here like a pair of cakravāka birds. Blessed with offspring, auspicious, may they enjoy long life.

**18.13.4**  Whatever sorcery was wrought upon the seat, the pillow, or the resting-place, whatever malefic rite was performed at the wedding bathing — that we now lay to rest.

**18.13.5**  Whatever misdeed, whatever defilement occurred at the wedding and in the wedding procession — all that trouble we wipe away upon the blanket of the saṃbhala.

**18.13.6**  Having deposited the impurity upon the saṃbhala, and the sin upon the blanket, we have become pure and fit for sacrifice; may they carry our life-spans forward.

**18.13.7**  That body of mine which is most dear to me is afraid of the garment. Do thou, O Lord of the forest, fashion her lower garment at the front — let us not suffer harm.

**18.13.8**  This is the crafted comb of the hundred-toothed kaṅkata tree. Let it scrape away the impurity of the hair, let it scratch away the impurity of the head.

**18.13.9**  From limb to limb, do you put down the disease away from her. Let it not reach the earth, nor the gods, nor heaven; let it not reach the wide atmosphere. Let it not reach the waters — this impurity, O Agni; let it not reach Yama and all the Fathers.

**18.13.10**  I bind you with the sap of the earth; I bind you with the sap of the plants; I bind you with offspring and with wealth. So bound and equipped, win this prize.

**18.14.1**  I am ama, thou art sā; I am heaven, thou art earth. I am mind, thou art speech. So let us two come together here and beget offspring.

**18.14.2**  May the foremost among us long for a wife; may the generous ones long for a son. Unharmed, may we follow together for the great winning of strength.

**18.14.3**  The Fathers who, beholding the bride, have come to this wedding procession — may they grant to this bride, the mistress of the household, prosperous shelter with offspring.

**18.14.4**  Those who have come before, stretching out the cord, having given offspring and wealth to her here — may they lead her along the path to what has not yet been reached. This virāj, rich in offspring, has conquered.

**18.14.5**  Awake fully, awaking with a good awakening, for long life, for a hundred autumns. Enter the houses with a well-disposed mind; may Savitṛ make your life long.

**18.14.6**  I release your cord, I release your reins, I release the yoke-straps and the fastening bands. Unharmed, in this light be auspicious to your husband.

**18.14.7**  Be gentle to your fathers-in-law; be gentle to your husband and his household. Be gentle to this entire clan; be gentle for the flourishing of these people.

**18.15.1**  Rise up, O Vājin who dwells within the waters; enter this kingdom filled with truth. May Rohita, who engendered all this universe, carry you well-borne to sovereignty.

**18.15.2**  The vāja who is within the waters has risen up; enter and ascend to your own sources. Bearing Soma, settle here the waters, the plants, the cattle, the four-footed and the two-footed.

**18.15.3**  You mighty Maruts, sons of Pṛśni, yoked with Indra, smite down the enemies. May Rohita hear you, O generous ones; the thrice-seven Maruts, full of sweet joy.

**18.15.4**  He climbed, Rohita climbed, he climbed fully — the embryo upon the lap of the mothers and of the births. Grasping those, he found the six wide spaces; looking ahead to a path, he has proclaimed this kingdom here.

**18.15.5**  Here Rohita has seized your kingdom; he has scattered your enemies; you have become fearless. For you, heaven and earth with their riches shall milk desire here with their mighty ones.

**18.15.6**  Rohita engendered heaven and earth; Parameṣṭhin stretched out that thread. There the unborn, the one-footed, took his seat; he firmly established heaven and earth with his power.

**18.15.7**  Rohita firmly established heaven and earth; by him the vault of heaven was propped up; by him the firmament was measured out and the realms spread; by him the gods found immortality.

**18.15.8**  Rohita touched all forms, gathering together both the sprouts and the climbers. Having climbed heaven with great greatness, may he anoint your kingdom with milk and ghee.

**18.15.9**  Your climbers, your sprouts, your ascenders, with which you fill heaven and the atmosphere — nourished by the sacred word and by milk, be wakeful in the clan, in the kingdom of Rohita.

**18.15.10**  Those clans of yours that were born from austerity, that followed the gāyatrī-calf — may they come here. May they enter you with an auspicious mind; the well-mothered calf Rohita comes toward you.

**18.16.1**  Rohita has stood erect above the vault of heaven, the youthful seer generating all forms. Agni blazes with sharp light; in the third wheel of the atmosphere the beloved things shine.

**18.16.2**  The thousand-horned bull, Jatavedas, fed with oblations of ghee, whose back is Soma, possessing good heroes — do not abandon me, I implore you, lest I abandon you. Bestow on me increase in cattle and increase in heroes.

**18.16.3**  Rohita is the begetter and the mouth of the sacrifice. With speech, hearing, and mind I offer oblation to Rohita. The gods go to Rohita with benevolent hearts. May he lift me up; going together, may he cause me to ascend.
