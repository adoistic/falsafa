**14.1.1**  Indra, knowing, carried the divine waters with both arms for Varuṇa; all the Ādityas consecrated him, that fierce king, for the great battle. May those waters, the rājasūya waters, be favorable to us.

**14.1.2**  [The four verses beginning with] 'Of golden hue' — [rubric indicating four verses of the Golden-Hued Waters hymn to be recited here].

**14.1.6**  The divine waters, honey-sweet, yielding strength, the rājasūya waters that bring well-being — those with which Mitra and Varuṇa were consecrated — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.1.7**  Those bright ones, sent forth by Varuṇa, move onward — divine waters that bear the golden spring; those that purify in concert with the sacred utterance — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.1.8**  The waters that carry from the ocean up to heaven, and from heaven release themselves upon the earth; those with which the sovereign Maruts roam — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.1.9**  Those that bear immortality, that carry sweet honey, those that the seers seized in the companionship of the gods; those by which Indra was led beyond his foes — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.1.10**  Those that fill wealth to the brim for the mortal who is devout and generous, who desires the gods; whose milk is inexhaustible among inexhaustible things — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.1**  Those that have golden ornaments, that bear banners, purifying themselves — the milk-cows that flow toward the ocean; those that Parjanya carries in the atmosphere — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.2**  When the Rathantara and the Bṛhat sāmans are sung, they invigorate lordly power and extend life; those by which the swift steeds are made to course in the three contests — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.3**  Those in whose company Indra the Vṛtra-slayer, lord of riches, drank the pressed Soma; those that the seven seers, the inspired poets, purify — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.4**  The seven seers consecrated Bharata; they established the realm in him, rich in sacrificial fees; the gods endowed Manu with vital breath for his offspring — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.5**  Those with which the wise ones sprinkle the eastward-facing sacrifice; those with which they purify the honey-mixed Soma; those by which all this stirring universe lives — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.6**  Those whose drops have become full of honey; those that swell with ghee-rich agreement and with sweetness; those waters that Soma leads forth by the sacred word — [may those waters be favorable to us].

**14.2.7**  Those at whose feet the strength-giving ghee flows; those that purify themselves in the wake of Soma's outpouring; pregnant ones, with young calves, rich in ghee — may those waters, the rājasūya waters, be favorable to us.

**14.2.8**  Inspired thoughts were born; those that know the way to heaven came forward through the sacred word to the places of the well-spoken hymns. The rājasūya waters gave forth their streams of milk; the god Savitṛ impelled all the worlds.

**14.2.9**  Soma the king, Bhava the king, Paśupati lord of beasts, Varuṇa the steadfast in vow — those gods who were present at the rājasūya, let them bestow upon you sovereign power, long life, and might.

**14.2.10**  He stood up, he rose, the inspired sage was born; he stepped over his foes, he wielded his arms in battle; he thrust Nirṛti far away toward the distant quarters; and with the sun he mingled, with its rays.

**14.3.1**  I beheld you descending, as if from heaven to earth; I beheld Rudra, the blue-throated, the crest-bearing, as he cast his shaft.

**14.3.2**  The dreadful one climbed down from heaven and stood established upon the earth. O people, behold this one — the blue-throated, the deeply crimson.

**14.3.3**  This Rudra, the healer with cooling medicines, the destroyer of no man's hero, comes hither. He has made your trembling to vanish utterly; may your feverish agitation depart.

**14.3.4**  Homage to you, O Bhava, in your brilliance; homage to you, O Bhava, in your wrath. Homage to you be unto both your arms, and homage also to your arrow.

**14.3.5**  That arrow which you hold in your hand to shoot, O lord of the mountain — make it auspicious, O Giriśa; do not harm my people.

**14.3.6**  With auspicious speech we address you, O Giriśa; so that for us all this world may be free of consumption and of glad spirit.

**14.3.7**  Your arrow that is most auspicious, your bow that has become auspicious, your quiver that is auspicious — with that grant us ease, that we may live.

**14.3.8**  That form of yours, O Rudra, which is auspicious, which is not terrible, which does not reveal evil — with that most tranquil body, O Giriśa, shine upon us.

**14.3.9**  That one yonder who is copper-red, tawny, and deeply crimson — and these Rudras here on all sides, stationed in the directions by thousands — from their wrath we seek protection.

**14.3.10**  I beheld you descending, the blue-throated, the deeply crimson; the herdsmen too beheld you, and so did the women fetching water; all beings too beheld you — homage to you, to you thus seen.

**14.4.1**  Homage be to the blue-crested one, the thousand-eyed, the swift; and likewise to his host of attendants — to them too I have rendered homage.

**14.4.2**  Homage to your weapon, to the bow unstrung, to the bold one; homage I have rendered to both your arms, and to your bow.

**14.4.3**  Release the bowstring from both tips of the bow; and the arrows that are in your hand — scatter those away, O blessed one.

**14.4.4**  Having unstrung the bow, O thousand-eyed, O hundred-quivered one, shattering the heads of the arrow-shafts — come to us as benign, as bringer of joy.

**14.4.5**  The bow of the crested one is without its string, and he is free of arrows, quiverless; his arrows are without venom, benign is his quiver.

**14.4.6**  May the missile of your bow encompass us on all sides as a protection; and your quiver — place it far off from this one.

**14.4.7**  The bolt that was in your hand, the bow that was yours, O most bountiful — with that weapon guard us on all sides, keeping us free from disease.

**14.5.1**  The well-flanked, wish-yielding hundred-cooked-rice-offering has come to us, swelling with milk; milking forth vital strength, without stumbling, it prolongs the life of the sacrificer.

**14.5.2**  I seize her who is fit for sacrifice, who is eager, who brings well-being, who is replete with vitality, free from disease, worthy of meditation; may my whole world be among the gods — believing, I cook the hundred-rice-portion.

**14.5.3**  Bind and apply the goddess, the muñja-grass-girt hundred-rice-offering — for she is the wish-yielding one; do not injure her with the stone in your exuberance: she passes to the gods beyond the enemy.

**14.5.4**  Seize with the sacred formula this all-divine hundred-rice-offering — a hundred sins are hers to absorb; pressing with the stone as with a mountain, cause him to ascend above to the heavenly world.

**14.5.5**  Boldly cutting and slicing it into pieces, driving away evil, mix it with the skin-ladle; anointed by means of this daughter of Virāj, may she milk desire upon desire for the sacrificer.

**14.5.6**  Even as you cut joint by joint, do not tarry; make the portions for me separately; she who formerly was human, the hundred-rice-offering of many forms and golden hue, has gone to the gods.

**14.5.7**  The two-footed, two-handed man, the great subduer, bears the tree-piece with its knife-tip at the front; with it, skilled, he shall carve apart the joints of the inviolable one — sunder the divine hundred-rice-offering into a hundred parts.

**14.5.8**  Cut this reddish piece from the skin well, and having measured it, divide it into a hundred parts; releasing all the portions together, bestow abundance of wealth upon the sacrificer.

**14.5.9**  With the Ṛc-verse I place the pot upon the fire; I set you upon the earth, above the earth; bearing the flesh in the waters, do not tremble; may the Piśācas not slay you with their weapons.

**14.5.10**  Rise upward, do not collapse; spread out the inner atmosphere; having crossed beyond all the Rākṣasas — then ascend to heaven.

**14.6.1**  Go to heaven, O hundred-rice-offering; become the path for a thousand; become ten thousand, become a hundred thousand — be inexhaustible: thus you shall mount the heavenly world.

**14.6.2**  They partake of the hundred-rice-offering in a hundred ways; he who gives her ascends a hundred rungs; the Vāmadevya and the Naudhasa are her two wings — established on the third vault, she abides above the firmament.

**14.6.3**  The hundred-rice-offering, arranging itself in a hundred ways, fashions a hundred forms as she goes to the sky; may that goddess, easy to invoke, grant us shelter, and likewise grant ease to those in such condition.

**14.6.4**  The hundred-rice-offering, measured out with the twelve-day rite, has created a hundred Pṛṣṭha-hymns as she traverses the sky; encompassing all sacrifices beyond, may she establish the giver in abundance of wealth.

**14.6.5**  Go toward the soma-pressing sacrificers who have offered oblations, O hundred-rice-offering; having pervaded all sacrifices, become the pathway to the fulfillment of desire.

**14.6.6**  Go forward, O hundred-rice-offering; with sacrifice pass beyond those who have not sacrificed; you have climbed up to the gods, to where you shine on high in the triple heaven.

**14.6.7**  The first hundred-rice-offering has the nourishment-footed one as its base; truth is indeed your middle, immortality is your crown; O goddess, you fill both the worlds.

**14.6.8**  Order indeed are the hips, and the thighs in the body; power indeed are the arms, and that serves for strength; the belly follows the due course, and further the flanks — the hundred-rice-offering has reached all the worlds.

**14.6.9**  The first hundred-rice-offering is endowed with sight; the sun is indeed your eye, and the moon also; in accord with all the gods and with the seasons, you, O goddess, shall satiate the giver with satisfaction.

**14.6.10**  A hundred are her milkings, a hundred her calves; they distribute her, cooked, in a hundred shares; the seven worlds established in heaven — these you have won, O first of the hundred-rice-offerings.

**14.7.1**  Measured in hundreds, surpassing a thousand, as you go forth you have conquered the worlds, foremost O Śataudanā. She has attained the Sarvavedasa rite and the Vājapeya; all worlds the Śataudanā has encompassed.

**14.7.2**  Indra partook of the Śataudanā first, graciously disposed toward the seven ṛṣis; by her he seized the strength and vigor of the asuras, by her he ascended the heights of the celestial worlds.

**14.7.3**  Here Viśvāmitra, Jamadagni, Atri, Bharadvāja, Gotama, and here Vasiṣṭha — these with Kaśyapa as the seventh, let them partake first of this cooked Śataudanā.

**14.7.4**  Luminous is the foremost Śataudanā; proceeding toward the heavenly light she fashions the three luminaries. Her givers live in prosperity; wherever they are, they rejoice in common festivity with the gods.

**14.7.5**  The great Śataudanā, whose wings are the meters, going toward heaven has brought forth the three chants. They have found the year, the overlord of seasons, as her lord.

**14.7.6**  Surpassing the Agniṣṭoma and the Atirātra, O Śataudanā — having attained all the sacrifices, spread wide through the fire-altar built in layers.

**14.7.7**  Surpassing what belongs to the Aśvamedha and the Dvādaśāha, O Śataudanā — having attained the three-night rite with the press-day, she equals the Vājapeya.

**14.7.8**  Those who have sacrificed with thousands of offerings, those who have presented oblations in the Agnihotra, those who have sacrificed with all sacrifices — all of these the Śataudanā attains.

**14.7.9**  Auspicious I make your joints, O blessed one; auspicious too are the portions of flesh that are consumed. Whatever bone the sacrificial slaughterer has broken — that which is disjointed in you, let Tvaṣṭṛ restore.

**14.7.10**  Whatever worlds have been won by sacrifice, whatever meters have been obtained — all those worlds does he attain who gives the Śataudanā.

**14.8.1**  Vibrant, a maiden, youthful, dwelling within the house — Night belonging to the divine Savitṛ and Bhaga; unharmed as a horse, easy to invoke, her splendor gathered, she has filled heaven and earth with her greatness.

**14.8.2**  Deep, she has descended over all things; tireless, she has ascended to the highest peak. Eager Night spreads out along the benign ridge, like Mitra with her own powers.

**14.8.3**  O Night, worthy of heroes, deserving of praise, blessed, nobly born — come hither; be well-disposed; may I be here with cheerful mind. Protect us with the riches born of heroes, the ample wealth in horses and in cattle.

**14.8.4**  Eager Night, you have assumed the splendor of the lion, of the piśāca, of the tiger, of the leopard; the redness of the horse, the wizardry of man — shining abroad, you take on many forms.

**14.8.5**  Propitious Night and Sun by day — the mother of frost, easy to invoke, be good to us. O blessed one, heed this hymn of praise whereby I honor you in all directions.

**14.8.6**  O Night of brilliant radiance, take pleasure in our praise as a king takes pleasure. May we be possessed of all heroes, may we be possessed of all wealth, as you shine forth continuously, one Dawn after another.

**14.8.7**  You indeed bear the name Śamyā; those who would despoil my wealth — here let Night drive those away; then shall no thief be found, then shall no enemy be found.

**14.8.8**  You are auspicious, O Night, like a pressed cup; youthful, you uphold all that is of the form of the cow. Seeing with full eyes, touching my forms, you have released the divine golden ornaments belonging to the Ādityas.

**14.8.9**  Whosoever today comes as a thief, a mortal of sinful life, an enemy — encountering him, may Night strike off his throat, strike off his head.

**14.8.10**  His feet go not forward as they go, his hands do not reach as they stretch. Whosoever approaches in filth — crushed, he departs. He departs indeed, he departs; he has departed onto the dry stump.

**14.9.1**  O Night, make the serpent blind, motionless as smoke, headless. Strike out the eyes of the wolf; strike the thief into the wooden trap.

**14.9.2**  O Night, whatever bullocks of yours are sharp-horned and swift-footed — with them carry us across today, ever carrying us beyond all difficult passages.

**14.9.3**  Night after night, passing through unharmed in body, may we cross over; may our enemies not cross, as those who cannot swim through the deep.

**14.9.4**  As a millet grain falling down, cast upward, is found no more — even so, O Night, cast down the one who plots evil against us.

**14.9.5**  Drive away the thief, the plunderer of dwelling, the cattle-stealer, and the robber; and also him who, having tied a rope about a horse's head, would lead it off.

**14.9.6**  Whatever iron-rich wealth they distribute today, O Night of great blessing — come, make us resplendent with it; thereafter you approach others.

**14.9.7**  Hand us over to Dawn entirely guiltless, O Night. May Dawn give us a share of the day; the day belongs to you, O Night of brilliant radiance.
