**2.1.1**  Powerless is the poison from the east, powerless what comes from the north; and this here from below is dissolved by the grain-gruel karamba.

**2.1.2**  Having placed the karamba grain-gruel crosswise — full-fatted and excellently conveyed — hunger, they say, has devoured you, O malicious one; let it not wound.

**2.1.3**  Your intoxication, O intoxicating one, we cause to fall like an arrow shot; you who surge forward like a boiling cauldron, we bring to a standstill by our utterance.

**2.1.4**  As one heaps up around a village, so we heap up around you; stand firm as a tree in its standing-place — in the pit dug by the spade let it not wound.

**2.1.5**  They have bought you round about with pavasta-grain and with hides of distant aspect; you are a herb of wide purchase — in the pit dug by the spade let it not wound.

**2.2.1**  Invoking Heaven-and-Earth, invoking Bhaga and the two Aśvins, invoking Bṛhaspati, I render the poison powerless.

**2.2.2**  Verily powerless shall this poison be, even as I have wished it to be; moreover, let a person eat of it — he shall be free from disease again.

**2.2.3**  Fear not — you shall not die; I protect you on every side. I have not known the potency of the poison, as one eating the foam of water.

**2.2.4**  The avertive speaker, the first divine physician, has spoken away — even before Indra's eyes, like cows — those words that are the bane of poison.

**2.2.5**  Whatever has been ground, whatever unground, whatever has been smeared, whatever may be smeared — O Gods, knowing all things, render the poison powerless.

**2.3.1**  From hereditary disease, from Nirṛti, from the calumny of kinsmen, from treachery, from the noose of Varuṇa I release you. Sinless I make you by sacred utterance; may Heaven-and-Earth be propitious to you.

**2.3.2**  Auspicious to you be Agni together with the waters; auspicious the cows together with the herbs; auspicious to you be the atmosphere together with the wind; auspicious to you be the four cardinal directions.

**2.3.3**  The four divine cardinal directions, whose consort is the Wind, upon which the Sun looks forth — in them I place this one unto old age; let the yakṣma-disease go forth, let Nirṛti depart into the beyond.

**2.3.4**  Just as the Gods, releasing the Sun, the embodiment of cosmic order, from the grip of darkness, sent it forth free of sin — so from hereditary disease, from Nirṛti, from the calumny of kinsmen, from treachery, from the noose of Varuṇa I release you.

**2.3.5**  Released is he from yakṣma-disease, from misfortune, from disgrace, from the noose of treachery — and also released from the grip of demonic seizure. Casting off ill fortune he has found ease; he has entered the blessed world of those who have done good.

**2.4.1**  The houseless, the bold, the clever one, the solitary-speaker, the devouring one — we destroy all the granddaughters of Caṇḍa, the perpetually pursuing ones (sadānvāḥ).

**2.4.2**  Whether you have arisen from hereditary disease (kṣetriya), or whether you have been sent by a man, or whether you were born from the Dasyus — perish hence, O perpetually pursuing ones (sadānvāḥ)!

**2.4.3**  I have swiftly encircled their abodes, as one drives cattle to the pen. I have conquered all your contests — perish hence, O perpetually pursuing ones!

**2.4.4**  We drive you out from the cow-pen, out from the womb, out from the cart; we chase you away, O daughters of Magundī, from the houses.

**2.4.5**  In that lower house over there, let all the miserly demons (arāyyaḥ) dwell; let sin be poured out there, and all the sorcerer-women (yātudhānyaḥ) as well.

**2.5.1**  Heaven and Earth, the broad mid-space, the mistress of the field, the wide-ranging, wondrous one — and the broad atmosphere guarded by the wind — let these all burn with affliction for me, even as I am burning.

**2.5.2**  Hear this, O Indra, drinker of Soma — I call upon you repeatedly with a burning heart: I shall hew down like a tree with an axe him who harms this mind of ours.

**2.5.3**  Hear this, O Gods, you who are worthy of sacrifice — let Bharadvāja recite the praises for me: let him who harms this mind of ours be bound in a snare and fastened into misfortune.

**2.5.4**  With the eighty-three chanters of Sāmans, with the Ādityas, the Vasus, and the Aṅgirases, may the merit of our fathers' sacrificial offerings and pious works protect us — I seize that man with divine consuming fire.

**2.5.5**  May Heaven and Earth shine after me; may all the Gods take hold of me. May the Aṅgirasas, the Soma-drinking ancestral fathers, let evil befall him who is the author of malicious will.

**2.5.6**  He who thinks too little of our sacred word, O Maruts, or who would revile the brahman being performed — let burning afflictions and crooked evils be his lot; let the sky burn down upon that hater of the brahman.

**2.5.7**  I lay your footstep upon the kindled Jātavedas (Fire who knows all beings); may Agni envelope your body; may your life-breath go to Yama.

**2.5.8**  The seven breaths and the eight marrows — these I hew asunder from you by the sacred word. Go to the dwelling of Yama, made ready as the messenger of Agni.

**2.6.1**  The seer (Vena) beheld that supreme footstep where the All becomes a single nest. There the milch-cow yielded forth this — the hosts who know the heavenly world sang after, as it was being born.

**2.6.2**  The Gandharva, though not knowing the immortal, proclaimed that supreme abode which lies concealed in the cave. Three footsteps are set down in the secret place; he who knows them becomes the father of the father.

**2.6.3**  He is our kinsman, he is the progenitor, he is the sustainer; he knows all the abodes, all the worlds. There where the Gods, having reached immortality, were set in motion in the common dwelling.

**2.6.4**  I have come around all the worlds; I stand attendant upon the first-born of cosmic order (ṛta). Like speech in a speaker, he who dwells in the worlds and is the nourisher — verily, this is Agni.

**2.6.5**  I have come around Heaven and Earth straightway, to behold the stretched-out thread of cosmic order (ṛta). The god, guarding his divine nature, the One asked after the common bond.

**2.7.1**  O Indra, be pleased and come, O hero; drink of the pressed honey-mead, delighting in it — charming for exhilaration.

**2.7.2**  Let the pressed Soma-draughts enter into you, O Indra; fill your belly, strengthen yourself, O Śakra — come hither to our devotions.

**2.7.3**  O Indra, fill your belly with this pressed honey-mead; may vigour attend you in your states of rapture.

**2.7.4**  Indra the irresistible conqueror smote Vṛtra; the wielder of the vajra overcame enemies in battles — in the rapture of Soma.

**2.7.5**  Hear my invocation, be pleased with my hymns, O Indra; exhilarate yourself with your own teams for rapture — for great battle.

**2.8.1**  Up have stepped these three: the tiger, the man, the wolf. The light is turned away, the sun is turned away, the god Vanaspati is turned away — may the enemies be turned away.

**2.8.2**  Let the wolf rush away by the farthest path, let the thief rush away by the far path; the tiger is the most distant of all.

**2.8.3**  Both your eyes and your jaws, O tiger, we crush; and therewith all your twenty claws.

**2.8.4**  Whatever has come together is scattered, whatever has scattered does not come together again — blunted are the beast's teeth, and its ribs are shattered.

**2.8.5**  We crush first the tiger that tears with its teeth; and therewith the thief, the serpent, the sorcerer, and also the wolf.

**2.8.6**  Do not seize upon, do not enter the house; go far away. Do not harm our bipeds and our quadrupeds — you are born of Indra, born of Soma.

**2.9.1**  This herb, honey-born, we dig up for honey's sake; you have sprung forth from honey — make us rich in honey.

**2.9.2**  At the tip of my tongue be honey, at the root of my tongue a honey-sweet — so that you may be impassioned for me, so that you may come after me.

**2.9.3**  I have come around you with the encircling sugarcane, for freedom from enmity — so that we two may never be hostile to each other, may never part from each other at any time.

**2.9.4**  Speak to King Varuṇa, and to the horse, and to the man — by the prosperous, auspicious path, O Pathyā Revatī, bring the wife to me by a good road.

**2.9.5**  May Mitra and Varuṇa grant me a wife; may the goddess Sarasvatī grant me a wife; may both the Aśvins, garlanded with lotuses, bestow a wife upon me.

**2.10.1**  O ten-tree, release this one from distress and from the seizure-demon! And do thou, O lord of the forest, lead him up into the world of the living.

**2.10.2**  He who made it shall undo it — he alone is the most skillful physician; he it is who has made the remedy for thee, and who practises healing.

**2.10.3**  The gods, the brahman-sages, and the plants have found thy cure; they have found today thy highest cure upon the earth.

**2.10.4**  He has come, he has risen up, he has rejoined the company of the living; he has become once more the father of his sons, the most blessed one among men.

**2.10.5**  This one has passed beyond affliction; he has gone to the citadel of the living — he whose plants number a hundred and whose remedies number a thousand.

**2.11.1**  For long life, for great exhilaration — ever unharmed and thriving — we wear the amulet jaṅgiḍa, the destroyer of viṣkandha.

**2.11.2**  From the crusher, from the bursting-pain, from viṣkandha, from tormenting affliction — may the amulet jaṅgiḍa, thousand-powered, guard us on all sides.

**2.11.3**  This one overpowers viṣkandha; this one drives away the rakṣas; may this jaṅgiḍa, the all-healer, protect us from distress.

**2.11.4**  With the amulet jaṅgiḍa given by the gods, bestower of bliss, we overpower viṣkandha and all the rakṣas-demons in combat.

**2.11.5**  May śaṇa and jaṅgiḍa both release thee from viṣkandha — one brought from the forest, the other from the cultivated field, from their essences.

**2.12.1**  Let the cattle that have strayed hence come hither — cattle whose companionship Vāyu has favored, whose forms Tvaṣṭṛ knows well; may Savitṛ settle them in this cattle-pen.

**2.12.2**  Let the cattle stream together into this pen; may Bṛhaspati lead them hither in his wisdom; may Sinīvālī lead them at their head, and may Anumati settle those that have returned.

**2.12.3**  Let the cattle stream together, let the horses and men stream together; let the prosperity of the grain come together — with the oblation that causes confluence I offer.

**2.12.4**  I pour together the milk of the cattle; I pour together strength and essence with ghee; united are our heroes in me and the cattle in their herdsman.

**2.12.5**  I bring hither the milk of the cattle; I have brought hither the essence of grain; I have brought hither our heroes, and the wife — this is the oblation.

**2.13.1**  May Aryamā unite you together, may Pūṣan unite you, may Bṛhaspati unite you; may Indra, the winner of wealth, unite you — flourish here, whatever wealth there is.

**2.13.2**  Come here, O cows, come right here; thrive here like mushrooms growing where they stand. Multiply right here; let there be concord with me among you.

**2.13.3**  Stay together with me, your herdsman; this cattle-pen of yours shall nourish you here. Becoming plentiful through the abundance of wealth, alive and thriving, may we sit down beside you.

**2.13.4**  We unite you with your well-seated cattle-pen, with riches, with excellent nourishment. By the name of the one born this day we unite you together.

**2.13.5**  In mutual understanding, undispersed, laden with dung-fuel in this cattle-pen, bearing the oblation dear to Soma — enter, each into your own stall.

**2.14.1**  As the Āditya rises, may he slay the worms; as the sun sets, may he slay with his rays the worms that are within the cow.

**2.14.2**  The worm that is all-formed, four-eyed, the spotted one, the white one — slain is that worm, slain-brothered, slain-mothered, slain-sistered.

**2.14.3**  Slain is the king of the worms, and slain is their chief; slain are his neighbours, slain are his neighbours round about.

**2.14.4**  I break off your two horns with which you gore; I also shatter that vessel in which your poison has been placed.

**2.14.5**  As Atri did, I smite you, O worm; as Kaṇva did, as Jamadagni did. By the sacred formulation of Agastya all your worms are slain.

**2.15.1**  That great grinding-stone of Indra, crusher of every worm — with it I grind up the worms with the millstone, as grain in a hand-mill.

**2.15.2**  The seen and the unseen I have crushed; I have also crushed the kurūru. The algaṇḍu-worms, all the śalūla-worms — with the sacred word we crush the worms.

**2.15.3**  I smite the algaṇḍu-worms with a great blow; the afflicted and the unafflicted have become devoid of essence. Those remaining and those not yet reached I overwhelm with the sacred word, so that not one of the worms shall survive.

**2.15.4**  The intestinal worm, the head worm, and the worm of the ribs, the avaskava, the vyadhvara — with the sacred word we crush the worms.

**2.15.5**  The worms that are in the mountains, those that are in the forests, those in the plants, within cattle, within the waters — those that have made our body their abode, may Indra smite them with a great blow.

**2.16.1**  O herb, thou who art the over-conquering victor that has crushed enemies — strike down these same ones in counter-attack, rendering them powerless.

**2.16.2**  The eagle Suparṇa tracked thee out; the boar dug thee up with his snout; Indra placed thee in his arms to spread thee against the Asuras.

**2.16.3**  Indra devoured the pāṭā plant in order to slay the Asuras; with that same herb may I overcome my enemies, as Indra overcame the hyenas.

**2.16.4**  O Rudra, healer with cooling medicine, blue-crested, active doer — strike down the hostile inquiry of him who assails us.

**2.16.5**  Strike down the hostile inquiry of him who assails us with Indra's might; speak over us with thy powers; consume him, and make me the superior.

**2.17.1**  As the wind churns the grass upon the earth, so do I churn thy mind, so that thou shalt be in love with me, so that thou shalt follow after me.

**2.17.2**  She has come here yearning for a husband; I have come yearning for a wife. As a neighing stallion, so have I come together with her through the grace of Bhaga.

**2.17.3**  O Aśvins, ye have brought together these two who are in love, and ye shall lead them together; may your minds come together, your eyes come together, and your vows come together.

**2.17.4**  What is within, let that be without; what is without, let that be within — O herb, seize the mind of the maidens of all forms.

**2.17.5**  [Uncertain text: she of the eagle-winged — she without wings —] here thy mind is fixed, as an arrow transfixes a young bud.

**2.18.1**  The fiery splendor that is in the lion, in the tiger, and in the pṛdāku serpent, that is in fire, in the brahmin, and in the sun — the auspicious goddess by whom Indra was augmented: may she come to us, united with radiant glory.

**2.18.2**  The fiery splendor that is in the elephant, in the leopard, and in gold, that is in horses, in men, and in cattle — [may she come to us, united with radiant glory].

**2.18.3**  The fiery splendor that is in the warrior-noble, in the battle-drum when it is stretched, in an army on the march, in the thunder, and in the war-cry — [may she come to us, united with radiant glory].

**2.18.4**  In the chariot, in the axles, in the swift charge of the bull, in Parjanya, in the wind, in the might of Varuṇa — the auspicious goddess by whom Indra was augmented [may she come to us, united with radiant glory].

**2.18.5**  The fiery splendor that is among the Rudras, that is among the Vasus, among the Ādityas, among the Maruts — the splendor that is among all the gods: may she come to us, united with radiant glory.

**2.19.1**  Whether on the fordable or the navigable side of the rivers our lord and ally abides — may all the gods protect him, so that he, living, may speak in the assembly.

**2.19.2**  Whether on the hither bank or indeed on the yonder bank, or on the desert plain, our lord and ally abides — [may all the gods protect him, so that he, living, may speak in the assembly].

**2.19.3**  Whether in the contest, whether in the endurance-trial, whether in the assembly our lord and ally abides — [may all the gods protect him, so that he, living, may speak in the assembly].

**2.19.4**  May they fall down, may they become inferior, those who battle against our patron, the munificent lord — may all the gods protect him, so that he, living, may speak in the assembly.

**2.19.5**  O Yama, O Death, do not harm him whom I have guarded with heart and mind — he who carries me across and whom I carry across, may he be rich in offspring, munificent, a lord.

**2.20.1**  Board this ship, the one without breach, the one capable of ferrying across — she who stands in the house of Narāśaṃsa and of Bhaga, furnished with a hundred oars.

**2.20.2**  Fumigate with bdellium — may the kine be free of consumption and unharmed; with the arrows of Rudra strike down the sorcerers, and those of King Bhava as well.

**2.20.3**  The two Rudras hold sway over bipeds and quadrupeds — may we stand under their patronage. Let them be honoured with ripe viṭṭha-grains; may we be in the good favour of the gods.

**2.20.4**  Pratīcī is your mother's name; Śatavāra indeed is your father's name. From them were you born, O Arundhatī, causing no harm.

**2.20.5**  You are named 'Mother' from your very nature as mother; you are as it were the very essence of immortality. O Arundhatī, you encompass all life — and this too.

**2.21.1**  O Agni, may propitious thought come to Śambhala; bring this maiden together with our portion. Pleasing in the choice assemblies, fair of speech — may she soon become united in fortune with a husband.

**2.21.2**  O Agni, this woman seeks a husband; may King Soma make her well-favoured. Bearing sons, may she become a queen; having gone to a husband, may she, the fortunate one, reign resplendent.

**2.21.3**  Fortune, cherished by Soma, cherished by Brahman, assembled by Aryaman — by the truth of the god Dhātṛ̥ I bring about the finding of a husband.

**2.21.4**  Just as the donkey, this fair and munificent one, has become beloved of the does, pleasant of abode — so may this one, cherished and mutual-beloved, be of Bhaga, pleasing her husband without obstruction.

**2.21.5**  Board the ship of Bhaga, the full one, the one that moves without stumbling; sail forth by her to the husband who is to be desired in return.

**2.21.6**  This gold, this bdellium, this bull-offering, and this Bhaga — these they have given you for your husbands, for the knowing of one who is desired in return.

**2.22.1**  Yoke the plows, stretch wide the yokes; sow the seed here in the prepared field. May the grace of Virāj be fruitful for us, and may the sickle come ever nearer to the ripened barley.

**2.22.2**  The wise ones yoke the plows, the steadfast ones stretch the yokes apart, seeking the favor of the gods. The bullocks and the men who plow — the plow and the share I anoint with the sphātī-plant for prosperity.

**2.22.3**  May the plowman go behind the draught animals with good fortune; may the share go breaking into the earth with good fortune. Whoever sacrifices to Śunā and Sīra with oblation — may the herbs be well-berried for him.

**2.22.4**  May the men go auspiciously with plow and bullocks; Bhaga with the plowshares, the Lord of the Field with the Maruts. May Parjanya stimulate and impel this seed; O Śunā and Sīra, make the grain plentiful here.

**2.22.5**  May Indra take hold of the furrow; may Pūṣan guard her. May she yield milk for us, year after year surpassing the last.

**2.22.6**  He has arisen — winner of chariots, winner of cattle, winner of horses, winner of gold — encompassed by glad speech. With his single-wheeled chariot, Savitṛ traverses the earth, filling it with portions of vital nourishment.

**2.23.1**  Having apportioned in succession the essence of cattle, of houses, of herbs — the luster and the life-span — do not harm my parents as you grow; proceed from the auspicious world toward a yet more excellent one.

**2.23.2**  Whether this field is undivided or divided, whether it belongs to the gods or to the ancestors, whether at sunrise or in human fashion — may the earth be benign to us, and heaven too.

**2.23.3**  Your share is nourishment, O divine pair of doors, the choicest part of the earth; I uphold you both with the sacred word. May our resting-place be auspicious and propitious, the gift granted by the gods, the fathers, and human beings.

**2.23.4**  The Gandharvas, the wise ones, told me of this abode of Viśvāvasu, this nest. Do not harm this treasure which we hereby entrust to you — a human being — for a hundred winters.

**2.23.5**  At the ever-inexhaustible spring, the Rudras rejoice, gods and fathers and human beings. This is the share, and the Lord of Shares is present; O gods, may we cross over the wide, unending autumns.

**2.25.1**  Those Ādityas who are Agni-led and Indra-powered, who know battles — established as foremost in battle, the priests pour oblations for victory.

**2.25.4**  By the weapon of the great copper-hued one, by the weapon of Śarva the warrior; by the Gandharva full of fiery brilliance, on his chariot that fights without an equal.

**2.25.5**  Sinīvālī and Anumati, bearing swift horses, quiver-bearing — conquering, they advance against the enemies together with Indra the ally.

**2.26.1**  Whatever you have struck down in a dream, or whatever falsehood you have uttered — may Agni, may Brahmā release you from that transgression, from that sin.

**2.26.2**  Whatever treachery you have committed at dice, or whatever you have done against friends — may Soma, may Brahmā release you from that transgression, from that sin.

**2.26.3**  What as a youth you have uttered among youths, or what among elders; what you have spoken having made a wager — that we make auspicious and free of disease.

**2.26.4**  For you, O Apāmārga, have become backward-fruited; drive away from me all the curses, driving them to something broader.

**2.26.5**  The Apāmārga plant is truly the sole lord of all herbs; with it we wipe away what has settled upon you — now move about free of disease.
