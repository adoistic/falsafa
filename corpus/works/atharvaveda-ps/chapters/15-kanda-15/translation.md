**15.1.1**  From all the quarters it blows together, resonant with heaven; from thence may Mādhava protect us with sweetness. May Agni the god, hard to cross, undeceivable, guard this sovereign power and protect us.

**15.1.2**  May the Rathantara protect us with its sāman-chants, all-formed with the Gāyatrī metre. By the twelve-day sacrifice the praise-hymn of the days — may the ocean of speech fill this might.

**15.1.3**  Fierce the conquest of the quarters, sustainer of life, pure on the bright day of might. With Indra as overlord, may he fill us from thence; may he uphold in me this sovereign power from all sides.

**15.1.4**  The great kingship, bearing the warrior-power, with grown virility, adorned with the strength of the Triṣṭubh, of fierce heroism — may Indra guard this lustre with praises through the fifteenfold, together with the wind and the ocean.

**15.1.5**  The eastern quarter, sharing in glory, itself glorious; all the gods, the luminous one on the days of the rainy season — let this sovereign power be hard to cross, its might unconquered, overpowering, one that overpowers.

**15.1.6**  In the Vairūpa sāman I declare this — may I be able; with the Jagatī metre I make it enter among the clans. All the gods by the seventeenfold — this fierce sovereign power, accompanied by flood-waters.

**15.1.7**  May the supporting quarter uphold this sovereign power; among those who hope for refuge may the might be as Mitra. May Mitra and Varuṇa, discerning on the autumnal days, grant great shelter to this sovereign power.

**15.1.8**  In the Vairāja sāman rests my wisdom; the virile force and might gathered through the Anuṣṭubh. May Mitra and Varuṇa protect this sovereign power, Mitra-like, with moist-giving strength, unto overlordship.

**15.1.9**  The universal sovereign of the quarters, the powerful one whose sāmans are of might — may the season of winter fill us through its extension. May the Bṛhatī and Śakvarī, born from healing, and the ghee-bright one protect this sacrifice of ours.

**15.1.10**  The heavenly one of good milking, rich in milk for us — may the divine goddess of the quarters, the ghee-bright one, protect us. You are guardian before and behind; O Bṛhaspati, yoke the southward speech.

**15.2.1**  O mistress of existence in the twenty-one-fold praise, in the wind pervading all — sing out for us. May the direction rich in ghee, O Savitṛ, unto overlordship; the direction rich in milk be our delight.

**15.2.2**  The upward quarter, the delight of the directions, of the plants — may Savitṛ impel us through the year, through the day. Rich in the Paṅkti of sāmans and metres — may the one who has no born enemy be comfortable to us.

**15.2.3**  The pillar of heaven, the support of the earth, the mistress of this world, wife of Viṣṇu — may she who quickens, all-pervading, of good oblation, be comfortable to us in the lap of Aditi.

**15.2.4**  The firm quarter, wife of Viṣṇu, not terrible, mistress of this — she who was thought of through might: may Bṛhaspati, Mātariśvan, and Vāyu, the joining winds, sing out for us.

**15.2.5**  He who is our father, our begetter, our disposer, who engendered among the existent what truly exists — he, desiring wealth through blessing, has entered into the first covering, into the boon.

**15.2.6**  Turn toward us, O Earth, together with the sacrifice, together with milk. May Agni, urged onward, the drinker of the final share, descend.

**15.2.7**  Whatever is bright in thee, O Agni, whatever is pure, whatever is radiant, and whatever is fit for sacrifice — that we bring to the gods.

**15.2.8**  Refreshment and vigour — I take these hence from the womb of sacrifice, from the abode of the great one. May it enter into our cattle, into our offspring; I cast away languor, debility, and disease.

**15.3.1**  You are the prolonger of life, you are called the inspired medicine. Therefore, O Añjana, you are for our peace — made into that which brings well-being.

**15.3.2**  The jaundice that arises again and again, the splitting of limbs, the visalpaka fever — let the añjana drive out entirely your every disease from your limbs to the outside.

**15.3.3**  The añjana, born from the earth, auspicious, life-giving to man — may it produce what is not ruinous, swift as a chariot, free from sin.

**15.3.4**  O Prāṇa, protect breath; O breath, be gracious to the living. O Nirṛti, free us from the bonds of dissolution.

**15.3.5**  You are the womb of the Sindhu, the flower of lightnings. The wind is breath, the sun is sight, the milk of heaven.

**15.3.6**  O divine Añjana, O Traikakuda, protect me on all sides. Neither the external herbs nor the mountain ones surpass you.

**15.3.7**  He released the strong middle portion — the destroyer of demons, the disperser of disease. Driving away all diseases, destroying them, the overpowering one expelled them from here.

**15.3.8**  Much falsehood, O King Varuṇa, has a man spoken. From that, O you of a thousand powers, free us from transgression on every side.

**15.3.9**  What we have said — \"O inviolable waters,\" \"O Varuṇa\" — from that, O you of a thousand powers, free us from transgression on every side.

**15.3.10**  May Mitra and Varuṇa follow after you, O Añjana. Having followed you far, may it grow again for enjoyment.

**15.4.1**  As one debt is discharged by another debt, carry the sorcery back to the house of its maker. O collyrium, O evil-hearted one of the eye-spell, shatter even the ribs.

**15.4.2**  Whatever ill-omen of evil dreams rests upon us, upon our cattle, or within our house — let that evil-hearted one who has gone home to his mother release it back to the dear one who sent it.

**15.4.3**  This mountain-born collyrium, grown mighty with the vigour and vitality of the waters, born from Agni upon Jātavedas — four-hero-bestowing — let it make all the directions and the sub-directions auspicious for you.

**15.4.4**  The collyrium that bestows four heroes is bound upon you; may all the directions be free from fear for you. Stand firm as the desirable Savitṛ stands; let these peoples bring tribute to you.

**15.4.5**  Apply one, fashion one amulet, bathe with one, drink one of them. May the four-hero-granting collyrium guard us from the four Nirṛtis, from the bonds of seizure.

**15.4.6**  May Agni guard him with the means sacred to Agni — for breath and outbreath, for life-span, for radiance, for vital force, for brilliance, for well-being, for good estate. Svāhā.

**15.4.7**  May Indra guard me with the means sacred to Indra — for breath and outbreath, for life-span, for radiance, for vital force, for brilliance, for well-being, for good estate. Svāhā.

**15.4.8**  May Soma guard me with the means sacred to Soma — for breath and outbreath, for life-span, for radiance, for vital force, for brilliance, for well-being, for good estate. Svāhā.

**15.4.9**  May Bhaga guard me with the means sacred to Bhaga — for breath and outbreath, for life-span, for radiance, for vital force, for brilliance, for well-being, for good estate. Svāhā.

**15.4.10**  May the Maruts guard me with their hosts — for breath and outbreath, for life-span, for radiance, for vital force, for brilliance, for well-being, for good estate. Svāhā.

**15.5.1**  O Agni, O god, giver of life-span, filling him with old age, ghee-faced and ghee-backed — drinking ghee and the lovely immortal essence of the cow, lead this man to old age as a father leads his son.

**15.5.2**  Bestow long life upon him, O Jātavedas; firmly place offspring in him, O Tvaṣṭṛ, from on high. Grant him the full swelling of riches, O Savitṛ — may this man of yours live a hundred autumns.

**15.5.3**  Establish this man in life-span and radiance, O Agni; make his seed dear, O Varuṇa, O King Mitra. Grant him shelter as a mother, O Aditi; may he be vigorous unto old age, O All-Gods.

**15.5.4**  May Agni further extend your life-span; may Soma further establish your nourishment. May Indra with the Maruts speak on your behalf; may Aditi together with the Ādityas grant you shelter.

**15.5.5**  The Revatī stars fashioned your pervasion; the Kṛttikās shaped you. The active ones stretched you out; the prayers, the Avāgnāyī constellations, commingled you in weaving.

**15.5.6**  A thousand threads they set along the borders; eighty threads the women spread in the middle, fearless. O divine women, for the deity Savitṛ as a garment — great indeed was the greatness of these goddesses.

**15.5.7**  Stand upon this stone; become firm as stone itself. Crush down those who wish harm; overpower those who array their forces against you.

**15.5.8**  With that by which the gods encompassed the divine Savitṛ — with that same means, O Bṛhaspati, encompass and establish this man for sovereignty.

**15.5.9**  Encompass this Indra and establish him for long life and great sovereignty — so that you may lead him to old age; long may he remain wakeful in sovereignty.

**15.5.10**  Encompass this Soma and establish him for long life and great hearing — so that you may lead him to old age; long may he remain wakeful in hearing.

**15.6.1**  Invest him, invest him with radiance; make death come only at old age, extend his life-span long. Bṛhaspati gave forth this garment to be put on by King Soma.

**15.6.2**  Go well to old age; put on the garment; be one who overcomes the curse of the young cows. Live a hundred autumns, brilliant in radiance; clothe yourself in the full swelling of riches.

**15.6.3**  Put on this garment all around for well-being; become one who protects against the curse of the sowers. Live a hundred autumns in fullness; lovely and living, may you distribute riches abundantly.

**15.6.4**  At every yoking we invoke the most fervent Indra; at every contest, as friends, we call him for aid.

**15.6.5**  Golden-hued, unaging, rich in heroes, dying only at old age — enter into your offspring thus. So has Agni declared; so too has Soma declared; so Bṛhaspati, Savitṛ, and Indra.

**15.6.6**  With this uppermost thread, the new garment fit for tying — preceding in course, manifold in form and ornament, with auspicious aspect, coeval in aging, rich in heroes — with this may the gods extend his life-span.

**15.6.7**  He whose skirt the Brāhmaṇas take hold of — loosening the waistband, extending his life-span — may the gods delight in that man's invocation of the gods; may well-being attend him always.

**15.6.8**  Take up a different garment; clothe yourself in it — I cut away this caul that clings to you. May the seers clothe you for old age; may Sūrya and Bhaga extend your life-span.

**15.6.9**  Him whose first garment I remove — may all the gods protect you, that one. May many brothers, well-grown, growing in prosperity, be born after you, O well-born one.

**15.6.10**  Unsmit, be unsmit; firm, unite with firmness. Crush down those who wish harm; overpower those who array their forces against you.

**15.10.1**  His countenance becomes like that of a thunder-cloud when, clad in armour, he goes into the lap of battle. With body unpierced, conquer thou; may the greatness of the armour carry thee through.

**15.10.2**  With the bow let us win cattle; with the bow let us win contests; with the bow let us win fierce battles. May the bow turn the enemy's desire away; with the bow let us conquer all armies.

**15.10.3**  Hissing repeatedly, she draws close to the ear, embracing her dear friend. Like a woman she whispers to him stretched upon the bow — this bowstring carries him safe through the assembly of battle.

**15.10.4**  Moving together like a woman at the assembly, may she nourish him in her lap as a mother nourishes her son. These two bowstrings, quivering and in accord, shall pierce the foes and repel the enemies.

**15.10.5**  The father of many, his offspring are many; descending into battle he creates wide space. The quiver, its fastenings, and all the armies — bound upon the back, he conquers when sped forth.

**15.10.6**  Standing in the chariot, the skilled charioteer drives the horses forward wherever he desires. Marvel at the greatness of the reins; the reins hold the mind in check from behind.

**15.10.7**  Making fierce clamour, the bull-handed warriors, spurring on the horses together with the chariots, destroy the enemies by advancing upon them with their forelegs, crushing the foes without wavering.

**15.10.8**  "Chariot-bearer" is his oblation-name — there where his weapon is laid and his armour rests. Upon that prosperous chariot may we be seated, all our days gladdening our hearts.

**15.10.9**  Sweet in assembly are our forefathers, bestowers of vigour, taking refuge in difficulty, mighty and profound; of brilliant host, strong in arrows, irresistible, heroic in battle, broad-ranged, overcoming hordes.

**15.10.10**  Brahman-knowing forefathers, partakers of Soma — may Heaven and Earth be auspicious and free of distress for us. May Pūṣan, increaser of cosmic order, guard us from evil-going; protect us — let no evil-voiced one have power over us.

**15.11.1**  May Bṛhaspati guard us from behind and from above, from below, from the one who would do us harm. May Indra guard us from the front and from the midst; may he, the friend, make wide space for his friends.

**15.11.2**  The eagle's feather she wears; her tooth is the fawn; bound with cowhide sinew she flies forth when sped. Where men surge together and scatter — there may these arrows grant us shelter.

**15.11.3**  The eagle is her garment; clarified butter her mouth; mind her gold; the arrows are the winged ones. Let them not strike us as they swoop from yonder — sped hence, O Savitṛ, may they conquer.

**15.11.4**  Like a serpent with its coils it winds about the arm, warding off the bowstring's stroke. The hand-guard, knowing all the ways of movement, a man shall guard a man from death.

**15.11.5**  They strike the ridge of their backs; they smite upon their haunches. O horse-whip, urge them on; plant the horses firm in the battles.

**15.11.6**  Vigour gathered from Heaven and Earth, might assembled from the forest trees, the swelling power of the waters — brought round with cowhides: with oblation worship the chariot as Indra's thunderbolt.

**15.11.7**  Thou art Indra's thunderbolt, the battle-face of the Maruts, the womb of Mitra, the navel of Varuṇa. So, O divine chariot, enjoying this our gift of oblation, receive thou the offered portions.

**15.11.8**  O Forest Lord, be thou of firm limb; be our friend who carries us forward, possessed of good heroes. Bound with cowhides, O chariot, make thyself firm; may he who mounts thee conquer what is to be conquered.

**15.11.9**  Cause Earth and Heaven to resound; let the wide-spread world take note of thee, in many places. O war-drum, in unison with Indra and the gods, drive the enemies away — ever farther away.

**15.11.10**  Roar aloud; lay power and might into us; thunder forth, pressing back misfortunes. Drive away ill-omen, O war-drum, from hence; thou art the fist of Indra — make thyself strong.

**15.12.1**  May they conquer going forward; may they conquer here in this fearful fray; may the banner-bearing war-drum resound aloud. May our horse-winged men take flight together; O Indra, may our chariot-warriors conquer.

**15.12.2**  May Indra prepare the footing for the chariot; may those whom Maghavan has mounted be victorious. Like a cowherd he yokes together with cattle; born unhurt, the foremost, he who would conquer.

**15.12.3**  Like the thunder of Parjanya is the roar of its breath; like that of Indra and Agni its clamour rings out. A warrior of a thousand, winning a hundred, winning ten thousand — O chariot, be gracious here.

**15.12.4**  The wings of an eagle, the arms of a deer, the fist of Indra, the battle-face of the Maruts — bound with cowhides thou art; make thyself firm.

**15.12.5**  He lifts his arms together, stretching them as one; both his flanks he adorns, fearsome in his gleaming. He drives forth the bright mantle with his powers; he casts off the new garments from the mothers.

**15.12.6**  Fierce are thy rims, fierce are thy tires, fierce are the pins of the great chariot. The tongue-shaft and the bamboo reeds, thy bendings are fierce; Agni is the eye, the forward-runner of the chariot. Grinding down the non-allies, go thou against the foes.

**15.12.7**  The spokes and the hubs — that is thy strength, O axle; fierce are thy two boxes together with those who sit in them. The leather thongs of the pins, the box-sheaths, the standing place, the fastening, and thy seat — all are firm.

**15.12.8**  Strength is the stall; one limb is the fawn — stride forth; press on into the great realm. The reins have slain Vṛtra with this one; fierce is he with lordship and progeny.

**15.12.9**  Mount, O conquering one; with swiftness overcome the rivals; a warrior of a thousand, grinding down, go against the foes. Perform heroic deeds in the span of the back-ridge — thus: welcome this very chariot of his.

**15.12.10**  May the eight eyes of the seers bow in unison; may the illumined horses run straight. May the pillar-post support thee like a father — as it revolves, hold him in thy lap; may his life-span be extended.

**15.12.11**  Sewn by the seers, he is fastened in the chariot; born in the mind of the bow, keeper of the bow. These broad-middled ones, fashioned by the seers, are to be cast before the horses for their welfare — may the gods grant thee shelter through them.

**15.15.1**  As far as the sky extends, as far as the earth extends, as far as the sun moves around — so far do you, O potent plant, protect all around, O Arundhati.

**15.15.2**  The uninjured one digs you up; for the sake of freedom from injury I dig you up. May all of our two-footed and four-footed creatures be without disease.

**15.15.3**  You are the sister of the gods; you are the companion who moves with the Maruts. Whatever living man you reach, that person shall not suffer harm.

**15.15.4**  Arundhati is your name; you are in the third heaven from here. There is the stalk of immortality — by means of that we call to you.
