## Key passage (TextRank #1)
> [p-a8460b] At this point, the wisest of the Athenians cast their eyes upon Solon. They saw that he was the one man least implicated in the errors of the time; that he was neither associated with the rich in their injustice, nor involved in the necessities of the poor. They therefore besought him to come forward publicly and put an end to the prevailing dissensions. And yet Phanias the Lesbian writes that Solon of his own accord played a trick upon both parties in order to save the city, and secretly promised to the poor the distribution of land which they desired, and to the rich, validation of their securities.

## Closes
> [p-e038e9] And as for the ridicule which many heaped upon him for refusing the tyranny, he has written as follows;— Solon was a shallow thinker and a man of counsel void; When the gods would give him blessings, of his own will he refused. When his net was full of fish, amazed, he would not pull it in, All for lack of spirit, and because he was bereft of sense. I had certainly been willing, for the power, and boundless wealth, And to be tyrant over Athens no more than a single day, Then to have a pouch flayed from me, and my lineage blotted out.
