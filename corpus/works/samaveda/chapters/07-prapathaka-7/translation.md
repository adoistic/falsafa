**7.1**  O Indra, bring to us the foremost, most powerful glory. When we would hold firm both heaven and earth, O wielder of the thunderbolt, O fair-cheeked one, you have filled them both.

**7.2**  Indra is king of the world, of all peoples — whatever of all forms is his pervades the earth. From that wealth he gives to the worshipper; he who urges on, though praised, comes forward.

**7.3**  His is this realm — yoked for the contest, for the people, the forest, the sun: great is the delight of Indra.

**7.4**  Loosen from us the highest snare, O Varuṇa, and loose the lowest; unloose the middle one. Then, O Āditya, in your covenant may we be sinless, free for Aditi.

**7.5**  Through you, O Soma flowing bright, may we gather perpetually the treasure won in battle. May Mitra and Varuṇa magnify that for us, and Aditi, the River, the Earth, and Heaven.

**7.6**  Make this one bull — make me this alone.

**7.7**  Flow around and grant wide space for us — for Indra the worshipful, for Varuṇa, for the Maruts.

**7.8**  With this may we, desiring to win, obtain all the glories of humankind, O lord.

**7.9**  I am the firstborn of the cosmic order; I bear the name of immortality before the gods. Whoever gives to me — thus does he declare — I, being food, eat him who eats no food.

**7.10**  In the Paruṣṇī rivers flows the bright milk.

**7.11**  The dappled one, the dawn, blazed forth; the bull, foremost among cows, bellows seeking strength throughout the worlds. Wise seers measured out his magic with his magic; the fathers, beholding men, received the embryo.

**7.12**  Indra alone — together with his two bay horses, yoked by his word — is mixed in with them. Indra, the thunderbolt-wielder, is golden.

**7.13**  O Indra, protect us in contests and in the thousand-prize races, O mighty one, with your mighty aids.

**7.14**  He whose name is Pratha and Sapratha — the offering of the Anuṣṭubh hymn, which is an oblation — from Dhātar shining bright and from Savitar and Viṣṇu, Vasiṣṭha brought forth the Rathantara chant.

**7.15**  Come, O Vāyu, with your team of horses; this bright one is offered to you. You shall go to the home of the one who presses the soma.

**7.16**  When you were born, O bountiful one, peerless, for the slaying of Vṛtra — then you spread out the earth, and you propped up the sky.

**7.17**  May Parameṣṭhin Prajāpati make firm in heaven as he makes firm the sky.

**7.18**  May your milky streams come together, may your strengths come together, may your virile powers come together, O soma that overwhelms the foe. Waxing to fullness for immortality, O Soma, place your highest glories in the sky.

**7.19**  You, O Soma, are all these plants; you gave birth to the waters; you produced the cows. You stretched out the wide mid-air; you with your light drove away the darkness.

**7.20**  I praise Agni, the household priest, the divine minister of the sacrifice, the invoker, the greatest bestower of treasure.

**7.21**  They conceived the first name of the cows; thrice seven the highest name they knew among peoples. Knowing those names, the dawning ones sang out — the ruddy, glorious cows appeared upon the earth.

**7.22**  Some go together, others come flowing together; the rivers fill the same trough. The pure waters flow toward him — pure, shining bright, the child of waters who does not eat food.

**7.23**  The auspicious young maiden of day has come forward, stirring up the banners of light. Night has become the auspicious one who brings to rest all the moving world.

**7.24**  Now of the mighty, ruddy, vigorous one declare to the assembly our speech for Jātavedas. A fresh pure thought flows clear as soma for the dear Agni Vaiśvānara.

**7.25**  Let all the gods hear my sacrifice — both heaven and earth, and the child of the waters — hear my hymn. May I not speak words reprehensible to you; in your graces may we rejoice to the utmost.

**7.26**  May heaven and earth grant me glory; may Indra and Bṛhaspati grant me glory. May Bhaga's glory find me; may glory be released toward me. May I be a proclaimer of glory at this seat of the glorious one.

**7.27**  Now I will proclaim the heroic deeds of Indra, the first great acts that the thunderbolt-wielder performed. He slew the serpent; he bored through the flank; he split open the bellies of the mountains.

**7.28**  I am Agni by birth, Jātavedas by name; clarified butter is my eye, immortality my mouth. The hymn of three elements, the measurer of the atmosphere — I am the ceaseless light, I am all the oblation.

**7.29**  Agni guards the foremost step of the bird; the swift one guards the track of the sun. Agni guards at the navel of the seven-headed one.

**7.30**  O Agni, you who find wealth — with milk you have granted us radiance to behold.

**7.31**  Spring is indeed delightful; summer is indeed delightful. After the rains come autumn; winter is indeed delightful; cool season is indeed delightful.

**7.32**  The Cosmic Person has a thousand heads, a thousand eyes, a thousand feet. Encompassing the earth on all sides, he extends beyond it by ten fingers.

**7.33**  Three-quarters of the Cosmic Person rose upward; one quarter of him was born again here. Thence he strode out in all directions over what eats and what does not eat.

**7.34**  The Cosmic Person alone is all this — whatever has been and whatever shall be. He is the lord of immortality, and also of whatever grows beyond by food. All beings constitute one quarter of him; three quarters of him are the immortal in heaven.

**7.35**  Such is his greatness, yet greater still is the Cosmic Person. He is the lord of immortality, and of all that grows and flourishes through food.

**7.36**  From him Virāj was born; from Virāj the Cosmic Person arose. Being born, he surpassed the earth from behind and also from in front.

**7.37**  I hold you two, O Heaven and Earth, as those of good nourishment, you who have spread out immeasurably and boundlessly. O Heaven and Earth, be gracious to us and release us from distress.

**7.38**  Your beard, O Indra, is tawny gold, and your two bay steeds are golden. You are praised by the poets, the sages who dwell in the woods.

**7.39**  Whatever brilliance gold possesses, whatever brilliance the cows possess, and the brilliance of truth and sacred prayer — with that let us be endowed.

**7.40**  He gave us strength together, O Indra; for he is master of this great power, O mighty one. Like wisdom, like manly valor and steadfast strength — make us smite our enemies among the foes.

**7.41**  Rise up together with your bulls, with your calves, bearing all forms, you whose udders are twofold. Let this broad wide space be yours; may these waters here be easy to drink.

**7.42**  Drive away misfortune from afar.

**7.43**  May the resplendent, mighty one drink the sweet soma-drink, bestowing long life on the lord of the sacrifice, undivided. He who is wind-impelled, who protects the people by himself, nourishes them abundantly, and shines in many ways.

**7.44**  The wondrous face of the gods has risen up — the eye of Mitra, Varuṇa, and Agni. He has filled heaven, earth, and the atmosphere. O Sun, you are the soul of all that moves and stands still.

**7.45**  This dappled bull stepped forth; he sat down before his mother. Going toward his father, he entered the realm of light.

**7.46**  Moving within, the luminous one breathes in and breathes out. The mighty bull has illumined heaven.

**7.47**  In thirty stations he shines forth; speech is offered to the bird. He responds each day with rays of light.

**7.48**  Like thieves those stars depart with the darkness of night, before the all-seeing Sun.

**7.49**  His bright rays have been seen spreading among the people, blazing like fires.

**7.50**  Swift, visible to all, O Sun, you are the maker of light. You illuminate the entire luminous realm.

**7.51**  You rise toward the hosts of gods; you rise toward mankind. You rise toward all the world to behold the light of heaven.

**7.52**  With that purifying eye of yours, O Varuṇa, you behold the active and bustling people.

**7.53**  You traverse the broad realm of space, measuring out the days with your rays. O Sun, you behold all created beings.

**7.54**  The Sun has yoked the seven shining mares, daughters of his chariot. By them he travels, self-harnessed.

**7.55**  Seven bay mares carry you in your chariot, O divine Sun. O flame-haired, far-seeing one.
