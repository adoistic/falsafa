**41**

The supreme consciousness — the supreme [Speech], {{parā / paśyantī / madhyamā / vaikharī:the four ascending levels of Speech}} — is the Self's self-touch of the infinite meanings dissolved within it; she, who bears all in a single act, is the mantric Speech and pure agency.

**42**

She draws Time of her own accord — Time that flows threefold, runs in three paths, and stands in three [stations] — outward, and from there back into the Self: thus is the blessed Parā the Drawer-of-Time [Kālakarṣiṇī].

**43**

Parāparā is the contemplation of the undivided being [now] internalized though [seemingly] differentiated; that same agency, resting on the lordly state of that very [Parā], is Paśyantī [the 'Seeing' Speech].

**44**

Madhyamā [the 'Middle' Speech] is sequential, gross, abiding in the inner faculty, its expressible content split apart by thought-construction, divided by the operation of the vital breath.

**45**

Through abiding in the condition of its own latent dispositions, even in the two prior states there can be the limitedness of the māyā-bound measurer; and the sequence of Paśyantī too is atom-fine [aṇu].

**46**

Entering in turn into those two states — the quiescent [śānta] and the thought-constructing [vikalpa] — the blessed one beholds, with wonder, the full expanse of his own self-awareness.

**47**

She, having for her essence the person's exertion through the striking of the points and organs of articulation, is Vaikharī [the 'Uttered' Speech], whose operations are these [articulate sounds] that fall within the range of hearing.

**48**

By means of these [forms of Speech], reciprocal discourse — whose essence is the transmission of knowledge from one to another — comes about, whether ordinary or scriptural, hemmed in by manifold contractions.

**49**

Thus, the presiding goddesses of these [phonematic] classes — the deities headed by Māheśvarī — never consume [i.e., devour into limitation] the one who knows the pervasion of Speech, even in the waking state.
