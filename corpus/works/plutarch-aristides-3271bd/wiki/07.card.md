## Key passage (TextRank #1)
> [p-0eba1a] Now, to resume, it befell Aristides to be loved at first because of this surname, but afterwards to be jealously hated, especially when Themistocles set the story going among the multitude that Aristides had done away with the public courts of justice by his determining and judging everything in private, and that, without any one perceiving it, he had established for himself a monarchy, saving only the armed body-guard. And besides, the people too must by this time have become greatly elated over their victory; they thought nothing too good for themselves, and were therefore vexed with those who towered above the multitude in name and reputation.

## Closes
> [p-9f5df6] He, astonished, asked the man what possible wrong Aristides had done him. None whatever, was the answer, I don’t even know the fellow, but I am tired of hearing him everywhere called The Just. On hearing this, Aristides made no answer, but wrote his name on the ostrakon and handed it back. Finally, as he was departing the city, he lifted up his hands to heaven and prayed—a prayer the opposite, as it seems, of that which Achilles made —that no crisis might overtake the Athenians which should compel the people to remember Aristides.
