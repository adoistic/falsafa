I will tell all truly, the things you ask of me,
from the very beginning; but if the tale runs long,
forgive me, master. For the maiden did not, as before,
loose her shimmering mouth of oracles in calm,
but, pouring out a measureless, all-mingled cry,
raised her voice frenzied from her laurel-eating throat,
mimicking the speech of the dark Sphinx.
Of these things, all that I hold in heart and memory,
you may hear, my lord, and, reckoning them over in a mind
close-pressed, thread your way through the riddles' baffling
paths, unwinding them, where a well-marked track
by a straight road guides the feet through darkness.
And I, slipping the topmost barrier of the cord,
set out upon the windings of these crooked words,
striking the first post like a winged runner.
Dawn was even now over the steep crag of Phegium
soaring on the swift wings of Pegasus,
leaving Tithonus in his bed near Cerne,
your half-brother by another mother.
And the sailors, from the smooth hollow of the rock,
were casting off, releasing from the land
the starting-ropes. And the stork-coloured maidens,
the down-footed, fair-faced ones, the daughters of Phalacra,
struck the maiden-slaying Thetis with their blades—
above Calydnae showing their white plumes,
their stern-ornaments, and their sail-cloths spread a fathom wide
to the northern blasts of the blazing hurricane.
Then she, loosing her god-filled, Bacchic mouth,
from the topmost ridges of Ate, built by the wandering ox,
Alexandra began her words from such a beginning:
Alas, unhappy nurse, burned once before
by the troop-bearing pines
of the lion of three nights, whom once in his jaws
the jagged hound of Triton mangled;
but living, a carver of livers, scorched
on the flameless hearths of the cauldron's heat,
he dripped the bristles of his scalp upon the ground—
the child-destroyer, the ruin of my fatherland,
who smote the unwounded breast of his second mother
with a heavy shaft, and in the middle channel
of the wrestling-father gripped his frame in his hands
by the steep bank of Cronus, where is the tomb
of earth-born Ischenus, the disturber of horses;
who slew the wild she-dog that watched the narrow
recesses of the Ausonian sea,
fishing above her cavern—
the bull-slaying lioness, whom again her father,
burning her flesh with brands, built up anew,
Leptynis, the not-trembling, the goddess of the underworld;
the man who once, by a swordless trick, despoiled
a corpse that long had welcomed Hades.
I see you, wretched one, set ablaze a second time
by the hands of Aeacus' line and of Tantalus,
who keep the remnants of fire at Letrina—
the body of the boy devoured in the smoke,
by the Teutarian wings of the herdsman.
All these things into the light the jealous wife,
having sent forth the boy who would accuse the land,
will bring, enraged by her father's reproaches
and for the sake of the bed and the imported marriage.
And she herself a drug-worker, beholding the deadly,
incurable wound of her bedfellow,
struck by the Giant-slaying arrows
of his foeman in arms, will share a common death,
from the topmost towers hurling her body headlong
with a rush down upon the newly-slain corpse;
and, barbed with longing for the dead,
she will breathe out her soul over the still-gasping body.
I groan, I groan for you, twice and three times, for the spear
again raised to the fight, and the plundering of homes
and the destroying fire that lights all to nothing.
I groan for you, my fatherland, and for the tombs of the diver
son of the daughter of Atlas, who once in a stitched vessel,
like a four-legged Istrian wicker-trap,
alone, wrapping his body in a hide,
like a Rhithymnian storm-petrel swam,
leaving the Zerynthian cave of the dog-slaying goddess,
Saon, the strong-built foundation of the Corybantes,
when, raining on all the earth, it crumbled it to dust,
the surging flood of Zeus; and the towers fell
down to the ground, and men swam their last
lot of fate, gazing on death before their eyes.
And acorns and oak-fruit and the sweet grape-cluster
the whales and dolphins and the seals that lust
after the beds of mortal males fed upon.
I see the winged firebrand running
to the seizing of the dove of Pephnos,
whom the moisture-roving vulture brings to birth,
shelled within the round egg's husk.
And you the descending Acherousian path
shall lodge as a sailor, a white-rump, not treading
your father's dung in the battered ox-stalls,
as before—you who measured beauty by three.
But instead of the cattle-stalls you shall pass the Ass's Jaws
and Las, and instead of the well-foddered crib
and the sheepfolds and the landsman's oar,
a ship shall carry you, and the feet of Phereclus,
to the two Salambae and the flats of Gytheium,
in which, against the dog-tails loosing the curved
teeth of pine, the anchors of the flood,
you shall lull to rest your nine-sailed fleet of leaps.
And, having seized the unwedded heifer, the wolf,
orphaned of her parentage by the two doves
and a second time fallen into the snare of alien nooses,
the captive caught by the fowler's wing,
as she even now burns at Thysae the first-fruits of sheep
among the saffron-flowers, and to the goddess Byne,
you shall run past Scandeia and the headland of Aegilus,
a blazing huntsman exulting in your prey.
And on the island, having poured out your longing for the snake
of Acte, the two-formed earth-born sceptred power,
you shall not see the second love, stale by morning,
a cold embrace, and out of dreams
grasping at the empty bed with empty arms.
For the husband of your Phlegraean bedmate,
the grim spouse of Torone, to whom laughter is hateful
and tears, who is unversed in and bereft
of both—he who once from Thrace to the seaward shore
furrowed by the outpourings of Triton
crossed the dry land, not in a sailing fleet,
but by an untrodden path, like some burrowing mole,
having pierced the inmost recesses of a hidden cavern,
and made his way beneath the sea,
escaping his children's stranger-slaying wrestlings,
and sending to his father the prayers that won a hearing
to set him back homeward, whence in his wandering
he came to Pallene, nurse of the earth-born—
he, like Gouneus, a worker of justice
and arbiter of the Ichnaean daughter of the Sun,
by hurling baleful reproaches will deprive you of marriage,
casting you out as you long for the dove-cote of your cousin;
who, not revering the tombs of Lycus and Chimaereus,
glorified with oracles,
nor the loves of Antheus, nor the holy rock
of Aegaeon, fellow-feaster with strangers,
you dared, accursed of the gods, to step beyond justice,
spurning the table and overturning Themis,
moulded in the ways of the she-bear that nursed you.
Therefore you shall pluck your bowstring's twang in vain,
striking up songs unfed and ungifted;
and weeping for your fatherland, burned of old,
you shall come embracing in your arms the phantom
of the five-bedded raving woman of Pleuron.
For the palsied, ancient granddaughters of aged Hals
with their threefold spindles spun out the doom
that her five-times-married bridals should be feasted upon.
Two ravening wolves shall she behold,
winged buzzards, eagle-eyed,
and him who from Plynos and Carian waters
sprang of his stock, a half-bred barbarian,
Epeius, not a pure Argive in his birth.
Whose grandfather once in her jaws the Ennaean
Hercynna, the Thurian sword-bearing Fury,
having minced him fleshless, entombed in her gullet,
devouring the gristle of his arm.
He, twice come to manhood, and having fled the heavy lust
and ravishing hand of Naumedon,
Erechtheus sent to the fields of Letrina
to grind smooth the rock of Molpis,
whose body had been carved up for Rainy Zeus,
to crush the son-in-law-slayer by unholy counsels,
the destroyer of fathers-in-law, which the son of Cadmilus
contrived. And the last man, having drained the cup,
plunged into the tombs that bear the name of Nereus,
having shrieked utter ruin upon his race—
he who guided the swift-footed Psylla as charioteer
and Harpinna, equal in her hooves to the Harpies.
And the fourth she shall behold, brother by blood
of the swooping falcon, whom of his brethren
men shall proclaim to have won the second place
in the death-dealing wrestling. And in his bed
the fifth, distracted out of dreams,
she shall waste away with her phantom-shaping face—
the bridegroom-to-be, the bedfellow of the Cytaean,
the stranger-Bacchant; whom once the exile of Oenone,
having raised to manhood the six-footed army of ants,
the Pelasgian Typhon, begets as father,
having alone escaped, of seven sons consumed by the spark,
the blazing ash.
And the one shall come by a backward-running path,
having drawn the blood-red wasps out of their holes,
as a boy stirs up a house with smoke;
and the others, the raw-flesh-eaters, having with stormy
hands purified the heifer who bore the army's namesake,
the pregnant mother in childbed of the Scyrian dragon,
whom her bedfellow, searching within the Salmydesian sea
for the beheader of Hellas,
shall long inhabit a foaming, whitening rock
by the outpourings of the Celtic marshy waters,
yearning for his wife, whom once in the slaughter a hind,
setting her throat forward, shall ransom from the swords.
And deep within the surf shall be told of
the bridegroom's lonely course among the saffron-flowers,
as he groans for the ruin and the empty voyage
and her changed and vanished form,
the old woman near the slaughter-pits and lustral water
and Hades seething from the depths with fire
of the bowl, which the black female shall blow up,
stewing the flesh of the dead in her cookery-craft.
And the one shall tread the Scythian land, lamenting,
longing for his wife for some five years.
And the others, about the altar of Cronus the foreteller,
with the mother who shared out the flesh of her infant children,
having fitted the second yoke of oaths,
shall arm in their arms the stout oar,
hailing Bacchus the saviour from former woes,
Sphaltes, with the Bacchic cry, to whom once in the recesses
of the Delphinian, by the caves of the gainful god,
with a bull the leader of a thousand shall begin
the secret lustral rites of the city-wrecking host.
To whom, paying the sudden grace of sacrifices,
the god Enorches, Phigalian, Faustarius,
shall hold back a lion from his feast, entangling its track in withes,
that it may not utterly destroy the corn-stalk at the root,
shearing it with tooth and ravening jaws.
Long since I see the coil of the trailing evils,
dragging through the brine and hissing against my fatherland
dread threats and fire-blazing harms.
Would that Cadmus had never planted you in sea-girt
Issa, a guide of foes,
a fourth offspring from wretched Atlas,
Prylis, the helper in razing your own kinsmen,
O seer most unerring toward the best;
nor would that my father had thrust away
the night-roving terrors of the Aesacian oracles,
but had hidden the two of us, for the fatherland's sake,
in a single fate, burning our limbs with Lemnian fire;
then no wave of so many evils would have flooded over.
And lo, the infant-slaying Palaemon beholds
the aged Titaness, bedfellow of Ogenus,
seething with cormorants that voyage in fleets.
And lo, two children are dashed to ruin with their father,
his collar-bones struck by the firm, ruling millstone,
who before had escaped the flute-player's
chest-destroying castings, by false rumours,
in which the grim butcher of children trusting,
the gull-reared net-hauler of the wicker traps,
friend of the samphire and the crooked sea-snails,
penned up in a chest the twin offspring.
And with them the wretch, not heeding the goddess
his mother's mindful commands, but tripped up by forgetfulness,
shall die face-down, his breast pierced by a sword.
And lo, Myrina groans, and the shoreline beaches
that have received the snorting of horses,
when the Pelasgian leap of the swift foot,
propping itself on the last strand, the blazing wolf,
shall whirl up the fountain-gleam from the sand,
opening the springs long hidden.
And lo, the dancer Ares scorches the earth,
striking up the bloody strain with the war-trumpet.
And all the land, ravaged before my eyes,
lies waste, and the fields bristle like a corn-crop,
glittering with spears; and the wailing, to my ears,
seems to come from the topmost towers,
reaching to the windless seats of heaven,
with the lament of women and the rending of robes,
as they receive one disaster after another.
That woe, O wretched heart, that woe shall gnaw you,
that highest of sufferings, when the rushing,
dusky spearman, fierce, an eagle
marking out the dry land with his wings
in a track stamped by his crooked, curved foot,
and screaming a harsh, unmingled cry from his mouth,
having snatched aloft the dearest nursling of your womb
and of the Ptoan father,
bloodying his body with talons and jaws,
stains the native fens and the ground with slaughter,
a ploughman cleaving the earth along a smooth furrow.
And taking the loan of the slain bull,
weighed in the exact balance of the scale,
and pouring in again an equal counter-price,
the Pactolian ingot, far-gleaming in the weights,
he shall sink into the bowl of Bacchus, bewept
by the nymphs who loved the gleam of Bephyrus
and the Leibethrian height above Pimpleia—
the corpse-seller, who, fearing his doom beforehand,
will even endure to put a woman's robe
about his body, touching the rattle of the shuttle at the loom,
and to be last to cast his foot on the foeman's land,
cowering, my brother, even in sleep before your spear.
O divine power, what a pillar of the house you shall destroy,
drawing away the prop of the ill-fated fatherland!
Yet not unpunished, nor without bitter toils
and grief, shall the robber Dorian host laugh,
exulting over the doom of the fallen,
but, running about the sterns his very last
course of life, he shall be burned with the pines,
calling upon, with prayers, Zeus the God of Flight
to ward off bitter dooms from those being sacked.
Then neither trench, nor the bulwark of the ship-stations,
nor the wing crowned with palisades,
nor copings shall avail, nor battlements;
but like bees confounded with smoke
and by the gusts of soot and the casting of firebrands,
stern-ornaments and prow-tops and the thrones of the steersmen
in close throngs the tumblers, leaping from the benches,
shall bloody the alien dust.
And many chieftains, bearing the first-fruits of Hellas
on their spear-points and swollen with their lineage,
your mighty hands shall destroy,
gushing with slaughter and craving battle.
And I shall bear a grief no less,
groaning for your tombs and through all time.
For piteous, piteous shall be that light I shall behold,
and highest of the woes of which finishing Time,
rolling the circle of the moon, shall be told.
Alas, I lament also your milk-sweet shoot,
O cub, the delightful embrace of your kin,
who, striking the wild serpent with the fire-bearing
lure of your arrows, and seizing the stricken one in inescapable
snares for a brief, unloved while,
by him you subdued, yourself unwounded,
beheaded, shall bloody your father's tomb.
Ah me, ill-fated, I bewail too the two nightingales
and your doom, wretched whelp.
Of whom the one, root and all, the earth that bore her,
gaping, shall hide in her hollow cleft,
as she beholds ruin close at the heels of her groans,
where the leap of the grandfather and the deaths of the bedded
on the ground, of the secretly-wed heifer, mingled
with the cub, are poured out—before she has tasted the gleam of life,
before she has bathed her limbs in the dew after childbirth.
But you to the savage bridal and the wedding
sacrifices the grim lion of Iphis shall lead,
imitating the lustral rites of his dark mother,
whom, having cut her throat into the deep cattle-pen,
a garland-bearing cow, the dread butcher-serpent
shall slay with the thrice-fathered sword of Candaon,
loosing to the wolves the first-slaughtered oath-offering.
And you, by the hollow captive shore,
an old man of the Dolonci, stoned by the people, with the arm
by reviling curses provoked,
a tunic of pelting stones shall hide,
when you change to the dark form of Maera.
And he who at Agamemnon's tomb subdued
shall beautify the base below with frost-pale hair—
the wretch, bought for the veil of his own sister,
come to the smoke-blackened fatherland,
having blotted out in darkness his once dim name,
when the snake, the heavy firebrand, raw-haired,
the seller of his native land,
having burned the brood that labours in the womb,
the dreadful ambush, draws out the bands of its belly
and unstrings them, and from the Sisyphean curved
fire-tail kindles an evil beacon, that cousin,
for those who have sailed out to narrow Leucophrys
and the two islands of child-eating Porceus.
And I, the wretched one who refuse marriage,
within the hewn stone of the maiden's chamber,
roofless, in a chamber without a ceiling,
having sunk my body deep into the gloomy prison,
I who cast from my bed the Thoraean Ptoan Horite god
as he longed for a loveless couch,
that, having won an imperishable maidenhood
to the last of old age, in emulation of Pallas,
the marriage-hating Laphria of the Gates,
then by force, a dove to the vulture's bed,
with curved talons, frenzied, shall I be dragged,
I who often called upon Budeia, the Aethyian Maiden,
as helper and champion of my marriage.
And she, turning her eyeballs up to the roof
of the carved timber chamber, will be wroth with the host,
fallen from heaven and the throne of Zeus,
a thing most prized by her lord and grandfather.
And for one man's outrage, of ten thousand children
all Hellas shall groan over the empty tombs,
not in bone-vaults, but perched on reefs,
holding no last ashes from the fire,
buried in urns, as is the law of the dead,
but a piteous name and the painted images of cenotaphs
bathed in the warm tears of parents
and children and the laments of bedfellows.
O Opheltes and Zarax, recess of the reefs,
and you crags, and Trychas, and rough Nedon,
and all the dens of Dirphossus and the Diacrian
hills, and the dwelling-place of Phorcys—
of how many groans of corpses cast up
with half-shattered planks shall you hear,
and of what surf of the surge, with no way out,
as the swell drags back with refluent eddies,
and of how many tunny-rows of men, furrowed,
about the frying-pans of the headland, of whom the descending
thunderbolt shall taste as they perish in the night,
when, leading the heavy-headed from drunkenness,
the plunderer shows a lantern, the guide of darkness,
sitting at his sleepless craft.
And one, like a diving halcyon, through a narrow
channel a wave shall carry, a naked sea-bream,
swept between the twin reefs.
And on the Gyraean rocks, drying his feathers
dripping with the sea, he shall drink a second draught of brine,
cast from the cliffs by the three-pronged spear,
with which the dread chastiser, his servant, wounding him,
shall force him to share the course with the whales,
as he boasts his cuckoo-vauntings of the empty wind.
And cold, cast up upon the shore, his corpse
the Dolphin's Sirian ray shall parch.
And, rotting amid the seaweed and the moss, his pickled flesh
the sister of Nesaea shall hide in pity,
the helper of Cynaetheus of the great discus.
And his tomb, neighbour to the petrified quail,
trembling, shall keep watch over the roar of the Aegaean sea.
And the Castnian and Melinaean goddess
the wretch shall revile beside Hades, reviling her with evil words,
who shall lure him with the inescapable snares of frenzy,
loves that are not loves, but having stripped off
the bitter wax-drawing trap of the Furies.
And all shall receive the pains of wailings,
as many as the Aratthus within and the hard-to-cross
Leibethrian gates of Dotium enclose,
to whom my marriage even by the Acherousian
surf shall long be groaned over.
For in the bowels of many shall be entombed,
devoured by the many-rowed jaws of sea-monsters,
a numberless swarm; and others, strangers in a strange land,
bereft of kinsmen, shall welcome their tombs.
For the one the Bisaltian land of Strymon's Eïon,
bordering on the Apsynthians and the Bistones,
the child-rearing crab, near the Edonians,
shall hide, before he sights the Tymphrestian crag—
him most hated of mortals by his father,
who made him a hostage, having bored out his lamps,
when he lay in the bastard bed of the dove.
And three the glens of Cercaphus shall entomb,
the gulls not far from the waters of Aleis;
the one, the swan of Molossian Cypeus Coetus,
led astray by the offspring of a female sow,
when, having drawn his rival into the clever
contest of figs, himself, baffled by the oracles,
he shall sleep the destined doom;
the other again, fourth of the descendants of Erechtheus,
own brother of the blazing one in the false paintings;
and the third, son of him who once dug up with the hard
mattock the offspring of the towers of the Ectenes,
whom Gongylates, Bulaeus the Miller, slew,
shattering his head with the expiatory scourge,
when the daughters of Night armed the brothers
against their father in the self-slaughtering frenzy of death.
And two by the outpourings of the streams of Pyramus,
slain by self-murdering slaughters, the hounds of Derainus,
shall raise their last battle-cry
beneath the heels of the towers of the Pamphylian maiden.
And a steep sea-eaten mound between them,
Magarsus, of the holy graves shall stand,
that they may not see, nor, sunk into the seats of those below,
behold each other's tombs bathed in blood.
And the five, having gone to Sphecia in Cerastia
and to Satrachus and the land of Hylates,
shall dwell beside Morpho the Zerynthian.
The one, driven by his father's reproaches
from the caves of Cychreus and the streams of Bocarus,
my own brother, as the slayer of the colt by the same father,
the bastard offshoot, the harm of his kin,
who poured out warlike madness among the flocks—
whom the hide of the raw-flesh-eating monster
made not pierceable by bronze in battle,
having won one road to Hades and the dead,
which the Scythian quiver hid—
when, burning offerings to Comyrus, the lion
cried aloud the prayers that won a hearing to his own father,
casting the cub into the arms of his comrade.
For he shall not persuade his son, how the Lemnian
thunderbolt of Enyo, never turned to flight,
the heavy-minded bull, by the gift of the most hostile of strangers
struck his vitals, having leapt the baleful
plunge upon the dagger to the self-wrought slaughter.
And he shall drive far from his fatherland the brother of Trambelus,
whom the sister of his father bore,
given as first spoils of war to the tower-digger.
Whom once, standing in the assemblies of the people,
the boastful sire who begot her urged
to set out as a dark supper for the grey hound—
three daughters—he who turns all the land to mud with brine,
when he belches forth the surges of his jaws,
shaking with violent third-wave all the ground.
And he, instead of a young bird, having drawn a scorpion to his throat,
wept to Phorcus the burden of an evil birth-pang,
wishing to learn the counsel of his woes.
And the second, come to the island as a husbandman,
a landsman, self-fed, of the offspring of the oak,
the wolf-shaped flesh-sharers of Nyctimus,
who before the moon thawed their store of oaken bread
with the ash of the fire at the height of winter,
shall dig for copper and draw from the pit
the clod, mining all the soil with his mattock.
Whose son the Oetaean point destroyed,
having crushed his frame in the sockets of the groin.
And the wretch learned the saying, taught with woe,
how the all-contriving doom of mortals rolls many things
in the midst between the lip and the cup's draught.
And he himself, all whitening with bright gore,
the point, having fallen, warded off his slayer,
striking unescapably the dancer's heel-tip.
And the third, son of him who seized from the hollow rock
the giant's arms, to whose secret bed
the Idaean heifer, self-summoned,
shall come, descending alive to Hades,
wasted away with laments, the mother of Munitus;
whom once, as he hunted, the Crestonian viper
shall slay, striking his heel with a wild dart,
when, a captive into the hands of his sire,
his father's mother shall cast the newborn cub
reared in the gloom. On her alone the Actaean
wolves fixed the yoke of slavery
in requital for the ravished raving woman,
of whom the carved shell-spiral
shields the temple, a defence against the bloody spear.
And as for the rest, the worm-eaten, untouched seal
of the house keeps watch, a great wonder to the natives.
Which to the ladder of the stars shall set the course
of the half-dead twin Lapersians;
whom never, O Zeus the saviour, to my fatherland
may you send as helpers to the twice-ravished cry,
nor, having armed their winged ships,
from the topmost stern cast a bare, swift foot
onto the landing-place of the Bebryces,
nor those mightier than these lions,
unmatched in valour, whom Ares loved,
and divine Enyo, and the thrice-born goddess
Boarmia Longatis Homoloïs Bia.
Otherwise, what the two handiwork labourers,
Drymas and Prophantus, the lord of Cromna,
hewed out of stone for the falsely-swearing king,
one day would not suffice the plundering wolves
to ward off the heavy, crushing assault,
though before the towers they have great Canastraean
giant, a native bar against the foes,
and him, the first to rush with a well-aimed cast
to strike, the avenger of the flocks.
Whom once the blazing falcon shall first initiate the spear,
the bold one, hurling his swift leap,
the best of the Greeks, for whom long since the shore
of the Dolonci, ready, makes tombs when he is dead,
Mazousia jutting out at the landward horn.
But there is, there is even beyond hope
a helper for us, kindly, the Drymnian
god Promantheus, Aethiops, Gyrapsius,
who, when into their houses the wandering Orthanes,
the harming, downward-rushing, bitter one, they receive—
those destined one day to suffer dread and accursed things—
and at the feast and in the harvest-libations
appease the unloved Cragus,
shall set a heavy strife in the midst of the gatherings.
And first with words they shall gnash at one another,
biting with revilings, made sharp,
and then shall fight in arms, cousins against cousins,
wishing to aid the marriages of their cousin-birds
and the violent thefts and ravishments of their kin,
the requital for the dowerless rape.
Truly many shafts the ford of Cnaecion
shall behold, hurled by the daring of the eagles,
unbelievable and astounding for the Pheraeans to hear.
The one, having pierced the hollow trunk of a dark oak
with cornel-wood, shall destroy one of the twin pair,
the lion that joined battle with the bull.
And the other, having ripped open the bull's flank with a javelin,
shall lay it on the ground. And against him a second

And the cub, seeking out the doom of his father's murder,
shall dip his sword with his own hand into the viper's vitals,
healing one kindred pollution with another evil.
And my consort, the lord of the slave-bride,
Zeus shall be called by the wily Spartans,
having received the greatest honours from the sons of Oebalus.
Yet not nameless among men shall my worship be,
withered straightway in oblivious darkness.
And a temple the chiefs of the Daunians shall build for me
by the banks of Salpe, and those who dwell in the city
of Dardanus, bordering on the waters of the lake.
And the maidens, when they wish to escape the yoke
of maidenhood, refusing as bridegrooms
those adorned with Hectorean locks,
having a blemish of form or a reproach of race,
shall embrace my image in their arms,
winning the greatest defence against marriage,
clad in the garb of the Furies and with their faces dyed
with magical drugs.
To those wand-bearing women shall I long be called
an immortal goddess.
And grief to many bereft of their maiden daughters
I shall again make for the women, who the war-leader,
the lawless-bedded one, the robber of the goddess Cypris,
long groaning over, into a hostile lot
shall send their children, deprived of marriage.
O Larymna, and Spercheius, and Boagrius,
and Cynus, and Scarpheia, and Phalorias,
and the city of Naryx, and the Thronian
streets of the Locrians, and the glens of Pyronaea,
and all the house of Hodoedocian Ileus,
you, for the sake of my impious marriage,
shall pay penalties to the Gygaean Agrisca goddess,
for the thousand-year time keeping in old age
your unwedded maidens by the awards of the lot.
For whom an unburied tomb in a strange land, strangers,
in the sand of the surge, wretched, shall be washed away,
when, having burned their limbs with barren plants,
Hephaestus casts the ash into the sea,
of her perished from the hills of Traron.
And others by night, like to those about to die,
shall come to the fields of Sithon's daughter,
by secret, off-the-road paths peering about,
until they run into the house of Ampheira,
weasels supplicating Stheneia with prayers.
And they shall sweep the goddess's floor, adorning it,
and shall hallow it with dew, having fled the unloved wrath
of the citizens. For every Ilian man
shall watch for the maidens, holding a stone in his hands,
or a black sword, or the bull-slaying
hard double-axe, or a Phalacraean branch,
craving to glut his hand thirsting for slaughter.
And the people shall praise unpunished him who has slain,
having engraved in law the race set apart for outrage.
O mother, O ill mother, not even your fame
shall be unheard of; but the maiden of Perseus,
Brimo Trimorphos, shall make you her attendant,
terrifying with night cries those mortals
who do not revere with torch-processions the images
of the Zerynthian queen of Strymon,
appeasing the Pheraean goddess with sacrifices.
And the island stake of Pachynus shall hold
a false sepulchre, august, from dreams,
reared by my mistress-arms,
buried before the streams of Helorus;
who by the shores shall pour libations for the wretched one,
fearing the wrath of the three-necked goddess,
because, having first cast a stone as a stoner,
he shall begin dark sacrifices to Hades.
And you, my brother, most loved of my heart,
prop of the halls and of the whole fatherland,
not in vain shall you redden the altar-base with the blood
of bulls, to the lord of Ophion's thrones
offering very many first-fruits of sacrifices.
But he shall lead you to the native plain
that is exceedingly hymned among the Greeks,
where his mother, skilled in wrestling,
having cast the former queen into Tartarus,
loosed the birth-pangs of her secret offspring,
having escaped the child-devouring,
unholy feasts of her bedfellow, nor did he fatten
his belly with food, having gulped the substitute stone,
wrapped in limb-binding swaddling-clothes,
having become a tomb, the raw-minded Centaur of the offspring.
And in the islands of the blessed you shall dwell, a great
hero, a helper against the arrows of plague,
where the sown people of Ogygus, persuaded
by the oracles of the Healer, Lepsian, Terinthean,
having drawn you up from the Ophrynian graves,
shall lead you, a saviour, to the tower of Calydnus and the land of the Aones,
when they are hard-pressed by a host in arms
sacking the land and the shrines of Tenerus.
And your fame the chiefs of the Ectenes
with imperishable libations shall honour as equal to the gods'.
And to Cnossus and the houses of Gortyn shall come
my woe, the wretched one's; and all the house
of the generals shall be overthrown. For not in peace
shall the rower drive his two-oared deck,
sailing to whirl up Leucus the guardian of the monarchy,
and weaving hatred with false contrivances.
Who shall spare neither his children nor his bedfellow
Meda his wife, savage in mind,
nor his daughter Cleisithera, whose bed her father
shall consent, a bitter consent, to a fostered serpent.
And all of them with unholy hands in the temple he shall slay,
outraged with mutilations at the Oncaean pit.
And the fame of the race of my ancestors again
the descendants shall one day greatly increase,
having won with their spears the first crown,
taking the sceptres of land and sea and the monarchy.
Nor, O wretched fatherland, shall you hide your glory,
withered, in darkness.
Such twin lion-cubs shall one kinsman of mine leave,
a race surpassing in might,
the offspring of the Castnian and Cheiras,
best in counsels, and not to be despised in battles.
Who first shall come and dwell in Rhaecelus,
by the steep crag of ivy and the horned
women of Laphystia. And from Almopia
as a wanderer-back Tyrrhenia shall receive him
and Lingeus, belching forth the stream of warm waters,
and Pisa and the sheep-rich glens of Agylla.
And with them, though an enemy, he shall mingle his friendly host,
having prevailed by oaths and prayers of supplication,
the dwarf, who in his wanderings searched out every recess
of sea and land. And with him the twin offspring
of the Mysian king, whose spear once Theoenus the Housekeeper
shall bend, having bound his limbs with withes,
Tarchon and Tyrsenus, blazing wolves,
sprung from the blood of Heracles.
There, finding a table full of victuals,
the table afterward devoured by his attendants,
he shall take the memory of ancient prophecies.
And he shall found a land in the places of the Boreigoni,
settled above the Latins and the Daunians,
thirty towers, having numbered the offspring
of the dark sow, which from the Idaean hills
and the Dardanian lands he shall convey by ship,
the nurse of as many piglets in her litter;
of whom in a single city he shall set up an image,
having stamped it in bronze, of her and her milk-nursed young.
And, having built a shrine to the Myndian Pallenis,
he shall set within it the ancestral images of the gods.
Which, having thrust aside both wife and children
and all his other rich store of treasures,
he shall honour above all, with his aged father,
covering them with robes, when the spearmen hounds,
having plundered by lot all the things of the fatherland,
to him alone grant a choice, to take from the house
what he wishes and carry off the loan.
Wherefore, judged even by his enemies most pious,
the fatherland most hymned in battles
he shall build, blessed, among his late-born children,
a tall tower about the glens of Circaeum
and the famous harbour of the Argo, great Aeetes,
and the waters of the Phorcean lake Marsionis
and the Titonian flood of the river that beneath the earth
sinks into the unseen depths of its hollow,
and the slope of Zosterius, where is the gloomy
dwelling-place of the maiden Sibyl,
roofed over with a hollow, cavernous chamber.
So many grievous evils shall they suffer
who are destined to lay waste my fatherland.
For what bond is there between the wretched mother of Prometheus
and the nurse of Sarpedon,
whom the sea of Helle and the Symplegades rocks
and Salmydesus and the inhospitable surge,
neighbour to the Scythians, shut off with hard frosts,
and the pure Tanais, cutting the lake in the middle
with its streams, bounds—most dear to mortals,
to the Maeotians who bewail the chilblains of their feet?
May they perish first, the Carnite hounds, the sailors,
who carried off the ox-eyed, bull-maiden girl
from Lerna, the freight-bearing wolves,
to bear her as a bride, a doom, to the Memphite chief,
and raised the firebrand of enmity for the two continents.
For again the heavy outrage of that rape
the Idaean boars, the Curetes, seeking
in requital, carried off a captive heifer
in the bull-shaped form of a ship,
Saraptian, to the Dictaean palace,
as a wife for the war-leader of Crete, Asterus.
Nor were they content, having taken equal for equal,
but a thieving host with Teucer
and with Scamander the Drauchian sire
they sent to the dwelling of the Bebryces,
to war with the field-mice, of whose seed
Dardanus begot my founders,
having married Arisbe, a noble Cretan maiden.
And second they sent the Atracian wolves
to steal, under a one-sandalled captain, the fleece,
guarded by serpent-warded watches.
Who, having come to Libyan Cytaea,
and lulled to rest with charms the four-jawed water-snake,
and lifted the curved ploughs of the fire-breathing bulls,
and had his body carved up in the cauldron,
not gladly seized the dragon's spoil,
but, having snatched the self-summoned destroyer,
the brother-slaying, child-avenging woman,
loaded her aboard the chattering Cissa,
that from the Chaonian benches sends forth
a mortal voice, skilled in courses.
And again he who drew up the shoes of stone
and the sword-belt and the sword of his father,
the son of Phemius, to whom Scyros, sheer above
the resounding cliffs below,
long since marks out the grievous tombs, the unburied castings,
having gone with the beast that drew the foeman's
milk from the well-suckled breast of the Tropaean goddess, Myste,
the belt-thief, stirred up a double strife,
both having robbed of Storne and from Themiscyra
carried off the bow-conquering Orthosia.
Whose sisters, the maidens of Neptune's daughter,
leaving strife, Lagmos and Telamos,
and the stream of Thermodon and the Actaean mount,
seeking unyielding vengeance and rapine,
beyond the dark Ister drove the Scythian
horses, sending forth a summoning cry
to the Greeks and the descendants of Erechtheus.
And all of Acte they sacked with the spear,
having burned the Mopsopian fields.
And my grandfather, having laid waste the plain of Thrace
and the land of the Eordi and the ground of the Galadraei,
fixed his borders about the waters of Peneus,
having put a hard yoke on her neck with fetters,
young in strength, most outstanding of his race.
And she in requital for these things, having sent her champion
the ox-driver, the six-sterned one, clad in a hide,
with shovels overthrew the steep crag,
which the perverse-minded Gorge in the lots of the gods
shall consecrate, the originator of woes.
And again the falcons, having left Tmolus
and Cimpsus and the gold-working waters of Pactolus,
and the stream of the lake, where the wife of Typhon
haunts the dread-bedded recess of the cavern,
revelled into Ausonian Agylla,
having joined in the spear-battles a grim wrestling
with the Ligurians and those who hold from the blood
the root of the Sithonian giants.
And they took Pisa and all the spear-won land
they subdued that lies near the Umbrians
and that goes upon the steep crags of the Salpians.
And the last firebrand kindles the ancient strife,
setting alight with flame the fire that slept before,
when he saw the Pelasgians dip the gleam
of the waters of Rhyndacus in foreign urns.
And she, frenzied again in vengeance,
shall pay back threefold, fourfold the harms,
sacking the shore across the strait.
First shall come, namesake of Lapersian Zeus,
a Zeus, who, coming down as a descender,
shall burn with the thunderbolt all the steadings of the foes.
With whom I shall die, and, dwelling among the dead,
the rest I shall hear, these things which now I am about to cry.
And the second, son of the man slain
like a mute fish in the casting-nets,
shall set ablaze the foreign land, having come
by the oracles of the Healer with a many-tongued host.
And the third, son of the oak-hewing lord,
the armour-moulding maiden of Branchus
having beguiled, to give the clod soaked
in streams to him who needed it as a loan,
to fit the seal of his fingers to the tablet,
shall settle the mountain monarchy of the lice,
having destroyed the first-hired Carian host,
when the harlot maiden, into wanton
mockery barking, shall revile the marriages,
that they make their bridals into pulley-drawn ones for the barbarians.
And the fourth, of the Dymantian seed,
the Lacmonian and Cytinaean Codri,
who shall inhabit Thigrus and the mount of Satnion,
and the peninsula, the journey's end of old,
utterly hated by the goddess Cyrita,
the all-shaped vixen's fire-tail,
the father, who with his daily mealings
healed the keen ox-hunger of his father,
as the blazing one cut foreign wings from the land.
And the Phrygian, avenging his brother's blood,
shall in turn ravage the nursing land
of the corpse-marshaller, who pronounces unflattering
judgments to the dead in an unloving manner.

having destroyed the cataracts, shall beautify the cheeks,
contriving a terror for the blood-drinking devourers.
By him all the Phlegraean land shall be enslaved

Brychon makes fat, the servant of the earth-born.
And of many woes by turns shall begin
Candaeus or Mamertus, or what must one call
him who is feasted in blood-mingled battles?
Not, indeed, shall the mother of Epimetheus yield,
but in requital for all she shall send forth one of the seed
of Perseus, a giant, for whom the sea shall be passable
on foot one day, and the land shall be sailed over
as he strikes the dry land with oars. And the houses of Laphria
Mamersa, set ablaze with flame
with the wooden bulwarks of their walls,
shall blame the oracle-babbler for the harm,
Psainynthus prophesying, the servant of Pluto.
And by the unmixed host all shall be devoured,
stripping off its bark-like double covering,
the fruit-bearing oak and the wild mountain-flourishing one.
And every flow of the mountain-streams shall be dried up,
as they slake greedily their black thirst.
And the cataracts of arrows hissing from afar
shall whistle above their heads, and, like Cimmerian gloom,
a shadow shall cover the air, dimming the sunlight.
And, like a brief-blooming rose, having made the Locrian
flourish, and having burned all, like a dry corn-stalk,
again he shall taste of a back-sailing flight,
as a maiden, near at hand, peering about,
dreads the gloomy wolf-light, the oaken tower,
terrified by the bronze-wrought blade.
And many contests and slaughters in the no-man's-land
shall loose of men, some in the wrestlings on land
as they contend about dread dominions,
others in the ox-turned backs of the earth,
until the blazing one shall lull the heavy tumult,
sprung from Aeacus and from Dardanus,
a Thesprotian both and a Chalastraean lion,
and, having overturned face-down all the house of his kin,
shall force the cowering chiefs of the Argives
to fawn upon the war-leading wolf of Galadrae
and to hold out the sceptre of the ancient monarchy.
To whom, after the sixth generation, a kinsman of mine,
a certain wrestler, having matched the might of his spear
of sea and land, and come to terms of reconciliation,
shall be hymned as most honoured among friends,
having taken the spear-won first-fruits of the spoils.
Why, wretched one, do I cry at length to the deaf rocks,
to the dumb wave, to the dread-haunted glens,
striking the empty noise of my mouth in vain?
For the Lepsian has robbed me of credence,
having smeared my words with false-speaking rumours,
and of my unerring prophetic mind, the foreteller of oracles,
in anger at the bed of which he longed to win.
Yet he shall make them true. And one, having learned with woe,
when there shall be no remedy to help the fatherland,
shall praise the god-possessed swallow.
So much she spoke, and back with her feet
she went within the prison. And in her heart
she groaned a last Siren-song,
a Mimallon of Claros, or the babbler of Melancraera,
daughter of Neso, or some Phician monster,
twittering her winding words past all interpreting.
And I came, my lord, announcing to you
this crooked tale of the prophetic maiden,
since you set me as a guard of the stone chamber
and to report and reckon over every word
truly, and sent me back as a swift courier.
And may the god make the rumours run out for the better,
he who cares for your throne,
preserving the ancient inheritance of the Bebryces.
