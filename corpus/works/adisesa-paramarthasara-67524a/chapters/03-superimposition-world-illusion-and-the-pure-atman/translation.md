**20**

Intellect, mind, and I-sense, the subtle elements (tanmātras) and the host of sense-faculties, and the aggregate of gross elements (bhūtas) — these, [products] of {{prakṛti / mūlaprakṛti|nature}}, competent only to bring forth and sustain the round of transmigration, are to be abandoned.

**21**

Merit and demerit, the constructs of pleasure and pain, dwelling in heaven and hell, birth and death, the [four] castes and life-stages — these do not exist here, in {{paramārtha|supreme reality}}.

**22**

Water in a mirage, silver in mother-of-pearl, a serpent on a rope — like the double-moon [seen] by one with diseased sight, the entire form of the world is an error ({{adhyāsa / bhrānti|superimposition / illusory error, as a snake on a rope}}).

**23**

Just as the one sun shines [reflected] in all reservoirs of water, so the {{ātman / paramātman|supreme Self}}, abiding in all the limiting adjuncts ({{upādhi|limiting adjuncts}}s), shines [as many].

**24**

Like space [present] within and without pots and the like, {{brahman|the absolute}} abides in all bodily lumps. The notion 'I' with respect to the body, which is not the Self, makes for the bondage of transmigration.

**25**

Free of all conceptual construction, pure, awakened, ageless and deathless, at peace, stainless, shining once for all, conscious — such is the {{ātman / paramātman|Self}}, all-pervading like space.

**26**

As juice, molasses, sugar-grains, jaggery, and candy are [but] transformations of sugarcane, so the differences of states, many in form, are [transformations] in the {{ātman / paramātman|supreme Self}} alone.

**27**

Discriminative knowing (vijñāna), the Inner Controller (antaryāmin), the vital breath (prāṇa), the Cosmic Person (virāj), the body, [the level of] genus, and finally the [individual] lump — these conventional designations (vyavahāra) regarding that Self are but particular states [of it].

**28**

There is no serpent on the rope — by what cause, then, arises the fear of a snake? Just so, the error of constructing duality is {{avidyā|nescience, the root of the rope-snake error}}; this [dual world] is not real.

**29**

This is that [great] darkness: that, through error, there is Selfhood [imputed] to the non-Self. Deluded men do not know Vāsudeva, the Self of all.

**30**

Spreading himself out, like a net, through the endless distinctions beginning with the vital breath, Vāsudeva [then] withdraws [it again] by his own power, as though at play.

**31**

By just those three — Viśva, Taijasa, and Prājña, called the first, the middle, and the last, [namely] waking, dream, and deep sleep, which are [all] of the nature of error — the {{turīya|the fourth, transcending the three states}} is veiled.

**32**

The God, as it were, deludes himself by his own {{māyā|deluding power}} in the form of duality; thus he [also] himself apprehends the Person ({{puruṣa|the conscious self}}), the Self, hidden in the cave [of the heart].

**33**

As, from a fire, by the risings of smoke, manifold shapes appear in the sky, so in Viṣṇu the creation, spread out as duality, appears by his own {{māyā|deluding creative power}}.

**34**

When the mind is calm [the Lord seems] as if calm, when it is glad as if glad, when it is deluded as if deluded; [but this is] only at the level of convention (vyavahāra) — the Lord is not so in {{paramārtha|supreme reality}}.

**35**

As the vault of the sky is not [really] soiled by [passing] clouds or by risings of smoke, so the supreme {{puruṣa|conscious self}} is untouched by the modifications of {{prakṛti / mūlaprakṛti|nature}}.

**36**

And [just as], although a single pot is covered with the grime of smoke and the like, the remaining [pots] do not [thereby] become defiled, so too here is the living self (jīva). [Note: 'eksaminn' is a scribal corruption for 'ekasminn'; the sense is 'although in a single pot'.]
