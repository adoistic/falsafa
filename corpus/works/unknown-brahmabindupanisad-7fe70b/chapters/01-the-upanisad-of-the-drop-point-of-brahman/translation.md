**1**

praṇava / oṃ (the sacred syllable OM). The mind, indeed, is declared to be of two kinds: the pure and the impure. The impure is that beset by desire and willful intention; the pure is that wholly free of desire.

**2**

The mind alone is the cause for human beings of [both] bondage and liberation. Attached to objects, it is held to be [the cause] of bondage; free of objects, [the cause] of liberation.

**3**

Since liberation is held to belong to this mind [only] when it is free of objects, therefore one who seeks liberation should make the mind constantly free of objects.

**4**

When the mind, its clinging to objects cast off and [itself] restrained within the heart, reaches the unmanī(bhāva) (the mind-transcending state in which thought ceases) state, then that is the supreme abode.

**5**

[The mind] is to be restrained just until it has gone to dissolution within the heart. This [alone] is knowledge and this [alone] is liberation; all else is [mere] elaboration of books.

**6**

[It is] neither the thinkable nor the unthinkable, [yet] the unthinkable [appearing] as the very thinkable. Then Brahman, freed from all partiality [toward either alternative], is attained.

**7**

One should join [the practice of] yoga by means of the audible [syllable, OM]; [then] one should meditate on the supreme as the soundless. For through the soundless mode of being, [true] being—not non-being—is realized.

**8**

That alone is the niṣkala (partless, undivided) Brahman, free of all distinction, nirañjana (stainless, untainted). Having known "That Brahman am I," one assuredly attains Brahman.

**9**

[Brahman is] free of distinction, infinite, devoid of [logical] ground and analogy, immeasurable, and beginningless—and [one is liberated] having known [it as] the supreme auspicious [reality].

**10**

There is no cessation and no origination, no one in bondage and no [path of] discipline, no desire for release and no release. Such is the paramārthatā (the state of ultimate truth/reality).

**11**

The Self is to be known as one and the same throughout waking, dream, and deep sleep. For one who has transcended the three states, there is no rebirth.

**12**

Truly there is but one bhūtātman (the Self present in all beings), established in every single being; it is seen as one and [yet] as many, like the jalacandra (moon('s reflection) in water).

**13**

As [with] the space enclosed within a pot, when the pot dissolves it is the pot that dissolves, not the space—just so is the jīva (the individual living self), which is likened to the pot [while the Self is the ghaṭākāśa (the space enclosed in a pot, simile for the jīva)].

**14**

[The bodily form,] like a pot, is of manifold shapes and is broken again and again; [the Self] does not [come to] know that it is broken, yet [the Self itself] knows [all] perpetually.

**15**

So long as one is veiled by the illusion of sound (word), so long does one abide in the [lotus of the] heart [bound to plurality]; but when the darkness is sundered, one perceives oneness—one [reality] alone.

**16**

The akṣara (the imperishable; also the OM-syllable) word [OM] is the [lower] supreme Brahman; on the cessation of which, that which is [the higher] imperishable [shines forth]. Knowing this, one should meditate on the imperishable, if one desires peace for oneself.

**17**

Two knowledges are to be known: the śabdabrahman (Brahman as sacred sound/word; the lower Brahman) and that which is the supreme. One who has become accomplished in the word-Brahman attains the parabrahman (the supreme, transcendent Brahman).

**18**

Having studied the book, the wise one—[having grasped its truth] through knowledge and realized insight—should discard the book entirely, just as one seeking the grain [discards] the palāla (chaff/straw left after threshing grain).

**19**

Of cows of many colors the milk yet has [but] one color; one should regard knowledge as [like] the milk, and the bearers of [outward] marks [the various doctrines] as [like] the cows.

**20**

Like ghee hidden within milk, vijñāna (realized, discriminative knowledge) dwells in every single being; it must be churned out unceasingly by the mind serving as the churning-staff.

**21**

Taking up [knowledge] as the eye, one should thereafter proceed [drawing up the knowledge] as fire [draws up the hidden ghee]. That Brahman—partless, undefiled, peaceful—is declared [to be realized as] "That Brahman am I."

**22**

And that which is the dwelling-place of all beings, which dwells over [and within] [all] beings as the bestower of grace upon all—That am I, Vāsudeva (Kṛṣṇa/Viṣṇu as the all-pervading Self).
