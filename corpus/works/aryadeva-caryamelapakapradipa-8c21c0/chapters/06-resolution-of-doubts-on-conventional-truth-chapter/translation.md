**CMP 6.1**

Through the cultivation of reality one attains a body resembling the rainbow (indrāyudha (rainbow, lit. 'Indra's weapon'; the illusory/rainbow body of the adept)). Thus [it is said].

**CMP 6.2**

When, with regard to aggregate, element, sense-field, faculty, and the rest, the two cognitions abide there equally, then, this great emptiness being present, one who has fallen asleep would indeed behold a dream, by the force of the wind.

**CMP 6.3**

Between the dreaming and the waking [state] there is no other difference; the one desiring the fruit of the dream would [merely] imagine [one]. By day and night the dream comes upon the creature; in the density of [the element] earth it has slept for a very long time.

**CMP 6.4**

When the fruit of the deed done is not [yet] ripe, the wind moves on again into [a new] birth here; when the fruit has become ripe, the wind, having departed, [moves] elsewhere—for swift indeed is death in the world.

**CMP 6.5**

Just as the Lord of Conquerors, established in the ten directions—and there is no marrow, bone, or flesh in his body—would [yet] enter [a body] for the welfare of beings, and accomplishes his task by way of fashioning the [bodily] elements [as illusion]—

**CMP 6.6**

—so, in due order, one whose mind is [as if] asleep while waking would [merely] wish for a fruit, [which is] a web of conceptual constructions. All dharmas are indeed dream-like; they are neither false nor not-false, for there is the absence of both [alternatives]. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 6.colophon**

[Here ends] the sixth chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages, concerning] conventional truth (saṃvṛtisatya (conventional, relative truth)). // 6 //
