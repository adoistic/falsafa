**CMP 9.1**

Sealing the cause by the fruit and sealing the fruit by the cause—[that] is to be contemplated; for one who [seeks] accomplishment otherwise, it does not arise in a billion eons. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.2**

Vajrasattva, the great King, is to be invoked again and again. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.3**

Having given up idle lounging (lit. "stretching out the feet") and abandoned the basket [-load] of saṃsāra, one should accomplish the foremost Vajrasattva, the mind ever exerted.

**CMP 9.4**

Having earnestly abandoned regret, sloth-and-torpor, and the rest—otherwise there would be no attainment, not even in a hundred billion eons. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.5**

Those who are fixed here in the [mere] state of a monk, and the people who delight in [dry] reasoning, and those fixed in the state of [proud] elders—to them one should not teach the reality. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.6**

Knowledge devoid of [tantric] means, and the [moral] training too, was taught for the disciples (śrāvakas), O great Hero; lay hold of [the deeper meaning] in their case [as well].

**CMP 9.7**

In the root tantra (mūlasūtra (the root tantra cited as authority)) too he says: "Those devoid of [this] knowledge desire [merely] the ten paths of wholesome action." Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.8**

(In MIA-flavored verse:) "You have trained in the vehicle of the disciples, O monk; [yet] one should course there in the bodhisattva-conduct. Course in the conduct of awakening among the qualities of a Buddha—this is to become the [very] ground of the Self-Existent One." Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.9**

By harsh, severe ascetic observances the body, afflicted, withers away; from suffering the mind is distracted, and from distraction the attainment [turns out] otherwise [than intended]. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.10**

In the root tantra too he says: "When practiced by harsh, severe ascetic observances, he is not accomplished; but when [reality] is served (and the consort enjoyed) through the enjoyment of all the sense-pleasures, he is swiftly accomplished." Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.11**

Passion, aversion, and delusion—these three are [like] adhering poison; they become [deadly] poison [only] when served with [further] poison [i.e. unwisely].

**CMP 9.12**

But again they become the nectar of immortality when served with the nectar [of wisdom]. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.13**

That by which the dull-witted is bound—by that very thing the wise one is wholly released; through the contemplation of awakening, all this is reversed.

**CMP 9.14**

That by which the deluded are bound and are crushed in the [hell] Raurava—by those very [things] they are released, with ease, through the power of wisdom. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.15**

Now, therefore, I shall fully expound the supreme, all-encompassing [maṇḍala]: the Saṃvara of the Net of Ḍākinīs of the Union of All Buddhas (Sarvabuddhasamāyoga-ḍākinījāla-saṃvara).

**CMP 9.16**

In the supreme, delightful secret [abode], ever abiding as the all-self, the glorious one consisting of all Buddhas, the blissful arising of Vajrasattva [dwells].

**CMP 9.17**

Adorned with the great divine jewel-nets of the Tathāgatas and the rest; thereupon, in [a palace] ablaze with an outspread canopy hung with bells—

**CMP 9.18**

—performing vajra-song and worship with songs and instrumental music, by means of flowers, incense, and the rest, and likewise with lamps, perfumes, and so on—

**CMP 9.19**

—they accomplish, in a dwelling or again in [a place] with a garden and the like, the Saṃvara of the Net of Ḍākinīs of the Union of All Buddhas.

**CMP 9.20**

There, first installing a seat, comfortable and soft to the touch, covered with the cloth of the universal lotus—that is the all-pure seat.

**CMP 9.21**

[There is] the Union of All Buddhas, the magical display of the Lord of Yoga; for there indeed [the deities] in the forms of glorious Vajrasattva manifest [themselves].

**CMP 9.22**

Consisting of all the elements, and likewise consisting of the root of life—by [the consorts] facing one's own tutelary deities, [there is] the setting-in-motion of the seal of attainment.

**CMP 9.23**

One should imagine the emblem-seal—cast, or fashioned, or [otherwise] wrought, finely painted, and bearing varied reflected images.

**CMP 9.24**

On those seats one should deposit all the [vital] breaths, each in its proper place; [the maṇḍala is] four-cornered, four-gated, adorned with four archways (toraṇas); and one should join vajra, jewel, lotus, and the rest with their gate-guardian.

**CMP 9.25**

With [consorts] facing one's own tutelary deities, with the women well prepared, lovely with their own seal-emblems—one should imagine the assembly-maṇḍala.

**CMP 9.26**

Four-cornered, four-gated, adorned with four archways, fitted with four [demarcating] threads, beautified with eight pillars—

**CMP 9.27**

—wrought with [pendant] necklaces and half-necklaces, ornamented with fillets, garlands, and festoons, resplendent with bells and banners, decorated with fly-whisks and the rest—

**CMP 9.28**

—and with crescent-moons and vajra[-tipped] corners at the junctions of the gate-turrets, adorned with varied cloths [bearing] bird-rows, gradations, finials, and the like.

**CMP 9.29**

With the enjoyments of all the goddesses, partaken of at will, through union with one's own tutelary deity, one should worship one's own self.

**CMP 9.30**

He is worshipped through the subsequent yoga, with the bliss of all union; and savoring [it] fully, he is accomplished through the supreme yoga.

**CMP 9.31**

By this, the one whose self is all the Buddhas would be accomplished, by the bliss of the elixir—[attaining] glorious Vajrasattva-[hood], [with] long life, youth, health, and true bliss.

**CMP 9.32**

[He becomes] the great body of all Buddhas, the eloquence (Sarasvatī) of all Buddhas, the great mind of all Buddhas, the great festal radiance of all Buddhas;

**CMP 9.33**

the great King of all Buddhas, sovereign of all Vajra-holders, lord of all the Lords of the World, supreme master of all the Jewel-lords.

**CMP 9.34**

Taking his delight with those [goddesses], he attains arising [as a deity] for the one [thus] proceeding; as the great Adept of all the goddesses, he is accomplished as a Universal Monarch (cakravartin). Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.35**

Daily, or monthly, or yearly likewise, or else according to [his] resolve, he should enact the Saṃvara of the Buddhas [as a dramatic play]. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 9.36**

Whether standing or seated or pacing about or however positioned, whether rejoicing or talking, here or there, in whatever manner—

**CMP 9.37**

—whatever takes on the character of a sense-path, that, by its very nature, even through an uncomposed [non-formal] yoga, he should bear as consisting of all the Buddhas.

**CMP 9.38**

Equal to space, space-like in form, comparable to the boundless ocean—[here] the method of the dharma of passion, [and even] aversion, [become] sport, an expanse of play.

**CMP 9.39**

Only the [bare] essential matter has been taken up, for the sake of instructing disciples; for how many further lifetimes could the teaching on passion be endured [if told in full]?

**CMP 9.colophon**

[Here ends] the ninth chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages], concerning the manifoldness of the bodhisattva's conduct and the arising and full awakening of dharma, by the method of the glorious Sarvabuddhasamāyoga-ḍākinījāla-saṃvara. // 9 //
