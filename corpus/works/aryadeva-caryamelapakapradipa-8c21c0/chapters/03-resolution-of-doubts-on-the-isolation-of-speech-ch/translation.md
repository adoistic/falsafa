**CMP 3.1**

The great jewel of five colors, only about the size of a mustard seed, one should always cultivate with effort at the tip of the nose through yoga. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 3.2**

[Cultivate] the breath as consisting of the five knowledges and having the nature of the five elements.

**CMP 3.3**

Having let it issue forth, one should imagine the great jewel of five colors in condensed form at the tip of the lotus-nose; this is remembered as prāṇāyāma (breath-control).

**CMP 3.4**

Then the Blessed One, the Great Vajra-holder, replied to the one who was expounding the meaning of the vajra-recitation, after the Teacher of the world had duly paid homage [to him].

**CMP 3.5**

This bodhicitta that has been revealed is only of mundane import (laukika (worldly, conventional)).

**CMP 3.6**

How then will those still to come [in the future] realize the transcendent (lokottara (supramundane, world-transcending)) [bodhicitta]?

**CMP 3.7**

The Dharma was indeed taught by you for the abandonment of all [false] views; how then is bodhicitta [taught such that] that very view is firmly fixed [again]?

**CMP 3.8**

Bewildered by the intentional, twilight language (saṃdhyābhāṣā (intentional, coded twilight-language of tantra)), they do not understand the meaning that is spoken; they grasp it just as it sounds, and accordingly they [merely] cook [their own confusion / ripen ill results].

**CMP 3.9**

Then the all-pervading Blessed One would say to Vajrapāṇi: "Excellent, excellent, O Lord of Secrets! By you the [need of those] to come [in future] has been [well] explained."

**CMP 3.10**

Bodhicitta is the wind, abiding in space, the very life-breath of beings, fivefold in nature and bearing ten names.

**CMP 3.11**

It is called the twelve-membered [chain of] dependent [origination]; by [its own] nature it becomes threefold. This, foremost of all the faculties, is the bodhicitta termed "wind."

**CMP 3.12**

Unmanifest, it is thus [called] subtle; [manifest,] it is called manifest. Resting upon it, a person performs deeds and engages objects and the rest.

**CMP 3.13**

[The rites of] pacifying, increasing, and likewise subjugating and fierce [magic]—all that [proceeds] from bodhicitta, from [its] resting upon the abode of the three realities.

**CMP 3.14**

And however many varied conventional designations of "light" (āloka (the inner radiances/lights of the dissolution sequence)) are imagined, they arise from bodhicitta; the conceptual constructions always [arise] from the wind.

**CMP 3.15**

The phenomenon of pleasure, pain, and the rest is produced from the [same] seed alone; this has the nature of bodhicitta, comparable to the [primordial] non-duality of the aggregates and the rest.

**CMP 3.16**

Through the single union of wisdom and means (prajñopāya (the union of insight (prajñā) and method (upāya))), bodhicitta becomes [the whole] world.

**CMP 3.17**

The master should recite [it] by himself, free of [discursive] meditation and study.

**CMP 3.18**

By the division of day and night, through the conjunction of moon and sun, the out-breath, pervading the world, is the one wind that is bodhicitta.

**CMP 3.19**

Having abandoned the fruit, good and ill, it becomes indeed space-like. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 3.20**

[Knowing] those named prāṇa, apāna, samāna, udāna, and vyāna, and [the five lesser winds] Nāga, Kūrma, Kṛkara, Devadatta, and Dhanañjaya—

**CMP 3.21**

—having made this image of oneself and then having generated the reality of the consort. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 3.22**

[The recitation-syllables/cyclic permutations:] koṭākṣa, koṭava, koṭa, koṭābha, and koṭīraka; kolākṣa, kolāva, kola, kolābha, and likewise kali. [These are the cycled phonemes of the vajra-recitation.]

**CMP 3.23**

Arising from the aperture of the nose, abiding in the five buddha-families, the fivefold wind, moving upward, courses through the body without cease.

**CMP 3.24**

From its conventional coursing through the nostril, issuing out from that door, these are fourfold: [those of] the left, the right, the pair [of both together], and the still [or blocked].

**CMP 3.25**

From the right, the element flows forth as the maṇḍala of fire; this red-colored mouth [-aspect], of the Lotus Lord [Amitābha], courses [there].

**CMP 3.26**

And from the left, the element flows forth, issued from the maṇḍala of wind, green and dark-hued; that of the Action Lord [Amoghasiddhi] courses [there].

**CMP 3.27**

The flow of the pair is the element of golden color, [together] with its triple [aspect], and the maṇḍala of earth (Mahendra); that of the Jewel Lord [Ratnasambhava] courses [there].

**CMP 3.28**

The still one [has] no outward flow; in a moment [it becomes] the water-maṇḍala (Vāruṇa); resembling pure crystal, that of the Vajra Lord [Akṣobhya] courses [there].

**CMP 3.29**

Having drawn up the whole element, held as both container and contained, the great body of Vairocana issues forth at the moment of death.

**CMP 3.30**

This [recitation of] the four maṇḍalas one should recite always, concentrated; the recitation of mantra-practitioners goes on continuously day and night, by the [proper] count of recitation.

**CMP 3.31**

He who, [counting] from one up through nine, is not bound by the ten—him one should know as unbound; he knows the supreme state.

**CMP 3.32**

The vowel- and consonant-phonemes, conforming to the number nine, in their unbound mutual combination—he who knows them is the Teacher of the world.

**CMP 3.33**

He who, having awakened, desires the attainment, the wealth of the fruit of liberation—he, formless, attains that very [goal] which is free of form.

**CMP 3.34**

Conjoined with the terminal element, abiding in the sixteen [phases] beginning with the first digit (kalā), joined with the set of five fives, and yoked with the three [or four]—

**CMP 3.35**

—with the nasal anusvāra and with the long [vowel], subject to the conjunction and elision of the grade-vowel (guṇa), the whole [recited] utterance would be short, neither manifold nor single.

**CMP 3.36**

By the meaning of the syllable ya, whatever is to be done is to be done by one who desires attainment; by the triad beginning with the r-sound (repha), the activity of the world proceeds.

**CMP 3.37**

The phonemes that are spoken from behind, and those again that face forward—these, [classed as] feminine, masculine, and neuter, are constructed [in correspondence] with the elements and the rest.

**CMP 3.38**

Joined below and above, known and applied with [discerning] intellect—what is taught by the six [is born] from the four, three, and one, [and is itself] born of the single existent that is without inherent nature.

**CMP 3.39**

Having yoked [the syllables] in the meaning of the first [triad] and the rest, the knowledge of the three times arises; like a dream or a magical illusion, having dispelled [the duality], one would [recognize/perform] the world within the triple realm.

**CMP 3.40**

This mantra is a withdrawal [of the senses] (pratyāhāra), born of a nature that is without inherent nature; the form transformed from it is what brings about the apprehension of the deity.

**CMP 3.41**

Conventional, abiding in the three realities, having the character of the recitation [arising from one's own] nature—this is the supreme bodhicitta, unnameable and unutterable.

**CMP 3.42**

That very [bodhicitta] would be the triad-as-one, without going forth and without coming, unceasing and wholly at peace, free of eternalism and annihilationism.

**CMP 3.43**

Its conceptual construction undone by the knower of the three times, having the character of non-difference from space—this indeed is the ultimate, having the character of being known each for oneself (pratyātmavedya (to be realized inwardly, each for oneself)).

**CMP 3.44**

In all activities indeed, in sitting and the rest, the knower of yoga should always recite [it]—the unnameable, the unutterable, [that which is] beyond the three times.

**CMP 3.45**

The recitations beginning with Pāṇḍarā are taught [to number] two hundred twenty-five; correctly multiplied by four, with the fourfold yoke, [they make] nine hundred.

**CMP 3.46**

And the nine hundred that is observed, with [its] twenty-four circuits, becomes—through their re-arising—twenty-one thousand six hundred [breaths per day].

**CMP 3.47**

Thus, in secret twilight-intention, the subtle yoga has been revealed; though free of [discursive] meditation and study, it is nonetheless called "recitation."

**CMP 3.48**

The syllable A is the foremost of all phonemes, of great import, the supreme imperishable; it is the great life-breath, the very non-arising, free of vocal articulation; it is the foremost cause of all expression, the radiant clear-light (prabhāsvara (clear light, luminosity)) of all speech. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 3.49**

[Cultivate] in the center of the heart the unstruck [sound], blazing like a lamp—the supreme, subtle imperishable, the syllable A, the supreme Lord. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 3.50**

These dharmas are designated through the sounding of words; yet neither dharma nor word is apprehended herein. And [only] by descending into the unity [of things], into [their true] dharma-nature, will one touch the unsurpassed, supreme acceptance (kṣānti). Thus [it is said].

**CMP 3.colophon**

[Here ends] the third chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages, concerning] the isolation of speech (vāgviveka (isolation of speech, a completion-stage phase)). // 3 //
