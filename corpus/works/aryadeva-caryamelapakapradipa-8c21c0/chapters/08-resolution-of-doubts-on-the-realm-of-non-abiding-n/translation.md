**CMP 8.1**

They call the coarse [level] that which consists of sound; the subtle, likewise, that which consists of mind; that which is devoid of mind is the supreme state of the yogins. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 8.2**

That reality is to be experienced for oneself; it cannot be told to another. It is to be reached through devotion and cultivation, and cannot be reached otherwise.

**CMP 8.3**

Having known reality, then having made devotion day and night—in that supreme nirvāṇa, the peaceful, unsurpassed state—

**CMP 8.4**

—then, by the power of that devotion, fashioned by the strength of cultivation, there arises in him a certain supreme form, born of bliss.

**CMP 8.5**

Arisen all at once ("dhag!") into manifest shape, blazing forth yet [also] taking the form of dissolution, illuminating all this—earth, atmosphere, and heaven—together with [all] moving and unmoving things.

**CMP 8.6**

By the force and power of cultivation, [it is] the blissful [state] consisting of wisdom and means, wholly freed from all afflictions, adorned with all [auspicious] marks.

**CMP 8.7**

Arisen from the knowledge of emptiness, free of duality, supremely blissful, endowed with the best of all aspects, free of grasped and grasper.

**CMP 8.8**

Cognition is illusion-like, pure, limpid, naturally stainless, transcending sound, smell, and taste, and likewise free of touch.

**CMP 8.9**

It is seen, supremely, only in samādhi, with the eye of knowledge—divine, like a shadow or an illusion, possessed of a divine form.

**CMP 8.10**

With its garland of surging waves of knowledge, [taking] manifold, various bodily forms—those who cultivate reality attain a body resembling the rainbow (indrāyudha (rainbow; the illusory rainbow-body of the adept)).

**CMP 8.11**

By the power of the yoga of cultivation and by the keeping of the pledges, such a form is attained—one that is not to be uttered even by the Conquerors.

**CMP 8.12**

Where there is no body, speech, or mind—the supreme, all-pervading place—there, by force of the [oral] transmission, one's own form is contemplated into being.

**CMP 8.13**

Oh, how marvelous! Oh, the peaceful, the supra-sensory! Oh, the supremely profound buddhahood, the highest state! Thus [it is said].

**CMP 8.14**

Oh the Vajra! Oh the Vajra! Oh the teaching of the Vajra! Where there is no body, speech, or mind, there [the divine] form is contemplated into being. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 8.15**

It is also said by the venerable Bhaṭṭāraka-pāda: "There is no hollowness in your body, nor flesh, bone, or blood; you have shown forth a body like a rainbow in the sky."

**CMP 8.16**

There is no disease, no impurity in [your] body, and no arising of hunger or thirst; [yet] by you, for the sake of conforming to the world, worldly activity has been displayed. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 8.17**

In the Sarvakalpasamuccaya too he says:

**CMP 8.18**

Having grasped the pure heart [-essence], [there is] the contemplation of the vajra-body; firm, substantial, without hollowness—he has attained the vajra-body. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 8.19**

(In Apabhraṃśa-flavored verse, addressing the goddess Prajñā:) "O you equal to space, free of dust, bearing the best of forms, bodiless, markless, daughter of wisdom; deep ocean of virtues, compassionate, peerless one—place your hand upon my head!" Thus [it is said].

**CMP 8.colophon**

[Here ends] the eighth chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages, concerning] the realm of non-abiding nirvāṇa (apratiṣṭhita-nirvāṇa (non-abiding nirvāṇa, the bodhisattva's non-localized liberation)). // 8 //
