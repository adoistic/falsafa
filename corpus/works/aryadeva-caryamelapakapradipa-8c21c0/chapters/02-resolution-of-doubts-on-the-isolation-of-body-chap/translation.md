**CMP 2.1**

In brief, the five aggregates (skandha (the five constituents of the person)) are proclaimed to be the five Buddhas; the adamantine sense-fields (āyatana (sense-bases)) themselves are the foremost maṇḍala (ritual circle, divine palace) of the bodhisattvas.

**CMP 2.2**

Earth is declared to be Locanā; the water-element is remembered as Māmakī; fire would be the one named Pāṇḍarā; wind is proclaimed [the consort] Tārā.

**CMP 2.3**

By means of form, sound, and the rest, the mantra-practitioner should always meditatively cultivate (bhāvanā (meditative cultivation, realization)) the deity.

**CMP 2.4**

Thus it was spoken by the Blessed One in the glorious Guhyasamāja (the Secret Assembly tantra, root tantra of this tradition) Great Yoga Tantra.

**CMP 2.5**

The [buddha-]families are taught to be of a hundred kinds, but in brief fivefold; and again they become threefold through the division into body, speech, and mind.

**CMP 2.6**

"In brief, the five aggregates are proclaimed to be the five Buddhas."

**CMP 2.7**

Resting on the five hearts [of the buddha-families], issued from the five [subtle] bodies, conjoined with the five winds, [the body] is made for the enjoyment of the five sense-pleasures. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 2.8**

Because it goes forth as the [out-]breathing (prāṇāyāma (breath/wind control)) and runs forth at all times through the sense-doors along their threads, it is called prāṇa (the forward-moving breath).

**CMP 2.9**

Because it carries away wind, urine, feces, and likewise semen and the rest, this is apāna (the down-voiding breath), as the yogins always observe.

**CMP 2.10**

That which constantly assimilates from every side what is eaten, chewed, licked, drunk, and sucked is called samāna (the digestive, equalizing breath).

**CMP 2.11**

By upward motion, by drawing together, and by the consuming of foods to be chewed and eaten, this [breath] is to be known as the action of udāna (the upward breath), through its union with cognition.

**CMP 2.12**

Pervading and sustaining, and going and coming and the like—because it pervades all the joints, it is called vyāna (the diffused breath).

**CMP 2.13**

Earth is declared to be Locanā; the water-element is remembered as Māmakī; fire is declared to be Pāṇḍarā; wind is proclaimed [as] Tārā.

**CMP 2.14**

Aggregate, element, and likewise sense-faculty—each fivefold, divided into five—each one of them is blessed and empowered by a Tathāgata: whence then could the deeds of saṃsāra arise [from them]?

**CMP 2.15**

Likewise the five external objects too, each one of them, are by the five Tathāgatas—each by its own—forever blessed; and the threefold knowledge rests upon the five.

**CMP 2.16**

One should contemplate the maṇḍala born of the all-adamantine, situated in the midst of the space-element, and should also perform the dissolution, if the bearer of the peaceful vajra so wishes. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 2.17**

The body of human beings is seen to be of fivefold nature, fixedly [composed] of just the five elements; cultivating their own mind, [also] fixed, by the existence and non-existence of those [elements], [practitioners] become Buddhas. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 2.18**

In the Khasama-tantra too he says (in Apabhraṃśa-flavored verse): "This whole world has the nature of the five Buddhas—behold the wondrous, variegated divine play! Therein indeed One, called Great Bliss, dances—One [yet] manifold—through the [varied] savor [of experience]." Thus [it is said].

**CMP 2.19**

"But in brief, fivefold."

**CMP 2.20**

Through the [meditative] settling of body, speech, and mind, [their] inherent nature is not apprehended; through the practice of the mantra-as-embodiment there is neither awakening nor [any reified] cultivation.

**CMP 2.21**

Having examined this in brief—the characteristic of body, speech, and mind—one should cultivate the concentration (samādhi (meditative absorption)) framed by mantra, conjoined with the [ritual] procedure. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 2.22**

[True] union does not arise in mere reflected images, in cast [icons] and the like; therefore, for the yogin of the great yoga of bodhicitta (awakening-mind; here also the subtle wind/seminal essence), the deities [are the practitioner's own reality].

**CMP 2.23**

The self is indeed the buddhahood of all the Buddhas, and the very heroic nature of the [awakening] being; therefore, through union with one's own tutelary deity, one should accomplish [realization in] the self alone. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 2.24**

With a body settled in mantra, with speech, and impelled in the mind, one should accomplish the supreme attainment (siddhi (spiritual accomplishment)), beloved as the contentment of the mind.

**CMP 2.colophon**

[Here ends] the second chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages, concerning] the isolation of body (kāyaviveka (isolation of body, a completion-stage phase)). // 2 //
