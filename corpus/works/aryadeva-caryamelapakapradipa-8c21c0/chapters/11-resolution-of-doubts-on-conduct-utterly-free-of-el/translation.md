**CMP 11.1**

On secluded mountains, and by rivers and springs, and in cremation-grounds and the like, this collection of meditations is to be practiced.

**CMP 11.2**

And the knower of reality should always, in truth, abandon the [ritual] practices and the rest; except for the "I-making" (ahaṃkāra) of Vajrasattva, he should make [no ego-clinging] elsewhere.

**CMP 11.3**

Even the [ritual] practices are not [needed to be] cognized when the pure reality is established—so long as that is being examined through union with the state of selflessness (nairātmya).

**CMP 11.4**

For one who abides in the state of non-inherent-nature and is endowed with divine means, whatever is prompted by intention is accomplished without [further] deliberation.

**CMP 11.5**

By the power of the yoga of cultivation, all that—whatever has the mark of attainment—presents itself of its own accord, in a single moment.

**CMP 11.6**

Should one regard the inner dharmas—form and the rest—it is called insight (vipaśyanā); should one imagine [them] as Akṣobhya and the rest, each in due number, it becomes calm-abiding (śamatha).

**CMP 11.7**

Because both of these are without inherent nature, [there is] that which is named the peace of suchness (tathatā); the yogin should make all the Buddhas enter into the maṇḍala of suchness. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 11.8**

In the great terror of the fire of nirvāṇa, not even an ash is released [unconsumed]; there reality is not found, nor the objects of the senses, nor the elements.

**CMP 11.9**

When the hero, free of conceptual construction, having forsaken the worldly mode of being, performs all [his] actions, then the Buddhas behold that.

**CMP 11.10**

He should move about like a child, [yet] with method, his doubts severed on every side; when the yogin is effortless, then the [spiritual] attainments rain down.

**CMP 11.11**

For those joined with every sin, well-fixed in the obscuration of delusion, through the yoga of the observance of the madman (unmatta-vrata), there come unfailing attainments within six months.

**CMP 11.12**

He would behold all the Buddhas himself; he is fulfilled with all desires; [there is] no waning and no diminishment—a body is born [for him] with life-span at his own will.

**CMP 11.13**

He fully attains the unsurpassed, supreme awakening without effort; without effort those eager for the bliss of all desires are accomplished.

**CMP 11.14**

Standing or seated, he should ever go to the profound state; through skill in the knowledge of the clear light (prabhāsvara (clear light, luminosity)), this is always the mark of the yogins.

**CMP 11.15**

By this yoga of meditation the jewel of the mind would become firm; and the Buddhas, established in awakening, grant [their] blessing-empowerment.

**CMP 11.16**

Having thus become settled, intent upon contemplation, the wise one, of concentrated mind, should cultivate [it] until the mind grows weary.

**CMP 11.17**

But when wearied, he should afterward wander about, acting as he pleases; with eyes slightly opened he should cultivate the vast awakening.

**CMP 11.18**

Laughing, talking, standing somewhere, somewhere he should set [his practice] in motion.

**CMP 11.19**

With mind attached to cultivation, [acting] in such a way that no weariness arises.

**CMP 11.20**

For the mantra-practitioner thus endowed with samādhi, free of conceptual construction, the unsurpassed state is attained, [all] limits of time abandoned. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 11.21**

[The signs of attainment are:] subtle form, light touch, pervasion and attainment-[at-will], radiance, steadiness, and mastery culminating in [the fulfillment of] every wish. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 11.22**

He sees [himself] as having attained the foremost knowledge of awakening, radiant as a Buddha; and he sees himself, lightly, as the enjoyment-body (saṃbhogakāya) of a Buddha.

**CMP 11.23**

He sees [himself] being worshipped by the great beings of the triple realm, and surely by Buddhas and bodhisattvas, with the five strands of sense-pleasure.

**CMP 11.24**

He constantly sees [himself] worshipped, the source of foremost great knowledge—[as] Vajrasattva, the great image, [as] Vajradharma of great renown.

**CMP 11.25**

He of great renown, [holder of] the secret vajra, sees his own image in a dream; the great Buddhas and the vajra-bearing bodhisattvas bow down [to him].

**CMP 11.26**

They will see such dreams, bestowing the attainment of body, speech, and mind—[and] a lovely goddess-maiden, complete with all ornaments.

**CMP 11.27**

Seeing a boy [and] a girl, he attains the accomplishment; he surely sees [himself] dwelling in the field of all the Buddhas of the ten directions.

**CMP 11.28**

With gladdened mind they give [him] the delightful treasury of Dharma—a body set upon the wheel of Dharma, surrounded by all beings.

**CMP 11.29**

He sees [this] at the time of yoga, established in the vajra of meditation. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 11.30**

Nothing is to be taken away from this, nor anything to be added; the real is to be seen as real—the one who sees the real is released. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 11.31**

And it is said in the Suvarṇaprabhāsa-sūtra: "The Buddha does not [truly] pass into final nirvāṇa, nor does the Dharma perish; [yet] for the ripening of beings he displays [the appearance of] final nirvāṇa."

**CMP 11.32**

It is also said in the Laṅkāvatāra-sūtra: "For nothing here arises through conditions, nor is [anything] ceased [by them]; it is only the [imagined] conditions that are constructed as arising and ceasing."

**CMP 11.33**

Therefore it is said: "With whatever state of being the minds of people are joined, by that they take on the nature of that—like a gem [that takes on] every color [reflected in it]."

**CMP 11.34**

That [process of] arising is conventional truth; death (the dissolution into clear light), indeed, is what is called ultimate truth. He who, through the guru's grace, becomes the knower of these two stages—he is a buddha-to-be.

**CMP 11.35**

The entry into the two truths [is] as if non-dual; though of unbroken form, it is indeed [a matter of] no [real] distinction. He by whom it is understood that "there is just unity of these two"—he is liberated.

**CMP 11.36**

Should someone fall from the summit of a great mountain, he might not wish to fall, yet he falls all the same; [so too] by the guru's grace, through the bestowed instruction, [the realizer] might not [even] wish for liberation, yet he is liberated all the same.

**CMP 11.37**

(The author's closing verse:) Reflecting on beings, and observing in the world the lack of capacity to attain reality—as if [gathering light] from a lamp-spark scattered by the breaking of the high waves of the ocean—approaching [the sources] little by little and gathering [them], this book has been composed by me; for those fortunate ones who are afraid of [taking] conventional truth [as ultimate], may it become a firebrand [to light their way].

**CMP 11.colophon**

[Here ends] the eleventh chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages, concerning] conduct utterly free of conceptual elaboration (atyanta-niṣprapañca (utterly free of conceptual elaboration)).
