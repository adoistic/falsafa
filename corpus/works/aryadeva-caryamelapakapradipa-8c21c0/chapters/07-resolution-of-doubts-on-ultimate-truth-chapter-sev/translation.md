**CMP 7.1**

Just as a lamp set within a pot does not shine forth outside, but once that pot is broken the lamp's flame thereupon shines forth—

**CMP 7.2**

—one's own body indeed is the pot; reality itself is the lamp; when [the pot] is broken by the mouth [-instruction] of the guru, buddha-knowledge becomes manifest.

**CMP 7.3**

He sees space [merge] with space arisen [from space], sky [merge] with sky; just so, this practice has been shown forth from the mouth of the guru. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 7.4**

These dharmas are designated through the sounding of words; yet neither dharma nor word is apprehended herein. And [only] by descending into the unity [of things], into [their] dharma-nature, will one touch the unsurpassed, supreme acceptance. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 7.5**

Homage to you, boon-granting foremost Vajra; homage be to you, Limit of the Real (bhūtakoṭi)! Homage to you, Womb of Emptiness; homage be to you, Awakening of the Buddhas!

**CMP 7.6**

Grant me, O great master, the vision of full awakening, the great knowledge of all the Buddhas, the unsurpassed all-emptiness.

**CMP 7.7**

Grant me, O great being, [that which has] the sole character of self-experience, freed from karma and birth; may I attain awakening here in this very [life].

**CMP 7.8**

Apart from the lotus of your feet there is no other refuge, O Lord; therefore be gracious, foremost of Buddhas, Hero of the world, Great Sage!

**CMP 7.9**

Having thus praised [him] with that divine, supreme rite of supplication, the glorious guru, an ocean of virtues, gives rise to compassion for the disciple—

**CMP 7.10**

—becoming serene of countenance, compassionate, and gladdened, he should impart [to him] the divine supreme pledge (samaya (sacred pledge, commitment)) that springs from the yoga-tantra.

**CMP 7.11**

Then the master, concentrated, should bring forth bodhicitta, and having deposited the bodhicitta in a vase or a conch—

**CMP 7.12**

—then, having summoned that disciple, blessed and empowered by all the Buddhas, he should bestow on him the pledge, endowed with the seal (mudrā) of the Conqueror.

**CMP 7.13**

The consecration (abhiṣeka (consecration, empowerment)) is to be given by the second Vajra-holder [the vajra-master], with auspicious uttered words and with the varied sounds of [many] instruments.

**CMP 7.14**

By the consecration of the triple realm, the disciple, with bowed head and joined palms—then, being urged thereto, [the master] should give to him what is [thereby] authorized.

**CMP 7.15**

The garland, the water, the fully-awakened [crown], the vajra, the bell, then the mirror, the name, and the authorization of [becoming] a master—this indeed is the sequence of consecration.

**CMP 7.16**

Then he should bestow upon him the mark of the outer full awakening, that which has been received in the [lineage] tradition, come down through the succession of gurus. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 7.17**

The yogin should constantly recollect the drop (bindu) that dissolves into clear water, in a river or a lake—[that] pure [drop], explained step by step in [its] sequence of distinctions; just as in a mirror the gradual vanishing of the breath-mist comes about and is no longer perceived in that manner, so the sequence [of practice], [namely] the apprehension of the condensed [drop], is to be discerned thus by those who follow the [proper] procedure.

**CMP 7.18**

Beyond the cultivation of all the limbs [of practice], free of conceiving and the conceived, wholly transcending [even] the [smallest] measure and drop—this is the supreme maṇḍala.

**CMP 7.19**

He who enters the stainless [reality] of all existents, in [their] forms and formlessnesses—the mind, awakened and impelled, [is] the maṇḍala, having the form of the maṇḍala.

**CMP 7.20**

"It is" and "it is not"—both are extremes; "pure" and "impure"—these too are extremes. Therefore, abandoning both extremes, the wise one does not even take a stand in the middle. Thus [it is said].

**CMP 7.21**

In the sense of "endless as space," the great resting-place of all beings—[that is the] glory, the splendor, the radiant King, the fulfiller of all hopes.

**CMP 7.22**

Oh the Buddha! Oh the Buddha! Oh the teaching of the Dharma! O bodhicitta, whose meaning is the pure reality, whose import is pure—homage be to you!

**CMP 7.colophon**

[Here ends] the seventh chapter, the resolution of doubts about the integration [of the stages, concerning] ultimate truth (paramārthasatya (ultimate, absolute truth)). // 7 //
