## Key passage (TextRank #1)
> [p-7b8e5d] When, however, a man wields superior power in a city which is open to the appeals of virtue, he should not give a footing to the base, nor command to those who are no commanders at all, nor confidence to those who deserve no confidence. But this is just what Nicias did when, of his own motion, he set Cleon in command of the army, a man who was nothing more to the city than a shameless brawler from the bema.

It was owing to Crassus that his city, but to his city that Nicias, suffered misfortune.
