2. C. Valerius Flaccus, Argonautica, Book Two.

Meanwhile, ignorant of the crimes and the grief, Jason
cleaves the deep; for Juno does not let him learn his father's
misfortunes, lest, burning, he turn aside from the midst of the waves
and rush rashly upon Pelias and the king's fates still standing in the way,
and abandon the deeds approved by the gods.
And now the rowan-clad heights of Pelion, levelled with the straits,
and the slanting temples of Tisaean Diana sink from sight;
now Sciathos has settled beneath the waters, now long Sepias
has receded; the Magnesian plain lifts up its horses
cropping the pasture: they think they have seen the Dolopian tombs,
and Amyrus entering the seas it had sought along the curving
shores, whose returning sails, with the river-wind,
they furl; the men bend to the oars. Then they salute
Eurymenae; the south wind, turning back, takes again the sail and the strait,
and as the Minyans seek the deep once more, Ossa
returns into cloud. Behold, the dread of the gods and Pallene,
condemned to war, and round about they see the monstrous shapes
of earthborn giants who once opposed the sky,
whom their mother, pitying, clothed in crags and tree-trunks and ridges
and built up into the air, mountains overturned.
Each one on his rock still keeps his threats and his battle
and his terror; the father himself shakes the storms and hurls from on high
thick thunderbolts; but the greatest terror is absent from those rocks,
Typhoeus, pressed beneath the Sicilian land.
Him, a fugitive, spewing sacred flames from his breast,
as they tell, Neptune himself, seizing him by the hair, bore off
into the deep and entangled him in the shoals, and as often as he rose again
with bloody mass and twisted the waves with his serpents,
gave him the Sicanian strait, and Aetna with its cities
brought upon him, pressing down his mouth. Fierce, he casts up the gnawed
foundations of the ridge; then all Trinacria together gasps,
while he stirs the mass flung upon his weary breast,
striving, and lays it back with a groan in vain.
And now the Hyperionian chariot drives toward the goals of the Iberian
sea, and the reins, carried forth, are loosed in the down-sloping
sky, when aged Tethys lifted up her palms and her bosom,
and the sacred Titan, the deep parted, resounded.
The hour had increased their fears, when they saw the face of the turning
Olympus, and at once the mountains and the places snatched
from their eyes, and round about the heavy darkness.
The very rest of things and the silences of the world terrify them,
and the stars, and the heaven studded with scattered fires.
And as one caught in an unknown region of roads,
who takes a night-wandering journey, rests neither with his ear
nor with his eyes, and the dark plain on either side increases
the fears of night, and the tree meeting him with larger shadows,
so the men tremble. But steadying their hearts,
the son of Hagnias says: 'Not without a divine power
do we steer this pine, nor did Tritonia teach me only the course;
often she herself has deigned to handle the keel.
Have we not learned by trial, when the day, sudden, the light put to flight,
bristled with rain? With what gales, by Jupiter, did we stand fast,
how great, and how often, and by Pallas' art,
in vain the steep swell of the tenth wave fell away?
Come then, O comrades; the sky shines unchanging,
and Cynthia has not risen with a swollen horn,
no redness on her face, and sure for such signs the Titan, whole,
sank into the waves and into unmixed gold.
Add that toward night the winds, both on sail and on sea,
press the harder, and the ship is swifter in the silent hours.
And indeed she shows me not those stars to follow
which, fallen from the pole, renew the sea: so great does Orion
now set, now Perseus hisses in the angry water:
but my guide is the serpent, which, never sunk in forbidden waves,
shines on its axis and coils its sevenfold fires.'
So he speaks, and recalls which faces of Olympus are sure,
the places of Pleione and the Hyades, by what star
the Sword may flash and by what light the Attic Bootes shine.
When he had said these words, with the gift of Ceres then they restore
their weary strength, and their hearts with a little Bacchus.
Soon they yielded to sleep; their stars guide the ship.

And now beneath the doubtful fires of the Eastern daughter of Atlas
the field grows white, and the fierce bears, driven from the sheepfolds,
seek safety and their homes, and the shore sends out to the deep
its scattered birds, when first Phoebus with his panting horses
drove Athos forth and scattered the day into the midst of the waves.
The sea is driven with eager oars, and the prows
tremble first in their course, and now Vulcanian Lemnos rises
from the topmost waters, dear to you through your various toils,
Lord of Fire; nor does the land drive you out for the madness and the crime
of its mothers, and it shames you not to remember your former service.

At the time when first Jupiter felt the hidden murmurs
of the heaven-dwellers rising, swollen by the newness of his reign,
and that the silences of celestial peace did not hold,
he first hung Juno from winged Olympus,
showing her the dreadful chaos and the punishment of the abyss.
Soon too, as Vulcan attempted to loose the chains of his frightened
mother, he rolled him down from the steep summit of heaven;
he fell from the pole through night and day
after the manner of a whirlwind, until at last on the shore of Lemnos
he crashed. When the sudden voice from there struck the city,
they find him leaning on a rock and pity him and warm him,
lingering, with alternate steps on his sick knee.
Hence, after the father granted him return to the heavenly heights,
Lemnos is dear to the god, nor is Aetna better known to fame,
nor the home of Lipare: these feasts, these temples,
the aegis once perfected and the wings of the dread thunderbolt shaped,
glad he visits. Opposite stands the ever-cold altar
of Venus in that place, since the goddess shuddered at the deserved
wrath of her husband, and silent chains held Mars.
Therefore she contrives a crime and stirs against deserving Lemnos
a frenzied destruction. For she does not wish to seem
kindly only: the same who binds her hair with smooth gold,
her starry bosom flowing free, is the same, fierce and vast,
her cheeks suffused with spots, most like the Stygian maidens
with their hissing pine-torches and their black mantle.

And now the day was here. The leader of Lemnos, who had routed
the Thracians in arms, who had dared to weave the ships with slender reed
and to cover the wicker frame with stretched hide,
brings back glad signs, and they moved the vessels full
of herds and of brides. And the barbarian dress
and the necklace are the mark of the place. The shout sounds over the sea:
'O homeland, O wife now anxious with various cares,
these handmaids we bring you as the prizes of a long war,'
when the goddess, troubled, hurls herself down through the clear sky
in a pitch-black cloud, and tracks wandering Rumour in the shade.
Her the almighty father, as she sings things worthy and unworthy
and scatters fears, keeps away from the calm regions
of the air: she, roaring, dwells beneath the lowest clouds,
goddess neither of Erebus nor of heaven, and wearies the lands
that are granted her. The bold first scorn her and then cherish her.
Soon she drives them all and shakes the towns with her stirring tongues.
Such a minister of crime and guile the goddess
seeks for herself, eager. The other sees first, and now flies of her own accord,
impatient, and now prepares her mouth, now rouses her ears.
Her Venus fires further and fills with these words:
'Go now, maiden, and glide down to sea-girt Lemnos
and overturn for me all the houses, as you are wont to run before
wars, when you feign a thousand trumpets and armed columns
on the plains and the snorting of countless horses.
Tell that the men will soon be here, captured by luxury and base
desire, and bringing the dear Thracian women to their beds.
This is your beginning; from this let grief everywhere
goad the raging mothers: soon I myself shall be present and lead them ready.'

She departs, and gladly is carried down into the midst of the city,
and seizes first Eurynome, whose threshold is nearest to Codrus,
worn out with cares and keeping her chaste bed:
she abides for her husband and wearies her handmaids
on the shores, who count over the times of the slow war
before the bed, and soothe their sleeplessness with long spinning.
To her the goddess, with tears and in the well-known dress of Neaera,
her cheeks struck, says: 'Would that I were not here a messenger to you,
O sister, or that the wave first overwhelm our griefs,'
she says, 'since at such a time for you your husband,
to one so deserving, whom you seek with vows and weeping,
alas, rages and plays slave to a captive's unworthy love.
And now they will be here, and a Thracian woman draws near your chamber,
not your equal in beauty, nor in skill with the distaff, nor in the praise of modesty,
nor the famous offspring of great Doryclus,
but a barbarian who pleases with painted hands and branded chin.
And yet perhaps you will console these misfortunes with another
marriage, and choose by a better fate a household:
but your offspring, robbed of a mother and condemned to a concubine,
unmans me, whom I see already eyeing the wretched askance
and the deadly feasts and the poisoned cups.
You know how we are a race like to flames; add that it is
their fathers' way for the Dahae to rage with blood. Now she will come hardened
with the milk of beasts, now with frost. But me too, driven out
by rumour from my husband, the striped one shall hold my bed,
and as a bride torn off upon a wagon.' So speaking, she cut short
her complaints and left her trembling with cares and tears.
She passes to Iphinoe, and fills with the same frenzies
the house of Amythaon and of Olenius; then through the whole city
she resounds, that they should all stir to drive the women out of Lemnos,
that the men and the Thracian women should rule the city. Grief and wrath rise:
each woman met tells and hears the same, nor to any
is the report unbelieving. Then with their voices they fill the gods and with complaints,
and now kisses upon their beds and kisses upon the very doorposts
they redouble, and with tears and with looking back they linger again.
They leap forth and revisit no more the dwellings of their men
and their chambers; they gather themselves together and beneath the bare stars,
crowded close, sharpen their lamentations and pray for dreadful
marriages and for Stygian torches at an unspeakable covenant.

Among these, in the midst, in the likeness of mournful Dryope,
Venus weeps, and the goddess, burning, presses on with savage laments,
and first: 'Would that Fortune had granted us to dwell
in the Sarmatian homes and to inhabit the dreary frosts,
to follow the wagons, or even to have seen our country's roof
driven by fire and the slaughter of its gods; for the rest of war
we endure. Does that madman destine me, me, to new
slaveries, or shall I, fleeing the city, leave my children behind?
Shall we not first arm our hands with the sword and the snatched fire,
and while they are silent and draw their sleep with a new bride,
shall love breathe some great deed?' Then, rolling her fiery
eyes, she shook her headlong children from her breast.
At once their minds are roused, and the hallowed groan of Venus
seizes the conquered hearts of the mothers: all of them
look out to the seas, and feign dances and cover the shrines
with festal foliage, and glad stand by for the men coming.
And now they seek their homes and tables; they recline in the high
porticoes; each man's wife, frenzied and hasty,
lies beside him, as beneath the night of the infernal abyss
Tisiphone reclines beside stunned Phlegyas and Theseus
and tastes the savage feast and the cups,
a kind of torment, and embraces them with black water-snakes.

Venus herself, shaking the pine-torch streaming with whirlwind,
gathers the darkness, and, girded for the fight, leaps
down trembling into Lemnos; with clouds and crashing light
the pole attends her, and the father augments her with honouring thunder.
Then, frenzied, she redoubles a new cry through the frightened
air, at which first Athos and the sea and vast
Thrace shuddered, and likewise upon their beds every
mother and every child stiffened, their breasts drawn tight.
Panic hastens up, and mad Discord from the Getic
stalls, and the dark Furies with their pale cheeks,
and Guile and Madness and the greater image of Death,
seen fierce with bare hands, as soon as at the summons
the war-bride of Mavors thundered and gave the signal.
Here Venus, beginning another crime and far more dreadful,
feigns the groans and the voices of the falling,
and bursts into the houses, and bearing a sobbing
mouth in her hand, her bosom drenched with fresh gore,
and her hair raised: 'Behold, the first I return, having avenged
my deserved bed; see, the day presses on.' Then with the lash she drives
the conquered into their chambers and finds swords for those who hesitate.
Whence shall I trace so many faces of crime, so many fates
of the slain? Alas, by what monstrous things has the order brought in the seer,
what a sequence opens itself. O who will stop me
as I sing the truth, and free our nights from this image.
They invade the entrances and the bodies once dear
to their own, some as they were, drowsy with feasting and wine,
some ready even to join hands and prepared
with great torches, certain men sleepless and watching all:
but to attempt flight, and to take up arms against them, fear
forbids: so vast did the hostile goddess make them appear,
and the well-known voice sounds greater than the husband's;
they only pressed their eyes with their hands, as if they saw the ranks
of the Eumenides, or Bellona flashing her steel above them.
This a sister, this a wife, and nearer still a daughter and a savage
mother, has power to do, and slaughters and drags those seized on their beds,
the womanly race, whom the monstrous Bessi
nor the Getic hands nor the wraths of the sea could lay low.
The gore flows in the chambers, and the gasping wounds smoke
in the breast, and on their beds, in wretched struggle, the trunks
roll down. Others fling dreadful torches upon the gables
and add the houses. Some flee in haste from the black
fires, but the hard wife besets them at the threshold,
and at the sight of steel they seek the flames again.
But others tear the Thracian women, the blot and the cause
of their frenzies; mingled groans and the barbarous cry
of those praying, and the unknown voices were filling the air.

But for you what mouth worthy of your mighty daring
shall I bring, glory and one praise of your falling country,
Hypsipyle? No ages shall steal you, named in my song,
away, if only the centuries endure in the Latin annals
and the Trojan household gods and the palaces of so great a realm.
Daughters and brides alike had rushed in, driven,
and now the whole island had blazed with the monsters scattered abroad.
She, arming her pious hands: 'Flee at once the city
and me, father; no enemy,' she says, 'no Thracians who have wronged us
hold the walls: this is our crime; do not ask who its author.
Flee now, now snatch this gift of a wavering mind, and the sword,
do you rather, take pity, hold yourself.' Then she takes up his limbs
and veils his head, and silent to the temple privy to Bacchus
hurries him, and stretching her hands from the first threshold:
'Free us from crime, father, and pity the pious
again,' she says. Then in the silent seat she set the trembling man
beneath the feet and the right hand of the god; he lies hidden, received
in the sacred robe; the choir and the triennial bronze return their
voices and their sound, and the lynxes fixed at the threshold roar.
When the queen saw Aurora rise on her rosy car
and the houses, wearied at last by the sleepless whirl,
fall silent, since the best deeds give a steadfast
spirit and greater daring for pious undertakings,
she put upon her father the garlands and the youthful hair and the garments
of Lyaeus, and set him in the midst of the car, and round about the bronze
and the drums and the chests full of silent dread.
She herself binds her bosom and her limbs with the servant's ivy
and shakes the vine-clad spear with windy blows,
looking back, that, veiled, he may hold the green reins
like the father, that the horns may swell from his snowy mitre,
and that the sacred cup may show forth Bacchus. Then with sharp
shriek she drove the strong doors, and is borne through the city
singing such things with her voice: 'Leave, O Bacchus, for me
the house dripping with slaughter; let the sea expiate you,
defiled with death, and I shall bring back the lotus-eaters to the temples as dragons.'
So she went forth amid the terrors; for the god himself makes her revered,
and, not unknowing, swells with panting breath.
And now the old man, far removed from the savage city in the silent
woods she had hidden, but Panic, conscious of the deed,
and the cheated Fury, trouble her by night and day.
She dares no longer to bear like dances (once the rites deceive),
nor to approach her father's groves by stealth,
and flight must be sought for the wretched man by various arts.
A ship was seen, worn out by the labours of the savage wave,
which a distant day had stored up for Thetis and Glaucus,
and the moon was scorching it with the suns and the hoary frosts.
Hither, through the dark silences of deep night, she snatches
her father headlong from the woods, and thus, mournful, she speaks:
'What a homeland, father, what great houses now you leave
empty of their youth. O dread plague, O ruin of the bitter
night. Can I entrust you to such a vessel,
dear parent? Can I keep you back amid such great perils?
Late, alas, we pay our crime to the Furies. Grant my prayers,
goddess, who now draw the sleep-bringing car over the sea.
Not peoples, not rich soil, not any realms
do I ask for my father; let it be allowed to depart from our native land.
When, my father saved, shall I be borne glad through the midst
of the city? When shall I see tears and beatings of the breast here?'
She had spoken. He, anxious, flees afar in the cut alder,
and comes to the groves of the Tauri and the savage shrine of Diana.
Here you, goddess, set him over the grim altar,
the sword given; nor is the delay long for you in the bloody lands:
soon the grove of Egeria, soon lofty Jupiter from Alba calls you,
and Aricia, not gentle to the one king alone.

His daughter seeks the citadel, where now the bristling band of mothers
had gathered. With hoarse roar they sit in the places of fathers
and of sons, and in the empty city upon the walls
they make new laws; they grant her, as deserving, the throne and the paternal
sceptre, and to the pious mind its own rewards return.
Behold, far off they mark arms making for Lemnos
with strong oars; the queen is hurried by a sudden tumult
and calls a council. They would not have lacked the wicked frenzy
to bear opposing weapons and hostile fires,
had not Mulciber broken the savage wraths of Venus.
Then too the seer Polyxo, beloved of Phoebus—
not her country, not her race is certain, but the greatest . . .
. . . and ambiguous Proteus feigns himself from the Pharian caves
to have steered his ways hither over the seas with his yoked seals;
often he hides himself in the lowest waters, and, lingering a little,
rises as if reporting in the flood the voices he has heard—
'Let us give them harbour,' she says, 'this ship, believe me, comes
by fate, and a kindlier god has turned hither over the sea, easier
than Lemnos, the Minyans; Venus herself, willing, grants that bodies be joined,
while the womb has strength and the maternal age suffices.'
The words please, and Iphinoe carries the prayers to the shore to the Greeks;
nor is the throng harmful, and they show no signs of the recent
crime, and Cytherea takes away the place's fear.
. . .
At once a huge bull, under the name of the chiefs,
she throws down, and now renders pious gifts to the unaccustomed
temples, and at this first altar of Venus the heifer grows warm.

They had come to the cliff, whose hanging rocks smoke
on the black ridges, and the air is cooked with vapours.
The son of Aeson halted, and here the queen urges them
to pray, and teaching the reasons: 'These caverns you see,
and behold the homes of Vulcan,' she says; 'give wine and prayers.
Perhaps this deed the thunderbolt now keeps silent in the cavern:
the night itself will give proof, when, stranger, you will marvel
at the murmurs of the enclosed flame and the sound of the beaten mass.'
Then she boasts the walls and the strength of the place and the ancient
wealth of her forebears. In the midst of the halls the handmaids
make ready the feasts; the fiery couch shimmers with Tyrian purple.
The Thracian band stands mourning their ancestral kings and their kingly
husbands, all those believed to have feared the marriage torches
and not to have touched the lady's holy bed.
Now the son of Aeson, now the queen took her place
in the midst, then the other chiefs; while their first hunger is conquered
by the sacred entrails, Bacchus goes round in the bowls, and all
the hall is silent; soon, the feasts begun, they beguile the hours
of night, and prolong them with talk into the late shadows,
and especially Hypsipyle, marvelling, asks of the leader's
fortunes, what fates draw him, what force of a king drives him,
or whence the mass of the Haemonian ship: she clings
to the talk of one alone, and little by little gathers the gentle fires,
no longer hard to love nor unkind to it returning,
and the god himself indulges the delays and the space for love.

Jupiter, rolling his eternal work, had moved the Pleiad
by the law of the sky with its cloudy star, and at once
all things rush upon the waves, and at one stroke of the god Pangaea
and Gargara and the mournful groves stood still in dread.
No fiercer at any other time does terror drive the mortal
nations; for then Astraea presses, then demands wrath
against the peoples from Jove, and, the lands left behind,
Saturnia invokes the stars with ceaseless complaint.
The black East wind follows, and with his great brothers
thunders on the Aegean, and the sea stretches toward the shores;
and on the fourth rising the daughter of Thespius sees the moon
thick with rains, and a long fear restraining the men's
undertakings and the waves. Still toward the new fires of the kinder goddess
the Minyans sit glad in the city, and free of care
they indulge the widowed chambers, and to draw out the storms in luxury
and now to wish no journeys, and they pretend not to hear
the Zephyrs calling, until the Tirynthian hero
could not endure their idleness, keeping watch himself over the ship and untouched by the city:
'The gods have envied us,' he says, 'merely for attempting the sea's expanse,
and our homes deserted, and the vows of fathers cheated
by sluggish time. And shall I myself stand by the men as they linger?
'O wretched, all of us who have joined ourselves to your deeds.
Give back Phasis and Aeetes and the perils of the Scythian sea,'
he says, 'son of Aeson: with you alone into the deep
the love of great things drew me, while there is hope for me to halt the Cyanean
mountains and to spoil another sleepless dragon.
If it is settled to dwell on the cliffs of the deep Aegean,
this my Telamon shall accomplish with me.' When these words were said,
not otherwise the son of Aeson, kindled by the bitter warnings,
as a warhorse, whom the cold earth in long peace
delights, sluggish, is vexed in idle circles to the left,
yet would wish the reins and the master, if the warlike
cry and the crash of the bronze, forgotten, should again fill his ears.
Then he calls Argus and Tiphys, and hastens that all be made ready
for the sea; the master with a mighty shout seeks
arms, men alike, and the oars scattered on the shore.

A new grief arises in the city, and lamentation through all
the houses, and the ancient look. That the walls are left to them,
behold, again; and when will be the time for sons, a race
to renew them, to wield the sceptres? Now the sad deed of the unspeakable
night, now those silences of the widowed dwelling
are more savage, because they dared to enter the chambers and the broken
bonds, and again to admit such cares.
Hypsipyle herself too, when she saw the sudden runnings along the shore
and the men departing from all Lemnos,
groans and addresses Jason with such a complaint:
'Does it now please to launch the sails at the first calm,
O head dearer to me than my father? Just now the savage
seas have grown quiet. So would the ship flee the harbour, if the harsh
Pleiad had held you on the shore of opposing Thrace.
Did we then owe delays to the sky and the course-holding
waves?' she said, weeping, and brings out gifts to cling to the dear
leader: a cloak and labours woven into it.
There she had pressed with the needle the rites privy to her saved
father, and the pious car: the savage ranks of the fearful
stand and give place; round about, in the green
wood, the bristling weapons tremble; in the midst the anxious father flees through the shadows.
A part had also rendered the rape from leafy Ida
and the glorious flight of the boy; soon, glad in the heaven,
he stood by the tables, nay, the very armour-bearer of Jove
receives the charming cups from the Phrygian attendant now.
Then, bearing a sword and the well-known emblem of Thoas:
'Receive it,' she says, 'that in wars and in the dust of mid-battle
I may be a companion, the flaming gifts which the father of the Aetnaean
god once wore, now worthy to be joined to your arms.
Go, remember, go, the land which embraced you in its quiet
lap first, bring back, and from the subdued shores of Colchis
the sails, by this Jason whom you leave in our womb.'
So she speaks, sinking upon the neck of her Haemonian husband.
And no less sad upon your neck, Orpheus, and on yours,
son of Aeacus, and the bride hangs upon either Castor.
Amid these tears the sluggish anchor is gathered from the sands.
Now the oars have snatched the ship away, now the breezes carry it;
the foamy track of the fleeing rudder follows after.
Then slender Lemnos and the Electrian land pass by,
hidden by Thracian rites. Here is the vast dread
of the deity and the atonements decreed for heedless tongues.
This land the storm, sent down by Jove, never dares to harm
with the wave. Of his own accord the god himself then roughens the waters,
when he forbids faithless sailors to touch his shores.
But meeting the Minyans on the land and at the shrines, the priest
Thyotes receives the guests, unbarring the secrets.
Thus far to the peoples, Samothracian rites, will I speak:
the rest, depart, and let us keep the dread for the hidden mysteries.
They, glad at the new sun and full of the gods,
sit at the benches. Now the cities the sailor had foreseen
he was leaving behind, and Imbros had drawn near the prow,
and the sun in mid-course had climbed the heavenly heights.
Then first the Thessalian ship touched the Dardanian sands
and by fate settled on the Sigean shore.
They leap down: here some lift the gleaming camp with light
sails, some break the spelt of the grain with a dragged stone,
another shows swift fire from the struck cliffs
with leaves, and feeds it with friendly sulphur.
Alcides, with Telamon for companion, while along the curving shore
in its winding bay they wander, a voice reaches their ears,
coming up plaintively, when the broken wave murmurs back.
Astonished, they checked their step and follow the empty
path of the voice. Now it sounds sure, and what man or god
did the maiden, abandoned to cruel death, not call upon?
The more keenly for this the men press on, resolved to help;
just as when a bull has filled the trackless place with bitter groaning,
bearing on his high back the lion breaking him with its bite,
a band of countrymen, roused, gathers from the scattered hut,
and the farmers with blind shouting.
Alcides halted, and straining his gaze upon the high
rock, sees the cruel manacles and the maiden's fainting face
and her eyes swelling at the first bendings of grief;
just as mournful ivory, lifeless yet shaped with much art,
or Parian marble takes on marks and names,
or the liquid colours render their wonders.
The leader says: 'What name and race are yours, maiden,
what is this lot, tell me; why do chains bind your hands?'
She, trembling, her eyes cast down with sad shame,
'I am not worthy of these ills,' she says; 'the last gifts
of my parents you see, the cliffs thronged with purple and gold.
We are the once-happy race of Ilus, until envious Fortune
fled from the house of Laomedon.
At the beginning a plague, and the balance of the air, driven from the clear
sky, the fields blazed with rival pyres,
when a sudden crash, and a flood moving the Idaean
woods together with the stalls: behold, suddenly there rose from the sea
a beast, a monstrous thing; this you could measure neither by any mountains
nor by our sea. To it, raging,
amid the embraces and lamentations of my parents, the first of youth
is given. This the oracles, this horned Hammon commands,

This the place and the most mournful face of the captive

such as the path of toiling Nemea, or of Erymanthus,

Meanwhile from afar Neptune gives the signal, and at once
the monster-bearing bays bellow, and the Sigean pest
gathers the strait, whose starry eyes tremble in a grey-green
cloud, and in order its curved mouth, with triple-forked,
lightning-like crash shakes, and, the sea remeasured,
the tail returns, and the lofty neck snatches up the opened bays.
That one, as it bears down through a thousand coils, the sea
attends, leaping at its side, and its own storm drives it rushing
to the trembling shores. Not with equal waves
comes the surge of the cloud-bringing South wind, nor does the African,
so great upon the deep, exult, nor when, full, with the reins of his fathers,
Orion lifts the sea with the blast of his two-footed horses.
Behold, the son of Aeacus is stunned that the leader grows fierce
with the frenzy of the longed-for fight, and rises from the couch,
huge in his array, and that the heavy quivers beat his back.
He, having prayed to his father and the gods of the sea and his own arms,
leapt upon the cliff, and shuddered at the sea moved
from its seats and the spacious coils of the lofty monster.
As when from the cold valleys of Hebrus Boreas
is lifted and hurls the flying clouds through the Riphaean
heights, and does not yet hold all in a pitch-black sky,
so that one at once thrusts forward its horrific mass and rocky
back, and comes on with a vast shadow. Ida trembles,
and the ships are dashed, and the prone towers rise again.
Alcides seizes his bow and presses it with the whole
cloud of his quiver. That one is no more moved than from its seat
is great Eryx, though the rains should wish to bring it down to the valleys.
Now the air is short and useless for the flying shaft.
Then indeed a roar, and the madness of his vain attempt,
and silent shame, and again the maiden growing pale.
He flings the arms from his hand, looks back at the cliffs
and the neighbouring rocks, and as much as antiquity, aided by the winds,
had thrust forward, or the sea's crash, so much he tears off from the lowest
seat of the sea, shaking it. And now with all its company
the monster is at hand, and now, nearest, gapes for the wretched prey.
Lifted up in the midst of the waters he stands, and Alcides receives
it as it rushes, and first with the rock the rising
neck he overwhelms; hence the vast blows of the knotted oak
he redoubles, until it is carried down to the lowest waves,
now wholly dissolved in the shoals; and the Idaean mother
and the choir and the River-gods howled on the topmost hills.
At once from the cliffs and the dark valley the shepherds
rise again and seek the city with great shouts.
Hence Telamon as messenger calls the comrades, and at once they themselves
shudder and suddenly see the ship in blood.
And no less to the cliffs and the peaks of the rough rock
Alcides darts, and, the holding bonds removed, takes away
the maiden's hands from the crag, and fits the arms
to his proud shoulders. Then making for the king he was passing the safe
shores with exulting step, as through the pastures a victor
a bull advances, now swelling in the neck, now taller in the shoulders,
when he revisits the high stalls of his accustomed herd
and his native grove and the loves he has avenged in war.

Against him, sent out from the long darkness,
a throng of Phrygians, and drawing his little son with his wife,
Laomedon; now he groans for his horses, now that the owed
gifts are demanded. A part rings the heights of the airy wall
and marvels at the youth in unknown arms.
Him, eyeing grimly, and slippery with sharp cunning,
the king approaches, and, ill-glad with a father's love, speaks:
'Greatest of the Greek-born, whom not seeking the Sigean
shores nor pitying the deaths of our Troy
chance itself has brought to these places, if true is
the report that your father is Jove and your race that of the highest
Thunderer, you are present as ours and come as one joined to us: one and the same
is the begetter and the honour of the stock, though by long shores we are sundered.
After how many tears for me, after how great atonements of fathers
you come late, how small now is the glory for your deeds.
But come now, bring your comrades within the brotherly walls;
let tomorrow's light show the yoked steeds from the open stalls.'
He had said these things, and silently turns over guile and dread
crime in his heart, that, shut in the chamber and heavy with sleep,
he might slay him, and mock the oracles, the quiver snatched away;
for he had heard that Pergamum was twice owed
to the Herculean shafts. But who could now overturn from Priam's realms
the fates? There abides, with unmoved courses, the . . . night,
and the race of the Aeneadae, and the honours of a better Troy.
'Us,' says the Tirynthian, 'to the mouths of the Scythian sea
the journey snatches; soon we shall be borne back hither to your shores
and I shall bring the promised gifts.' Then indeed to the gods he called
the more, and assented to them. The faithless promises of the tyrant
the Phrygians now, and the perils of wretched Troy, were weeping.

Hence the canvas is spread with all its wings into the night,
and glides past the shores and the mounds of ancient Ilus
and the Dardanian father; with wakeful sport at once they see
all the festivities; here from the wave, here from sacred fires Ida
flashes, and the horror-sounding Gargara answer the boxwood pipe.
Then, when now they had held the silences of the mid-sea
and the whistling breezes help, they were approaching the Phrixean
waters and the narrow throat once without a name.
But behold, at first light, gaping, the swift wave
terrified the ship, and Helle stood forth with her fillets,
now the sister of Panope and Thetis, and now in her left hand
holding a golden sceptre. While she smooths the waters, she beholds
the chiefs and the leader, and addresses Jason with calm words:
'You too from the Haemonian lands across unknown seas
the fates bear to a realm hostile to your house and most like
to our fate; again Fortune scatters the grandsons
of Aeolus, and, unhappy race, you seek the Scythian river.
A vast land beyond, a long sea (fail not in your undertakings),
and Phasis himself far off, but true, will give you the mouth.
Here is a secret grove, and twin altars stand on green
mounds; here first bear with your hand the pious offerings to Phrixus,
and to his ashes, I pray, render my words:
not, brother, through the silences of the Stygian bank, as you think,
am I driven; in vain you search, dear one, the ways of empty
Avernus; for me, driven by the cliffs and the waves,
the storm does not break: at once Cymothoe and Glaucus came beneath me
with swift hand as I rushed down; the father of the deep himself
gave me these very seats, these realms with even-handed power,
nor does our bay envy the waves of Ino.'
She had spoken, and bore her mournful face beneath the calm seas
with a groan, as her ancestral griefs returned.
Then, pouring wine upon the sea, the leader thus begins:
'Glory of the waves and of your race, Cretheian maid,
open the way and drive my course, goddess, with a favouring passage,'
and he sends in the ship, and flies between the cities in the midst,
where the tide rages with shallow waters, and Europe, more monstrous
with its broken shores, escapes Asia pressing upon it.
These lands too, and the fields linked to the nations,
thus with the sea beating, I think, Neptune's spear once
and the long toil of opposing time cut away,
as it cut the Sicilian and the Libyan flank, and Janus
was stunned at the crash, and Atlas, ruler of the western mountains.
Now they pass the heights of Percote and Parium, infamous
for its broken shallows, and Pitya, and Lampsacus
is left behind by the ship, which neither the triennial rites
of Ogygian Bacchus nor the Phrygian frenzy brings in from secret caves,
but its own god snatches it to Venus. Its altars
above the city and the veils of the lofty temple they see.
Hence the land is rarer, and on every side the vast sky
again, and the prospect beginning into another world.
A land lies with its middle bay between the Pontus and Helle,
as if brought forth from the bottom of the sea; for, wicked,
it has thrust forth its fields into the blind shallows, and over the seas with a long ridge
drives the shore: here on one side, with bordering coasts,
a part holds old Phrygia, on the other the pine ridges of the divided mountain.
Nor far off, at the confines of the narrow sea, rises
a city, set on gentle ridges; king of the rich land
is Cyzicus. He, when he saw the new signs of the Haemonian
keel, himself of his own accord runs forth to the first waves
and marvels at the men, and embracing the right hand and clinging
begins: 'O band of Aemathia, now first known
to our lands, and an image to me greater than the report.
Yet not so far removed nor steep is this land,
nor long impassable to the peoples of the Eastern light,
since I behold such leaders entered, so many strong men.
For though the bristling land here nourish savage races,
and the roaring Propontis flow about me with its swollen mouth,
your good faith and like rites and gentle in worship
are the hearts in these places too for me; far off the savage valour
of the Bebrycian, far off the harshness of the Scythian rite.'
So he speaks, and snatches them glad, and at once bids the hospitable
roofs be opened, and grants to the temples sacred honours.
The couches stand with gems and gold, and the tables with kingly
array, and a hundred matched attendants, young
of body; some bear the feasts in their hands, some the golden
cups, graven with the chances of recent wars.
And as he first offers the bowls to the Greek leader,
Cyzicus says: 'This harbour the enemy terrifies for me,
this army it brings back by night, these turned backs of the Pelasgians
you see, this is my fire that feeds upon the ships.'
The son of Aeson rejoins: 'Would that now wrath might bring
the Pelasgians and attempt to clash with their wonted thefts,
and all the band pour itself upon the ships: you will see
the friendly arms, nor after this night any more battles for you.'
So he speaks, and amid these, with various words, the long night
snatched its turns, and likewise the following day in a like course.
